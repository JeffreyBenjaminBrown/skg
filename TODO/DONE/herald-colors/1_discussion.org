*** Summary
The vision is coherent and mostly implementable. Its central idea is that a
herald's color states its *importance tier*, or for a few heralds its
*polarity*, rather than its kind. That idea is also the principle the code
lacks: today the server names colors by hue, the clients name some faces by
hue and others by meaning, and two of the hue names are swapped. My main
recommendation is to make the tiers a real vocabulary everywhere: wire
keywords, Emacs faces, Neovim highlight groups, and docs.

What still needs deciding: about a dozen heralds the vision does not
assign, the colors of ancestor flags and fraction parts, and a collision
between the proposed diff X and the existing existence-change X. The
smaller items are open questions at the end.

********* Claude says: here's the engine, and the problem is smaller than I said
********** How the engine turns metadata into heralds
The rule table in 'server/heralds.rs' is a tree of rules shaped like the
metadata. Each rule has a label, and it fires when the metadata
contains that label at the same position, e.g. the rule 'hiddenFolder'
fires on the atom 'hiddenFolder'. A firing leaf rule emits one /token/.
A token is the rule's literal strings joined with ':', and the whole
token gets the rule's one color.

The renderer then lays tokens out in rule-table order, with a space
between consecutive tokens. A token from a rule marked ABUT gets no
space before it, so it glues onto its predecessor. Finally the renderer
deletes "structural" colons, meaning any colon that touches a
non-alphanumeric character. That is how a rule like '("3" "{")' once
became =3{= rather than =3:{=.
********** Why ☮ was baked into the label strings
Put =☮= and =It hides these…= as two strings in one rule, and you get
one token, =☮:It hides these…=. The colon touches ☮, so it is
stripped, leaving =☮It hides these…= with no space, in one color.
'leaf_ro!' avoids this by gluing "☮ " onto the label at compile time,
so the rule has a single string. But that string is still one token,
so it is still one color.
********** The fix, and my correction
I said earlier that a separate ☮ token would need a trailing space or a
separator rule. That was wrong. Each rule scans the metadata
independently ('skg--apply-ordinary-leaf' in
'elisp/skg-sexpr/skg-lens.el'), so two rules may match the same atom.
Then '(CRUCIAL hiddenFolder "☮")' followed by
'(MESSAGE hiddenFolder "It hides these…")' emits two tokens, which
the renderer separates with its ordinary space: =☮ It hides these…=,
in two colors. No ABUT and no colon stripping is involved. The cost is
one extra rule per write-protected label, and 'leaf_ro!' disappears.

**** Relation sides could be a table
The vision's per-relation rules form a small table: for each relation
and side, whether the side is usually visible elsewhere, and its tier.
That table belongs in one place, not spread across conditionals.

The table below includes the decisions made above.

| Relation   | in-side (N nodes R it)          | out-side (it Rs N nodes)                                                         |
|------------+---------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| contains   | medium                          | normal; low on a write-protected occurrence (budget-truncated ones included); unintegrated numerator medium |
| textlinks  | medium; substantive subset high | normal                                                                           |
| subscribes | high                            | medium                                                                           |
| overrides  | low                             | medium                                                                           |
| hides      | medium                          | normal                                                                           |

Four rules apply on top of the table, as agreed under [[*Ancestor flags]]:
1. Tiers are ordered normal < low < medium < high < crucial.
2. Floors: 'a' is at least low, and 'b' and higher are at least medium.
3. Carriers: a side's numeral carries its tier; if it shows no numeral,
   its ancestor flags do. A carrier shows the greater of its floor and
   the side's tier, and every other glyph shows just its floor.
4. Birth-explained sides: a side whose only members are ancestors that
   the birth relation already accounts for has tier low. This subsumes
   the earlier "parent as sole container is low" rule; e.g. 'aC' is low,
   and a subscribee's 'bS' is medium because of b's floor.
A fraction's slash takes its side's tier, the same as the denominator:
in =2/5L= the 2 is high, and the slash and 5 are medium.
