* The rules
Everything decided in the discussion, consolidated. This section is the
specification the phases below implement. It uses the names that phase 1
introduces: a node /follows/ another (formerly "subscribes to"), and
/mentions/ another (formerly "textlinks to").
** Styles
A herald's color states its importance tier, or for a few heralds a
polarity or kind, never its hue. There are ten styles. Each has
one name everywhere: in the shared style file, on the wire, in the
Emacs faces ('heralds-STYLE-face') and in the Neovim highlight groups
('SkgHeraldSTYLE').

| Style       | Look                                     | Meaning                         |
|-------------+------------------------------------------+---------------------------------|
| crucial     | black on cyan '#00ffff'                  | the reader must understand this |
| high        | black on yellow                          | high interest                   |
| medium      | white on orange '#d2691e'                | medium interest                 |
| low         | white on olive '#5e5e20'                 | low interest                    |
| normal      | white on blue                            | ordinary                        |
| message     | black on white                           | prose meant to be read          |
| stop        | white on red                             | polarity: bad, or not yet       |
| go          | black on green '#00bb00'                 | polarity: good, done            |
| nonstandard | white on purple '#8b00ff'                | requests and diff facts         |
| yucky       | pink '#c84286' foreground, no background | error-flavored, but not too bad |

Unused reserve: foreground/background inversions. A comment beside the
style definitions says so, in case more styles are ever needed.

Skg dictates every face in a skg view, in both clients: a black
background, a white default foreground, and skg's own faces for the Org
elements a view contains. Nothing from the user's theme shows, so no
style needs to work on a light background.
** Relationship heralds
*** Letters
The five relation letters are normal: C (contains), M (mentions;
formerly L), F (follows; formerly S), O (overrides the view of) and H
(hides). A letter whose
relation explains this occurrence's presence (its /birth/ relation) is
high instead. Only the letter changes; its counts keep their own styles.
*** Tier order
normal < low < medium < high < crucial.
*** Side tiers
Each relation has an in-side ("N nodes R it") and an out-side ("it Rs
N nodes").

| Relation   | in-side                         | out-side                                                        |
|------------+---------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| contains   | medium                          | normal; low on a write-protected occurrence                     |
|            |                                 | (budget-truncated ones included); unintegrated numerator medium |
| mentions   | medium; substantive subset high | normal                                                          |
| follows    | high                            | medium                                                          |
| overrides  | low                             | medium                                                          |
| hides      | medium                          | normal                                                          |

The rationale for most normal rows: those members are usually visible
elsewhere (view-children, the text, the hidden-in/outside-followed
folders).
This rationale is documentation only, not a mechanism.
*** Floors, carriers, and birth-explained sides
**** Ancestor flags
are the lowercase letters (a = parent, b = grandparent,
…; folders count as generations) that replace a count when ancestors
account for it.
**** Floors
Ancestor 'a' is at least low. 'b' and higher are at least medium.
Every other glyph has floor normal.
**** Carriers
A glyph /shows/ a tier by being drawn in that tier's style. A side's
tier, from the table above, is shown by one designated part of the
side, its /carrier/. To /carry/ the side's tier is to be that part. The
carrier is the side's numeral, if the side shows one; otherwise it is
the side's ancestor flags, all of them together.

A carrier shows the greater of its floor and the side's tier. Every
other glyph on the side shows just its floor, except the slash (below).
For example, on the follows in-side, whose tier is high, =2aF= draws
the 2 high and the 'a' low (its floor). =aF= has no numeral, so the
'a' is the carrier and is drawn high.

A fraction's numerator is a subset with a tier of its own, e.g. the
substantive subset of mentioners, which is high. It gets its own
carrier, chosen the same way from the numerator's glyphs, and the
side's carrier is chosen from the denominator's glyphs. On the
mentions in-side (medium, substantive subset high), =1/5aM= draws the
1 high, the 5 medium and the 'a' low. =a/5M= draws the 'a' high,
because it carries the subset's tier, and the 5 medium. When the
denominator shows nothing, as in =5/M=, only the slash shows the side's
tier.
**** Birth-explained sides
A side whose only members are ancestors that the birth relation already
accounts for has tier low. For example, 'aC' (the parent is the sole
container) is low, and a followed node's 'bF' (its follower is the
grandparent) is medium, because of b's floor.
**** The slash
A fraction's slash takes its side's tier, the same as its denominator.
In =2/5M=, the 2 is high and the slash and 5 are medium.
*** Property counts
A (aliases), I (extra IDs) and ⚑ (flags, formerly P) are low. (⚑ is
proposed: F now belongs to follows.)
*** Markers inside relationship heralds
ĥ (overrides here) is crucial.

** Diff heralds
"staged:" becomes a go-styled ✓, and "unstaged:" becomes a stop-styled
✗. The existence axis X becomes N (node), and the membership axis M
becomes R (relationship). N, -N, R and -R and the other diff facts are
nonstandard-styled. The atoms become 'addedN', 'deletedN', 'addedR' and
'removedR', replacing 'newX', 'removedX', 'newM' and 'removedM'.

A text change reads =text changed ✓ ✗=, showing whichever marks apply:
the words nonstandard, the ✓ go, the ✗ stop. (Today: =text changed :
staged unstaged=.)
** Link annotations
The optional suffix after a body-text link shows the target's home
repo.

| Target state                              | Suffix                 | Style  |
|-------------------------------------------+------------------------+--------|
| resolved                                  | =[⌂:pub]= etc.         | normal |
| inactive repo                             | =[⌂:inactive]=         | yucky  |
| lookup pending                            | =[⌂:…]=                | yucky  |
| lookup failed (renamed from =unavailable=) | =[⌂:lookup failed]=   | yucky  |
| missing (a broken link)                   | none                   |        |

A broken link's label is yucky and underlined. Underlining marks
linkness and is independent of the style.
** Names
Principle (Jeff's): one concept, one name. Sweeping every term is out of
scope, but any multiply-named term found while preparing or executing
this plan is in scope.

Prose and code may spell a name differently, because code is compact
enough to invite misreadings that prose doesn't. For example,
'home_skg_repo' could be read as ((home skg) repo), so code writes
'skgrepo'. Prose, by contrast, avoids unfamiliar compound words.

| Concept                                            | Name                                                              |
|----------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| a directory of .skg files ("source")               | prose: Skg repo. Code: 'repo', or 'skgrepo' where Git repos       |
|                                                    | share the scope (e.g. 'home_skgrepo')                             |
| a Git repository                                   | prose: Git repo. Code: 'gitrepo'                                  |
| where a node lives                                 | prose: home repo, or home Skg repo for more detail.               |
|                                                    | Code: 'home_repo', or 'home_skgrepo'                              |
| X subscribes to Y                                  | X follows Y; roles follower, followed; herald F                   |
| X textlinks to Y                                   | X mentions Y; roles mentioner, mentioned; herald M                |
| X hides Y from its subscriptions                   | X hides Y; roles hider, hidden (name proposed)                    |
| the Org text construct =[[id:…][…]]=               | link (proposed: "link" means only this)                           |
| boolean file properties                            | flags (herald ⚑ proposed, folder "flags", =req:flags=)            |
| aliases, extra IDs and flags ("quals")             | properties; in code 'Property', 'PropertyFolder'                  |
| A, I and ⚑ together                                | property counts                                                   |
| a non-node view position ("scaffold")              | no general name; say which kind (see the viewnode types below)    |
| a dead scaffold                                    | dead viewnode ('DeadViewnode'; lowercase n)                       |
| mentioners with body, content or several mentions  | substantive (formerly interesting)                                |
| cannot be edited here                              | write-protected (not read-only)                                   |
| why this occurrence is here                        | birth (not reason-for-being)                                      |
| ĥ                                                  | overrides-here                                                    |
| diff axes                                          | N (node), R (relationship)                                        |
| deletion                                           | keep 'DELETED', 'delete' and 'deletedN', each for its own meaning |

The viewnode types become (Jeff's sketch, spelled out):
#+begin_example
viewnode = vognode | propertyFolder | property | partnerFolder
         | bufferRoot | deadViewnode
vognode  = active | inactive | phantom
#+end_example
A vognode now means "represents a graphnode", no longer "represents a
graphnode that exists". The Rust types are spelled 'Viewnode',
'ViewnodeKind', 'ViewnodeStats' and so on, lowercase n throughout.

The graphnode types get 'Graphnode' names ([[*1g. the graphnode types]]),
and each relation gets one name ([[*1a. the relations]]).
