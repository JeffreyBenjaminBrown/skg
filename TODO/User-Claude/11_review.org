***** TODO Jeff : Ed, from the top
Whose experience: again mine, in the tool, mirroring your Emacs
commands.

What you do in Emacs. Every relationship (this node contains that one;
this node subscribes to that one) is recorded in some skgrepo, its
"relRepo"; usually the default one, chosen by privacy. C-c s r
('skg-set-relRepo') changes the relRepo of ONE relationship, the one
between the headline at point and its parent in the view; it prompts
you for the repo and you save. C-c s R ('skg-set-relRepo-recursive')
does it for a whole subtree at once, and because a subtree holds
relationships of several kinds, it first shows a menu asking which
kinds you mean: the relation of a plain child to its parent (contains),
of a node listed in a "subscribes to" folder (subscribes), of a node
listed in an "overrides" folder (overrides). You tick the kinds, then
choose the repo, then save.

What I do with the tool. For one relationship I write 'relRepo:NAME'
into the headline's handle. For a subtree I add the word 'subtree':
'relRepo:NAME subtree'. The tool has no menu, so it has to decide which
kinds I mean. It decides from where I wrote the words: on a plain
headline, the contains relationships of that subtree; on a "subscribes
to" folder, the subscriptions under it; on an "overrides" folder, the
overrides under it.

The choice you may overturn: letting the headline's position stand in
for your menu. It cannot express "contains and subscribes at once", as
one ticking of your menu can; for that I would write the word on two
headlines, which is two saves. The alternative is an explicit kind
word, e.g. 'relRepo:NAME subtree:contains,subscribes'. I chose the
inference because one kind at a time seems the common case and the
inferred kind is the one a reader of the compact view would expect.
