** Q1. A perspective's skgrepo list
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16): most private first
A perspective's list is its owner's skgrepos most private first (the
reverse of the config's owned order), then every other skgrepo in the
config's declaration order. This replaces the provisional answer
below. A third author's mirror may stand in for a note when the
perspective's owner has none (Jeff: "yes good").
Slices 2 and 3. Jeff's rule (decision 4, quoted under "Agreed
behavior") ranks mirrors by "my list of skgrepos". Which list is it,
for a perspective whose owner is O?
*** Provisional
O's skgrepos in the config's declaration order
('SkgConfig::skgrepos_in_declaration_order'), then every other
skgrepo in declaration order. For the user, O's skgrepos are the
owned ones; for a foreign owner, those 'SkgConfig::skgrepo_owner'
groups under it.
*** Consequences to weigh
**** A third author's fork can stand in for a note
Today only the followed owner's overriders substitute
('followable_overriders_of' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph/override_resolution.rs' keeps only those
whose owner is the follower). Under the provisional list, where the
perspective's owner has no mirror of a note N, the first mirror in
declaration order stands in N's place, whoever wrote it. That is what
Jeff's example says ("one only in your X and your Y shows to me as in
your X"); the alternative, "only O's skgrepos rank; with no mirror of
O's, N shows as itself", contradicts that example when N is your Y.
**** Owned declaration order is privacy order, most public first
The config's owned entries are in privacy order
('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Skgrepos": "The order of the owned
entries is load-bearing"). So between two of my own mirrors, the more
public one shows. A private fork C of my own public note N will no
longer show in N's place (today C, an owned overrider, does), and an
owned chain D (private) forks C (public) forks N (foreign) will show C,
not D. The alternative is to rank an owner's own skgrepos most private
first. Jeff's "(my X, my Y, your X, your Y)" does not say which X is.
** Q2. Which notes are a note's mirrors
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16): as provisional, transitive in both directions
Slices 2 and 3.
*** Provisional
The mirrors of N are N and every note joined to N by a path of
'forks' relationships, followed in either direction, every
relationship and every note on the path visible under the skgrepo
restriction (as 'followable_overriders_of' gates one hop today: the
relationship's relRepo and the forker's home skgrepo unrestricted).
Mutual forks make the direction of a fork a poor guide to which note
is "the original", and a closure has no cycle problem.
*** Alternatives
- Direct forks only: N and the notes that fork N. Today's chains (D
  overrides C overrides N, all owned, shows D for N:
  'resolve_override' walks the chain) would stop at C.
- The forkerward closure only (today's walk, minus ownership). A
  note's forkees would then never stand in its place, under any
  perspective.
*** Consequence to weigh
Under the provisional answer, siblings are mirrors of each other (R and
T both fork N: R is a mirror of T), and a forkee can stand in its
forker's place: in a view from Jeff's perspective, my note R that forks
Jeff's N shows as N wherever R stands as content.
** Q3. Ties: several mirrors in the first-ranked skgrepo
Slices 2 and 3. Decision 2 lets two of one owner's notes fork one
note, possibly in one skgrepo, and an owned chain often lives in one
skgrepo. Which of the tied mirrors stands?
*** Provisional
Let T be the mirrors whose home skgrepo comes first in the list.
1. If T holds one note, it stands.
2. Otherwise, if exactly one member of T is forked by no other member
   of T, it stands. (An owned chain D forks C forks N, all in one
   skgrepo, shows D, as today.)
3. Otherwise N itself stands, if it is in T (mutual forks within one
   skgrepo, and two forks of N in N's own skgrepo, show N as itself:
   today's resolver likewise refuses to choose between two overriders,
   'resolve_override', "candidates . len () != 1").
4. Otherwise the member of T nearest N along 'forks' relationships in
   either direction, and among equals the least pid.
*** Alternative
Rule 4 replaced by "N itself": where my two forks of a foreign N tie,
N shows raw. Today that case cannot arise (monogamy).

** Q6. Forking a note one already forks
Slice 3. Today the fork operation refuses ('ForkAlreadyExists') when
the user already owns an overrider of the note: 'validate_fork_view_requests'
('server/from_text/buffer_to_viewnodes/validate_tree.rs') for C-c m f,
'validate_fork_specs_in_graph' ('server/from_text/fork.rs') for the
implicit fork; both call 'existing_owned_overrider_of'. Decision 2
removes monogamy from the graph's invariants. Does the operation keep
its check?
*** Provisional
It keeps it, reworded ("you already fork this note; your fork is X"),
as a guard of the operation, not an invariant: an edit of a foreign
note shown raw (a view opened on it) that silently made a second clone
would more often be an accident. A deliberate second fork is made by
listing the note in an owned note's forks folder (claiming), which nothing
refuses.
** Q7. A note forking itself
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16), and widened
As provisional, and more: "Also self-containment would be silly, and
self-hide. Self-link is okay though." So a save that leaves an owned
note containing itself, or hiding itself, is refused too, by the same
check ('SelfContains', 'SelfHides' beside 'SelfFork'; neither has a
rule today), and the nodeMerge drops the merged note's own IDs from
its 'hides' as it does from 'contains' and 'forks'. A link to itself
stays legal. No note in any skgrepo either server reads contains,
hides, subscribes to or overrides itself today (counted read-only by
file pid), so the migration meets none.
Slice 3. Today an owned self-override is refused as a one-node
'OwnedOverrideCycle' ('validate_override_invariants', via
'resolve_override', which meets the input again). Removing the cycle
check (decision 3) would admit 'N forks N', typed into N's forkee
folder or made by a nodeMerge of N with a note it forks
('three_nodeMerged_graphnodes' in
'server/nodeMerge/nodeMergeInstructionTriple.rs' filters the
acquirer's own IDs from 'contains' but not from 'overrides' or
'subscribesTo').
*** Provisional
A save that leaves an owned note forking itself is refused (a new
'SelfFork' violation, in the module where the cycle check was), and
the nodeMerge drops the merged note's own IDs from its 'forks', as it
does from 'contains'.

** Q11. Order within the forks folder (new)
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16): as provisional
Slice 6. Jeff, earlier: "Forkees are listed before forkers". The
forkee part of the folder is the note's own ordered 'forks' list (its
order is the user's, editable, as the subscribee folder's is:
'Folder::goal_is_ordered'); the forker part comes from the other
notes' files, in no order of the note's (as the subscriber folder's:
'goal_is_ordered' false).
*** Provisional
The forkees in the note's 'forks' order, mutuals among them where that
list puts them, then the forkers that are not forkees, in graph order,
which the refresh keeps as it finds them (it moves no forker-only
member, as in an unordered folder), always after the last forkee.
*** Alternative
Forkee-only, then mutuals, then forker-only. The folder's order would
then differ from the list's, and a save would rewrite the list in the
folder's order, moving the mutuals to its end.
** Q12. A forker-only member deleted, detached or attached (new)
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16): deleting it hides it
Jeff: "that should hide it". This replaces the provisional answer's
first item. A member of the forks folder that forks the note but that
the note does not fork, absent from the saved buffer where the
baseline showed it, joins the note's 'hides', as an absent member of a
forkee as such does today ('resolve_visibility'); the save reads the
folder's forker-only members for that, though they claim nothing. A
forker the note hides is not drawn in the forks folder. It is listed
in the hidden-outside folder (plan 2, decision 8), whose members are
editable: removing it there unhides it, and it is drawn in the forks
folder again. The other items stand as provisional.
Slice 6. What a member's claim means in the one folder is settled by
the code (behavior 5, "What a claim means there"): a claim says "this
note forks it". What the user can do to a member it does not fork:
*** Provisional
- Deleted: the save reads nothing for it (it claimed nothing), and
  the refresh draws it again, claiming nothing, as it reinstates a
  missing member of a write-protected set today.
- Attached (C-c s a, or a claim typed by hand): the save makes the
  note fork it, so it becomes a mutual. This is the gesture for
  forking a forker from the folder.
- Detached: it already claims nothing; the clients' detach says so
  and changes nothing ('skg--set-claim' in 'elisp/skg-metadata.el':
  "This headline claims nothing already; nothing to detach.").
- A relRepo request (C-c s r) on it: refused, by the clients and the
  save, since its record is in its own file, as on a subscriber-folder
  member today.
** Q13. A mutual deleted or detached (new)
*** Changed after Q12 was settled; still open
Provisional now: deleting a mutual both stops the note forking it and
hides it, so that a deleted member always leaves the folder, as Jeff's
answer to Q12 makes a deleted forker do. Detaching it only stops the
fork; it then stands as a forker, claiming nothing.
Slice 6.
*** Provisional
The save drops it from the note's 'forks' (it claimed, and is gone or
claims nothing now), as for any forkee. It still forks the note, so
the refresh draws it again among the forker-only members, claiming
nothing. A deleted forkee-only member is gone after the save, as a
subscribee-folder member is today.
** Q14. When the forks folder is drawn unasked (new)
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16): as provisional
Slice 6. Today 'add_default_folders' ('server/refresh/visit.rs') draws
the subscribee folder on an editable occurrence an editable-view
request extends, when the node subscribes to something the skgrepo

*** Provisional
The forks folder is drawn there when the note has any fork partner the

** Q15. The diff view and the one folder (new)
*** Settled by Jeff (2026-11-16): as provisional
Slice 6. Decision 6 keeps the diff view out of scope beyond renaming,
but its folder logic assumes one relation side per folder kind:
'changed_partner_folder_kinds' ('server/diff_view/changes.rs') sorts
kinds into 'IN_FOLDERS' and 'OUT_FOLDERS' by
'Folder::relation_member_role'; the processed-mark leaf of a folder
member is keyed by that one role ('server/diff_view/processed_requests.rs',
near its call of 'relation_member_role'); and the changed-folder labels
say "(this file)" or "(other files)" ('folder_heralds.changed_labels'
and 'other_files' in 'shared/herald-styles.json'). One folder kind with
two roles breaks each.
*** Provisional
In the diff view the forks folder is a changed folder when a
relationship of either side changed; each member's change leaves are
keyed by the side or sides that changed (the note's own record when the
note forks it at HEAD or in the worktree, the member's record when the
member forks the note at either); a mutual whose two relationships both
changed carries both leaves, and a processed mark on it marks both. Its
changed-folder label is "its forks changed", and it is in neither
'other_files' nor the "(this file)" set; the clients that read
'other_files' ('elisp/skg-processed.el', its Neovim twin) treat a
member by its side. This is more than renaming; Jeff accepts or
narrows it.

* Vocabulary
Checked against 'docs/glossary.org' and the code on 2026-11-16
('coding-advice/names.org', "Check a plan's words before the run").
** forks, forker, forkee
The relation and its roles (Jeff's): "Forker forks forkee". Recorded
by the forker. A *fork of N* is a note that forks N.
*** The fork operation keeps its name
'docs/glossary.org', "clone, and the fork operation", names the
gesture that clones a foreign note. It keeps its name and is always
called "the fork operation"; the note it makes is a *clone*, which
forks the original. Where the glossary says "forks" for fork
operations awaiting confirmation ("ask for confirmation (forks, Hoist,
strandeds, delete-from)", "plus forks and nodeMerges"), slice 9 makes
it say "fork operations".
** mirror (coined)
The *mirrors* of a note N: N and the notes Q2 says, under the skgrepo
restriction. Not to be confused with "mirror" as a plain verb in a
few docs ("The save is its mirror", 'docs/data-model_technical.org').
** a perspective's skgrepo list (coined)
The ordered list of skgrepos a perspective ranks mirrors by (Q1). Not
"rank", which the glossary keeps for privacy ("a foreign skgrepo has
no privacy rank"), and not "order", which names the privacy, fold and
declaration orders.
** the mirror shown
For a note N at a position where substitution acts, under a
perspective: the mirror Q1 and Q3 choose. It stands in N's place,
marked '(overridesHere N)' until slice 8 and '(inPlaceOf N)' after.
** mirror substitution

*** The one-time migration tool
'tools/migrate-forks-2026-11/migrate.py', with 'README.org' and a test

**** What it reads
'migrate.py [--dry-run] [--marks FILE]... DIR...': every '*.skg' file
under each DIR (skipping '.git'), grouped by pid across the DIRs, so
that an owner passes all their skgrepos at once and a telescope's
sections are seen together; and each processed marks file named.
**** What it writes
For each pid:
- Each section's 'subscribesTo' list (members and anchors, in order)
  becomes its 'forks' list, written where 'subscribesTo' stood.
- Each 'overrides' target that no section of the pid subscribes to is
  appended, as a member, to the 'forks' of the section that recorded
  the override (its relRepo is kept); the 'forks' key goes where the
  server's serializer puts it (after 'contains').
- An 'overrides' target that some section subscribes to is dropped;
  the relationship is recorded once, where the subscription stood, and
  the report counts each one whose section differs.
- A target listed twice in one section's 'forks' is kept once.
- A target that is one of the pid's own IDs is dropped and reported

- The 'overrides' key and its list are deleted.
Only top-level keys at the start of a line are touched, so titles and
bodies never are. A file whose lists are not in block style, or that
holds an October 2026 key, is left unchanged and reported, and the
tool exits nonzero. A file it migrated holds no retired key, so a
second run changes nothing.

In each marks file, every mark whose leaf is a relationship of
'SubscribesTo' or 'Overrides' is dropped, and the count reported
(see the pitfall "The processed marks file is all or nothing").
**** What it reports
Files changed, relationships paired, subscribe-only, override-only,
overrides dropped as recorded elsewhere, duplicates, self-forks, marks
dropped; then, per DIR that is in a gitrepo, the line to commit it
("git -C DIR commit -am 'Migrate to forks'"), since the diff view
refuses a HEAD in the old format (Q5). '--dry-run' writes nothing and
reports the same counts. The README gives the counts above, so that
each owner can compare.

** 3. Mirrors, and which one stands in a note's place
*** Where substitution acts: unchanged
Where a note N stands as content of an editable occurrence in a
content view, and, in a view switched to one owner's perspective, at
the view's roots; never in the diff view, never for a member of a
partner folder, a chain node, a ghost, a search hit or a root under
the root authors' perspective ('substitute_overriders',
'substitute_roots', 'server/refresh/visit.rs'). The children of a
note shown raw where something else would stand in its place are
raw too, one level ('is_overridden_and_shown_raw', which exempts the
subscribee folder's members).
*** Which mirror stands
The mirror shown for N, under the perspective of the tree N stands in
(the root's author under the root authors', else the perspective's
owner: 'override_follower_of_tree_containing'), is chosen from N's
mirrors (Q2) by that owner's skgrepo list (Q1), ties broken by Q3.
When it is N, nothing substitutes. The perspective's owner need not
own the mirror shown.
*** A carrier that is a mirror but not the one chosen
Today 'substitute_overriders' leaves a carrier alone when it is on
the followed owner's chain, even if not its end
('carrier_on_override_chain_followed_by'). Every mirror of N is honest
under the new rule, so that test would never re-point a carrier after
a switch of perspective. The refresh will re-point every carrier that
is not the mirror shown, and stop substituting where the mirror shown
is N itself ('stop_substituting', unchanged).
*** The tamper check
'overridesHere_part_errors' ('validate_tree.rs') will accept a
carrier that is any mirror of its original under the unrestricted
skgrepo restriction (so that a switch of the restriction does not
refuse a save of a part honest when drawn), and refuse any other,
naming the mirror shown as today it names the chain's end.
*** Every other reader of the old resolution
Each follows mirrors, by the same function:
- 'candidate_owners' ('overrides_followed.rs'): the owners of the
  visible mirrors of each original (Q4).
- 'forks_the_view_will_not_show' ('fork.rs'): warn when the mirror
  shown for the original, under the edited occurrence's tree's
  perspective, is not the new clone.
- 'member_it_stands_for' ('resolve_visibility.rs') and 'stands_for'
  ('file_what_is_written_in.rs'), which today walk overrides from
  overrider to overridden: a child stands for the list member that is
  its mirror, the nearest along 'forks' first, then the first in the
  list. (Their comment's reasoning, "whichever view the user found it
  in", holds the more now that a mirror in either direction can stand

- 'suppressed_result_gnids' ('server/serve/handlers/text_search.rs'):

  chosen is owned. A foreign hit still never suppresses. An owned

- The update-relevant neighborhood ('update_relevant_neighborhood_over',
  'override_walk_from_seeds'): the two monotonic override walks become

**** What each member shows (the heralds)
The relationship heralds already tell the sides apart by ancestor

letter 'K' (Q8):
- a forkee-only member: =bK= (b forks it), with any other forkers
  counted before the letter (=2bK=: N and one more fork it);
- a forker-only member: =Kb= (it forks b), with its other forkees
  counted after (=Kb3=);
- a mutual: =bKb=.
The birth letter (low) follows the side that stands as a member:
'(forks in 2)' on a member that claims, '(forks out 2)' on a member
the graph makes a forker of N (that side stands by its warrant, as a

'viewnodestats.rs'); a mutual carries both. 'birth_facts_for_folder'
computes them per member instead of per kind. Today a subscribee
shows =bS= and a subscriber =Sb= in their two folders
('docs/api-and-formats.org', the birth table); nothing else is added.

A claiming member's '(relRepo NAME)' fact and request are N's record

A pending detachment is, to both clients, a headline that claims
nothing, is warranted, and stands under a parent that reads claims

* Pitfalls the slices must respect
** Live servers and data
Ports 1730 and 1761 are Jeff's live servers ('target/release/skg' with
'data/jeff/skgconfig.toml' and 'data/claude/skgconfig.toml'), which
read each other's skgrepos as foreign and have no hot reload of code.
Never start, stop or connect to them, never pass those ports or
configs to anything, never build the release profile. Never run the
migration tool on 'data/'. Isolated instances only
('.claude/skills/verify/SKILL.md'). Every commit leaves the tree green.
** An unknown key is dropped silently, and the next save erases it
'GraphnodeOnDisk' has no 'deny_unknown_fields'; serde ignores a key it
does not know. Once slice 5 removes the two fields, a file still
holding them would load with neither, and the save's decomposition
would rewrite the section without them ('docs/api-and-formats.org',
"The .skg file format": "saving decomposes the node back into
sections"). That is why the refusal (Q5) lands in the same slice as
the field's removal, before any test runs against the new struct.
** The processed marks file is all or nothing
'ProcessedStore::load' ('server/diff_view/processed_store.rs') reads an
unparseable file as an empty store, and the next change overwrites it.
'RelationshipKey' serializes 'NodeRelation' by variant name. So once
