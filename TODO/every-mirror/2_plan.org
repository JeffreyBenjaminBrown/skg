** Q1. Reading the "After" of Jeff's example "forking a forker"
Slices 4 and 5. Jeff's example, after I cut y from your x (a forker
as such) and put it under my x:
#+begin_example
  - (me, editable) x
  -- its forkers
  --- (you, writable) x
  ---- z
  -- its forkers
  --- (you, writable) x
  -- y
#+end_example
The cut made my x fork your x, so your x is both my forker and my
forkee. Two readings:
- The coordinator's: the second "its forkers" is a slip for "its
  forkees". But "Forkees are listed before forkers" puts the forkees
  folder first, and the dedup rule buries a mutual under the forkers
  folder, write-protected; this reading keeps your x writable under
  the forkers folder, ahead of the forkees.
- The other: the first "its forkers" is the slip. Then the forkees
  folder comes first, holding your x writable with z, and the forkers
  folder holds your x again, which the dedup rule would bury
  write-protected in the mutuals folder (Jeff wrote it "writable").
Provisional, the one consistent with the dedup rule and the
one-editable-occurrence rule:
#+begin_example
  - (me, editable) x
  -- its forkees
  --- (you, writable) x
  ---- z
  -- its forkers
  --- and these mutuals (they fork b and are forked by b)
  ---- (you, write-protected) x
  -- y
#+end_example
(If your x has no other forker of mine, the forkers folder holds only
the mutuals folder.)
** Q2. Adding a new child under a forker as such or a forkee as such
Slices 2 and 3. Whose content does it join?
Today, a headline typed under an editable subscribee as such is
created as a node of its own (it is an editable, save-eligible
occurrence, 'visit_occurrence' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/traverse.rs'), but
joins no list: the subscribee as such is not save-eligible, so no
'SetContains' is emitted for it, and 'resolve_visibility' leaves a
claiming child the subscribee's list does not name alone (its module
comment: "This does not touch it. The refresh of the save's rerender
makes it vanish"). No orphan warning is given, since
'is_created_as_an_orphan'
('server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/mod.rs') exempts a
child of a parent that reads claims. The rerender's claims rung then
removes it from the view ('vanish_if_unwarranted',
'server/refresh/visit.rs'). So today the typed node lands on disk,
contained by nothing, and disappears from the view without a word.
'a_headline_typed_under_the_subscribee_itself_files_nothing'
('tests/fork.rs') covers only the filing.
Provisional: under an owned forkee or forker as such it joins the
shown node's content, inserted after the shown sibling it follows in
the buffer (first when it leads); under a foreign one the save is
refused, with a message saying to make the node one's own first (fork
it from a view of the node as itself).
** Q3. Cutting from a forker as such when my node is foreign
Slice 5. Cutting a child from a forker as such F under my node N
records 'N forks F' and maybe a hide, both in N's file. When N is
foreign there is nothing owned to record them in (today
'resolve_visibility' skips a foreign subscriber silently: 'if ! config
. skgrepo_is_owned (...) { continue; }', and the refresh then redraws
what was cut).
Provisional: the save is refused, with a message naming N and saying
that only a node of one's own can fork another. The same refusal
replaces today's silent skip for a cut from a forkee as such under a
foreign N.
** Q4. Is a foreign forkee or forker as such "editable"?
Slices 2 and 3. Jeff: "They are not editable, of course, if they are
foreign nodes. But content can be cut from either one, thereby hiding
it." A cut is read only where the save reads claims, which is under an
editable occurrence ('parent_reads_claims',
'server/refresh/ancestry.rs'); under a write-protected one no child
claims, and nothing can be cut.
Provisional: a foreign one holds its node's editability as an owned
one does (its children claim, so a cut is read), but its title and
body edits are refused by the text claim, as today's subscribee as
such's are ('validate_text_claims'); its herald says it is foreign.
** Q5. Which occurrence holds a node's editability when it is both an as-such member and ordinary content?

Provisional: no new rule; level order decides, and C-c s e moves

* Vocabulary
Check each word against 'docs/glossary.org' (as plan 1 leaves it) and
the code before the run ('coding-advice/names.org').
** forks, forker, forkee
Plan 1's relation and roles: the forker forks the forkee. "Fork" also
names the fork operation, the gesture that clones a foreign node
('docs/glossary.org', "clone, and the fork operation"); plan 1 decides
how the two are told apart, and this plan follows it. Here "fork"
alone is always the relation.
** forks folders
Both folders of the 'forks' relation: "its forkees" and "its
forkers". The code names are plan 1's; this plan writes
'Folder::Forkees' and 'Folder::Forkers' for them.
** member as such: forkee as such, forker as such
A forkee as such is an occurrence standing, warranted, in the forkees
folder of my node N (N forks it), as a subscribee as such stands today
('subscriber_of_subscribee_as_such', 'server/refresh/goal.rs'). A
forker as such (coined) is an occurrence standing, warranted, in N's
forkers folder, not in its mutuals folder. "Member as such" means
either.
** what a member shows
The content goal of a member as such M under N: M's content that N
neither contains nor hides, by the records the skgrepo restriction
shows, in M's order. For a forkee as such this is today's
'less_what_the_subscriber_integrates' ('server/refresh/goal.rs'); a
forker as such gets the same, by the same function.
** mutual, mutuals folder (coined)
A mutual of N is a node N forks that also forks N. The mutuals folder
('Folder::Mutuals', atom 'mutualsFolder', coined) stands last in N's
forkers folder and lists N's mutuals, write-protected. Label, from
Jeff's: "☮ and these mutuals (they fork b and are forked by b)", the
letter styled apart (b is N, two generations above the folder).
** repeat
An occurrence of a node whose editability another occurrence in the
buffer holds ('Walk::editability_is_held', keyed by
'Walk::editability_key'). The glossary's "editability rule" uses the
word without defining it; slice 1 adds the definition.
** cut
To delete a child's headline from under a member as such and save,
whether or not it is pasted elsewhere. A cut child is one the member's
list names that the buffer no longer shows claiming under it.
** drawn folded
A folder is drawn folded when each of its members carries 'folded':
a folder's collapse lives on its members ('Walk::added_compound_folders',
'server/refresh/mod.rs').

* How the editability rule and the node budget bound the recursion
Every editable member as such is an editable occurrence carrying an
editable-view request, so it gets both forks folders of its own, whose
members are drawn editable in turn: the recursion now runs through
forkers as well as forkees. Three things stop it:
- Each node is editable at most once per buffer. A member whose node's
  editability is held is drawn write-protected
  ('drawn_occurrence_editability' asks 'Walk::editability_is_held'),
  and 'hold_editability' refuses a second holder at a visit; a
  write-protected occurrence gets no default folders
  ('add_default_folders' returns unless 'editable_gnid' is some). So
  the recursion meets each node of the fork graph at most once, and N
  reappears under its own members only as a repeat (every member of
  N's forkees folder has N among its forkers).
- Each editable occurrence charges the budget once, at its visit
  ('consume_editableView_request'); once spent, members are drawn
  write-protected and draw nothing more.
- Mutuals are repeats by construction, so the mutuals folder draws no
  recursion.
A popular note with many forkers now spends its budget across them
level by level; content deeper in the view is then drawn
write-protected. That is the budget's purpose ("need not count
exactly, only stop an explosion", 'editability.rs' module comment).

** Repeats depend on level order
