#+title: Forests through mirrors
* What Jeff asked
"Most kinds of forest should traverse through mirrors. ... the user
sometimes requests a containerward forest. That thing should traverse
not just in the containerward direction, but also in any mirrorward
direction."

A forest is what a folder request draws ('C-c l', skg-for-ai's 'forest
KIND ID'): the partners of a note in one relation, each with its
containerward chain (its containers, theirs, and so on up). The mirrors
of a note are the notes joined to it by forks, either way
('docs/mirrors.org', "Mirrors").
* What is built
The containerward chain passes through mirrors
('server/refresh/chain.rs', 'list_mirrors_of_chain_occurrence'; the
docs: 'docs/mirrors.org', "Forests through mirrors"). The member of a
partner folder, and each note of its chain, that has a fork partner
holds its forks folder, first among its children, as a search hit
already does. The folder lists its mirrors, write-protected, each
with its own containers in a folded containers folder, whose members
carry their chains in turn. A set of mirrors is listed once per view,
at the first of its notes the refresh reaches
('Walk::pids_whose_mirrors_are_listed'), so a cycle of containment and
forks ends.
** How a mirror step is drawn, and why
As its own level, in a forks folder, rather than as one node for the
class. Two reasons. The every-mirror work draws a class as one node
only where substitution acts, in content; the chain is a raw position
('docs/mirrors.org', "Mirror substitution": "partner folder members and
the containerward chain" show the raw node), so each chain note must
stay itself. And a forks folder under a write-protected note, members
write-protected with their containers in a containers folder, is
already what a search hit shows, and what the root of a content view
shows for its own mirrors; nothing new to learn.
** An example, as skg-for-ai draws it
N is mine; MN, mine, contains it and forks TA, theirs; TR contains TA.
A, theirs, is N's forkee, and Q, theirs, forks A.

#+begin_example
  * N
  ** ☮ These also contain it.
  *** MN ☮
  **** Its mirrors are these.
  ***** TA ☮ bK ⌂:theirs
  ****** ☮ These also contain it.
  ******* TR ☮ (folded)
  **** MR ☮
  ** Its mirrors are these.
  *** A bK ⌂:theirs
  **** ☮ These also contain it.
  ***** TA ☮ (folded; listed under MN already, so no forks folder here)
  ****** TR ☮
  *** Q ⌂:theirs
  **** ☮ These also contain it.
  ***** TQ ☮ (folded)
#+end_example

Before, MN showed only MR: TA and TR, where their version of MN sits,
were missing.
* Every traversal, before and now
** The containerward chain under a containers folder ('C-c l c', 'forest containers', a fresh view's root)
Before: containers only. The root's own mirrors were listed, with
their containers, by the root's default forks folder, but no note of
the chain listed its mirrors. Now: through mirrors, as above.
** The chain under the other partner folders (mentioners, hides, the forks folder's members, search hits)
The same code, so the same change: every chain passes through
mirrors. The step of the folder itself is unchanged; see the choices
below.
** The contentward tree of a content view
Before and now: through mirrors, by substitution (one mirror stands in
each place), and an editable note's forks folder lists the others.
** The forks folder ('C-c l f', 'forest forks')
Before and now: every mirror, flat. Mirrors are its relation; it
needs no change. Its members' containers pass through mirrors now.
** Search
Hits were already grouped by class, each hit's forks folder listing
the rest. Its containers chains pass through mirrors now.
** 'C-c v a' (ancestry)
Client-side: it copies the headline's ancestors in the buffer; no
graph walk. An ancestry through a chain's forks folder now shows the
mirror step, since the view does.
** The diff view's chains and pruned ancestry
Not built: see the choices.
** Org export
Follows stored 'contains'; not a view. Left alone: an export writes
what a user's notes contain.
** Path finding
The server has none.
** Search prominence
A containerward walk that ranks hits; not drawn. Left alone.
* Choices left to Jeff, not built
** Mentioners of a note's mirrors
A link names one ID. Is a link to your fork a link to my note?
Options:
*** A. Exact, as now
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** ☮ These link to it.
  *** L1              (L1's text links to N)
#+end_example
*** B. Every mirror's mentioners in one folder
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** ☮ These link to it.
  *** L1              (links to N)
  *** L2              (links to M, N's fork: no herald says so today)
#+end_example
This needs new vocabulary to say which mirror each names.
*** C. Per mirror, under the forks folder
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** ☮ These link to it.
  *** L1
  ** Its mirrors are these.
  *** M
  **** ☮ These link to it.
  ***** L2
#+end_example
Existing vocabulary: 'C-c l m' on M draws exactly this today.
*** Recommendation
A by default; C drawn when asked ('C-c l m' on a note with mirrors
could also ask for it on each member of its forks folder). A link is
its author's choice of version, and following it lands exactly there
('docs/mirrors.org', "Direct visits are exact"). The "IDs compared as
equivalence classes" commits of 2026-11-17 do not bear on this: they
equate one note's own IDs (its pid and extra IDs), not its mirrors.
** Hides
'C-c l h' lists who hides a note and what it hides. A hide is local
to one fork pair: F, forking G, hides x of G's content. Through mirrors
the hiders folder of N would also list F', which hides M, a mirror of
N, in another forkee's content:
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** ☮ (hiders folder)
  *** F               (F hides N)
  *** F'              (F' hides M; N is shown everywhere F' shows anything)
#+end_example
Recommendation: an exception. A hide of M says nothing about N.
** The diff view
Its chains, and the pruned ancestry that picks its roots, walk the
union of HEAD and the worktree; it substitutes nothing. Through
mirrors:
#+begin_example
  * P (changed)
  ** P's chain
  *** Q
  **** (forks folder)
  ***** Q' (theirs, unchanged)
  ****** (Q''s containers)
#+end_example
Recommendation: an exception. Its chains place a change; a mirror of
an unchanged container adds reading, not placement.

* Found on the way, fixed
A forks folder under a write-protected note (a search hit's, or now a
chain note's) drew its forker-only and indirect members unwarranted at
the second refresh: the printer writes no 'noClaim' where claims are
not read, so read back they claimed. 'set_warrants' now clears such
claims first, as 'mark_claims' did a step later.
