* Refactoring for dev: the server's longest functions, measured
  The territory: all of 'server/', chosen by length rather than by
  part. A script outside the repository counted, for every 'fn' in
  'server/' outside 'test_utils' and '#[cfg(test)]' items, the lines
  from its 'fn' line to its closing brace (a nested 'fn' counts inside
  its outer one too, and again by itself). The twenty longest, as
  measured before this round, were read and sorted into two kinds.
  A "seam" function has stages that run in sequence, a loop body that
  is a unit, or a block repeated for two cases. A "flat" one is long
  only because it is a table or one match, or is already split into
  named nested functions.
  Six of the seam kind were split. Each extracted function says what
  it does in the domain's words and takes only what it uses; effects
  keep their order, errors, warnings and log lines keep their text and
  target, and an early return is still the caller's.
  Each split was checked by the whole Rust suite
  ('cargo test --no-fail-fast'), and the round by 'bash/all-tests.sh'.
** A second round: four more in the server, two in each client

   'repeat' against 'end' and 'until'); see "the clients, measured".

   the tail of 'prepare_save_under_mutation_gate',
   'infer_hides_from_contains_removals', 'skg-validate-linkstack-buffer'

   The measuring scripts are not kept; the paragraphs above and this
   one say what they count.
