** Are there principles I've missed?
*** Claude says: what I would add
**** never detach; re-kind in place
You state it for phantoms and for diff data. Make it the law
for every headline: the refresh's only operations are to
change a headline's kind or marks where it stands, to
reorder siblings, to create a missing child, to merge
siblings of one key, and, once at the end, to prune
childless tombstones. Nothing moves to another parent, and
nothing with children is deleted. "Retain as non-member" and
your merge rule are instances.
**** normalize before reconciling
The per-parent rules rely on invariants the input does not
guarantee: one editable occurrence per node, and non-members
under every write-protected occurrence. One pass over the
whole buffer establishes both before any parent is
processed; then no rule needs to re-check them. Today those
checks are spread through the walk.
**** a fresh render is a refresh of a stub
'render_initial_view' already completes a stub of one root
per requested node, each with a containers-folder request;
the diff view's first drawing completes a stub over the
straddle. If the rebuild keeps that, there is one code path,
and refresh (refresh (b)) = refresh (b) is the test that the
rules are consistent.
**** the graph dictates extent only where the buffer asked
"Recompute every value the graph dictates" must not mean
draw everything the graph has; the graph is cyclic. The
buffer says how much is drawn: an editable occurrence shows
its whole content list (bounded by the node budget); a
write-protected occurrence shows the children the buffer
had, corrected, with nothing added; a folder shows all its
members. Stating this per kind is what keeps the refresh
finite.
**** requests are consumed, never carried
The output carries no view request. A request the refresh
cannot fulfil becomes a warning, not a headline that
pretends.
**** the round trip through the metadata is exact
Every headline the refresh writes parses back to the same
viewnode, and the save reads the marks the refresh meant
('nonMember', 'writeProtected', the kind). The notes in
'metadata-format.org' sort each part into save input or
display fact; the rebuild should keep that sorting as a
checked table, since "must not lie" is addressed to the
save's reader as much as to the user.

**** TODO Claude says: override substitution
A content view shows an overrider in the place of the
overridden node, recording '(overridesHere ID)'. When the
override relationship vanishes, the headline stands for a
node that is not in the list, and the node that is in the

**** two editable occurrences in the input
The user yanks a subtree, so the buffer holds two editable
copies of one node. The first wins and the second is
write-protected, as today. The graph does not dictate it,
but the save needs it: two editable copies would be two
sources of one node's text. The ladder under "principles"
covers it.
**** a root whose node vanished
A root is a member of nothing, so it becomes a phantom and
stays; a deleted phantom may already stand as a root today,
and roots are never pruned. The buffer-wide tombstone
request would then leave a tombstone root, which the rules
allow.
**** a restricted placeholder with children
When the restriction changes, an occurrence becomes a
placeholder or a placeholder becomes an occurrence, in
place. A placeholder that holds children reveals that
something hidden has children; by your note on privacy,
acceptable.
**** the search buffer
Its roots are hits, members of nothing, enriched later with
folders and stats by a second pass. If a root needs no
warrant, the enrichment is a refresh of a buffer whose roots
are write-protected occurrences with a containers-folder
request, and the search buffer needs no path of its own.
