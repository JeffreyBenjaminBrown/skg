* Words this plan uses
The glossary defines the refresh and its vocabulary. The words
below are new in this plan, or change with it.
** vanish
A headline of a view vanishes when the refresh removes it, and
everything under it, from the view, for one of two reasons: the
graph no longer relates it to its viewparent (a claiming child
under a parent whose claims the save reads, which the claims
rung removes), or the graph no longer has its graphnode (an
occurrence in a content view, a root included, which the ID
rule removes where today it leaves a tombstone). Coined here,
for the glossary. Before this plan such a child was marked
=noClaim= and kept, shown ⊥, until the next save left it out of
the list, and such an occurrence became a tombstone.
** buffer corrections, as this plan leaves them
After slice 0 there are none: the refresh changes a view and
says nothing of it. The six kinds the glossary listed (a member
drawn, a headline entombstoned, a child marked =noClaim=,
duplicate siblings merged, a headline re-kinded, an edit
request dropped) remain things the refresh does, and the
glossary keeps a sentence on each; none is recorded, reported
or sent. "Buffer correction" stays a term of plan 06 in the
glossary's history and leaves the live vocabulary.
** no restriction, unrestricted
After slice 3 the skgrepo restriction is always one skgrepo,
the most private one shown, and denotes that skgrepo and every
more public one, as a skgrepo-set does today. A connection is
*unrestricted* when the most private skgrepo it shows is the
most private skgrepo the config has; the clients append
" (no restriction)" to that skgrepo's name where they show the
skgrepo restriction, and the words never travel on the wire or
sit in the config. The reserved set name "all" goes.
** the subscribee-visibility step
The step of the save that, for a subscribee shown editable
under its subscriber, reads what the buffer shows of the
subscribee's content and rewrites the subscriber's hides to
match ('server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/
resolve_visibility.rs', lines 55 to 95 at a97b32f2). Named
here; the glossary takes it.
** unknown graphnode, unknown viewnode, restricted viewnode
Jeff's words of 2026-10-30. An unknown graphnode is the
graphnode an ID names that the graph does not have; an unknown
viewnode is the headline standing for it; a restricted viewnode
is the headline standing for a graphnode the skgrepo
restriction keeps out. They replace the bare "an unknown", "a
reference to an unknown node" and "a restricted headline" in
user text, docs and messages (slice 6); code identifiers keep
their names.
** the metadata kind, 'MetadataKind'
After slice 4, the enum the parser reads a headline's metadata
into, one variant per kind of headline, each carrying exactly
the parts that 'shared/metadata-fields.json' lists for that
kind's atom.
** How to say it in messages
A message to the user says "an unknown viewnode", "a restricted
viewnode", "orphan graphnode" with its noun, "reads no claims";
it names a headline's kind by the glossary's word, never by a
Rust name; "restriction", "narrowing" and "widening" always take
"skgrepo".

* Agreed behavior
What the code does when the plan is complete. Behavior not
mentioned here is unchanged. The present tense says what the
code does today; "will" says what it will do after the slice
named.
** A child the graph no longer relates vanishes (slice 0)
Today the claims rung marks =noClaim= a child that claims
membership under a parent whose claims the save reads when the
graph does not warrant the relationship, keeps the child shown
⊥, and the next save leaves it out of the list; an occurrence
whose node is gone and whose parent's list does not name it
becomes a tombstone, "something stood here", keeping its
children, pruned when childless. After slice 0 such a child
will vanish at the refresh, with everything under it. A member
whose record alone a skgrepo narrowing keeps out will vanish
the same way, the save will keep its record (restored from
disk, as today), and the skgrepo widening will draw the member
again when the goal names it; so the mark that a save read as
a detachment after a widening is never made, and the record is
never deleted. An occurrence of a node another view deleted
will vanish with its subtree in a content view; in the diff
view it stays a ghost, as today. What stays as it is: a
=noClaim= the user wrote on a warranted child (a pending
detachment), the children of a parent that reads no claims,
the drawn chain nodes, and the tombstone for a folder, a
property member or a change viewnode standing where its kind
cannot.
** No buffer correction is reported (slice 0)
Today the refresh records six kinds of change as buffer
corrections, a policy of four situations says which are
reported, the save and rerender responses carry them in
=(corrections ...)=, and the clients show them in their
warnings buffer. After slice 0 the refresh will record and
report nothing: the types, the record, the policy, the wire
field, the clients' display of corrections and their tests
will be gone; the warnings buffer will keep the server's
startup warnings and the save's warnings. The integrate request
will refresh its buffer once, with no second pass and no filter
by viewnode ID; the walk will keep no sign of "drawn first" and
no set of added folders; the tail will keep no rule about what
it pruned.
** Three defects of the review's tests (slice 1)
Today the walk's map from a graphnode to its one editable
occurrence is keyed by the ID as written, so an occurrence
written under an extra ID can lose its editability to one the
refresh draws elsewhere, and in the diff view an acquirer can
be left with no editable occurrence after a nodeMerge; the
hidden-outside folder's goal reads each subscribee's content
without the skgrepo restriction's gate, so under a narrowing a
visible hide of a graphnode a subscribee contains by a
restricted record shows in neither hide folder and a restricted
subscribee's private record can be inferred; the
subscribee-visibility step never asks whether a hide's record
is visible, so a save under a narrowing deletes a restricted
hide whose graphnode the narrowing shows as the subscribee's
content, and records a public hide of a graphnode only a
private record explains. After slice 1 the map will be keyed
by the pid and the pass before the walk will not give the
editability to an occurrence the ID rule will make a ghost; the
hidden-outside goal will read each subscribee's content through
the gate; and the subscribee-visibility step will read the
subscribee's content and the subscriber's hides through the
gate and leave a restricted hide alone.
** Every write obeys the skgrepo restriction (slice 2)
The principle, Jeff's of 2026-10-30, stands in the docs: no
save, nodeMerge, fork, deletion, commit or other write may
remove, move, rewrite or create a record, a section or a node
file the skgrepo restriction keeps out, nor act on the buffer's
silence about such a record. Today nine writes break it, as the
review's report 'restriction-edits.org' numbers them. After
slice 2: a fork (finding 2) will copy only visible records into
the clone, each floored at the more private of its relRepo and
the clone's home; a new hide's relRepo (finding 3) will be at
least as private as the subscription and the contains record
that explain it, and the inference of hides from removals will
read only visible subscriptions, contents and hides; a headline
for a restricted graphnode with a visible nodeRepo (finding 4)
will be suppressed by the graph's home of the pid, with a
truthful warning; a delete's propagation (finding 5) will stop
for the user's choice where a record the skgrepo restriction
keeps out names the graphnode: cancel, delete from visible
records only (the restricted records stay as they are), or
delete from all owned records (the restricted recorders
rewritten too), carried as a confirmation round trip like the
strandeds confirmation; a nodeMerge (finding 6) will be refused
under a skgrepo restriction when either graphnode has a
restricted section, as a delete is; a save whose new or moved
record would land in a restricted section (finding 7) will be
refused, naming the skgrepo, as the fork's refusal does; the
delete-references cleanup (finding 8), the restore and
readiness requests (finding 9) and the import (finding 10) will
be refused unless the connection is unrestricted (for the
import, unless its destination is visible). The whitespace
strip (finding 11) and the export stay as they are by Jeff's
choice, their docs saying so (slice 6).
** One encoding of the skgrepo restriction (slice 3)
Today "no skgrepo restriction" is encoded as the reserved set
'all' and as the absence of a set: sixty-one functions take
'Option<&SkgrepoRestriction>', the save turns 'all' into None,
and the gated accessors treat None and 'all' differently, which
made the stats quadratic under 'all' in plan 08. After slice 3
the skgrepo restriction will be one skgrepo, the most private
one shown, denoting it and every more public skgrepo;
'SkgrepoRestriction' will hold that skgrepo and the set it
denotes; one method, 'is_unrestricted', will say whether the
most private skgrepo shown is the most private the config has,
no function will take an 'Option' of it; the wire's request to
set the restriction will take a skgrepo name, the list of sets
will list the skgrepo names in privacy order with no "all", and
the display request will answer a skgrepo name; the config's
default will be a skgrepo name, the most private skgrepo by
default; the clients will show every skgrepo by its name, with
" (no restriction)" appended to the most private one, in the
prompt and the displays; the refusals that required 'all' will
require an unrestricted connection; a record whose relRepo
names a skgrepo the config lacks will be visible, and a
graphnode whose home does, unrestricted, under every skgrepo
restriction (today under 'all' alone and never, respectively);
and the config will refuse two skgrepos of one name as today.
** The parser's natural shape (slice 4)
Today 'ViewnodeMetadata' holds the parts of every kind as flat
fields beside 'kind : MetadataKind', which carries parts only
for the gnid-only kinds, so a relRepo request has three places
it may sit and a gnid not yet read is an empty string. After
slice 4 'ViewnodeMetadata' will keep only what every headline
may carry (the viewnode ID, the fold and focus state, the
readiness request, the derived facts read back for the round
trip), and 'MetadataKind' will carry, per variant, exactly the
parts 'shared/metadata-fields.json' lists for the kind's atom:
for an occurrence the gnid and home skgrepo as 'Option's until
the parse completes them, the claim, write-protected, the one
edit request, the relRepo request, 'overridesHere' and the view
requests; for a ghost the gnid, the claim and the restoreNode
request; for the unknown and restricted viewnodes, one variant,
the kind, the gnid, the record's relRepo, the relRepo request
and the claim; for an alias the record's relRepo, the relRepo
request and the claim; for an ID, a flag, a folder, a change
viewnode, a tombstone and the buffer root, their own variants
with the parts their atoms allow. 'viewnode_from_metadata' will
be one match, each arm building its 'ViewnodeKind' from its own
parts; the parse will be driven by the table, a field the table
does not list for the kind refused by the table; the refusal
texts 'tests/metadata_grammar.rs' pins will be unchanged, the
one that says "A Restricted vognode" apart (slice 5).

* Pitfalls the slices must respect
** the pending detachment must survive
