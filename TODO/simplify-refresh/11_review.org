* Verdict
The plan did what it set out to do. Seven slices in four hours,
31 commits, every suite green at every slice's end but the one
failure older than the plan, eleven entries of the problems file
closed, the data loss of plan 08's review gone in every variant
the probes drove, the buffer corrections gone with their

writes of the skgrepo restriction refused or floored and a tenth

skgrepo restriction with no 'Option' and no reserved set, the

items done in nine commits, and the docs and glossary brought to
the new words with a reader's pass. The reviewers grade most of
plan 08's findings fixed: of its eighty-odd items, about fifty
fixed, the rest in part or moot by decision. What needs work the
review found by running: an explicit fork copies a restricted
graphnode's title and body into a visible clone and publishes
text stored below home with no Hoist confirmation; a connection
narrowed before a config reload shows a skgrepo the reload
added; a strandeds raise creates a restricted record and a
pasted member rewrites a private file (two entries slice 2 filed
as traced); two advisory reads and four refusals tell of
restricted homes; Neovim's dispatcher hides a bug's traceback and

two glossary sentences false since slice 0 that no reader caught,
the words' residue where phrases broke across lines, the save's
confirmation plumbing that slice 2 copied once more, and
twenty-two behaviors with no test. Every fix the reports name is
cheap but one (the weave's rewrite of restricted sections, which
I recommend documenting). Decide 1 to 3 and 7 are the corrective
slice; 4 to 10 are your judgments on what the slices did beyond
the plan; 11 to 18 are the next words, tidy, docs and tests.

*** the skgrepo restriction, the two questions, the third
A connection's skgrepo restriction is one skgrepo, the most
private shown; it denotes that skgrepo and every more public one
(what the glossary calls the skgrepo-set), and it keeps out every
more private skgrepo of the config. A record is restricted when
its skgrepo is kept out, and visible otherwise. A graphnode is
restricted when its home skgrepo is kept out; a restricted
viewnode is the headline that stands for a restricted graphnode.
The code asks the skgrepo restriction two questions, "is this
record visible" and "is this graphnode restricted". A third
question, "is this skgrepo among the ones shown", survives at
three sites: the get-file-path request, the fork's choice of a
clone's skgrepo, and one check in the save. For a skgrepo the
config does not name the two questions answer visible and not
restricted (slice 3's rule), and the third answers "not shown". A
connection is unrestricted when its most private skgrepo shown is
the config's most private; the clients then show that skgrepo's
name with " (no restriction)" appended. Slice 3 made this the one
encoding of the skgrepo restriction, in place of two (a set named
'all' and the absence of a set); "the one encoding" below means
that work.
*** the rebuild request
'skg-rebuild-session-lifetime-stores' (by M-x;
':SkgRebuildSessionLifetimeStores' in Neovim) reloads the config
and the graph from disk for every connection.
*** the explicit fork, the implicit fork, the clone
A fork makes a clone of a graphnode: a new graphnode that
subscribes to and overrides the original and copies its title and
body. The explicit fork is asked by the user, by the fork command
('skg-fork-node', 'C-c m f'; '<localleader>mf' in Neovim) or by a
fork request written by hand into a headline's metadata (the
wire's '(viewRequests fork)'); the save then shows the fork-
confirmation buffer and asks for approval, in Neovim with the
question "Fork the listed node(s)?". The implicit fork is the
save's own, when the user edits a graphnode they do not own.
*** text below home, the Hoist confirmation, the text release
A graphnode's title or body may be stored in a section of its
file more private than its home skgrepo; the glossary calls that
text overPrivateText, and this review says "text below home".
Moving such text up to the home skgrepo's section is the
glossary's Hoist repair; a save that would make that repair stops
first with the Hoist confirmation, which asks whether to. The
text-release confirmation is the save's other question about such
text: whether to include it or keep it withheld where the save
would publish it.
*** the strandeds, the raise, the reset
After a save moves a graphnode's home or drops a subscription, a
hide or other relationship may be left recorded in a skgrepo more
private than its new default; such a relationship is stranded.
The save moves some to their new skgrepo itself (a strandeds
raise) and lists the rest in the strandeds confirmation, where
the user may reset them to their new defaults.
*** the anchored weave
A private section of a graphnode's file stores its contains and
subscribesTo records as runs: short lists, each placed after a
placement anchor (the glossary's word) that names a more public
member, so that the private members keep their place among the
public ones. When a save rewrites the public list, it weaves each
private run back in after the anchor it followed.
*** land, the landing check
A record lands in a skgrepo when the save writes it into that
skgrepo's section. The landing check is slice 2's check, in the
save, that no record would land in a skgrepo the skgrepo
restriction keeps out and none would leave one; either is
refused, naming the skgrepo.
*** the delete-from confirmation
A save whose deletes would strip (remove from other graphnodes'
lists) records the skgrepo restriction keeps out stops with
"Delete from: (c)ancel, (v)isible records only, (a)ll owned
records" (slice 2); the owned records are those in the user's own
skgrepos.
*** the two advisory reads
Two requests of the wire, named in the API doc, that a client
sends to the server before a prompt: 'relRepo info', which the
relRepo gesture ('skg-set-relRepo', 'C-c s r'; '<localleader>sr'
in Neovim) asks to fill its prompt's default and current skgrepo,
and 'flag state', which the flag gesture ('skg-set-flag-search-
matching', 'C-c s x'; '<localleader>sx') asks for a graphnode's
home skgrepo, its flag's value and whether the user owns it.
*** the hides folder, the hidden-outside folder
Under an occurrence of a graphnode s, the hides folder ('skg-
show-folderOf-hidesFromSubs', 'C-c l h'; '<localleader>lh') lists
the graphnodes s hides from its subscribers, write-protected; the
hidden-outside folder lists the hides of graphnodes no subscribee
of s contains, and is editable.
*** the switched view, the fresh view
A switched view is a buffer drawn under one skgrepo restriction
and refreshed after a switch to another ('skg-restrict-repo-set',
'C-c v l'; '<localleader>vl'); a fresh view is drawn under the
current skgrepo restriction from nothing. I take it as a rule
that the two should show the same, since the refresh is meant to
bring a buffer to what a fresh drawing would show.
*** the tombstone, ⊥, the orphan graphnode, the pinned buffer
A tombstone is a headline that stands where a folder or a
viewnode can no longer be said, holding its children if it has
any (a childless tombstone is pruned). ⊥ is the mark a client
draws for a member no relationship the view can see explains (the
server's word for it is 'noWarrant'). An orphan graphnode is a
graphnode that no list the skgrepo restriction shows contains. A
pinned buffer is a test's expected buffer text, compared whole.
*** the dispatcher, the response dispatcher
Neovim has two guards against errors. The dispatcher is the
function every key and Ex command runs through since slice 5
('call_showing_refusal'); it catches an error and shows its
message as one line. The response dispatcher is the older code
that routes each of the server's answers to its handler, inside a
guard of its own that only logs an error; the Neovim deviations
doc names the first and not the second.
*** the attribution line
The last line of a commit message, "Co-Authored-By: ...". The
earlier slices wrote "Co-Authored-By: Claude Fable 5.1
<noreply@anthropic.com>"; slices 5 and 6 wrote "Claude Opus 5.5",
the name of the model that made their commits.

*** the twins, the stats pass, the approvals
The twins are the two clients' functions of one purpose, which

when it sends the save again after a confirmation; that second
sending is a retry.

** 1. A skgrepo narrowing after a config reload
*** The setup
Two skgrepos, "public" then "private". Four graphnodes: P, a and
S, homed in "public", and h, homed in "private". P's file says P
contains a and S in its "public" section and contains h in its
"private" section; P's private section also contains hc, a fifth
graphnode homed in "private". The connection is narrowed to
"public", so h and hc are restricted. Then, with the connection
still open, the config file gains a third skgrepo, "secret",
after "private". S's file moves S's home to "secret". P's file
gains a "secret" section that contains a sixth graphnode, a2,
homed in "public". The rebuild request runs.
*** What the test saw
The connection keeps its old skgrepo narrowing to "public". Under
it, a view of P shows S by its title, with the home "secret"; it
shows a2, by the record in P's "secret" section; and a view
rooted at S opens. h is still drawn as a restricted viewnode and
hc is still absent, as they were before the reload. Asked about a
record in "secret" and about a graphnode homed in "secret", the
two questions answer visible and not restricted. The get-file-
path request, which asks the third question, refuses "secret"; so
the server disagrees with its own rendering. For contrast the
test then built a skgrepo restriction by name from the reloaded
config, as the fix would. That one keeps "secret" out: S is drawn
as a restricted viewnode, a2 is not drawn, and a view rooted at S
is refused. Last, a connection with no skgrepo restriction that
was open before the reload still answers unrestricted and shows
everything; but it still names "private" as the config's most
private skgrepo, where the config's most private is now "secret".
*** What the fix does
At every swap of the config, the server rebuilds each
connection's skgrepo restriction by its name, so a skgrepo added
more private than the one shown is kept out; and the third
question is removed in favor of the two. The fix is cheap. It is
Decide 1 (c).

** 7. Neovim's dispatcher hides bugs, swallows refusals
Open. Two faults of Neovim's error handling since slice 5; I
confirmed each in the two Lua files.
*** A bug shows as a refusal
Every key and Ex command runs through the dispatcher, an error
guard that treats every error alike and strips every "file and
line" prefix from the message. So a genuine Lua error in a
command (an attempt to index a missing value, say) shows as one
line with neither traceback nor file, and nothing reaches the
log. Emacs keeps a refusal ('user-error') apart from a bug
('error'), which its debugger can trace.
*** The fork's approval is swallowed
The fork's approval (the "Yes" to "Fork the listed node(s)?")
runs inside the response dispatcher's own guard, so its refusals
("Pick a repo for each clone first") go to the log and the
approval silently does nothing. A comment beside it says this was
fixed, and the Neovim deviations doc's claim that every refusal
shows as one line is untrue for this one.
*** The fix, cheap
The dispatcher logs the traceback of an error that is not a
refusal and shortens only a refusal's message (a refusal marked
as such, or starting with "skg:"); the fork's approval moves out
of the response dispatcher's guard. I recommend it in the
corrective slice.
