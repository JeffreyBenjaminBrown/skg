*** The three states of a teammate
**** asleep: a named transcript on disk, no process, no tokens
Claude Code keeps every session's conversation under
'~/.claude/projects/'. A stopped session is just that file.
It survives reboots, and it keeps its compaction summary, so a
long life does not mean an ever-growing context.
**** awake and idle: a background process, waiting
'claude --bg -n NAME "..."' starts a session with no terminal.
It shows up in 'claude agents --json' and in every other
session's peer list. While idle it makes no API calls (tested:
the cost report of an idle background session stayed at its
last turn). Claude Code stops an unattached idle background
process after about an hour; the conversation stays on disk.
**** awake and working: a turn in progress
A turn ends when the teammate stops talking. Nothing runs
between turns unless something sends the next message.
*** Passing control: the wake primitives
**** From a shell, to a sleeping teammate (tested)
'claude -p --resume NAME "message"' wakes the transcript named
NAME, runs one turn on the message, prints the reply, and
sleeps again. The turn is appended to the same transcript, so
the next wake remembers it. I created a session that was told a
codeword, killed it, resumed it by name, and it answered the
codeword; a second resume knew it had been asked once before.
'--max-budget-usd' caps that one turn's spend, and
'--output-format json' reports the cost.

One caveat from the docs: if NAME is currently running in the
background, this command attaches to it instead of sending, and
exits 1 when its output is piped. So a dispatcher must check
'claude agents --json' first, and use the next primitive for a
running session.
**** Between running sessions (tested)
Every session has 'ListAgents' and 'SendMessage'. I started a
background Haiku session, sent it "what is the codeword?", and
it woke from idle, ran a turn, and sent "HERON" back to me.
Delivery is local, over a Unix socket; it never touches
Anthropic's servers, and it costs the receiver one turn. A
'notify_when_idle' flag gives the sender one notice when the
receiver finishes, so one teammate can hand work to another and
hear back without polling.
**** Resuming a background session in place
'claude --bg --resume ID' (no other flags) restarts a stopped
background session under the same ID; with flags it starts a
copy and says so (tested, by accident). After a reboot,
'claude respawn --all' restarts every background session.
**** What does not exist
Nothing can message a teammate that has no process. Nothing in
this container runs on a timer by itself: there is no cron
daemon, and Claude Code's in-session cron fires only while that
session's process is alive and idle (it is restored on resume,
but missed fires are not caught up, and recurring tasks expire
after seven days). So someone has to be awake to do the waking.
*** The design I propose: a dumb dispatcher and skg mail
**** The mail is already designed
Jeff and Ed settled it: each teammate keeps 'outboxes' with one
'to X' node per correspondent, and 'inboxes' holding a fork of
every 'to <me>'. A message is a new top-level child of 'to X'.
That is the whole protocol; the dispatcher adds nothing to it.
**** The dispatcher is a shell script, not an LLM
A script that watches the skgrepos (inotify, as the server
does) and, when the .skg file of some 'to X' changes, runs
'claude -p --resume X "You have mail; read your inbox."' if X
is asleep, or posts the same line into X's inbox socket if X is
awake. It costs no tokens while nothing happens, needs no
context, and cannot forget. Ed's daemon log ('skg-for-ai.log',
one line per re-render of a held view) is an alternative
signal; the raw file watch is simpler and needs no daemon.
**** Corey is not the always-on process
I considered making myself the watcher, with a '/loop'. It
would mean a Fable turn every tick to decide "nothing to do",
and a process that must be pinned to stay alive. The script is
better at that job than I am; I parachute in when the team's
dynamic needs it, as your notes on me ask.
**** Timers, if we want them, come from the same script
A heartbeat ("Scarlett, check the disk once an hour") is one
more line in the script. Jeff's host cron could instead run
'claude -p --resume' directly, but the host cannot reach a
container session's socket, and the script keeps everything
inside the container where the data is.
**** What a wake costs
A woken teammate re-reads its whole transcript. After more than
an hour asleep the prompt cache is cold, so a 200K-token
transcript costs about two dollars of input before the first
word. Three consequences: wake on batched mail, not per
message; keep transcripts short, with compaction and with
knowledge in the graph rather than in context, as the team node
already asks; and prefer sending a teammate a pointer ("read
your inbox") over a long message, since the inbox is read with
the cheap skg-for-ai tool, not carried in the transcript.

******** Judgment in the message, arithmetic in the dispatcher
Every review lands here. The sender, who has judgment, tags
the message; the dispatcher, which has none, sorts by the tag
and by numbers it can keep itself. That matches your framing:
the agents choose, and the dispatcher carries out.
******** A class on each message, three levels
Management calls them classes of service, CS calls it static
priority with deadlines, social choice calls it a closed rule
on the agenda. Three words suffice: 'expedite' (wake now),
'standard' (wake at the next quiet moment), 'batch' (wait
until the inbox is full or old). The waker writes the word in
the message; the dispatcher wakes the highest class first.
******** Fair share with aging, from the dispatcher's ledger
CS's stride scheduling, management's starvation guard and
social choice's wake tokens are one idea: the dispatcher logs
every wake and its cost, and among equal classes it wakes the
teammate who has had the least. That is a few lines of
arithmetic over a log file, and it prevents one loud pair from
starving the rest. The same ledger answers Scarlett's budget
question and lets us see, later, which wakes were worth it.
******** Feed the bottleneck, which is you
Management's first pick. Your attention is the scarcest
resource, so mail to you is batched by default, and teammates
should hold back work you cannot absorb rather than queue it.
Your note "don't write to me for its own sake" says the same.
******** The waker may nominate; the dispatcher decides
Your "guess whose work is most urgent and pass the mic" is the
CS and social-choice recommendation too, with one guard: a
nomination is a tag the dispatcher honours within the class
and share rules, not a direct wake, so a chain of eager
nominations cannot run the budget down.
******** What I would build, and what I would leave out
Build: the class word, the wake ledger, the quiet-period
coalescing from the batching answer above, and least-woken
tie-breaking. Leave out for now: deadlines ('reply-by'
fields), scrip tokens that refill, auctions and lotteries,
per-wake verdicts. Each is a plausible second step, and the
ledger will tell us whether any is needed.

****** TODO Corey says: yes, one terminal per teammate.
A running teammate: 'claude attach NAME' opens its background
session in your terminal; detach and it keeps running. A
sleeping teammate: 'claude --resume NAME' opens it in your
terminal, where it wakes with its memory; when you exit it
sleeps again. Both are one command, and a teammate you are
talking to still receives dispatcher and peer messages, which
appear in your terminal as they arrive.

The one constraint: a session should be open in one terminal
at a time. Two interactive resumes of the same session
interleave their turns into one transcript. The dispatcher must
also not use 'claude -p --resume' on a session you have open,
since that would try to attach; it will check the running list
and use the message socket instead, as it does for any awake
teammate. So the dispatcher sees your conversation partner as
"awake", which is right.
