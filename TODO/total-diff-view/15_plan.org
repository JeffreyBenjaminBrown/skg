* Words this plan uses
The glossary ('docs/glossary.org') defines most of these; the rest are
defined here, and slice 10 adds them to the glossary.
- The diff view is the view opened by C-c v d: up to four buffers,
  "all changes", "merges", "skgrepo changes" and "order-only changes",
  each rendered over the graph straddle. A buffer kind is which of the
  four a buffer is ('DiffViewBufferKind').
- The graph straddle ('GraphStraddle', 'server/graph_straddle/') holds
  the before-graph (the owned skgrepos as committed at HEAD) and the
  after-graph (the live graph) and compares them. A node's presence is
  Both, OnlyBefore or OnlyAfter. A partner of a node in some role is
  one entry of the union of the two graphs' lists, with its presence
  and its relRepo in each graph ('Partner').
- The pruned change view (until slice 9 "the PCSVF") is the
  viewforest the "all changes" buffer renders, defined by four sets
  ('PcsvfSets', renamed in slice 9): C, the changed nodes; R, the
  content origins reached from C by walking containerward through the
  union of the straddle; U, the nodes to extend contentward (C, R and
  every node on those walks); D, the deduplicated content origins, the
  buffer's roots. 'docs/diff-view.org', "C, R, U and D", defines them.
  A node is in C when its own file records a change, or when a
  relationship leaf whose relationship exists in one graph only names
  it as member ("in-changed"); so the node of every removed member is
  in C, and so in U.
- A change-forest leaf ('ChangeLeaves' in
  'server/diff_view/change_leaves.rs') is one change a user can mark
  commit-ready: a node and an aspect of it (existence, text, home
  skgrepo, an alias, an extra ID, a flag) or a relationship and an
  aspect of it (existence, relRepo). The recorder of a leaf is the
  node whose file records it. The leaves a node records are
  'ChangeLeaves::leaves_under'; a node's relationship to its
  viewparent is recorded by the viewparent, not by the node.
- A mark is a user's declaration that a leaf is commit-ready, stored
  with the leaf's fingerprint in the readiness store. A readiness
  request ('(readyRequest OBJECTS)' or '(unreadyRequest OBJECTS)' in a
  headline's metadata) is what a readiness gesture (C-c r r, C-c r h
  and their recursive forms) writes before saving; the save consumes
  it. The readiness summary is the save's count of ready and changed
  files; a file is ready when every leaf it records is marked
  ('readiness_of_file').
- A diff object is what a fact or a request names: 'node', the node's
  own existence; 'rel', its relationship to its viewparent; 'all',
  every leaf the node records. A headline's '(leaves N OBJECTS)' fact
  lists the objects a gesture there may name and N, how many distinct
  leaves they name together.
- A node's sign is its presence as the diff view shows it: '+' (new
  since HEAD, herald '∃+'), '-' (deleted, '∃-') or, from slice 4, '~'
  (the node exists in both graphs and records at least one leaf,
  herald 'M', "modified"). A relationship's sign is '+' or '-' ('~+',
  '~-'). The signs are the '(presence ...)' part of '(diff ...)' from
  slice 3, and the bare atoms of '(diff ...)' before it.
- An occurrence of a graphnode is a viewnode that stands for it (an
  unrestricted vognode). A buffer has at most one editable occurrence
  of a node; the others are write-protected, and the server draws no
  content under a write-protected one. A member is a child viewnode
  whose 'affectsParent' is true; a non-member has it false. A removed
  member is an unrestricted vognode, non-member, carrying the sign
  '~-', standing where a relationship the before-graph had and the
  after-graph lacks once stood ('is_unrestricted_removed_member'). A
  historical phantom is the diff view's image of a deleted node,
  write-protected, with its old content beneath it.
- The editability rule (until slice 10 "the draw rule"; the module
  comment of 'server/to_org/expand/editable.rs') decides, at each
  occurrence view completion reaches in level order, whether it is
  editable: an occurrence without a request is editable when its
  node is unclaimed in the editable map and write-protected when
  another occurrence claimed it first. The editable map is built
  empty at every completion; only an editable occurrence claims.
- View completion extends a viewforest: it adds viewnodes to one that
  stands (until slices 9 and 10, "expands" and "draws"). The server
  renders a viewforest when it produces its org text
  ('server/org_to_text.rs'); a rerender is the request that recompletes
  a view and sends its text. Org folding keeps the words "fold" and
  "unfold" everywhere.
- The per-visit step ('process_unrestrictedVognode_changes',
  'server/diff_view/changes.rs') runs at an editable occurrence's visit
  after the content reconciler and adds the diff view's viewnodes: the
  members' signs, the removed members, and for a node in C its old
  text, home skgrepo move, "it acquired" folder, changed alias, ID and
  flags folders, changed partner folders and changeset markers.
- The shallow model subtree (until slice 9 "the fresh drawing") is the
  scratch 'Tree<Viewnode>' a visit builds of what a vognode should
  hold, which the diff view's reconciler matches the vognode's
  children against. It is shallow: it includes other vognodes but not
  what their own visits draw. The folder reconciler's input, a
  folder's goal list ('FolderGoalItem'), is a flat list of what each
  member should be, not a tree, and keeps its name.
- Two reconcilers run at an editable node's visit: the older one
  ('complete_managed_children'), which reconciles the content list and
  each folder's members, and the diff view's
  ('reconcile_children_with_fresh_drawing', renamed in slice 9). A
  managed child is one a reconciler drew and may detach; the user's
  viewnode ('is_the_users_viewnode') is one neither drew: an
  unrestricted vognode born 'Unremarkable', carrying no sign, and not
  a member of the folder it stands in. A match key ('MatchKey') is
  what identifies a viewnode among its siblings for the diff view's
  reconciler: the ID it stands for, or its kind.
- A commit readiness aggregate (until slice 10 "a derived
  commit-readiness aggregate") is a thing with leaves under it: a
  folder, a changeset marker, a node, a file, the whole changeset. It
  is ready when every leaf under it that can be ready is.
- A change headline (slice 8) is a headline whose metadata carries a
  '(leaves ...)' fact; it is ready when its '(ready ...)' names every
  object its '(leaves ...)' lists, and otherwise unready.
- An already-staged entry (until slice 7 "a pre-staged entry") is an
  entry git's index already holds when stage-and-commit runs, staged
  by a hand or another tool.
- The startup warnings channel: 'load_config' returns warnings beside
  the config, 'main' records them once on the runtime
  ('record_startup_warnings', 'server/types/env.rs'), the server serves
  them on request, and each client shows them in its '*SKG Server
  Warnings*' buffer on connect.

** The diff view's facts nest under '(diff ...)' (slice 3)
A headline's diff-view facts and its readiness request form one list:
'(diff (presence SIGN...) (leaves N OBJECT...) (ready OBJECT...)
(readyRequest OBJECT...))', the last part being '(unreadyRequest
OBJECT...)' for an unmark. The parts come in that order, and a part is
absent when it has nothing to say: 'presence' when both signs are
None, 'leaves' when no object names a leaf, 'ready' when nothing is
ready (a bare '(ready)' keeps its meaning, "the single leaf is
ready"), the request when none is pending. A headline with nothing to
say carries no '(diff ...)'; '(diff)' is never written. The form sits
where '(diff ...)' sits today: inside '(node ...)', '(deleted ...)',
'(unknown ...)' and '(historical ...)', and at top level on the
properties other than 'bodyDiff'. For instance '(skg (node (id 7)
(repo main) (diff (presence node+ rel+) (leaves 2 node rel) (ready
node))))'. A changed folder's '(KIND changed)' and bare '(ready)'
('FolderChangeFacts', 'org_to_text.rs:300') stay as they are: a folder
carries no leaves fact and no '(diff ...)'.

** A removed member follows the editability rule (slice 5)
A removed member in content position (under an unrestricted vognode or
under a historical phantom) is no longer always write-protected: it is
created editable, with its body from the after-graph, when its node is
in U (it always is, see "Words"), and the editability rule at its own
visit then keeps it editable when its node is unclaimed and
write-protects it when another occurrence claimed first, exactly as
for a member. An editable removed member's visit draws its content
list and its change viewnodes like any editable occurrence's; a
write-protected one draws nothing of its node, as today. A removed
member in a partner folder stays as the folder's other members are
('FolderGoalShape::Removed'). The one encoding of a removed
relationship is then: an unrestricted vognode, a non-member, with the
sign '~-'; "write-protected" leaves the encoding, and
'is_unrestricted_removed_member' already tests nothing about
editability.

Consequences:
- 'mk_removed_member_viewnode' ('server/diff_view/change_viewnodes.rs:186-199')
  builds the viewnode editable ('Editability::Editable { body, ...}'
  with 'node.body' from 'straddle.after'), 'AffectsParent::False', when
  the node is in U, and write-protected otherwise; its callers,
  'reconcile_removed_content_members' ('changes.rs:540-588') and
  'append_historical_phantom_content' ('change_viewnodes.rs:138-160'),
  pass U (the sets come from 'pcsvf_sets_of_buffer').
- 'adopt_fresh_facts' ('fresh_drawing.rs:211-229') keeps its one
  direction: it write-protects an existing occurrence when the fresh

** All-caps (slice 12)
