* Words this plan uses
Terms the glossary defines already are used as it defines them:
viewforest, viewnode, occurrence, editable and write-protected,
member and non-member ('affectsParent'), folder, viewparent, role
graft, phantom (deleted, unknown, historical), restricted

worktree's), the editability rule, the readiness request, the
edit request, the view request, the stage-and-commit. Terms this
plan coins or fixes:
** view id, vid
A view id is the identifier of one viewnode within one buffer,
unique for the life of the buffer. On the wire it is the atom
'(vid X)', where X is one or more lowercase letters and digits
(base 36: two characters cover 1,296 viewnodes). In prose, in
names and in messages it is "view id"; 'vid' is the wire's word
and the field's name only.
** owner
The owner of a viewnode is the reconciler that created it, or
nobody when the user typed or pasted it. A member of a content
list is owned by the content reconciler and a member of a folder
by that folder's reconciler, whoever typed it, because membership
is a fact of the graph that its reconciler maintains. The owner
is recorded per view id in the view's state, never inferred from
a viewnode's shape.
** warrant
The graph warrants a child when the model its reconciler builds
from the graph has an item with the child's key; a second child
with a key a sibling has is unwarranted. "Warrant" is ordinary
English here (to call for), not a term of the code; the code
should say "stale" and "duplicate".
** key
A child's key is what a reconciler matches it by: for an
occurrence of a graph node, the node's ID as the live graph
resolves it; for an entry of a property folder, the entry itself;
for any other viewnode, its kind. The code's names are 'MatchKey'
and, in the older reconciler, the goal item's key.
** convert, dead viewnode, prune
To convert a child is to keep it under a kind the buffer can show
truthfully: a vognode leaf and every non-vognode become a dead
viewnode; a vognode with a subtree becomes a write-protected
non-member. A dead viewnode ('ViewnodeKind::DeadViewnode') has no
title, ID or facts and is pruned once childless. The prune is the
existing postorder pass 'prune_self_deletable_when_empty', which
runs after every completion and before the buffer is rendered.
** the three kinds of change
A node's existence ('(node added)', '(node removed)'), a
relationship's existence ('(rel added)', '(rel removed)'), and
the node's other properties ('(properties changed)'). The Rust
type is 'Existence { Added, Removed }' for the two slots and a
boolean for the properties. "Presence" and "sign" as names of
these go; the heralds keep their glyphs ('∃+', '∃-', '~+', '~-',
'M').
** restore requests
'(editRequest restoreNode)' and '(editRequest restoreRel)', two
edit requests the diff view's buffers offer; the commands are
'skg-restore-deleted-node' and 'skg-restore-removed-membership'.
** membership
A membership is the relationship that causes a node to be drawn
as a member of a list or folder; the member is the node. "Removed
member", "stale member", "former member" and "new member" name
the node by its membership's fate and stay.
** How to say it in messages
A message that concerns one headline names its view id as "view
id X" after the node's ID; a message about a node's several
occurrences lists their view ids.

** Every viewnode has a view id (slice 1)
*** The atom and the field
Every viewnode will carry a view id: 'Viewnode' gains a field
'vid : Option<Vid>', 'None' until the view assigns one, and the
printer writes '(vid X)' as the last part of the '(skg ...)'
wrapper, on every kind of viewnode (vognodes, phantoms, folders,
properties, dead viewnodes, the restricted placeholder). The
parser reads it into the maybe-placed viewnode and the field. X
is one or more characters from the lowercase letters and the
digits; the view assigns them from a counter in base 36, so the
first 1,296 are two characters and the length grows only when the
counter needs it.
*** Assignment and uniqueness
A view id is assigned when a viewnode without one is found in the
view's forest at the end of a completion (one pass, after the
prune), and never reassigned: a parsed viewnode keeps the vid the
buffer carried. The counter lives in the view's state
('ViewState' in 'server/types/views_state.rs') with the forest.
When the parser meets two headlines with one vid, the first in
buffer order keeps it and the second loses it (it is a copy the
user made, and will get a fresh one); a headline with no vid is
one the user typed. A vid once used is never reused within the
buffer's life, so a vid that reappears after a cut and paste
names the same viewnode moved.
*** The ownership record
'ViewState' gains a table from view id to the viewnode's owner
(an enum 'Owner': the content reconciler, a folder reconciler by
folder kind, the role-tree attach, the diff view's reconciler,
the stub roots, or the user, which is the default for a vid the
table lacks). A reconciler that creates a viewnode sets an
in-memory field 'owner' on it (not printed); the
end-of-completion pass records the owner of every newly assigned
vid in the table, and drops table entries whose vid is no longer
in the forest. After a parse, a viewnode's owner is looked up by
vid before any reconciler runs. Slices 4 and 5 read this table;
slice 1 only builds and maintains it, and tests that it
round-trips.
*** The clients
Both clients hide the whole '(skg ...)' sexp and look atoms up by
name, so they need no change to display the atom; the conformance
list 'emittable_metadata_atoms' in 'server/heralds.rs' learns
'vid' with a vacuous rule, and 'tests/shared/herald-rules.sexp'
is regenerated as its test says. The save round-trips the atom
untouched; the clients' save path strips nothing of it.
*** The tests
The comparison helpers will ignore the atom:
'assert_metadata_eq!' ('server/metadata_canon.rs') and the
pruned-change-view helpers 'node_lines' and
'assert_lines_in_order' ('tests/pruned_change_view/common.rs')
strip '(vid X)' before comparing, and a shared function
'without_vids' does it for any other whole-buffer assertion that
needs it. Expected-buffer literals that are parser inputs need no
vid. Literals of expected output change only where a helper
cannot be used; the agent rewrites those by script and says in
its commit how many. The round-trip tests ('tests/idempotence.rs'
and the pruned change view's "the reconciled buffer must be the
buffer saved") hold because a parsed vid is preserved. New tests:
a vid survives a save and a rerender; a copied headline gets a

** Convert, then prune: the older reconciler (slice 4)
*** The rule
A reconciler never detaches. A child it owns that the graph no
longer warrants (a stale child or the extra copies of a
duplicate) is converted: a vognode leaf and every non-vognode to
a dead viewnode; a vognode with a subtree to a write-protected
non-member, which keeps its title and ID, extends nothing, holds
no editable claim, and can be adopted back when its membership
returns. After every completion the postorder prune removes dead
leaves (and, as today, childless deleted phantoms and empty
write-protected folders), never a root. A viewnode nobody owns
(the user's) is never converted, so it holds its ancestors alive.
*** One convert function
'keep_stale_child_holding_the_users'
('server/update_buffer/util.rs') and the duplicate detach in
'complete_managed_children' become one function
'convert_unwarranted_child', which takes the child and whether it
is a leaf, applies the rule above, and is the only place that
changes a stale or duplicate child; the detach of a childless
stale child and the "Duplicates are redundant: always detach"
branch go, and so does focus transfer on detach there. The
function sets 'writeProtected' on a kept vognode as well as
'affectsParent' false; it does not touch 'birth' (today's keep
path resets it, which made a kept role graft read as the user's).
*** Duplicates
Of two or more children with one key, the copy kept as the member
is the editable one, else the first in buffer order; the others
are converted. The save accepts a copy under a vognode only as a
write-protected non-member (two editable copies are refused as
"Multiple occurrences of node X are editable", two members as
"non-ignored content children must be unique"), which is the form
the rule produces.
*** The folder validators
'partnerFolder_children_have_distinct_skgids' ('local.rs') counts
every unrestricted child; it will count members only, as the
content list's check does, so that a converted copy may stand
beside the member. Every folder validator refuses a dead viewnode
today; each will accept one, since a dead viewnode is what a
stale entry with a parked non-member under it becomes. The flags
surface check ('write_protected_edits.rs') will ignore dead
viewnodes and non-members among the folder's children.
*** The folder reconciler adopts a returning key
When a folder's goal has a key and an unmanaged child with that
key stands in the folder (a non-member, write-protected or not),
the reconciler adopts that child as the member and creates none;
'mark_goal_children_as_folder_members' ('child_data.rs') runs
before the create path, and the create path skips a key an
existing child has. This closes the first bug in
'TODO/problems.org' of 2026-10-23 (two identical members after a
membership returns, and the save refused).
*** The skgrepo-set switch
The prepass ('server/update_buffer/skgrepo_switch.rs') converts
unrestricted vognodes of a now restricted skgrepo to restricted
vognodes and prunes restricted leaves; it will also convert a
deleted or unknown phantom whose node's home skgrepo (or, for an
unknown, whose relRepo) is now restricted, to a dead viewnode,
which the prune then removes if it is a leaf and keeps titleless
if it holds a subtree. This closes the second bug of 2026-10-23
(a deleted private node's title and body shown after a
restricting switch). The third observation of that day (a private
membership between public nodes still visible when the member has
a subtree) stays as it is, by decision; the problems entry will
say so and move to 'TODO/DONE/'.
*** Hidden content
A content member of a subscribee that the subscriber hides, found
under the subscribee with a subtree, becomes a write-protected
non-member there, like every other stale vognode with a subtree;
the detach of 'detach_hidden_subscribee_content' goes with the
rest. Its metadata will carry the same '(rels ...)' stats the
hidden folder's members carry (contained by the ancestor two
generations up, hidden by the one four up), so the heralds can
say so.
*** Orphaned folders
'dispose_orphaned_folder_child'
('server/update_buffer/ancestry.rs') detaches a member leaf of a
folder whose viewparent became a phantom, a restricted vognode or
a dead viewnode; it will convert instead, by the same function.

** Two restore edit requests (slice 7)
*** On the wire
'(editRequest restoreNode)' and '(editRequest restoreRel)' are
bare atoms beside 'delete' in 'NodeEditRequest' ('RestoreNode',
'RestoreRel'). 'restoreNode' is accepted on a historical phantom
and nowhere else; 'restoreRel' is accepted on an unrestricted
vognode that is a removed member (content position) or a member
of a partner folder carrying '(rel removed)', editable or
write-protected, and nowhere else. Two doors open for this: the
parser refuses every edit request on a deleted or historical
phantom today ("Unknown deleted key: editRequest"), and will
accept 'restoreNode' alone on a historical one; an edit request
lives in 'Editability::Editable' today, so a write-protected
occurrence carries none, and 'restoreRel' becomes the second
exception beside the relRepo request, carried on the vognode
outside 'Editability'. A 'restoreRel' on a historical phantom is
a parse error whose text says "This node is deleted; restore it
first (skg-restore-deleted-node)"; a 'restoreNode' on anything
but a historical phantom is refused likewise; the
one-request-per- node rule holds ("node editRequest may appear
only once").
*** What the save does
Both are collected with the save's graph edits, nodes before
memberships, so that a membership restored in one save may name a
node restored in it, and both are consumed after the commit where
every edit request is consumed; a save that stops for a
confirmation or fails leaves them in the buffer. 'restoreNode'
writes the node's file from the before-graph's copy
('GraphStraddle::before', an 'InRustGraph' holding title, body,

before-graph's inbound indexes ('contained_by', 'subscribers_of',
'overriders_of', 'hiders_of'): each recorder gains the node in
that list at the index it had at HEAD, clamped to the list's
length, in the skgrepo that recorded it, unless the recorder is
itself deleted (skipped) or lists it already. An out-link to a
node still deleted is kept as HEAD recorded it and shows as an
unknown phantom. 'restoreRel' re-adds the one relationship the
headline stands for, as HEAD recorded it, computed by
'relationship_to_viewparent'
('server/diff_view/readiness_requests.rs'), which already yields
the relation, the recorder and the member; a request on a

* Pitfalls the slices must respect
Facts about the code as it stands, each with its place, that an
agent would otherwise rediscover or trip over.
** The saved view is rebuilt from the parsed buffer
After a save, the saved view's forest is the one rerendered from
the parsed text ('update_buffer.rs', the saved-view branch), so
every per-viewnode state that must survive a save rides in an
atom; the parser treats an unknown atom as a hard error ("Unknown
node key", "Unknown metadata key"). Collateral views and
"rerender all views" re-complete a clone of the stored forest
instead. An ego_tree 'NodeId' is never stable across a save.
** The parser builds the twin struct
Headlines parse into the maybe-placed viewnode
('server/types/maybe_placed_viewnode.rs') and are placed into
'Viewnode' later; a new field goes on both, and the 64 'Viewnode
{' struct literals in 'server/' and 'tests/' must each gain it
(slice 1 of plan 15 did this by script; do the same).
** The completion runs every reconciler in one BFS, then prunes
'complete_viewforest' ('server/update_buffer/complete.rs') visits
nodes breadth-first, each visit running its node's reconcilers,
and calls 'prune_self_deletable_when_empty' at the end, before
the buffer is rendered; the prune is postorder, collapses a chain
of dead leaves in one pass, stops at a kind it does not prune (an
unrestricted vognode), and never prunes a view root. The
end-of-completion pass slice 1 adds (vid assignment and the owner
table) must run after the prune, so that a pruned leaf never
spends a vid.
** The two reconcilers and their order at a folder
At a changed folder's visit the older reconciler
('complete_managed_children_in_viewforest', through
'reconcile_partnerFolder_children_against_goal_list_with_deleted_extraIds')
places the members first, and then
'attach_memberRoleTrees_under_straddle_folder_members' hangs the
member role trees under them with the diff view's reconciler
('server/diff_view/partner_folders.rs'). The older reconciler
never looks below the children it manages.
** What each reconciler manages today
Content ('reconcile/content.rs'): unrestricted vognodes with
'affectsParent' true and unknown phantoms; non-members,
restricted vognodes, deleted and historical phantoms, folders,
properties and dead viewnodes are unmanaged. Partner folders
('child_data.rs', 'skgid_a_folder_child_stands_for'): members,
removed members, unknown and historical phantoms; other
non-members are unmanaged, which is why a kept non-member whose
key returns is duplicated today. Alias, ID and flags folders:
entries of their kind only. The diff view ('changes.rs'): front
change viewnodes by kind, removed members by shape, and under
phantoms and folder members everything with a match key but the
user's. Slices 4 and 5 replace the shape tests of ownership with
the owner table; the membership tests stay.
** Non-members sort first and can take the editable claim
'order_children_as_non_vognodes_then_ignored_then_content'
('content.rs') puts non-members ahead of members, folders move
unmanaged children first, and the editability rule gives the
claim to the first occurrence in level order whatever its
'affectsParent'. A kept non-member left editable (today's keep
path) therefore steals the claim and spends budget; slice 4
write-protects it.
** What a dead viewnode is and is not
'ViewnodeKind::DeadViewnode' has no title, ID, facts or match
key; the parser discards text typed on its line; its children are
visited but it is not extended or charged; it cannot be editable;
'mark_orphans_under_dead_parents_false' makes its member children
non-members; and the parser refuses it inside every folder kind,
under a historical phantom and at the top level (slice 4 opens
the folders).
** The duplicate the save accepts
Under an editable parent two editable copies of one node are
refused ('Multiple_Defining_Viewnodes'), two members are refused
('nonignored_children_have_distinct_skgids', members only), and a
copy that is both write-protected and a non-member is accepted;
in a checked folder 'partnerFolder_children_have_distinct_skgids'
counts every unrestricted child and refuses it (slice 4 changes
this). Confirmed by the throwaway runs of 2026-10-23
('tests/results/review16/throwaway2/', if present).
** A write-protected occurrence emits nothing
The save reads nothing under a write-protected occurrence
('traverse.rs'), a member child there is refused
('MemberChildOfWriteProtectedOccurrence'), and the rerender does
not extend it ('extend_true_content_at_unrestrictedVognode'
clobbers and returns), so its existing children stay as they are;
only its folders reconcile, at their own visits. A deleted line
under a write-protected parent that the model still has comes
back (a role graft silently, a folder member with a repair
warning).
** Edit requests live in the editable half
'NodeEditRequest' sits inside 'Editability::Editable', so a
write-protected occurrence carries none and the parser turns
'delete' and 'merge' there into
'EditRequestOnWriteProtectedOccurrence'; the relRepo request is a
separate field for that reason. A phantom's grammar ('(deleted
...)', '(historical ...)') accepts the keys id, repo and diff
only. Edit requests are consumed in
'consume_edit_requests_after_save' after the commit; the
text-release challenge returns after consumption, so a retried
save may carry a consumed request.
** The before-graph is whole
'GraphStraddle::before' is an 'InRustGraph' built from HEAD's
blobs ('server/graph_straddle/build.rs', 'git_snapshot.rs'),
cached by HEAD identity, holding every node whole and the inbound
indexes; nothing today writes a node from it, and
'complete_from_rust' is crate-private.
** Relationship of a folder member
'relationship_to_viewparent' ('readiness_requests.rs') returns
(relation, recorder, member) for a folder member from the
folder's kind and viewparent, and nothing for the two filter
folders and the acquired folders; several parameters named
'recorder_pid' hold the viewparent (slice 0 renames them).
** The clients hide the whole sexp
Both clients overlay or conceal the entire '(skg ...)' sexp and
look atoms up by name; an atom without a herald rule shows
nothing and breaks nothing; the server's conformance test
requires every emittable atom to have a rule, so a new atom needs
a vacuous one and the regenerated
'tests/shared/herald-rules.sexp'.
** Test helpers compare substrings by exact text
'node_lines' matches "(node (id X) " and 'assert_lines_in_order'
finds lines by substring ('tests/pruned_change_view/common.rs');
'assert_metadata_eq!' ignores atom order but not an atom's
presence; 'tests/idempotence.rs' requires byte-identical
rerenders. The integration suite's expected buffers are literal.
** Fixed temp paths collide
The Rust tests use fixed per-test temp paths; run one suite at a
time, and rerun a failing test alone before reading it as a
failure. 'import-md-and-org' fails at baseline
('TODO/problems.org').
** The post-commit check is blind to leftovers
It asserts that none of '(diff ', 'changed)', 'changesetMarker',
'oldText', 'historical', 'homeSkgrepoMove', '(ready' remains; a
parked non-member passes it, so slice 5's tests assert the
leftovers by name.
