This document answers three questions you asked in
'18_review.org': whether birth was a bad idea and where it is
used; whether a stronger dedup among siblings would solve the
duplicate problems; and whether it would let both birth and
ownership go. An Opus agent listed every read of birth and of
owner in 'server/' and every place where two siblings can stand
for one node; a throwaway test ran the four claims that were only
read. The reports are under 'tests/results/review18/'.

* Terms
** birth
A viewnode's *birth* says why it was drawn: unremarkable, or as a
role graft of one role, written '(birth roleGraft container)' on
the wire. The *birth check*, run at every render, asks the graph
whether the relationship still holds, and clears the birth if
not.
** owner
A viewnode's *owner* is the pass that drew it (a reconciler, the
role-tree attach, the stub roots) or the user. It lives in memory
per buffer, keyed by vid, and never on the wire; a headline whose
vid the table does not know is the user's.
** key, warrant, match
A reconciler's model lists items by *key*: a node ID for a
member or a removed member, an alias text, a folder kind. The
graph *warrants* a child when the model has its key. To *match*
is to pair a model item with an existing child of its key.
** adoption and merge
Your proposal is a *merge*: among siblings, one occurrence of a
node wins, and the content of the others moves under it. The
weaker form, an *adoption*, the code already has in two places:
a reconciler whose model has a key takes the existing child of
that key, whoever drew it, instead of creating a second; a second
child of a key already matched is the one rule's duplicate.
** foldering, and the chain
To *folder* a role tree is to draw every partner as a member of a
folder of its role, never as a graft directly under a vognode.
The *chain* is the containerward tree's shape today: under a
node its containers, under each of those its containers, up to
the roots, with no folder between the levels.

** What they decide, in plain terms
An owner is read in the diff view alone; the content reconciler,
the folder reconcilers and the role-tree attach stamp it and
never look at it. The diff view reads it twice. First, when it
matches its model against the children of a node, it considers
only the children it owns. Second, when a child it owns has no
item in the model, it converts the child; and under a stale child
it converts everything it or the attach drew, leaving yours and
the content reconciler's alone.
** The first read causes the duplicates
A child another pass drew is never matched, so the diff view
draws a second one. Four cases were run. The alias folder you
asked for gets a changed one beside it. A containerward graft
under a root that once contained the node now containing it gets
a removed member of that node beside it, and the graft is tagged
'(rel removed)' though what it stands for holds in both graphs.

member beside it, or, when both stood there before the drop, both
come to read as removed members. The role trees under a folder's
member are drawn again beside yours. Matching by key among all
siblings, as the other reconcilers do, removes all four.
** The second read is needed only after a conversion
A removed member carries '(rel removed)' in the buffer text from
the render that drew it until the completion that finds it stale,

kind; the agent traced the parse and the rerenders and found

at the moment of conversion the child says what it is. Only after
the conversion, when its change facts are cleared, do a retained
removed member, a graft whose birth was cleared and a
write-protected copy you pasted become one shape, and nothing

on, and a childless one was pruned at once. The one place that
read the owner on later completions was the subtree under a stale
child, which it converted recursively by owner; by change facts

owner rule did.
** What foldering adds
Without foldering one thing under a vognode still needs an owner
or a birth to be recognized: a graft drawn directly under it,
which after the birth check clears its birth is a plain
write-protected non-member. With every partner in a folder,
nothing the server draws under a vognode is unmarked: folders by
kind, removed members by change facts, phantoms, changeset

the vids keep their other jobs: identity across renders, the
messages, fold state.

* What a merge rule would have to decide
** Which copy wins
The code's nearest word is "defining", the editable occurrence.
With no editable copy a tie-break is needed, and a
write-protected member must beat a non-member graft, or the save
drops the membership, since the content list and the editable
folders are read from the buffer.
** What the key is
The collected ID, not the node's own, for the overrider's sake.
Restricted vognodes have none. Phantoms have IDs: may a phantom
lose to a live occurrence?
** What the loser loses
Its vid is retired; its readiness and edit requests, its focus
and fold state are lost or must move.
** What happens to the loser's children
Under a write-protected loser they are non-members; under an
editable loser they are members, and the convert makes children
non-members without write-protecting them, so editable
non-members can arrive under an editable winner. Copies of the
winner's members collide, so the merge recurses. Your notes
change position. A graft loser under a member winner loses its
role; a member loser under a graft winner loses the membership
unless the member always wins.
** Where it runs
After the parse it would silently change what the save writes. At
each visit, before that node's reconcilers, is the natural place;
the prune is too late. It must also run for collateral views and
rerender-all, which re-complete a stored forest.

******* [Claude] you are right for a stale copy
For a stale member there is no gain that a better rule would not
give. The reason I gave was the editable claim: a buffer holds
one editable occurrence of a node, the walk that assigns it is
breadth-first, and the second throwaway run before plan 17 saw a
stale copy, met first, keep the claim while the node's real
member, met later, was write-protected. But that is a fault of
the walk, cured by letting a member occurrence win over a
non-member one when neither carries a request; a retained copy
would then never steal the claim. And write-protecting has a cost
I understated: a write-protected occurrence may hold no members,
so m's content under it becomes notes, '⊥ mc', though m still
contains mc. Retained editable, m keeps its content as content,
and you can go on editing m where it stands, as a note under k.

The gain is real in one case, the extra copy of a duplicate. The
save refuses two editable occurrences of one node, so when a
member and a copy stand under one parent the copy must be
write-protected. So the rule for the plan: a retained copy keeps
the editability it had, its content stays content when it is
editable and becomes notes when it is not; the extra copies of a
duplicate are write-protected; and the editability walk prefers a
member to a non-member among occurrences without a request. That
changes what slice 4 built, which write-protects every retained
copy, and its tests; "retain" then means "keep in place as a
non-member", with nothing said about write-protection.
