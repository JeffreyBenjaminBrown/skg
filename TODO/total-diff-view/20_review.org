**** TODO [Claude] Yielding would write-protect the member itself
Define: the editability walk visits the occurrences in level
order. An occurrence made editable without a request of its
own holds its node's claim tentatively. The preference: when
the walk reaches an editable member occurrence of a node whose
claim a tentative non-member occurrence holds, the non-member
yields: it is write-protected with its whole content subtree,
and the member takes the claim, so that its content is
extended. The slice's test shows the plain case. Suppose the
worktree holds k contains l and m, and m contains mc. In the
view of k, k is editable and m stands under it with mc. In a
second view the user moves m into l and saves. In the first
view's rerender, m under k is stale, is retained as a
non-member keeping its editability, and holds m's claim
tentatively, at level two. At level three the walk meets l's
member m, editable; the retained m yields:
#+begin_example
  * k
  ** l
  *** m
  **** mc
  ** ⊥☮ m
  *** ⊥☮ mc
#+end_example
The exception needs a cycle. Suppose instead that m contains
n and n contains m, and the retained m holds its content:
#+begin_example
  * k
  ** ⊥ m
  *** n
  **** m
#+end_example
If the lower m were an editable member, the preference would
have the upper m yield, and 'writeProtect_content_subtree'
would write-protect the upper m with everything under it, the
lower m included: the member meant to take the claim would
lose its editability, and the walk would have write-protected
both. So an ancestor never yields ('prior_is_ancestor' in
'take_claim_from_tentative_nonMember'), and the lower m is
left to the ordinary rule, write-protected as a later
occurrence of a claimed node. In practice the lower m arrives
write-protected already, since a buffer holds one editable
occurrence of a node; the guard is for the shape, which a
cycle makes possible.
