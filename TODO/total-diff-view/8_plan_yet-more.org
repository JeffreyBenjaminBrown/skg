** Scope
Four changes, which together remove every piece of save-time and
rerender-time logic that depended on what a buffer looked like
before: a write-protected occurrence is validated against the graph
alone; write-protection becomes something the user requests and the
server performs; the total diff view no longer makes occurrences
editable or appends roots on its own after a save, and a command does
both on demand; and a short housekeeping list. The refusal that the
previous plan's slice 3 added is removed.

** Words this plan uses
Terms from 'docs/glossary.org', one line each, and three this plan
settles.
- The TDV (total diff view) is up to four buffers that show the
  graph's uncommitted changes, HEAD against the worktree. The graph
  straddle ('GraphStraddle { before, after }') is what it compares:
  the before-graph read from HEAD, the after-graph the live graph.
- The sets of a buffer's PCSVF ('pcsvf_sets_of_buffer'): C is the
  changed nodes, R the content origins the containerward walk reaches
  from C, D the deduplicated origins (the buffer's first roots), U the
  nodes to unfold contentward (C, R and the walks). The sets are
  recomputed from the refreshed straddle at every rerender; this plan
  calls those the fresh sets. A buffer's standing roots are its roots
  when a rerender begins.
- An occurrence of a graphnode is a viewnode that stands for it
  (an unrestricted vognode). A buffer has at most one editable
  occurrence of a node; the others are write-protected. An editable
  occurrence has its content list drawn under it; the server adds
  nothing under a write-protected one. A content occurrence is a root
  or a child of an unrestricted vognode; a folder occurrence is a
  member of a partner folder.
- A member is a child viewnode whose 'affectsParent' is true: the
  save reads it as part of its parent's content. A non-member has
  'affectsParent' false; it sits under the node without belonging to
  its content, and the client shows '⊥' on it. A headline the user
  types arrives as a member (the parser's default, and
  'na_affectsParent_under_visible_parent_becomes_isContainer').
- An editable-view request ('ViewRequest::Editable', the metadata
  atom 'editableView', C-c s d) asks for an occurrence to become the
  editable one. The draw rule ('apply_editable_draw_rule') grants it
  at the occurrence's visit, write-protecting the occurrence that was
  editable by default together with its content subtree
  ('writeProtect_content_subtree'), and passes the request to the
  content children (the cascade), bounded by the node budget
  ('initial_node_limit'); in a TDV only a node outside U is charged
  against the node limit.
- The write-protected-occurrence validator is
  'errors_and_normalize_new_writeProtected_occurrences'
  ('server/from_text/write_protected_edits.rs'), one of the checks a
  save runs on every buffer; today it compares the saved buffer's
  write-protected occurrences with the server's last rendering of
  that buffer. Its sibling 'errors_for_nodes_of_u_left_without_editable_occurrence'
  is the TDV-only refusal the previous plan added. Both go.
- The editable-occurrences step is 'make_freshU_editable_at_first_occurrences'
  ('server/pcsvf.rs'): before completion of a TDV buffer after a
  save, it makes an occurrence editable for every node of the fresh U
  that has none. With it, 'contentOrigins_missing_from_buffer' and
  'append_contentOrigins_to_buffer' append roots for origins the
  buffer draws nowhere, and 'complete_appended_roots' draws them. This
  plan stops running all of that after saves and keeps it for a
  command.
- Three settled words: this plan says "make editable" and
  "write-protect", never "promote" or "demote"; it says "non-member"
  for 'affectsParent' false; and it never says "pre-pass".

* Agreed behavior
** A write-protected occurrence is validated against the graph alone
The save judges each write-protected occurrence of the saved buffer
by the live graph, with no reference to any earlier rendering.
- Its title and its home skgrepo equal the node's in the graph, or
  the save is refused as an edit to a write-protected occurrence,
  naming the node and the field.
- Its alias, ID and flags folders, when present under it, equal the
  node's in the graph (aliases as a list, IDs and flags as sets), or
  the save is refused the same way. A folder that is absent expresses
  no opinion, as the editable-set folders already work.
- Its children: a member child is refused, with a message that names
  the node and says a child typed under a write-protected occurrence
  cannot become its content, and that the user may request an
  editable occurrence (C-c s d) or mark the child as not affecting
  its parent. Non-member children are the user's own and are not
  checked.
- Nothing is normalized: the save never changes a child's
  'affectsParent'.
The matching of occurrences with the last rendering, by node and path
from the buffer root, is deleted, and with it the notion of a "new"
write-protected occurrence. 'flags_surface_errors_against_graph',
which already does the graph-based version of the flags check, is the
model.

** The TDV no longer refuses a save for leaving a node without an editable occurrence
The user may write-protect the only editable occurrence of a changed
node. The readiness facts and gestures work on write-protected
occurrences, so the node keeps its readiness herald; its change
decorations and content are no longer shown there, and the readiness
summary counts the node as unshown. The check, its error variant
'NodeOfU_LeftWithoutEditableOccurrence', its message, its tests and
the sentences the docs gained for it go.

** Write-protection is requested, and the server performs it
'skg-set-write-protected' no longer writes 'writeProtected' into a
headline. In its place, two commands write view requests:
- 'skg-request-write-protected' (C-c s w; Neovim
  ':SkgRequestWriteProtected', 'sw') writes '(viewRequests
  writeProtectedView)': at the occurrence's visit in the next
  rerender, the server makes the occurrence write-protected (title and
  body resynced from the graph, as 'clobberWriteProtectedViewnode'
  does), makes each of its direct unrestricted children a non-member,
  and consumes the request. The children keep their own editability
  and their own subtrees.
- 'skg-request-write-protected-recursive' (C-c s W; Neovim
  ':SkgRequestWriteProtectedRecursive', 'sW') writes '(viewRequests
  writeProtectedSubtreeView)': the same, applied to the occurrence
  and its whole content subtree, as 'writeProtect_content_subtree'
  does for the draw rule, every child under every write-protected
  node in it becoming a non-member.
The occurrence arrives at the save still editable, so edits it
carries are saved first; the write-protection follows in the
rerender. A request on an already write-protected occurrence is a
no-op. A write-protect request and an editable-view request on the
same occurrence contradict each other and are refused; on different
occurrences of the same node they are the move of editability, and
are allowed. 'writeProtect_content_subtree', wherever it is used, now
also makes the children it passes non-members, so that no
write-protected occurrence the server draws ever holds a member.
Hand-editing 'writeProtected' into a headline's metadata keeps
working for a leaf and is refused for an occurrence with members, by
the rule above; the docs stop suggesting it.

** An editable node adopts a non-member child that is its content
When the content reconciler draws an editable node's content list and
finds, for an entry of that list, no member child but a non-member
child standing for the same node, it makes that child the member,
keeping the viewnode and its fold state, instead of creating a second
one beside it. Where a member child for the entry exists, non-member
children are left alone as today. This is what lets editability move
back to an occurrence whose children were made non-members when it
was write-protected, and it also ends the double drawing that parking
a content node under an editable parent produces today.

The adopted child will in most cases need to be moved,
to reflect its order among the other content.
** The view follows U only on command
After a save, a TDV buffer's sets are recomputed and completion draws
by the fresh U wherever it draws something new (a member outside U is
a write-protected leaf; nothing the server drew editable is
write-protected again by the server). But the server no longer makes
any occurrence editable, and no longer appends roots, on its own. A
node of C with no editable occurrence is unshown, and the readiness
summary counts it; the summary line in both clients names the command
instead of saying "reopen it".

The command, 'skg-tdv-show-unshown-changes' (C-c r u; Neovim
':SkgTdvShowUnshownChanges', 'ru'), works on a TDV buffer. The client
refuses with "Save the buffer first." if the buffer has unsaved
changes, else sends the request "show unshown changes" with the view
ID. The server refreshes the straddle ('current_graph_straddle_in_slot':
the after-graph becomes the connection's current live graph, and the
before-graph is read again from the owned skgrepos' HEAD only if HEAD
has moved since it was read; otherwise the cached pair is reused),
takes the buffer's registered viewforest, and for every node of the
buffer's C that has no editable occurrence:
makes its first content occurrence in level order
editable (body from the live graph), else its first folder
occurrence, else, when it has no occurrence at all, remembers it. For
the remembered nodes it appends their content origins that the buffer
draws nowhere, as stub roots in ID order, deduplicated with the
standing roots pinned ('contentOrigins_missing_from_buffer' with the
standing roots), and completes them. It then rerenders the buffer
through the ordinary TDV rerender path, which draws the content under
each newly editable occurrence by the rule for U, and streams the
rerendered buffer and the readiness summary as a save does. The
command does not care who write-protected an occurrence: the user
asked for every change, and gets one editable occurrence per node of
C.

** What stays as it is
Roots linger. "Drawn", for the readiness summary, means the node has
an editable occurrence or a historical phantom in content position.
A node of U is editable with its whole content list when the buffer
is first drawn and after the command. The editable-view request
behaves as the previous plan left it. Readiness, marks and the
stage-and-commit command are untouched.

* Pitfalls the slices must respect
** the editable map and 'writeProtect_content_subtree'
The draw rule's subtree write-protection removes the nodes it
write-protects from the editable map ('visited'), so that their
editable copies can be drawn elsewhere in the same completion. The
request's non-recursive form must do the same for the one occurrence
it write-protects, and the recursive form for the subtree, or a later
visit will treat them as the editable copies.
** the content reconciler matches members only
'complete_relevant_children_in_viewforest' is called with the
relevance "affectsParent is true", so a non-member child is never
matched to a goal entry and a member is created beside it. The
adoption of slice 1 happens before that call: for each goal entry
with no member child, a non-member child standing for the entry's
node becomes the member.
** typed headlines are members by default
A headline the user types under any node carries no 'affectsParent'
and is made a member by the parser, so a child typed under a
write-protected occurrence is exactly the case slice 0 refuses. A
child the user marks '(affectsParent false)' by hand passes.
** the write-protect request must be applied before the occurrence expands
In 'visit_normal_node', the editable-view request is handled before
'expand_true_content_at_unrestrictedVognode'. The write-protect
request goes in the same place: once applied, the occurrence is
write-protected and the expansion takes the write-protected path
('clobberWriteProtectedViewnode'), drawing nothing under it. In a
TDV, 'tag_content_members' then runs on the write-protected path with
no sets; it tags members only, so it must be extended to tag
non-member children whose node the straddle lists as content, or the
'(diff ...)' presence facts vanish from the children at the moment
they become non-members.
** the save handler refreshes the straddle and threads U into validation
'update_from_and_rerender_buffer_with_approvals' refreshes the
connection's straddle before the save and passes the buffer's U into
'buffer_to_validated_saveplan_with_fork_skgrepos_and_previous_view_in_graph',
and 'server/from_text.rs' passes the previous viewforest to the two
validators. After slice 0 no validator needs the previous viewforest
or U; remove the parameters down to 'server/from_text.rs', keeping
the straddle refresh if the save's own TDV path still needs it (the
rerender does).
** the command runs on the registered viewforest
The server's copy of a view is what the client holds only after the
client applied the last rerender, which is why the client saves
first. The command mutates the registered viewforest and rerenders it
through 'rerender_view' with the TDV buffer kind, then registers and
streams the result as 'stream_prepared_rerenders' does for other
rerenders.
** the TDV's write-protected titles come from the live graph
A write-protected occurrence in a TDV buffer shows the after-graph's
title for a node in both graphs; slice 0's comparison uses the live
graph, which is the after-graph. Historical phantoms and removed
members are not unrestricted vognodes and are not checked.
