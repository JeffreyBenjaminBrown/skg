#+title: What Jeff should do, between and around plans

These are the habits directed at Jeff rather than at the planner or
the executing agents, distilled from the total diff view effort
(October 2026; post-mortem in 'TODO/total-diff-view/12_total-review.org').
They are short because most of the cost was elsewhere; the planner's
habits are in 'plans.org' and the review method in 'code-reviews.org'.

* Decide the shared model before the first plan that touches it
The questions that took three review rounds to settle (which
occurrence of a node is editable, what a write-protected occurrence's
children are, what a request means on each kind of node) were
answerable from first principles before any code was written, and
you answered them that way in the end ("let's not think about how
things got there, just what they are"). When a discussion document
names a model that several slices will touch, settle it there, as
states and transitions, before saying "write the plan".

* Process a review in one sitting, or mark what is left
A review processed in two halves led to a plan written from the
first half, with the second half's decisions arriving after the run.
When you cannot finish, mark the unprocessed items so they can be
found ("jeff has yet to process"), as you did in the post-mortem, and
say so; the planner then writes the plan from the processed part
only and leaves the rest out, rather than guessing.

* Keep one list of decisions, and point at it
Decisions live in the discussion documents' resolved items and in
review threads, and the planner has to find them. A decision that is
on record and then forgotten (partial commits are dangerous; the save
refusal you later retracted) costs a round either way. When you
reverse a decision, say so where the original is, so that the list
stays one list.

* Ask for the vocabulary check after each plan
The two-question check in 'plans.org' ("what terms did this plan
coin; what does its code cite") costs one agent and ten minutes. Ask
for it when a plan finishes, before the review, so that a confusing
word is caught before the next plan spreads it.

* Schedule the full post-mortem at milestones, not at the end of each plan
Structure (where a feature should live, what to reify) is only
visible once the behavior has settled. Ask for the full method of
'code-reviews.org' when a feature's behavior has stopped changing,
and the short check otherwise.

* Mark your replies so the planner can find them, and expect the same back
Your rule, which works: a reply of yours gets a keyword; a reply of
the planner's under an item of yours is marked TODO so you can find
it; where you merely agree, the planner changes your TODO to DONE
without replying.
