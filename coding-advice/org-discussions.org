* Discussing plans and reviews in Org files
Jeff and Claude discuss plans, reviews and design questions
asynchronously, inside Org files under 'TODO/<topic>/' (for instance
'1_discussion.org', '2_plan.org'). Jeff reads and answers in Emacs.
* The two things that matter
** Searching for "\* TODO" finds everything that needs attention
Every heading that needs the other's attention is marked TODO. Each
of us marks our own new headings TODO, and each of us clears only the
TODOs on headings we wrote, once the other's reply has been read. So
Claude marks every reply it writes TODO, including a plain agreement,
and never changes the keyword on a heading of Jeff's: Jeff closes his
own TODOs himself after reading the reply. Other keywords (ONGOING,
AWAITS, DONE) are informal notes on the state of a heading; nothing
depends on them.

A TODO is for whoever didn't write it. A TODO in Claude's voice is for
Jeff, and a TODO in Jeff's voice is for Claude. A TODO of Jeff's that
already has a 'Claude says' child is answered and waiting for Jeff to
close it; Claude leaves it alone.
** The voice switch is visible
Every reply begins its headline with an author marker: 'Claude says'
or 'Jeff says'. Jeff often abbreviates his to 'js'; Claude always
writes the full 'Claude says'. More title text after the marker is
optional, e.g. 'Claude says: yes for data, and I'd stop there'.
Headings beneath a marked heading are by the same author until another
marker starts a reply, so the marker need not be repeated.
* Habits that help
** Replying
Reply to one of Jeff's headings as a child of it. Never alter his
words or his keywords; adding children is fine. Claude may
revise its own text, for instance to fold in decisions, but should leave
Jeff's direct edits to Claude's text in place.
** Folding decisions
When Jeff settles something, fold it into the body (a table, a plan
step, an open-question answer), and leave a one-line child saying
where it went, e.g. 'Claude says: folded into the relation-sides
table.'
** One answer per question
When one question appears in several places, answer it once and point
there from the others, so Jeff processes each issue once.
** Open questions
A plan or discussion response ends with an open-questions section. It
is self-contained: it defines its terms, so Jeff can answer it without
reading the body. Each question gets its own headline, with a one-line
answer added once it is answered. A question that is also discussed
in a section links there.
** Form
Prefer headlines to bullets, and let the tree be as irregular as the
content; see [[file:ORIENTATION.org][ORIENTATION]]. Prefer an org subtree to a table: a
table with long cells is unreadable in Emacs. No line is longer than
65 characters; a headline that would be longer moves its tail into
the first sentence of its body. Use single apostrophes around code
references. An org example inside a '#+begin_example' block has
every line indented two spaces, so that Emacs does not read its stars
as headlines. Do not commit planning files during discussion rounds;
Jeff stages and commits them himself.
** Two conventions that worked, and one rule
When a later decision changes what an earlier section says, add
a child 'TODO UPDATED [Claude] ...' to the earlier section rather
than editing it, so that Jeff meets the change where he reads.
While Jeff edits the main document, Claude answers in a
sidechannel file beside it ('temp-for-claude-to-edit.org') into
which Jeff copies the threads; Claude then edits only the
sidechannel, even to apply a change Jeff approved there, and a
change to the main document waits until Jeff says it is free.
Jeff sometimes closes a heading by prefixing 'DONE AGREED : ';
it closes like DONE.
** Clarity
Every sentence should have a subject and a verb.
Every new term should be introduced/defined before it is used.
