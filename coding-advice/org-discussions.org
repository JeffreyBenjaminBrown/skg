* Discussing plans and reviews in Org files
Jeff and Claude discuss plans, reviews and design questions
asynchronously, inside Org files under 'TODO/<topic>/' (for instance
'1_discussion.org', '2_plan.org'). Jeff reads and answers in Emacs.
* The two things that matter
** Searching for "\* TODO" finds everything that needs attention
After either of us finishes a pass, every heading that needs the other's
attention is marked TODO, and nothing else is. So when you finish
processing a TODO, clear it: mark it DONE, ONGOING, or something else,
or remove the keyword. Which of those you choose doesn't matter, and
Jeff is not consistent about it either. Other keywords (ONGOING,
AWAITS, DONE) are informal notes on the state of a heading; nothing
depends on them.

A TODO is for whoever didn't write it. A TODO in Claude's voice is for
Jeff, and a TODO in Jeff's voice is for Claude.
** The voice switch is visible
Every reply begins its headline with an author marker: 'Claude says'
or 'Jeff says'. Jeff often abbreviates his to 'js'; Claude always
writes the full 'Claude says'. More title text after the marker is
optional, e.g. 'Claude says: yes for data, and I'd stop there'.
Headings beneath a marked heading are by the same author until another
marker starts a reply, so the marker need not be repeated.
* Habits that help
** Replying
Reply to one of Jeff's headings as a child of it. Never alter his
words; changing his keyword and adding children is fine. Claude may
revise its own text, for instance to fold in decisions, but should leave
Jeff's direct edits to Claude's text in place.
** Folding decisions
When Jeff settles something, fold it into the body (a table, a plan
step, an open-question answer), and leave a one-line child saying
where it went, e.g. 'Claude says: folded into the relation-sides
table.'
** One answer per question
When one question appears in several places, answer it once and point
there from the others, so Jeff processes each issue once.
** Open questions
A plan or discussion response ends with an open-questions section. It
is self-contained: it defines its terms, so Jeff can answer it without
reading the body. Each question gets its own headline, with a one-line
answer added once it is answered. A question that is also discussed
in a section links there.
** Form
Prefer headlines to bullets, and let the tree be as irregular as the
content; see [[file:ORIENTATION.org][ORIENTATION]]. Use single apostrophes around code
references. Do not commit planning files during discussion rounds;
Jeff stages and commits them himself.
