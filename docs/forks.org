* Forks

A *fork* turns a /foreign/ node — one in a skgrepo you do *not* own,
hence write-protected — into an editable *clone* in a skgrepo you /do/ own.
The clone keeps a live link to the original, so the original's later
additions still reach you, but your edits never touch the original.

See also [[file:./sharing-model.org][the sharing model]] for the underlying
relations (=forks=,
=hidesFromSubs=) and =glossary.org= ("clone, and the fork operation",
"subscribee as such", "override substitution").

** The implicit gesture: editing a foreign node

You do not run a special command. You just *edit the foreign node*:

1. Make the foreign node /editable/ with the editable-view
   request, =C-c s e=. (A foreign node is write-protected until you do this.)
2. Edit it however you like — its title, its body, add / remove / reorder
   its children, at any depth.
3. Save.

Any real change to a foreign node is read as a request to fork it. (An
unchanged editable foreign node forks nothing; saving it is a no-op.)

The save does not skgsave-commit blindly. The server first returns a
*fork-confirmation buffer* and asks you to approve. The buffer opens
with a foldable instructions headline, then two levels per fork: each
top headline is the *clone-to-be* (your edited title), and under it
is the *original* it forks (its real id and foreign skgrepo,
write-protected). One headline cannot honestly stand
for both, since you may have re-titled the clone and the two live in
different skgrepos.

Each clone needs a skgrepo you own. If your edit already *specified*
one — the fork was forced by new children whose metadata explicitly
names one owned skgrepo (see "Adding new content", below) — the
clone-to-be shows that skgrepo, settled, and you are not asked again.
Otherwise its skgrepo starts as the placeholder =PICK-A-REPO=, with a
suggested skgrepo noted just above it (the inferred skgrepo for a
foreign fork, or your config-first owned skgrepo otherwise), and the
client *prompts you to choose* for each such clone — the suggestion
is the prompt's default; =S-left= / =S-right= cycle the skgrepos you own.
You can also set a skgrepo by hand: point on the clone-to-be headline,
then =C-c s s=. Approving while any placeholder remains is refused,
loudly; a deliberate choice keeps a fork from silently landing in a
default skgrepo.

- *Approve* (=C-c C-c= in that buffer, or answering the prompt)
  skgsave-commits the forks into the skgrepos you chose and completes the save.
- *Decline* (=C-c C-k=, or just leave the buffer) aborts the /whole/
  save — nothing is written. The confirmation buffer stays open, so you
  can still search it for the IDs involved.

** Adding new content under a foreign node

Appending a *new* headline under a foreign node is also just an
edit: it changes the foreign node's child list, so it forks it. The
new node itself needs an owned skgrepo too:

- A *bare* new headline (no metadata) rides the fork: it lands in
  the same skgrepo as the clone — whatever the confirmation flow
  settles on — just as a new node under an owned parent lands in that
  parent's skgrepo.
- A new headline whose metadata *names an owned skgrepo* (e.g.
  =(skg node (skgRepos (nodeRepo my-notes)))=) both places the new node there and
  /specifies/ the clone's skgrepo (when all such new children agree),
  so the confirmation flow asks nothing beyond the approve.
- A new headline that explicitly names a *foreign* skgrepo is an
  error, as ever: foreign skgrepos cannot be written.

** What the clone is

When you fork a foreign node =N=, the clone =C= is a new node in a skgrepo
you own. =C=:

- copies =N='s title, body, and *child list* — the child IDs only, a
  /shallow/ copy, not =N='s whole subtree (this is what makes forking
  cheap). Each child keeps its privacy: one =N= records in a more
  private skgrepo than =C='s is recorded in =C='s section of that
  skgrepo. Under a skgrepo restriction =C= copies only the children
  you can see; the rest stay =N='s, and surface under =C= as
  unintegrated content after a skgrepo widening;
- *forks* =N=, recorded once, which does two things. =N='s later
  additions surface to you as unintegrated content (below). And =C=
  and =N= are mirrors, so /mirror substitution/ shows =C= wherever =N=
  would otherwise appear as content, from your perspective, whose
  list of skgrepos ranks yours first — you see and edit =C= in =N='s
  place from then on, without =N='s containers being rewritten to point
  at =C=. (Not in a view rooted at a foreign node: that shows what the
  root's author would see, so =N= stays there, unedited, until you
  switch the view to your perspective, =C-c v o=, as the save's
  warning says; see
  [[file:./override.org][override.org]], "Mirror substitution");
- *hides* any child of =N= that your forking edit /deleted/, so what
  you dismissed does not come straight back as unintegrated forked
  content (below). Later deletions from =C= hide likewise — see
  "Editing the clone", below.

=C='s skgrepo is the one the confirmation flow settles on: specified by
your edit, or picked at the prompt (see above). When nothing specified
it, the /suggestion/ is the skgrepo *inferred* from =N='s nearest
owned ancestor in the view, or, with no owned ancestor, your
config-first owned skgrepo. So a foreign node with no owned ancestor
still forks — it just suggests a default you confirm or change. The
fork can only fail for lack of a skgrepo if you own *no* skgrepo at
all. (The inference takes =N='s /immediate/ owned container, never a
distant owned node reached by skipping a foreign ancestor.)

=N= itself is left completely untouched on disk.

** The explicit gesture: forking a node you own

Editing a foreign node forks it implicitly. To fork a node you *already
own* — for example a public clone =C= you made above, which you now want
to layer a /private/ version =D= on top of — there is an explicit
command, *=skg-fork-node=* (=C-c m f=, alias =skg-fork=).

Put point on the node's headline and run it. It refuses if the buffer has
unsaved changes ("Save the buffer before forking.") — so the clone is
built from the node's saved state, exactly what you see. Otherwise it
marks the node and auto-saves, and you get the *same fork-confirmation
buffer* as the implicit fork: pick the clone's skgrepo at the prompt —
e.g. your /private/ skgrepo — (or set it with =C-c s s=), then approve
with =C-c C-c=, or decline with =C-c C-k= (which here also strips the
fork request so the next save does not re-fork).

Two differences from the implicit fork:

- The original is *yours*, so it is /not/ dropped — your edits to it
  still save normally; the gesture only /adds/ the clone that forks
  it.
- The *suggested* skgrepo is your *config-first* owned skgrepo (the
  first =[[repos]]= you own in =skgconfig.toml=), since there is no
  foreign ancestor to infer from.

The result is a *fork chain*: =D forks C forks N=. Chains
of any length are legal, and so are cycles (see
[[file:./sharing-model.org][the sharing model]]). When all skgrepos are unrestricted,
viewing =N='s container shows the chain's end =D=, where =D= is in
=C='s skgrepo or a more private one (your perspective ranks your
skgrepos most private first; see [[file:./override.org][override.org]],
"Which mirror stands"); restrict the
skgrepo-set to hide =D='s skgrepo and the same view shows the middle =C=
instead — either way =N= stays in the container's contents. If you point
=skg-fork-node= at a node that is /itself/ a shown substitute (one
carrying an =overridesHere= part), it forks the node on screen — the
fork shown — deepening the chain, never re-forking the node it stands for.

If you already fork the node, =skg-fork-node= is rejected by the same
guard (below); deepen the chain by forking your /fork/, not the
already-forked node.

** Unintegrated content: the subscribeeFolder

Because =C= forks =N=, a generated *subscribeeFolder* appears
under =C= listing =N= as a /subscribee-as-such/. Its content is exactly
what you have *not* yet integrated: a subscribee-as-such shows only the
children the subscriber (=C=) neither hides nor already contains. A fresh
clone has ~C.contains == N.contains~, so this starts *empty* — every
child you copied is excluded as already-contained. As the original gains
new children you have not pulled in, they appear here.

*** An example

Suppose this document, =mysticism=, is yours, and =eggs= belongs to your
friend Egg Man (a skgrepo you do not own):

#+begin_example
,* eggs                              (Egg Man's, write-protected to you)
,** health
,** recipes
,** mystical
,*** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
#+end_example

You make =mystical= editable, edit it (say you rename it to
=eggy mysticism=), and save. The confirmation buffer lists =mystical=;
you approve. Now your graph has a clone of =mystical= that you can file
under =mysticism=. Wherever =mystical= would be shown, you see your clone
instead, marked as standing in for the original:

#+begin_example
,* mysticism                         (yours)
,** common objects with mystical associations
,*** eggy mysticism      «Kĥ» (shown in place of Egg Man's "mystical")
,**** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
,*** subscribeeFolder
,**** mystical           (the subscribee-as-such; empty so far)
#+end_example

When Egg Man later extends =mystical=:

#+begin_example
,* eggs
,** mystical
,*** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
,*** Egg limpia (curanderismo)       -- NEW
,*** The philosophical egg           -- NEW
#+end_example

his two new children — which you have not integrated — show up under the
subscribee-as-such:

#+begin_example
,*** eggy mysticism      «Kĥ»
,**** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
,*** subscribeeFolder
,**** mystical
,***** Egg limpia (curanderismo)     (unintegrated)
,***** The philosophical egg         (unintegrated)
#+end_example

Since [[file:./tree-folding-is-powerful.org][branches can be folded]], even
hundreds of new originals' children need not clutter your view. To
integrate one, move it into your clone's own content; it then drops out
of the subscribee-as-such (it is now contained). You can also hide
individual children.

*** Writing in an unintegrated child integrates it

Writing anything within an unintegrated child, where the
subscribee-as-such draws it, integrates that child for you: typing
a reply under it (which forks it, if it is foreign), editing it, or
editing anything below it. The save appends the child to the
subscriber's content, by the ID the original's list names, and moves
its headline, with what you typed, to the end of the subscriber's
content; the count of unintegrated children falls by one. Your fork
of it, if the edit made one, is drawn in its place there, since a
view of your own note follows your forks. Read as mail, with the
subscribee folder of your fork of someone's inbox note as the list of
messages you have not touched, a reply files the message it answers.

This happens only where the save writes the subscriber's content: an
editable subscriber in a skgrepo you own. It files only a child the
original's list names (not a node you placed under the
subscribee-as-such yourself), and nothing the subscriber already
contains, hides, or holds a fork of. Where the subscriber is
someone else's (you are reading another's fork of a note), nothing is
filed, and if your edit forked something there, the view, drawn from
that owner's perspective, goes on drawing the mirror that owner's
skgrepo list ranks first, usually the original; the save warns that
it does, and that switching the view to your own perspective
(=C-c v o=) shows the fork.

** Editing the clone: deletions become hides

Suppose the clone =C= and the original =N= both contain a child =X=,
and you delete =X= from =C= (or move it elsewhere). Without more, =X=
would immediately resurface under =C= as unintegrated forked
content — you removed it, =N= still contains it. So the save records a
hide: =C= now =hidesFromSubs= =X=, and the subscribee folder
stays quiet about it. Putting =X= back among =C='s contents removes
the hide again. (This is the same rule the forking edit itself
follows: children of =N= you deleted /while/ forking are born hidden.
And it holds for any owned note that forks something, not just
clones.)

A removed child stands for the member of =N='s contents that is its
mirror (=docs/override.org=, "Mirrors"). If =N= contains =X= and
someone forked =X= into =X'= (=X'= forks =X=), =N='s contents
still name =X=, as every container's do; =X'= is shown in its place
only where the perspective ranks it first. So removing =X'= from
=C= hides =X=, just as removing =X= would, unless =C= still contains
=X=. That is how you dismiss the author's reply under a note of yours:
the reply is the author's fork of your note, so place it in =C=,
save, remove it and save again. Containing =X'= does not integrate
=X=, though: while =C= holds =X'=, =X= still shows as unintegrated, and
placing =X'= drops no hide of =X=.

Each save says what it hid and stopped hiding, since no edit shows a
hide: "Hides 'X' from 'C', as content of 'N'." And a child that left
a subscriber without hiding anything earns "Hides nothing:", with the
rule that held, most often that the child is no content of =N= and
a mirror of none of it. Emacs shows the lines in the echo area after the
save, Neovim notifies them, and skg-for-ai prints them in the report
before the save as well as after it ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Save
buffer").

** Taking back a fork

Where your clone =C= of =N= is drawn in =N='s place, the list it stands
in still names =N=, not =C=. Under someone else's note, in a view
switched to your perspective (=C-c v o=), that is how your reply to
one of their messages looks: your fork of the message, with your reply
under it, in the message's place. Deleting =C= there (=C-c <backspace>=
in Emacs, =<localleader>dd= in Neovim, =delete= in its handle for
skg-for-ai) deletes =C= and leaves their note as they wrote it: its
list keeps =N=, the save asks to fork nothing, and the redrawn view
shows in that place the mirror of =N= your perspective now ranks
first: =N= again, unless another fork of it ranks before =N=. (Not in a subscribee folder, the list
of what a subscriber of yours has not integrated: there the deletion
hides =N= from your subscriber, as below.)

Under a note of your own, deleting =C= where it is drawn in =N='s
place takes =N= out of your note as well, and, where your note
forks =N='s container, hides =N= from it: there the list is
yours to change, and the deletion is read as the removal of =N= too.
To keep =N= in your note, delete =C= from a view where it is not drawn
in =N='s place, such as a view of =C= itself.

Deleting =C= takes =C= out of every list of yours that names it, as
deleting any node does, and deletes its own records, what it hides
among them. It deletes nothing it contains. A child of =C= that no
other note contains, such as your reply, is left in no list (an
orphan graphnode); to delete it as well, mark it for deletion in the
same save. The recursive delete (=C-u C-c <backspace>=) will not do
it where =N= has children: =C= contains them too, they are foreign,
and the save refuses to delete them.

Removing =C='s headline, without deleting it, is another gesture: it
takes =N= out of the list =C= stood in, as removing any child does.
Under someone else's note that is an edit of their note, so it forks
it, and the save asks first.

** Once forked: the fork operation's guard

If you try to fork something you already fork, the save is rejected
with a message naming your fork of it — edit that fork instead. This
guards the gesture, not the data: an edit that silently made a second
clone would more often be an accident. Any number of notes, yours
among them, may fork one note; to fork it a second time deliberately,
make another note of yours fork it.

A note may not fork itself, nor contain a note it forks or a note that
forks it: a fork is the same note, and containing it is like containing
oneself. The save refuses each.

** Conclusion

You and Egg Man focus on exactly what each of you cares about. Your
"changes" to Egg Man's data never affect Egg Man's experience. If you
make public that you integrated his note, and he has chosen to see such
information, an indicator in his view of =eggs= shows that his =mystical=
branch lives on in a document you own, which he can follow to discover
your =mysticism=.
