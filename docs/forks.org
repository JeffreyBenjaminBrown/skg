* Forks

The *fork operation* turns a /foreign/ node — one in a skgrepo you do *not* own,
hence write-protected — into an editable *clone* in a skgrepo you /do/ own,
which forks it (so the clone is a fork of the original).
The clone keeps a live link to the original, so the original's later
additions still reach you, but your edits never touch the original.

See also [[file:./sharing-model.org][the sharing model]] for the underlying
relations (=forks=,
=hides=) and =glossary.org= ("clone, and the fork operation",
"forkee as such", "mirror substitution").

** The implicit gesture: editing a foreign node

You do not run a special command. You just *edit the foreign node*:

1. Make the foreign node /editable/ with the editable-view
   request, =C-c s e=. (A foreign node is write-protected until you do this.)
2. Edit it however you like — its title, its body, add / remove / reorder
   its children, at any depth.
3. Save.

Any real change to a foreign node is read as a request to fork it. (An
unchanged editable foreign node forks nothing; saving it is a no-op.)

The save does not skgsave-commit blindly. The server first returns a
*fork-confirmation buffer* and asks you to approve. The buffer opens
with a foldable instructions headline, then two levels per fork
operation: each
top headline is the *clone-to-be* (your edited title), and under it
is the *original* it forks (its real id and foreign skgrepo,
write-protected). One headline cannot honestly stand
for both, since you may have re-titled the clone and the two live in
different skgrepos.

Each clone needs a skgrepo you own. If your edit already *specified*
one — the fork was forced by new children whose metadata explicitly
names one owned skgrepo (see "Adding new content", below) — the
clone-to-be shows that skgrepo, settled, and you are not asked again.
Otherwise its skgrepo starts as the placeholder =PICK-A-REPO=, with a
suggested skgrepo noted just above it (the inferred skgrepo for a
foreign fork, or your config-first owned skgrepo otherwise), and the
client *prompts you to choose* for each such clone — the suggestion
is the prompt's default; =S-left= / =S-right= cycle the skgrepos you own.
You can also set a skgrepo by hand: point on the clone-to-be headline,
then =C-c s s=. Approving while any placeholder remains is refused,
loudly; a deliberate choice keeps a fork from silently landing in a
default skgrepo.

- *Approve* (=C-c C-c= in that buffer, or answering the prompt)
  skgsave-commits the forks into the skgrepos you chose and completes the save.
- *Decline* (=C-c C-k=, or just leave the buffer) aborts the /whole/
  save — nothing is written. The confirmation buffer stays open, so you
  can still search it for the IDs involved.

** Adding new content under a foreign node

Appending a *new* headline under a foreign node is also just an
edit: it changes the foreign node's child list, so it forks it. The
new node itself needs an owned skgrepo too:

- A *bare* new headline (no metadata) rides the fork: it lands in
  the same skgrepo as the clone — whatever the confirmation flow
  settles on — just as a new node under an owned parent lands in that
  parent's skgrepo.
- A new headline whose metadata *names an owned skgrepo* (e.g.
  =(skg node (skgRepos (nodeRepo my-notes)))=) both places the new node there and
  /specifies/ the clone's skgrepo (when all such new children agree),
  so the confirmation flow asks nothing beyond the approve.
- A new headline that explicitly names a *foreign* skgrepo is an
  error, as ever: foreign skgrepos cannot be written.

** What the clone is

When you fork a foreign node =N=, the clone =C= is a new node in a skgrepo
you own. =C=:

- copies =N='s title, body, and *child list* — the child IDs only, a
  /shallow/ copy, not =N='s whole subtree (this is what makes forking
  cheap). Each child keeps its privacy: one =N= records in a more
  private skgrepo than =C='s is recorded in =C='s section of that
  skgrepo. Under a skgrepo restriction =C= copies only the children
  you can see; the rest stay =N='s, and surface under =C= as
  unintegrated content after a skgrepo widening;
- *forks* =N=, recorded once, which does two things. =N='s later
  additions surface to you as unintegrated content (below). And =C=
  and =N= are mirrors, so /mirror substitution/ shows =C= wherever =N=
  would otherwise appear as content, from the perspective of =C='s
  skgrepo, whose list of skgrepos ranks it first — in a view of a note
  of that skgrepo you see and edit =C= in =N='s place from then on,
  without =N='s containers being rewritten to point at =C=. (Not in a
  view of a note in =N='s skgrepo: that is drawn from =N='s skgrepo,
  where =N= itself stands, so =N= stays there, unedited, until you
  switch the view to =C='s skgrepo's perspective, =C-c v o=, as the
  save's warning says; see
  [[file:./mirrors.org][mirrors.org]], "Mirror substitution");
- *hides* any child of =N= that your forking edit /deleted/, so what
  you dismissed does not come straight back as unintegrated forked
  content (below). Later deletions from =C= hide likewise, unless
  another note of yours keeps the child — see "Editing the clone",
  below.

=C='s skgrepo is the one the confirmation flow settles on: specified by
your edit, or picked at the prompt (see above). When nothing specified
it, the /suggestion/ is the skgrepo *inferred* from =N='s nearest
owned ancestor in the view, or, with no owned ancestor, your
config-first owned skgrepo. So a foreign node with no owned ancestor
still forks — it just suggests a default you confirm or change. The
fork can only fail for lack of a skgrepo if you own *no* skgrepo at
all. (The inference takes =N='s /immediate/ owned container, never a
distant owned node reached by skipping a foreign ancestor.)

=N= itself is left completely untouched on disk.

** The explicit gesture: forking a node you own

Editing a foreign node forks it implicitly. To fork a node you *already
own* — for example a public clone =C= you made above, which you now want
to layer a /private/ version =D= on top of — there is an explicit
command, *=skg-fork-node=* (=C-c m f=, alias =skg-fork=).

Put point on the node's headline and run it. It refuses if the buffer has
unsaved changes ("Save the buffer before forking.") — so the clone is
built from the node's saved state, exactly what you see. Otherwise it
marks the node and auto-saves, and you get the *same fork-confirmation
buffer* as the implicit fork: pick the clone's skgrepo at the prompt —
e.g. your /private/ skgrepo — (or set it with =C-c s s=), then approve
with =C-c C-c=, or decline with =C-c C-k= (which here also strips the
fork request so the next save does not re-fork).

Two differences from the implicit fork:

- The original is *yours*, so it is /not/ dropped — your edits to it
  still save normally; the gesture only /adds/ the clone that forks
  it.
- The *suggested* skgrepo is your *config-first* owned skgrepo (the
  first =[[repos]]= you own in =skgconfig.toml=), since there is no
  foreign ancestor to infer from.

The result is a *fork chain*: =D forks C forks N=. Chains
of any length are legal, and so are cycles (see
[[file:./sharing-model.org][the sharing model]]). When all skgrepos are unrestricted,
viewing =N='s container from the perspective of =D='s skgrepo shows
the chain's end =D=, where =N= is not in that skgrepo (in its own
skgrepo =N= itself stands; see [[file:./mirrors.org][mirrors.org]],
"Which mirror stands"); restrict the skgrepo-set to hide =D='s
skgrepo and the same view shows =C=, where =C='s skgrepo is declared
before =N='s — either way =N= stays in the container's contents. If you point
=skg-fork-node= at a node that is /itself/ a shown substitute (one
carrying an =inPlaceOf= part), it forks the node on screen — the
fork shown — deepening the chain, never re-forking the node it stands for.

If you already fork the node, =skg-fork-node= is rejected by the same
guard (below); deepen the chain by forking your /fork/, not the
already-forked node.

** Unintegrated content: the forks folder

Because =C= forks =N=, a generated *forksFolder* ("It forks these, or
these fork it.") appears under =C= listing =N=, claiming, as a
/forkee as such/. The same folder lists, after
the forkees, the notes that fork =C=, and then =C='s other mirrors,
each claiming nothing (deleting one hides it, attaching it makes =C=
fork it: [[file:./sharing-model.org][sharing-model.org]], "The
forks folder"; see "Every mirror, flat", below). A forkee's content is exactly
what you have *not* yet integrated: a forkee as such shows only the
children its forker (=C=) neither hides nor already contains, and that
no other note of yours contains (see "What you keep elsewhere",
below). A fresh clone has ~C.contains == N.contains~, so it starts
showing no content: every child you copied stands, folded, in its
folder of what you keep elsewhere. As the original gains new children
you have not pulled in, they appear among its content.

*** An example

Suppose this document, =mysticism=, is yours, and =eggs= belongs to your
friend Egg Man (a skgrepo you do not own):

#+begin_example
,* eggs                              (Egg Man's, write-protected to you)
,** health
,** recipes
,** mystical
,*** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
#+end_example

You make =mystical= editable, edit it (say you rename it to
=eggy mysticism=), and save. The confirmation buffer lists =mystical=;
you approve. Now your graph has a clone of =mystical= that you can file
under =mysticism=. Wherever =mystical= would be shown, you see your clone
instead, marked as standing in for the original:

#+begin_example
,* mysticism                         (yours)
,** common objects with mystical associations
,*** eggy mysticism      «Kĥ» (shown in place of Egg Man's "mystical")
,**** forksFolder
,***** mystical          (the forkee as such; no content so far)
,****** ☮ It contains these, and you keep them elsewhere.
,******* Which came first, the chicken or the egg?   (folded)
,**** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
#+end_example

When Egg Man later extends =mystical=:

#+begin_example
,* eggs
,** mystical
,*** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
,*** Egg limpia (curanderismo)       -- NEW
,*** The philosophical egg           -- NEW
#+end_example

his two new children — which you have not integrated — show up under the
forkee as such:

#+begin_example
,*** eggy mysticism      «Kĥ»
,**** forksFolder
,***** mystical
,****** ☮ It contains these, and you keep them elsewhere.
,******* Which came first, the chicken or the egg?   (folded)
,****** Egg limpia (curanderismo)     (unintegrated)
,****** The philosophical egg         (unintegrated)
,**** Which came first, the chicken or the egg?
#+end_example

Since [[file:./tree-folding-is-powerful.org][branches can be folded]], even
hundreds of new originals' children need not clutter your view. To
integrate one, move it into your clone's own content, or into any
other note of yours; it then leaves the forkee's content for its
folder of what you keep elsewhere. You can also hide individual
children.

*** Every mirror, flat

A note's forks folder lists every mirror of it, each once: what it
forks, what forks it, and the rest of the family, each note joined to
it only through others (a fork of what it forks, say), an /indirect
member/ ('docs/glossary.org', "the forks folder"). Since forking is
transitive in this sense, no member of the folder draws a forks
folder of its own, which would only restate the family: if =a= forks
=b=, =b= stands in =a='s folder and =a= in =b='s, and neither again
under the other. Asked for one (=C-c l f= on the member), a member
draws it.

The order: first what the note forks, in its list's order, which you
edit there; then what forks it, by the declaration order of their
home skgrepos in your config, then pid; then the indirect members,
nearest first, then by the declaration order of their homes, then
pid. Only the first part's order is yours: the refresh puts a member
of the other two that you moved back where the graph and the config
put it.

An indirect member is treated as a forker-only member is: it claims
nothing; it shows what your note neither contains nor hides of its
content, and no other note of yours contains (see "What you keep
elsewhere"); deleting it from the folder hides it in your note, and it
stands then in the folder of what your note hides outside its fork
partners, where removing it unhides it; cutting a child from it makes
your note fork it and hide the child, unless your note, or another
note of yours, keeps the child after the save (see "Cutting from a
forker");
and its heralds have no =b= flag toward your note, since it neither
forks your note nor is forked by it. Every member explains a hide
alike: a hide of something only an indirect member contains stands
under that member, in its folder of what your note hides in it. Its
own hides of your note's content are not drawn under it: they apply
to what it forks, not to your note.

*** What you keep elsewhere

A child of a member as such is integrated when you keep it somewhere:
when your note (whose forks folder shows the member) contains it, or
when any note of your graph does, your graph being the notes of your
owned skgrepos, as the skgrepo restriction shows them, the member
itself excepted ('docs/glossary.org', "integrated"). A member shows
only what is not integrated and your note does not hide. So moving
someone's child into a note of yours, or under one, needs no hide:
skg sees that you keep it.

What the member contains that you keep elsewhere stands in its folder
=integratedElsewhereFolder= ("☮ It contains these, and you keep them
elsewhere."), first among the member's folders, before the folder of
what your note hides in it, drawn when it holds something, under an
editable member. It is write-protected, its members are folded, in
the member's list's order, and each carries a containers folder,
folded, naming where you keep it (its containers, but those the view
already shows above it). A child your note hides stands in the hidden
folder only, even when you keep it too: the hide is the explicit
record, and that folder is where moving it out removes it. A hide
that keeping makes redundant stays, harmless; moved out of the hidden
folder, the child is unhidden, and stands then in the folder of what
you keep elsewhere. No save removes such a hide by itself.

Before, with your =x= forking someone's =x=, which contains =a=, =b=
and =c=, and your =y= containing =a=:

#+begin_example
,* x                    (yours, editable)
,** forksFolder
,*** x                  (theirs, bK)
,**** ☮ It contains these, and you keep them elsewhere.
,***** a                (folded)
,****** ☮ These also contain it.
,******* y              (yours)
,**** b                 -- cut this, and paste it under your z
,**** c                 -- cut this alone
#+end_example

After the save, your =z= contains =b=, your =x= hides =c= alone, and
=b= joins =a= in the folder of what you keep elsewhere. Remove =a=
from your =y=, and =a= shows under their =x= again, with no hide.
Under a skgrepo restriction that keeps out where you keep a child
(your =y= in a private skgrepo, the view showing only the public
one), the child is not integrated there, and shows among the member's
content.

The save hides a child cut from a member, or removed from your note
while a member contains it, only where no note of yours but that
member contains it after the save: a note the save writes, by its
contents as the save leaves them, any other as the graph has them.
Removing a child from your note that another note of yours keeps
hides nothing, and the save says so ("Hides nothing: 'd' left 'x',
but 'z' keeps it."). A cut from a member claiming nothing still makes
your note fork it (see "Cutting from a forker"), whether or not it
hides anything ('tests/hides_by_integration.rs').

A child the saved buffer shows in a member's folder of what you keep
elsewhere, or of what your note hides in it, was not cut from the
member. That matters for a stale view: after another view stopped
keeping the child (or unhid it), the graph says the member shows it,
and the view still shows it set aside; the save reads it as the view
shows it, hides nothing, and writes nothing of your note for it
('tests/members_as_such_and_stale_buffers.rs').

The diff view keeps its forks folders as they were: a member there
shows what you keep elsewhere among its content, and counts it among
what it shows ('(unintegrated N)'), and no such folder is drawn. A buffer drawn before this rule shows such children under a
member, claiming; the next refresh leaves them out, and a save of it
before then hides nothing it did not draw.

*** Writing in an unintegrated child integrates it

Writing anything within an unintegrated child, where the
forkee as such draws it, integrates that child for you: typing
a reply under it (which forks it, if it is foreign), editing it, or
editing anything below it. The save appends the child to the
forker's content, by the ID the original's list names, and moves
its headline, with what you typed, to the end of the forker's
content; the count of unintegrated children falls by one. Your fork
of it, if the edit made one, is drawn in its place there, since a
view of your own note is drawn from its skgrepo, where your fork is
homed. Read as mail, with the
forks folder of your fork of someone's inbox note as the list of
messages you have not touched, a reply files the message it answers.

This happens only where the save writes the forker's content: an
editable forker in a skgrepo you own. It files only a child the
original's list names (not a node you placed under the
forkee as such yourself), and nothing the forker already
contains, hides, or holds a fork of. Where the forker is
someone else's (you are reading another's fork of a note), nothing is
filed, and if your edit forked something there, the view, drawn from
that note's skgrepo, goes on drawing the mirror that skgrepo's list
ranks first, usually the original; the save warns that it does, and
that switching the view to your fork's skgrepo's perspective
(=C-c v o=) shows the fork.

*** Adding a child under a member as such

A member as such of the forks folder, forkee or forker, shows its
content in part, so the save never reads what stands under it as its
whole list. But a child you place under it that its list does not
name -- a headline you type there, or a node you move there (cut from
elsewhere and pasted) -- joins that member's content, right after the
sibling it follows, when you own the member; the place it was cut
from loses it, as any move does
('add_children_placed_under_member_as_such' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/resolve_visibility.rs').
Under a member someone else owns the save is refused: only its owner
can change its content, and to add a child you must first make the
node your own, from a view of it as itself.

A stale view does not put back a child another view removed from the
member: the view's baseline shows that child under the member too, so
the member, which the graph changed under the view, is left as the
graph has it, with a warning; and if the view edits the member, the
save is refused ('server/serve/handlers/save_staleness.rs').

*** Cutting from a forker

A note that forks yours stands in your note's forks folder too, as a
/forker as such/, claiming nothing, showing what it adds to your
note: its content less what yours contains or hides, and less what
another note of yours contains; so does an indirect member (see "Every mirror, flat"). Cutting a child
from it (deleting the child's headline there, whether or not you
paste it elsewhere) makes your note fork it: a forker as such that a
cut is made from becomes a forkee too. Your note hides
the child unless it, or another note of yours, contains it after the
save, as a cut from a forkee does (see "What you keep elsewhere").
Before:

#+begin_example
,* x                    (yours, editable)
,** forksFolder
,*** x                  (theirs, Kb: it forks your x)
,**** y                 -- cut this, and paste it under your x
,**** z
#+end_example

After the save, your x forks theirs, contains =y=, and hides
nothing; theirs is a mutual, claiming, among the forkees:

#+begin_example
,* x                    (yours, editable)
,** forksFolder
,*** x                  (theirs, bKb)
,**** ☮ It contains these, and you keep them elsewhere.
,***** y                (folded)
,**** z
,** y
#+end_example

Cut without the paste, =y= is hidden in your x, and stands under
theirs, in its folder of what your x hides in it. The fork is
recorded in your note's file, so where your note is foreign (you are
reading another's note) the save is refused, as a cut from a forkee
there is. The view the save redraws gives the forker's headline its
claim, as every view the server draws does (see "Claims in a redrawn
view").

*** Forks of one note meet

Two notes R and T, both yours, fork one note N. N contains X1 to X4
and hides Z; R contains X1, X2, X3; T contains X1, X3, Y, Z and hides
X2. Each view shows the others, through its flat forks folder:

#+begin_example
,* R                    (editable)
,** forksFolder
,*** N                  (2bK: R and T fork it; editable)
,**** integratedElsewhereFolder     (X1, X2, X3, folded: R keeps them)
,**** X4                -- what N adds to R
,*** T                  (no b: an indirect member, editable)
,**** integratedElsewhereFolder     (X1, X3, folded: R keeps them)
,**** Y
,**** Z
,** X1
,** X2
,** X3
#+end_example

N shows only X4, what R neither contains nor hides; T, which R does
not fork and which does not fork R, stands there too, an indirect
member, showing Y and Z, what R neither contains nor hides. What each
contains that R keeps stands in its folder of what you keep elsewhere
(see "What you keep elsewhere"). In N's own view R and T are forkers
as such:

#+begin_example
,* N                    (editable)
,** forksFolder
,*** R                  (Kb: editable, showing nothing of its own)
,**** integratedElsewhereFolder     (X1, X2, X3, folded: N keeps them)
,*** T                  (Kb: editable)
,**** integratedElsewhereFolder     (X1, X3, folded: N keeps them)
,**** hiddenInForkPartnerFolder     (Z, folded: N hides it)
,**** hiddenByForkerFolder     (X2, folded: T hides it)
,**** Y
,** X1
,** X2
,** X3
,** X4
#+end_example

Each member of a forks folder that is no repeat also carries its
containers folder, folded (here none, as nothing contains R, N or
T).

Cutting X4 from N in R's view hides X4 in R; the redrawn view shows
X4 in N's folder of what R hides in it. In N's view, cutting Y from T
and pasting it into N's own content makes N fork T and contain Y,
hiding nothing new. T is then a mutual (=bKb=), claiming, first in
N's forks folder, showing nothing (N contains or hides all of T's
content), Y joining its folder of what you keep elsewhere; R
follows; Y stands in N's content. T's folder of what it
hides of N's content (X2) goes with the change, since that folder is
drawn under a forker as such only. Each of these saved views, saved
again unchanged, writes nothing ('tests/forks_meet.rs';
'tests/integration/forks-meet/' drives the same scenario through each
client).

*** Claims in a redrawn view

In a forks folder a member claims exactly when your note forks it;
taking its claim away (a detachment) and saving ends the fork. So
every view the server draws gives each member the claim the graph
gives it: after any save, in the view saved, in each other view the
save redraws, and in any view another client saves, a member your
note now forks claims, and a forker-only member claims nothing
('claim_forks_folder_members_as_the_graph_does' in
'server/refresh/visit.rs'). A view the server redraws holds no
gesture of yours still to be saved: it is one the server drew, or
one whose gestures the save just carried out. Without this, a view
drawn before another view made your note fork a forker (by a cut
from it, say) would show that forker claiming nothing, a detachment
nobody wrote, which the next save of that view would carry out.

What the server cannot tell from a detachment is text you send back
yourself: undoing past a save and saving the old text again, where
the forker claimed nothing, ends the fork, as any detachment does.

** Editing the clone: deletions become hides

Suppose the clone =C= and the original =N= both contain a child =X=,
and you delete =X= from =C= (or move it out of your notes). Without more, =X=
would immediately resurface under =C= as unintegrated forked
content — you removed it, =N= still contains it. So the save records a
hide: =C= now =hides= =X=, and the forks folder
stays quiet about it. Putting =X= back among =C='s contents removes
the hide again. (This is the same rule the forking edit itself
follows: children of =N= you deleted /while/ forking are born hidden.
And it holds for any owned note that forks something, not just
clones.) Moving =X= into another note of yours instead records no
hide: you keep it there, so =N= does not show it (see "What you keep
elsewhere"), and the save says "Hides nothing: 'X' left 'C', but 'D'
keeps it." Hides are symmetric: the same holds of a note that forks
yours. If =F= forks =C= and contains =X=, =C='s forks folder shows
=F= with what it adds to =C='s content, so removing =X= from =C=
hides =X= there too ("Hides 'X' from 'C', as content of 'F'."), and
=X= then stands under =F=, in its folder of what =C= hides in it,
not in the folder of what =C= hides outside its fork partners. A
forker =C= hides is drawn nowhere, so it explains no hide; the floor
of a hide's privacy still counts its records, since a reader of the
hide sees them all the same ('docs/glossary.org', "hides" under
"relation, relationship, member, membership, partner").

A removed child stands for the member of =N='s contents that is its
mirror (=docs/mirrors.org=, "Mirrors"). If =N= contains =X= and
someone forked =X= into =X'= (=X'= forks =X=), =N='s contents
still name =X=, as every container's do; =X'= is shown in its place
only where the perspective ranks it first. So removing =X'= from
=C= hides =X=, just as removing =X= would, unless =C=, or another
note of yours, contains =X= after the save. That is how you dismiss the author's reply under a note of yours:
the reply is the author's fork of your note, so place it in =C=,
save, remove it and save again. Containing =X'= does not integrate
=X=, though: while =C= holds =X'=, =X= still shows as unintegrated, and
placing =X'= drops no hide of =X=.

Each save says what it hid and stopped hiding, since no edit shows a
hide: "Hides 'X' from 'C', as content of 'N'." And a child that left
a forker without hiding anything earns "Hides nothing:", with the
rule that held, most often that the child is no content of =N= and
a mirror of none of it. Emacs shows the lines in the echo area after the
save, Neovim notifies them, and skg-for-ai prints them in the report
before the save as well as after it ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Save
buffer").

** Taking back a fork

Where your clone =C= of =N= is drawn in =N='s place, the list it stands
in still names =N=, not =C=. Under someone else's note, in a view
switched to your fork's skgrepo's perspective (=C-c v o=), that is how your reply to
one of their messages looks: your fork of the message, with your reply
under it, in the message's place. Deleting =C= there (=C-c <backspace>=

** Once forked: the fork operation's guard

If you try to fork something you already fork, the save is rejected
with a message naming your fork of it — edit that fork instead. This
guards the gesture, not the data: an edit that silently made a second
clone would more often be an accident. Any number of notes, yours
among them, may fork one note; to fork it a second time deliberately,
make another note of yours fork it.

A note may not fork itself, nor contain a note it forks or a note that
forks it: a fork is the same note, and containing it is like containing
oneself. The save refuses each.
