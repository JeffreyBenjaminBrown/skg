* Glossary
The words Skg uses, in two parts: the terms a user meets, and the
terms of the implementation. Links inside it name headlines.

A term marked "a term of plan 06", "of plan 08" or "of plan 10" was
coined by that plan of the refresh's rewrite (October 2026; plan 06
tidied the refresh, plan 08 fixed the skgrepo restriction and tidied
again, plan 10 ended the buffer corrections and made every write obey
the skgrepo restriction).
The other terms are older, or ordinary words.

** Terms a user meets
What the clients show, what the heralds and the messages say, and
the words of the user docs ('docs/COMMANDS.org',
'docs/COMMANDS-nvim.org', 'docs/heralds.org').

*** graphnode, viewnode

A *graphnode* is a node of the graph, which a =.skg= file records; a
*viewnode* is a node of a view, which a buffer shows as a headline.
A graphnode can appear in one view as several viewnodes, or as none,
and some viewnodes stand for no graphnode: a folder, for instance,
names a relationship between its viewparent and its children. Say
"graphnode" and "viewnode" rather than "graph node" or "view node";
bare "node" is fine where only one kind is possible. The
implementation sorts viewnodes by whether they stand for a graphnode
(see "vognode, non-vognode").

*** "child" and "parent"

They are shorthand for 'viewchild' and 'viewparent' (likewise
viewancestor, viewdescendant). Code may use the short forms, since it
almost always handles one tree at a time. Docs should spell out the
long forms wherever a reader might mistake a child for content, which
in user-facing docs is almost everywhere.

There are two concepts of node in skg: graphnode and viewnode.
Viewnodes are nodes in a view, and some of them correspond to nodes in the graph,
but some of them don't -- they describe their parent,
or the relationship between their parent and their children,
or maybe something else.

The Skg graph contains a few (currently five) formal relationships -- =contains=,
=linksTo=, etc. In the graph, there is no concept of parent or child;
those are only meaningful in a view.
Where the code says "child" or "parent", it means the relationship between viewnodes
-- e.g., in Emacs, the relationship between headlines in an Org buffer
-- and might or might not correspond to a graph relation.

For one example, see the discussion of alias nodes in
[[file:../coding-advice/architecture.md][the architecture documentation]].

*** title != headline

Headline is a term from org-mode. It refers to a line that begins with some asterisks, followed by at least one whitespace, followed by at least one non-whitespace. The text after the first chunk of adjacent whitespace is what I'm calling the 'title'.

*** view, buffer, viewforest

A *view* is one open window onto the graph: a content view, which
shows nodes and what they contain, or a search view. A *buffer* is
the editor's container for one view (an Emacs or Neovim buffer). A
view's *viewforest* is its tree of viewnodes, under a buffer root;
say "viewforest" rather than "viewtree" or "forest". The buffer as a
whole is named by its viewforest id (see "viewnode ID, vnid,
viewforest id").

A *content view proper* is a content view that is nothing else: not
a buffer of the diff view, which the server registers as a content
view ('ViewforestId::ContentView') marked as one of the diff view's
buffers ('OpenViews::is_content_view_proper' in
'server/types/views_state.rs'). A view request for a node that is a
root of an open view switches only to a content view proper.

A *view root* is a root of a viewforest: a level-one headline.

A *viewbranch* is any subtree of a viewforest: a viewnode with all its
descendants. A view request asks the server to add viewbranches: a
folder (with its members' chains), the flags folder. A
viewbranch may be degenerate,
with one viewchild or none; compare "branching viewnode".

*** viewnode ID, vnid, viewforest id

A *viewnode ID* identifies one viewnode within one buffer, for the
life of the buffer: the atom =(vnid X)=, X one or more lowercase
letters and digits, that begins every viewnode's metadata
('docs/api-and-formats.org', "The metadata headers"). "vnid" is the
wire's word; prose and messages say "viewnode ID", and code
'ViewnodeId' and 'viewnode_id'. A message names a headline by its
viewnode ID, which =C-c g v= finds (see "how a message names a
headline"). The view gives one to each viewnode that lacks one at
the end of a refresh and never reuses one, so a viewnode ID that
reappears after a cut and paste names the same viewnode moved. A
parsed headline carrying a viewnode ID the view does not know (typed
by hand, or brought back by an old copy of the buffer's text) loses
it and gets a fresh one. A switch of the skgrepo restriction keeps the viewnode ID
of everything the new skgrepo restriction still shows. The view's
'ViewnodeIdRecord' ('server/types/views_state.rs') holds the counter
and the viewnode IDs of the viewforest as last rendered.

A *viewforest id* names a whole buffer, not one viewnode:
'ViewforestId' in code, the key =view-id= on the wire, and "view ID"
in the clients' prose (see "view, buffer, viewforest").

*** how a message names a headline

A message from the server -- an error, a warning, a refused save --
names each headline it concerns as an org subheadline of its own,
below the message's own lines: the headline's title, or
'(untitled)' (a folder, say); then '- graphnode ID: X' when the
headline carries one; then '- viewnode ID: X. To find it: C-c g v X'
(in Neovim, '<localleader>gv X'), or '- not yet saved' for a
headline typed or copied since the last render, which has none.
=C-c g v= ('skg-goto-viewnode-id') puts point on the headline with
that viewnode ID and opens the folds around it. In code it is
'HeadlineName' ('server/types/errors.rs'); see
'docs/api-and-formats.org', "How a message names a headline".

*** herald

A *herald* is a short styled token a client shows at the start of a
headline in place of a part of its metadata, when heralds are on
(=C-c v h=; =<localleader>vh= in Neovim): =☮= for write-protected,
=⊥= for unwarranted, =restricted= for a restricted viewnode, and so
on. The server serves the rule table that maps metadata to heralds,
and both clients draw by it. 'docs/heralds.org' is the legend of
every herald and its style. Decorations on text that is not metadata
(the label after a link, say) are *annotations*, not heralds.

*** metadata

Most headlines are prefixed with some metadata.
In Emacs, that metadata is represented as an s-expression like
(skg (vnid X) KIND (key value) bare_value ...):
its viewnode ID, the position atoms, the headline's kind (its *kind
atom*: =node=, =ghost=, =unknown=, =tombstone=, =aliasFolder= and so
on), then any
number of key-value and bare-value parts in any order
('docs/api-and-formats.org', "The metadata headers").
The metadata is used for Rust to know what to do with it,
and for the client to let the user know how to interpret it.
It is not abbreviated "md", which means Markdown (as in
=import-md-and-org=).

*** declared parts, derived facts

Metadata holds two classes of thing ('docs/api-and-formats.org',
"The metadata headers"). The *declared parts* are what the client is
responsible for and the save or the refresh reads: the kind, the
viewnode ID, the position atoms =focused=, =folded= and =bodyFolded=
('FoldAndFocus' in code), =(gnid ID)=, =(skgRepos ...)=, =noClaim=,
=writeProtected=, =(inPlaceOf N)=, =(viewRequests ...)= and
=(editRequest ...)=. A headline whose metadata names no kind is a
node. The *derived facts* are what the server computes at every
rendering and nobody reads back: everything in =(view ...)=, the
*view facts* (the relationship facts, the warrant, =cycle=, =repoBoundary=,
=omittedBody=) and in =(diff ...)= (the change facts). The parser
skips both forms whole, so editing them changes nothing. The one
load-bearing part of a substitution, the inPlaceOf part
=(inPlaceOf N)=, is declared; see "mirror substitution".

*** view request vs edit request

Both are requests the client writes into metadata and the server
consumes. A *view request* (=(viewRequests ...)=: =folder=,
=flags=, =editableView=, =writeProtectedView=,
=writeProtectedSubtreeView=) asks for more of the view
(viewbranches) or changes which occurrence of a node is editable; the
refresh fulfills it and drops it.
(=fork= is also written as a view request, but the save consumes
it.) An *edit request* (=(editRequest ...)=: =delete=, =merge=, =flag=,
=relRepo=, and in the diff view =restoreRel= on a removed membership
and =restoreNode= on a ghost) asks the save to change the graph; a
successful save consumes it, and a failed save leaves it in the
buffer. The two restore requests are described in
'docs/diff-view.org', "Restoring what a change removed". A
*processed request* (see "processed request, processed gesture") is a
third kind, written in the same form: =(editRequest ...)= holds at
most one edit request and one processed request. The save consumes a
processed request after its graph edits succeed, to mark or unmark
changes processed, and it changes neither the view nor the graph.
In code a node's own edit request is a 'NodeEditRequest', kept in
'Editability::Editable'; a request about a headline's relationship to
its viewparent (=relRepo=, =restoreRel=), which an occurrence, an
unknown viewnode, a restricted viewnode or an alias may carry, is a
'RelationshipEditRequest'; a
processed request is the viewnode's 'ProcessedRequest'.

*** gesture

A *gesture* is a client command that writes requests into headline
metadata for the server to apply at a save. The relRepo gesture
(=skg-set-relRepo=), the flag gesture (=skg-set-flag-search-matching=),
the fork gesture (=skg-fork-node=) and the processed gestures are
kinds. The fork and processed gestures save at once; the others leave
the save to the user.

*** occurrence

One appearance of a graphnode in a view: the vognode standing for a
node the truth has ('Vognode::Occurrence', the struct 'Occurrence' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs'). A graphnode can occur several times in
one buffer, at most once editable. Facts that depend on the position
(=cycle=, the warrant, the relRepo in =(skgRepos ...)=,
=(inPlaceOf N)=, and the =(birth ...)= facts, which name the
relationship to the viewparent) belong to the occurrence; graphStats
('GraphnodeStats') belong to the graphnode.

*** editable, write-protected

Each occurrence is either *editable* or
*write-protected* ('Editability' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'). Edits to
an editable occurrence's title, body and children are saved; a
write-protected occurrence shows no body and saves nothing of its own,
so a save is refused if one differs from the graph in its title,
skgrepo, aliases (one lacked, reordered, or with a relRepo request) or
flags; a title or skgrepo as the buffer's baseline shows it, which the
graph changed since, is no edit (see "unedited"). No child of a
write-protected occurrence claims membership,
whatever it carries: a child the user typed under it, which stays
unwarranted, since no save reads claims there, is saved as an
orphan graphnode (see "claims membership"). A buffer may hold at
most one editable occurrence of a node,
so that two occurrences cannot contradict each other. Make one
editable with =skg-set-editable= (=C-c s e=), which writes a
=editableView= view request; make one write-protected with
=skg-request-write-protected= (=C-c s w=; =C-c s W= for its whole
content subtree), which writes a =writeProtectedView= or
=writeProtectedSubtreeView= view request.

*** relation, relationship, member, membership, partner

As in TypeDB, a *relation* is a kind of relationship: =contains=,
=linksTo=, =forks=, =hides=. Each names its two roles:
container contains content, mentioner linksTo mentioned, forker forks
forkee, hider hides hidden. A forks B says two things: A shows
B's content that A neither contains nor hides, nor the user keeps in
another note of theirs (see "forkee as such" and "integrated"), and A
and B are mirrors (see "mirror"). =hides= means
"hides from its forks", from every member of its forks folder: the
hider keeps the hidden node out of what it shows of every member its
forks folder shows, a note it forks, one forking it, or an indirect
member, that it does not hide
('forks_folder_members_shown' in 'server/refresh/goal.rs'; see "the forks
folder"). A member that contains the hidden node *explains* the hide:
the view draws it there, under that member, and a hide no member
explains stands in the hidden-outside folder (see "compound partner
folder"). The save's inference of hides from removals, the edits of
the hidden-outside folder, a nodeMerge and the hide report all read
the same members. The floor of a new
hide's relRepo ('hide_skgrepo' in
'server/from_text/supplement_from_disk.rs') asks another question,
of records rather than of a view: whose readers can see why the
hider hides the node. So it counts a wider set, the *partners on
record*: every fork partner the graph has, either way, whatever the
skgrepo restriction, a forker the hider hides included, that
contains the hidden node; the hide is no more public than the most
public such pair of records (the =forks= record and the =contains=
record). A forker the hider hides explains no hide, but is a partner
on record. A *relationship* is one instance of a relation: this
node contains that one. (Graph theory would call it an edge; we don't,
because the word is opaque to newcomers.)

Either end of a relationship is the other's *partner*. One of the
two records the relationship in its file (see "recorder, carrier");
the other is a *member* of the recorder's list. In a view, the
children of an occurrence or a folder are its *members* too, those
of one monomorphic list: an occurrence's content, or a folder's
partners on one side of some relation with the folder's viewparent.
A property folder's members are its *property members*: aliases, IDs
or flags ('PropertyMember' in code).

A *membership* is the relationship that causes a node to be drawn
as a member of a list or folder; the member is the node.

*** claims membership, 'noClaim', pending detachment, detach, attach, orphan graphnode

A child *claims membership* when the save will read it as a member
of its viewparent's list; it is then a *claiming child* (a term of
plan 06). That is a
declared part: the bare atom =noClaim= says no, its absence yes
('claimsMembership' in code, on an occurrence, a ghost, an unknown
viewnode, a restricted viewnode, an alias or an ID;
'docs/api-and-formats.org', "Content view"). It means something
only where the save reads the claims of its viewparent's children:
under an editable occurrence, and under an editable folder
(forks, hidden-outside, alias) of one
('parent_reads_claims' in 'server/refresh/ancestry.rs'). In the
forks folder a claim says "the folder's viewparent forks it", so a
forker-only or an indirect member, drawn there by the refresh,
claims nothing (see "the forks folder").

Every other viewparent *reads no claims*. This is the one list of
them, which the user docs cite: a write-protected occurrence; a
write-protected folder (a containers or mentioners folder, say), and
any folder of a write-protected occurrence; a ghost, an unknown
viewnode and a restricted viewnode; a tombstone; and the buffer root, whose
children are the view's roots. Under any of them no child claims,
whatever it carries, the server writes no =noClaim=, and detach and
attach refuse. A child that
claims nothing is in no list: the save neither adds nor removes a
relationship for it, and what it shows of its own node is saved or
not by its editability, as at any occurrence. So a headline typed
under a viewparent that reads no claims is saved as an
*orphan graphnode*, a node that no list contains, and the save warns of each
one, naming its headline ('orphan_warnings' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/mod.rs'); of a
headline typed at the top of the buffer, a new view root, it does
not warn. Under a
tombstone, as under a ghost or a folder, the headline takes its
skgrepo from the nearest occurrence above.

"Orphan" always takes its noun. An orphan graphnode is the one above
(a term of plan 08; plan 06, which took the word from
'docs/COMMANDS.org', used it without its noun); an *orphan viewnode*
(a term of plan 08) is a headline the parser cannot place under a
parent, being deeper than its parent allows. The weave's "orphaned
run" ('weave_orphaned_run_reattaches_leftward' in
'tests/unit/weave.rs') and the database's "orphaned records"
('server/dbs/init.rs') keep their nouns too.

Under an editable parent a child that claims and that the graph
does not relate to its viewparent (see "warranted, 'noWarrant',
'ghostWarrant'") vanishes (see "vanish"): one whose relationship a
save in another view removed, one whose record alone a
skgrepo narrowing keeps out, a child a viewnode merge moved there. A child
there that claims nothing is one the user marked =noClaim= to
detach it: a *pending detachment*, herald "detach", which the next
save takes out of the list. In the forks folder a member claiming
nothing is a pending detachment only where the folder's viewparent
still forks it on disk (its '(rels (forks (in ...)))' names ancestor
generation 2): a detached forkee or mutual, not a forker-only or an
indirect member.
Every view the server draws gives each member there the claim the
graph gives it, so only the user's own unsaved gesture makes one
('docs/forks.org', "Claims in a redrawn view").
No wire says it; each client sees it and
writes the atom =pendingDetachment= into the metadata it hands the
herald rules ('docs/heralds.org', "What the server serves, and what
the clients add"). 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The
refresh: how a view follows the graph", says how claims and warrants
move.

Under a viewparent that reads claims, to *detach* a child is to write
=noClaim= on it, so that the next save takes it out of the
viewparent's list, and to *attach* it is to remove the atom, so that
the next save puts it in (both terms of plan 06): two gestures on
the declared part, no request, though the herald =detach= of a
pending detachment is styled as one ('skg-detach' and 'skg-attach',
C-c s t and C-c s a;
':SkgDetach' and ':SkgAttach' in Neovim). Both refuse under a
viewparent that reads no claims. "Detach" says what the user does to
a claim, never what the refresh does to the tree: the refresh never
removes a child.

*** warranted, 'noWarrant', 'ghostWarrant'

A child *is warranted* when the truth relates it to its viewparent as
its position implies: content its occurrence contains, a partner of
the folder's relation in a folder, a container of the node above it
in the containerward chain, a property of the node in a property
folder. In the diff view HEAD alone may warrant it: a *ghost warrant*,
for a relationship HEAD has and the worktree lacks (see "removed
member, former member"), or for a ghost under a parent that contained
it at HEAD. The warrant is a derived fact, recomputed at every
refresh, and written in =(view ...)= as =noWarrant= (herald ⊥) or
=ghostWarrant= (herald ᗣ), nothing meaning warranted by the truth; a
root carries none, and a ghost no =ghostWarrant=, its kind being its
own herald ('Warrant { ByGraph, ByHead, No }', 'warrant' in
code). 'Warrant::ByGraph' is the truth's warrant (see "the truth"):
the graph as the skgrepo restriction lets it be seen; a root, whose
position implies no relationship, carries it too, and the wire
writes nothing for it. Claim and warrant are independent: a child
the user typed under a write-protected parent, which stays
unwarranted, since no save reads claims there, carries no =noClaim=.
A tombstone has no goal, so its children carry no warrant fact, but
for an orphan graphnode, which no list contains: it is unwarranted
there too. In the write-protected hides folder (=hiddenFolder=) a
graphnode its viewparent hides by no record vanishes, so that a
graphnode a save unhid leaves it; a hide the skgrepo restriction
keeps out stays, unwarranted ('mark_claims' in
'server/refresh/visit.rs'). In the hidden-by folder
(=hiddenByForkerFolder=) under a member that is no forker as
such, a forker a cut made a forkee, every member vanishes, so the
folder goes, as a fresh view would not draw it
('is_hiddenByForkerFolder_under_no_forker_as_such').
The refresh keeps an unwarranted child where it stands; it never
removes a child.

*** stands as a member

An occurrence *stands as a member* of its viewparent when the
relationship its position implies is one its derived facts may
explain: its birth heralds and its relRepo. Where the viewparent
reads claims (see "claims membership"), that is its claim; at the
top of the buffer it always does; in a folder that reads no claims
(a container folder, say, or any folder of a write-protected
occurrence), it does when the truth warrants it;
anywhere else (under a write-protected occurrence, say) it does not
('stands_as_member' in 'server/refresh/viewnodestats.rs').
It is a third notion beside the claim and the warrant, since it is
neither: a warranted member of a write-protected folder claims
nothing but stands as a member, and a warranted child of an editable
occurrence that the user marked =noClaim= stands as none. It is not
written; the stats pass computes it where it computes those facts.

*** true-content subtree

The *true-content subtree* of a headline is the headline and every
occurrence below it reached through occurrences that claim
membership: its content, its content's content, and so on. A
folder, a property member, a ghost, an unknown viewnode, a
restricted viewnode, a tombstone and a child carrying =noClaim= end
it, with everything under them. The recursive gestures walk it: the
recursive flag gesture ('skg-set-flag-search-matching' with =C-u=,
=C-u C-c s x=; =<localleader>sX= in Neovim) and the recursive
skgrepo gesture ('skg-set-repo' with =C-u=, =C-u C-c s s=;
=<localleader>sS=).

*** partner folder

Recall that viewnodes and graphnodes are distinct.
Some viewnodes correspond to individual graphnodes.
Other viewnodes mediate a relationship between their parent and their children.
For instance, a 'forks folder' viewnode contains its parent's
mirrors: the nodes its parent forks, those that fork it, and those
joined to it only through other mirrors:
#+begin_example
  * note
  ** forks folder
  *** forkee
  *** forker
#+end_example

In that example, the first viewnode represents a graphnode,
as do the last two. The note forks the forkee, and the forker forks
the note. The second viewnode, though, corresponds to nothing in the
graph. It exists only to name the relationship between its children
and its parent.

A *partner folder* is the general concept. In code it is a variant of
'Folder', an enum that also holds the three property folders (see
"Folder, and its per-kind rules").
Recall that there are four relations in the graph:
containment, link, forking, and hiding.
Because a node can be on either side of each relationship --
it can be the container or the content, the hider or the hidden, etc. --
there are many kinds of partner folder. Most list, as their children,
the nodes that play one role toward their parent. For each relation,
one folder could list the members its parent records, and another the
recorders that record its parent; for forking, one folder lists both:

| relation      | members its parent records | recorders of its parent |
|---------------+----------------------------+-------------------------|
| containment   | not implemented[1]         | containerFolder         |
| link          | not implemented[2]         | mentionerFolder         |
| forking       | forksFolder[3]             | forksFolder[3]          |
| hiding        | hiddenFolder               | hiderFolder             |

1. A contentFolder would be silly: a node's contents are already its
   ordinary viewchildren.
2. A mentionedFolder would be silly: the links are already visible in
   the node's own text.
3. One folder, 'Folder::Forks', holds every mirror of the note, each
   once: its *fork partners* (the nodes it forks, and the nodes that
   fork it), whichever roles each plays, and its indirect members: see
   "the forks folder".

The four compound partner folders (see "compound partner folder")
each join several relationships -- forking, containment and, for
three of them, hiding:
- an *integratedElsewhereFolder*, under a member as such of a forks
  folder, lists that member's contents which the user keeps elsewhere
  (see "integrated");
- a *hiddenInForkPartnerFolder*, under a member as such of a forks
  folder (a forkee or forker as such), lists that member's contents
  which the note whose folder holds it hides;
- a *hiddenByForkerFolder*, under a forker as such, lists what
  that forker hides of the note's content;
- a *hiddenOutsideOfForkPartnersFolder*, last in a forks folder, lists
  the nodes its parent hides that no member the folder shows
  contains, a forker it hides among them.

The diff view adds two more, for nodeMerges since HEAD:
- an *acquiredByFolder* ("it was acquired by"), under an acquiree's
  ghost, holds its acquirer;
- an *acquiredFolder* ("it acquired"), under an acquirer, holds the
  ghosts of the nodes it acquired.

Membership edits in the forks and hidden-outside folders (and the
alias folder, a property folder) are graph edits. The others are
write-protected: their membership follows from the graph (see
"write-protected set"), and they carry the ☮ herald on their folder
headline, meaning "this folder cannot be changed from here" -- the
same sense ☮ (=writeProtected=) carries on a node.

*Default presentation is intentionally narrow.* An occurrence that an
editable-view request extends -- a fresh content view's roots, and
the editable content the request draws under them -- gets its
=forksFolder= automatically where it has a fork partner, either way,
by a record the skgrepo restriction shows ('add_default_folders' and
'has_a_fork_partner' in 'server/refresh/'), and a fresh content view a
=containerFolder= on each root that has containers, which the view's
stub asks for ('stub_of_fresh_content_view' in
'server/update_buffer.rs'). The other relation folders require an
explicit folder request (the =C-c l= commands), even when their
herald counts say they would be nonempty. The same rule applies when
a member of any partner folder is extended as editable. In a content
view each member as such under an editable occurrence -- a forkee as
such or a forker as such -- is drawn as content is (see "forkee as
such" and "forker as such"), so it is extended, but it gets no forks
folder unasked: the folder it stands in lists every mirror already.
A forks request on it (=C-c l f=, '(folder forks)') draws one there.
An editable member as such gets, first, its nonempty
=integratedElsewhereFolder=, of what the user keeps elsewhere, then
its nonempty =hiddenInForkPartnerFolder=, the hidden-in compound
partner folder specific to how the note sees that member; and an
editable forker as such that forks the note, after them, its nonempty
=hiddenByForkerFolder=, of what it hides of the note's content.
The diff view draws no default folder. Under each member of a partner folder hangs its
containerward chain, under a member of the forks folder in a
containers folder of its own (see "foldering, the containerward
chain, chain node").

*** the forks folder, fork partner, forker-only member, mutual, indirect member

The *forks folder* ('Folder::Forks', atom =forksFolder=, label "It
forks these, or these fork it, or these fork or are forked by others
here.") is the one partner folder of the
relation 'forks'. Under an occurrence of a note N it holds every
mirror of N (see "mirror") but N, each once, by records the skgrepo
restriction shows: it is *flat*. Its members are N's *fork
partners* -- each note N forks, and each that forks N -- and its
indirect members. A *mutual* forks N and is forked by it; a
*forker-only member* forks N and is not forked by it. Its *indirect
members* are N's other mirrors, joined to N only through other
mirrors (a fork of N's forkee, say). A fork partner is direct: an
indirect member is no fork partner of N, and the code says "members
of the forks folder" ('forks_folder_members_shown') where it means
both. In a
content view a forkee or mutual stands there as a forkee as such, a
forker-only or an indirect member as a forker as such (see "forkee
as such" and "forker as such"). No member of a forks folder shows a
forks folder of its own unless asked (=C-c l f= on it).

Its goal ('forks_folder_goal' in 'server/refresh/goal.rs') is N's
forkees in the order of N's 'forks' list (mutuals among them where
the list puts them), then its forker-only members, by the
declaration order of their home skgrepos, then pid, then its
indirect members ('indirect_members'), nearest first, then by the
declaration order of their homes, then pid; each tail less what N
hides. The order of the tails is the graph's and the config's: the
refresh puts a member moved there back in it. A forker or an
indirect member N hides stands in the hidden-outside folder instead,
which the forks folder holds last. A mirror reached only through a
note N hides is still a member: the hide leaves out that note alone.

The folder is editable, and a member's claim there says "N forks
it": a forkee or a mutual claims, a forker-only or an indirect member
claims nothing ('GoalItem::claimable'), and the save reads the
claiming members as N's 'forks'. The heralds tell the sides apart: a
forkee shows =bK=, a forker-only member =Kb=, a mutual =bKb=, an
indirect member neither 'b' flag, since it has no fork relationship
with N. What deleting, detaching, attaching or cutting from a member
does is in 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The forks folder".

*** compound partner folder

A compound partner folder is a partner folder whose membership joins
more than one relationship, as against the ordinary partner folders,
which read one relation role. There are four. Three join three
nodes -- a note N, a member S of N's forks folder and a hidden node
H -- by S's membership there (N forks S, S forks N, or S is a mirror
of N through others), by a hide of H, and by a containment of H.
The fourth, the *integrated-elsewhere folder*
(=integratedElsewhereFolder=, label "☮ It contains these, and you
keep them elsewhere."), stands under S where S is a member as such of
N's forks folder, first among S's folders, and holds S's content that
the user keeps elsewhere (an owned note but S contains it, N among
them when the user owns N: see "integrated"), less N's hides, in the
order of S's list.
It is write-protected, its members folded, each carrying a
containers folder, folded, naming where the user keeps it (its
containers but S's view-ancestors), and no chain directly. A child N
hides stands in the hidden-in folder only.
The *hidden-in compound partner folder* (=hiddenInForkPartnerFolder=,
label "☮ It contains these, but c hides them.") stands under S inside
N's forks folder, where S is a member as such (a forkee or forker as
such), and holds N's hides that S contains, in the order of N's
hides list. The label is the same in both positions: the viewparent
S contains, and N, three generations up, hides. The *hidden-by
compound partner folder* (=hiddenByForkerFolder=, label "☮ a
hides these, which c contains.") stands under S where S is a forker
as such that forks N, after the hidden-in folder, and holds S's hides that N
contains, in the order of S's hides list: what the forker deleted
from N's content. It is write-protected: S's hides are its owner's
editing, done in S's own view. Under a forkee as such it is not
drawn, nor under an indirect member, whose hides do not apply to
N's content; one standing under a member that became a forkee holds
nothing and goes. The *hidden-outside
compound partner folder* (=hiddenOutsideOfForkPartnersFolder=, label
"b hides these, but no fork partner shown contains them.") stands last in N's forks folder and holds N's hides that no
member of N's forks folder contains, in the order of N's hides
list. The members are those its forks folder shows, forkees,
forkers and indirect members alike ('forks_folder_members_shown'), so a
hide is there exactly when no member of the same folder explains it:
a hide of something only a forker or an indirect member contains
stands in that member's hidden-in folder instead. A forker or an
indirect member N hides stands there, since the forks folder does
not draw it (nor, so, count it as a member), and removing it there
unhides it. The note whose hides a compound folder shows is its
*hider* ('CompoundFolderParties::hider'), and, for the two folders
under a member as such that show a hide, the note whose content holds
what is hidden is its *container* ('CompoundFolderParties::container'):
the member for the hidden-in folder, N for the hidden-by folder. The
integrated-elsewhere folder shows no hide and has neither: its goal
reads the member and N ('note_of_member_as_such'). The hidden-in,
hidden-by and hidden-outside folders read each relationship by the records the skgrepo
restriction shows: under a skgrepo narrowing, a hide of H that S
contains only by a restricted record stands in the hidden-outside
folder, and a hide recorded in a restricted section stands in
neither. The save reads the hidden-outside folder's edits against
the same partners ('apply_hiddenoutside_edits'), so a buffer that
keeps that folder never hides again what a gesture under a forker
as such unhid.

*** the folder's viewparent

A folder's *viewparent* is the headline the folder stands under: the
node whose relationships the folder lists. A gesture on a member of
the folder that edits the member's relationship (the relRepo
gesture, 'skg-set-relRepo', =C-c s r=; =<localleader>sr= in Neovim)
edits the relationship between the member and the folder's
viewparent, and refuses where the folder's viewparent is
write-protected. Say "the folder's viewparent", never "the folder's
anchor" ("anchor" is reserved; see "placement, more public anchor").

*** write-protected set

A write-protected set is a generated folder whose membership follows
from graph facts rather than direct edits to that folder.  A view
may preserve an order for the set locally, but adding or removing an
item in the generated folder does not add or remove the underlying
graph membership fact.  No child of such a set claims membership, so
the save reads nothing there: an item the user typed inside it,
which stays unwarranted (⊥), since no save reads claims there, is
kept where it stands.  If the
refresh finds that a real member is missing from the view, it
reinstates that member (see "reinstated").

For instance, if nodes R and T hide node N, the user might land
at a view like the following:
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** hiderFolder
  *** R
  *** T
#+end_example
The hiderFolder ("folder of hiders") is a write-protected set.
Thus the user could flip the order of R and T in the hiderFolder,
and this change would be respected in the display, but have no effect
in the graph. The user could insert X under hiderFolder,
but because it is a write-protected set, X claims nothing there: it
is shown unwarranted (⊥), and has no effect on N.
(If the user wants X to hide N, they must modify X, not N.)
The user could delete R, but it is reinstated as soon as they save,
because, again, the hiderFolder is a write-protected set.

*** "forkee as such"

In Skg some nodes fork others: the forker forks the forkee.
(See [[file:./data-model_technical.org][the technical data model]].)
A node that plays the forkee role can be viewed as an ordinary node,
or /as/ a forkee. In the latter case it appears
underneath the relevant forker, claiming, in its 'forksFolder':
#+begin_example
  * forker
  ** forksFolder
  *** forkee1
  *** forkee2
  ...
#+end_example

In the above view, forkee1 and forkee2 are both "forkees as
such": a member of a forks folder that claims (and,
in the refresh, that the graph warrants). They are not just forkees,
but they are being shown /as/ forkees. A forkee as such shows its
content less what the forker contains or hides and what the user
keeps elsewhere ('what_the_member_shows' in
'server/refresh/goal.rs'; see "integrated").

In a content view, under an editable forker, the refresh draws a
forkee as such as it draws content: write-protected, carrying an
editable-view request, which its visit consumes, so that it is
editable unless another occurrence holds its node's editability or the
node budget is spent (see "editability rule"). A foreign one is drawn
editable too. A forkee as such the buffer already shows
write-protected stays so at a plain rerender; an editable-view request
on its forker (=C-c s e=) passes through the forks folder to it.

Showing a node as a forkee changes the way edits are interpreted. Its
own list is never read there, since it shows its content in part. Its
title and body are saved when the user owns it, and refused when it
is foreign (a text claim: they must stay as on disk). Cutting one of
its children, or showing again one the forker hides, edits what the
forker hides (see "the forkee-visibility step"). A child placed under
it that its list does not name -- a headline typed there, or a node
moved there from elsewhere -- joins its content, after the sibling it
follows, when the user owns it; under a foreign one the save is
refused, saying to make the node one's own first
('add_children_placed_under_member_as_such' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/resolve_visibility.rs';
'docs/forks.org', "Adding a child under a member as such").
Its delete, nodeMerge and flag requests are ignored. A member of the
folder claiming nothing -- a forker-only or an indirect member, or a
forkee the user detached -- is no forkee as such but a forker as
such.

*** "forker as such", member as such, what a member shows

A *forker as such* is an occurrence member of the forks folder of a
note N that claims nothing there. The term is defined by the claim,
since the claim is all the save can read of a member (it reads the
buffer alone): in a view the server draws, every member claims as the
graph says ('docs/forks.org', "Claims in a redrawn view"), so a forker
as such there is a forker-only member, a note that forks N and that N
does not fork, or an indirect member, a mirror of N joined to it only
through others (see "the forks folder"); the messages call either "a
member claiming nothing". The term is named for the commoner case:
an indirect member need fork nothing of N's (where A forks B and B
forks C, C is an indirect member of A's forks folder), and is a
forker as such all the same. In a buffer the user has edited, it may also be a
forkee the user detached (a pending detachment), until the save ends
that fork. (A mutual claims, so it stands as a forkee as such.) A
*member as such* is either a forkee as such or a forker as such: an
occurrence member of a forks folder. The refresh also asks that it be
warranted ('note_of_member_as_such'); the save reads its position and
claim alone, as it cannot know a warrant.
*What a member shows* of its content, its *unintegrated content*, is
its content that is neither N's own, hidden or contained by N, nor
integrated, contained by an owned note but the member, by the records
the skgrepo restriction shows, in the member's order
('what_the_member_shows' in 'server/refresh/goal.rs';
'note_of_member_as_such'; see "integrated"); the '(unintegrated
COUNT ...)' part of its view stats counts it, an indirect member's
too. What the member contains that the user keeps elsewhere stands in
its integrated-elsewhere folder (see "compound partner folder"). For
a forker that is what it adds to N; one that adds nothing shows
nothing but its folders. In the diff view, which draws no
integrated-elsewhere folder, a member shows its content less what N
contains or hides alone, and its '(unintegrated COUNT)' counts that.

*** integrated, the user's graph, kept elsewhere

A child of a member as such S of a note N's forks folder is
*integrated* -- the user *keeps it elsewhere*; the two say one thing
-- when a note of the *user's graph* but S contains it. The user's
graph is every note homed in an owned skgrepo, as the skgrepo
restriction shows it: a note the restriction shows, by a record of
the child it shows ('owned_notes_containing' in
'server/refresh/goal.rs'). So N counts when the user owns it. What a
foreign N contains is N's own content, kept by its owner: S does not
show it (see "what a member shows"), and it is not integrated. A note
containing a mirror of the child does not contain the child. S shows
what is neither integrated nor N's own, contained or hidden by N (see
"what a member shows"); what is integrated, but for what N hides,
stands in S's integrated-elsewhere folder
(=integratedElsewhereFolder=, 'Folder::IntegratedElsewhere'; see
"compound partner folder"). So moving a child into an owned note
needs no hide: a cut hides only what no owned note but the member
keeps after the save (see "cut"). A hide that keeping makes
redundant is kept, and stands in the hidden-in folder
('docs/forks.org', "What you keep elsewhere").

A forker as such is drawn as a forkee as such is: in a content view,
under an editable N, editable unless it is a repeat or the budget is
spent, with the default folders of a member as such (no forks folder
unasked), and an editable-view request on
N passes to one standing write-protected. Under it stand the folder
of what N hides in it and, where it forks N, the folder of what it
hides of N's content (see "compound partner folder"). The save reads it as it reads a
forkee as such, never as its whole list: an absent child is one N
contains or hides, or the user keeps elsewhere, never removed from the
forker. Its title and body
are saved when the user owns it, and refused when it is foreign; a
child placed under it (typed, or moved there) joins its content when
the user owns it, and is refused otherwise; its delete, nodeMerge and flag requests are
ignored. What the buffer shows under it is the forker-visibility
signal (see "the forker-visibility step"): cutting a child from it
makes N fork it, so that it stands as a forkee as such (a mutual,
where it forks N).

*** the forker-visibility step

The *forker-visibility step* is the step of the save that, for a
forker as such F shown editable in the forks folder of a note N,
reads what the buffer shows of F's content
('resolve_forker_visibility' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/resolve_visibility.rs'):
a child typed there joins F's content (refused under a foreign F); a
child of F that N hides, shown claiming under F, is unhidden in N, as
under a forkee; and a child F showed that the buffer no longer shows
claiming under F was cut from it (see "cut"). A cut from a forker
makes N fork F, so that F, if it forks N, becomes a mutual, and N
hides each child cut that neither it nor an owned note but F contains
after the save (one pasted into N's content, or into any note of the
user's, is not hidden). The view the save redraws
gives F's headline its claim, so that it stands among the forkees,
not as a pending detachment: every view the refresh draws gives each
member of a forks folder the claim the graph gives it
('claim_forks_folder_members_as_the_graph_does' in
'server/refresh/visit.rs'; 'docs/forks.org', "Claims in a redrawn
view"). Where the same save
removes the claim of a mutual (a detachment), a cut from it only
hides: the detachment stands. A foreign N can record neither a fork
nor a hide, so a save that cuts from F or unhides under it is refused,
naming N ('refusal_to_change_what_a_foreign_note_hides'), in the words the
forkee-visibility step uses. The fork rules still bind: a save that
would make N fork F while N contains F is refused. The step reads F's
content and N's hides and content by the records the skgrepo
restriction shows, as the view drew them: F shows its content less
what N contains or hides and what the user keeps elsewhere, by the
graph before the save, wherever it is a mirror of N under the
restriction ('is_mirror_of'), as an indirect member is. A forker the
graph lacks (typed there) has no list to lose: what it shows is its
contains.

*** cut

To *cut* a child from a member as such (a forkee or forker as such) is
to delete its headline from under the member and save, whether or not
it is pasted elsewhere. A cut child is one the member's list names,
and the view showed there, that the buffer no longer shows claiming
under it, nor in the member's folder of what the user keeps elsewhere
or of what the note hides in it: a stale buffer may still show a child
there after another view stopped keeping it, or unhid it, and the
save reads that child as *set aside*, not cut. The member's own list
never changes: a cut edits the note
whose forks folder holds the member. A cut from a forkee hides the
child in the note unless the note, or a note of the user's graph but
the member, contains it after the save (see "integrated" and "the
forkee-visibility step"): a note the save writes by its contains as
the save leaves them, any other by the graph. A cut from a forker
does that too, and makes the note fork the forker (see "the
forker-visibility step"). Removing a child from the note while a
member contains it hides it by the same rule: only where some member
containing it would show it after the save
('infer_hides_from_contains_removals').

*** the forkee-visibility step

The *forkee-visibility step* (a term of plan 10) is the step of
the save that, for a forkee as such shown editable under its
forker (the note that forks it), reads what the buffer shows of
the forkee's content and rewrites the forker's hides to match: content shown there,
claiming, is unhidden, and content the view drew there (what the
forker neither hid nor integrated before the save, by the graph, and
the buffer does not show in the forkee's folder of what the user
keeps elsewhere or of what the forker hides in it) and no longer
shows, which neither the forker nor an owned note but the forkee
contains after the save, is hidden ('resolve_visibility' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/resolve_visibility.rs').
It reads the forkee's content and the forker's hides by the
records the skgrepo restriction shows, as the view drew them, so a
hide recorded in a restricted section is neither removed nor moved,
and no hide is written of a member that only a restricted record
explains. Only a forker the user owns records hides: a save whose
step would hide or unhide anything in a foreign forker is refused,
naming the forker ('refusal_to_change_what_a_foreign_note_hides').

*** role tree, and the -ward adjectives

A *role tree* is what a view shows of a node's partners in one role
(see "relation, relationship, member, membership, partner"): for the
content role, the node's content; for either role of 'forks', the
members of the node's forks folder that play that role; for every
other role, the members of the node's partner folder of that role,
each with its containerward chain. A role, unlike a relation, is directed, so a
role tree goes one way.

The adjectives containerward, contentward, mentionerward,
forkerward, forkeeward, and so on name the role a role tree
follows: a containerward role tree follows the container role.

*** foldering, the containerward chain, chain node

To *folder* a role tree is to draw every partner as a member of a
partner folder of its role, never directly under the node. Every
role tree but the contentward one is foldered, and is
write-protected throughout. A folder request ('(folder containers)'
and its siblings, the =C-c l= commands) draws one; a fresh content
view draws a containers folder on each root; the search enrichment
(what a text search draws under each hit, in the one message that
answers it) draws a containers folder under each hit, its
forks folder when it has fork partners, and its
alias folder when an alias of its node matched.

The *containerward chain* is the one tree that goes on below a
folder. Under each member of a partner folder, of any kind but the
diff view's two acquisition folders, hang that member's containers,
as write-protected occurrences claiming nothing; under each of
those, its containers;
and so on to the roots, or to the config's 'max_roleTree_depth'. A
container met again on the way is drawn once more, as a leaf. The
nodes of a chain are *chain nodes*. A member of the forks folder is
the exception, outside the diff view: its chain is itself foldered.
Under it the refresh draws a containers folder, drawn folded (only
its headline shows), which leaves out the member's view-ancestors,
and each member of that folder carries its chain; a repeat (see
"editability rule") gets none, and shows nothing. So a fork
partner's containers never look like its content. A buffer drawn
before this rule holds chain nodes directly under such a member,
which the refresh reads as its content (unwarranted under a
write-protected member); reopening the view redraws it. Nothing hangs under a ghost
member, nor under a property member, and the member's
partners in the folder's own role are not drawn below it: a folder
request on the member draws them. No request extends a chain; a
folder request on a chain node draws a folder under it. A chain node
carries no change facts and takes no processed marker; a
folder's member does both. Nothing in a chain node's metadata says
that it is one: it is known by where it stands.

*** extend, rerender, refresh

The server *extends* a view when it draws more of it: the content of
a node shown editable, or a viewbranch a view request asks for. A
*rerender* redraws a view, after a save or a request: it is a
*refresh* of what the view holds (see "the refresh" among the terms
of the implementation), which keeps what the user put in it. Org
folding keeps the words "fold" and "unfold"; drawing more of a view
is not called unfolding.

*** baseline, stale, edited, unedited

A buffer's *baseline* is the text the server last drew for it, as
the client installed it (without the focus and fold metadata the
client removes), before any edit. The clients' names call it the
*clean baseline*; the two names mean one thing. Emacs keeps it in
'skg-clean-baseline', Neovim in
'vim.b.skg_clean_baseline', 'tools/skg-for-ai' in the view's
'.full.org' file; each recaptures it whenever a response redraws the
buffer, and sends it with every save. A new empty view's baseline is
the text the client created it with. *The baseline's writes* are the
Save instructions that saving the baseline itself would produce,
parsed, supplemented and filtered of no-ops as the saved text is:
a baseline viewnode equal to the graph's graphnode yields none; one
that differs, or whose graphnode the graph lacks, survives. *The
buffer's writes* are the graphnodes the saved buffer writes or deletes:
the nodeInstructions its text yields, filtered of no-ops and of its
unedited stale graphnodes, plus the original of each fork clone. A
view is *dirty* while its buffer holds edits not yet saved (the
client's modified flag), and *clean* otherwise. (The
"baseline" of a git stage in 'server/types/git.rs', HEAD for the staged
changes and the git index for the unstaged, is a git notion, not a
buffer's baseline.)

A graphnode among the baseline's writes is *stale* in the buffer:
the baseline's copy of it no longer matches the graph, or the graph no
longer has it. The graph changed it under the buffer.

The buffer *edited* a graphnode when the buffer's copy of it (the Save
instruction the saved text yields for it, supplemented and filtered of
no-ops) differs from the baseline's copy, both compared as a view reads
a graphnode back (relationship lists by distinct pid, the body without
trailing whitespace), and left it *unedited* when the two are equal:
the user did not edit it, and the buffer merely still shows what the
server drew. A delete is an edit, never unedited. A save leaves an
unedited stale graphnode out, with its home skgrepo move and any fork
of it, writes the rest, and warns of it ("Left as the graph has it:
..."); the redraw shows the graph's version
('without_unedited_stale_saves').

An *edited stale graphnode* is a stale one the buffer edits (writes
from a copy unlike the baseline's, or forks) or deletes: writing the
buffer's copy would undo a change the user never saw. A save with an
edited stale graphnode is refused whole
('server/serve/handlers/save_staleness.rs'). Staleness is decided by
content, not by time: a restart, a rebuild or a whitespace strip makes
nothing stale by itself, and a graphnode changed and changed back is
not stale. "Stale" applies to a graphnode; by extension, a buffer or
view is stale when it holds a stale graphnode's copy, and a save or
write is stale when it would write one. The stale error names what
changed at its own grain ('StaleAspect': the title, body, content,
aliases, extra IDs, flags, forks, hides or home
skgrepo), not as diff aspects: it says "body" where they say "text".

The records in 'TODO/' that predate these words say "outdated" for
stale, "untouched" for unedited, and "stale" alone for edited stale.

*** delta (redraw delta), stripped form

A response that redraws a view may carry its *delta* (in full, its
*redraw delta*): the viewnode IDs of the headlines whose text changed
between the text the client shows and the new text, so that the
client can rewrite only those lines. It compares the two texts in
their *stripped form*, the text with the fold and focus atoms
('focused', 'folded', 'bodyFolded') removed from each headline's
=(skg ...)= form, since the client holds those as org folds and
point rather than as text. A response without a delta means "whole":
the client redraws the whole buffer. The rule, and when the delta is
"whole", are in 'docs/api-and-formats.org', "The redraw delta"; the
server computes it in 'server/view_text_delta.rs'.

*** text search, search view, enrichment

A *text search* (=C-c f=) answers with one message, which opens a
*search view*: an ordinary view, named by its query (=search:TERMS=),
whose roots are the hits, best first, each a write-protected
occurrence of its node. Its buffer is the *search buffer*, a synonym
the docs and the code use for the search view. The *enrichment* (the search enrichment) is
what the search draws under each hit, with the refresh every view is
drawn with: its containers folder, whose members carry the
containerward chain; its forks folder, when it has fork partners;
and, for a hit found through an alias, its alias
folder, the one folder not folded. Under a skgrepo restriction that
excludes overPrivateText telescopes, what the enrichment would draw
of such a node is a tombstone. See 'docs/api-and-formats.org', "Text
search".

*** tombstone, entombstone

A *tombstone* is a viewnode with no title, no ID and no change facts,
shown as "something stood here", left where a headline stood that can
stand there for nothing, and holding that headline's children. The
refresh *entombstones* a headline (makes it one) when, in the diff
view, its gnid names nothing the view may show (a node HEAD lacks
too, or a restricted node that no record the skgrepo restriction
shows names; in a content view such an occurrence vanishes instead,
see "vanish"), when it describes its viewparent from where it cannot (see
"the refresh's rungs, unsayable" among the terms of the implementation), when
the change it described is gone, and when it is a folder left with
nothing in it that the save does not read and that is not the flags
folder. A text search leaves one where it excludes an
overPrivateText node (see "text search, search view, enrichment").
The refresh prunes a childless tombstone before the buffer is
rendered, a root included, so a user sees one only where it
holds something. The save discards anything typed on its line, and no
child of a tombstone claims membership (a headline typed under one,
taking the skgrepo of the nearest occurrence above, is saved as an
orphan graphnode; see "claims membership"); every folder accepts one
among its children. In code the kind is 'ViewnodeKind::Tombstone',
and on the wire the atom =tombstone=.

*** reinstated

A goal item that no child stands for is *reinstated*: the refresh
draws it, as a new viewnode, in a folder and in an editable
occurrence's content. So a member the user deleted from a
write-protected folder comes back at the next save, since its
membership is a fact of the graph, edited from the other side of the
relationship; and a member that a save in another view added appears
in this one. "Reinstate" names only this:
a child the buffer already holds is never drawn again, but re-kinded
and re-warranted where it stands.

*** what the refresh changes silently
The refresh brings a view up to date with the graph and says nothing
of what it changed: no save or rerender response reports it, and no
client shows it. A *buffer correction* (a term of plan 06, gone with
plan 10) was such a change, recorded and reported; the six kinds it
named remain things the refresh does.
**** a member drawn
It draws a member the graph has and a list the view already showed
lacks (see "reinstated").
**** a headline entombstoned
It entombstones a headline that can stand where it is for nothing,
keeping its children (see "tombstone, entombstone"); where, in a
content view, an occurrence's node is gone or restricted and its
viewparent's list does not name it, the occurrence vanishes instead
(see "vanish").
**** an unwarranted child
It makes a child that claims and that the graph does not relate to
its viewparent vanish, where the viewparent reads claims (see
"vanish").
**** duplicate siblings merged
It merges duplicate viewnode siblings, siblings standing for one
node under one parent, into one headline holding all their children
(see "viewnode merge" among the terms of the implementation).
**** a headline re-kinded
It re-kinds a headline to what the graph says it is, where it
stands (see "the ID rule").
**** an edit request dropped
It drops an edit request a headline's new kind has no slot for.

*** vanish
A headline of a view *vanishes* (a term of plan 10) when the refresh
removes it, and everything under it, from the view, for one of two
reasons. The graph no longer relates it to its viewparent: a child
that claims membership under a viewparent whose claims the save
reads, and whose warrant is not 'ByGraph' (the claims rung,
'vanish_if_unwarranted' in 'server/refresh/visit.rs'). Or the graph
no longer has its node: an occurrence in a content view, a view root
included, whose node is gone or restricted and whose viewparent's
list does not name it (the ID rule's tombstone answer,
'rekind_vognode' in 'server/refresh/id_rule.rs'). A view whose roots
all vanish is an empty buffer. What vanishes is not detached: the
graph, as the view sees it, already lacks the relationship, so a save
of the view has nothing to remove; a record a skgrepo narrowing keeps
out the save keeps, as it keeps every restricted record, and the
skgrepo widening draws the member again, where the goal names it. A
=noClaim= the user wrote on a warranted child (a pending detachment)
stays; so do the children of a viewparent that reads no claims, a
chain node, and, in the diff view, a ghost and a tombstone. The word
is unrelated to 'server/git_ops/vanished.rs', which asks at what
commit a node's file left the git history.

*** ghost, unknown viewnode

The two headlines that stand for a gnid the graph has no graphnode
for (in the implementation, two vognodes: see "gnid-only vognode").
A *ghost*, drawn only in the diff view, stands for a node HEAD has
and the worktree lacks, write-protected, with HEAD's title and body
(=ghost (gnid ID) (skgRepos (nodeRepo REPO))=); it accepts
=restoreNode= and no view request, and is shown until it is
restored or its deletion is committed ('docs/diff-view.org', "deleted
and new nodes"). A ghost claims
membership only where its viewparent's list in the worktree still
names its gnid, a dangling reference to a node HEAD has, and the save
then keeps that gnid in the list, as it keeps an unknown viewnode's;
anywhere else it claims nothing. An *unknown viewnode* (a term of
plan 10; the wire's atom =unknown=, herald "Reference to unknown
node.") is the headline standing for an unknown graphnode, a gnid its
viewparent's list names and no graphnode answers to (see "unknown
graphnode, dangling ID"). In a content view an occurrence whose
graphnode a save deleted becomes an unknown viewnode where its
viewparent's list still names the gnid, and otherwise vanishes (see
"vanish"); in the diff view it becomes its ghost. In a content view
a ghost is re-kinded by the ID rule: an unknown viewnode where the
list names it, else a tombstone.

*** unknown graphnode, dangling ID

An *unknown graphnode* (a term of plan 10) is the graphnode an ID
names that the graph does not have: the ID is one that some node's
contains, forks or hides list names but
that no graphnode answers to, as primary or extra ID; such an ID is
*dangling*. A view shows it as an unknown viewnode (see "ghost,
unknown viewnode"), which claims membership, since removing it would
edit the list. A *dangling partner* is a member of a list whose ID is
dangling; in the diff view, one the worktree lost is an unknown
viewnode carrying '(rel removed)'. The command
'skg-investigate-unknown-node' (=C-c t r=; =<localleader>tr= in
Neovim) says what git history knows of an unknown graphnode, and
'skg-delete-references-to-absent-node' (=C-c d a=; =<localleader>da=)
removes the references to it.

*** record, restricted, visible

A *record* (a term of plan 06, as are *restricted* and *visible*
here) is a relationship as one node's file states it, in one
skgrepo's section (see "recorder, carrier" and "privacy order and
relRepo"); its skgrepo is its relRepo. A record is *restricted* when
the skgrepo restriction keeps its relRepo out, and *visible*
otherwise. A node is *restricted* when the skgrepo restriction keeps
its home skgrepo out; in the diff view its home is the worktree's, or
HEAD's when the worktree lacks the node. A member of a goal is
visible when its record and its node's home are both unrestricted.
The code asks the two questions by
'SkgrepoRestriction::record_is_visible' and
'SkgrepoRestriction::node_is_restricted' ('server/skgrepo_sets.rs'),
which share one body, and by nothing else: the records of a list it
shows are 'SkgrepoRestriction::visible_records'; whether the graph
shows the node a gnid names, 'InRustGraph::gnid_presence_gated' and
'InRustGraph::node_is_shown' answer by the second question; and
whether a connection is unrestricted,
'SkgrepoRestriction::is_unrestricted'. With no skgrepo restriction every skgrepo is
shown; under any skgrepo restriction a record whose relRepo the
config does not name is visible, and a node whose home skgrepo the
config does not name is unrestricted.

*** restricted viewnode

A *restricted viewnode* (a term of plan 10; on the wire =restricted
(gnid ID) [(skgRepos (relRepo REPO))] [noClaim] [(editRequest
...)]=) is the headline standing for a graphnode the
skgrepo restriction keeps out (a restricted graphnode; see "record,
restricted, visible"). It stands where a visible record names a
restricted graphnode, or where a view opened before a
skgrepo narrowing is rooted at one (the view is the user's own
request; opening a view of a restricted graphnode is refused). It carries the record's gnid and relRepo
and nothing of the graphnode: no title, no body, no home skgrepo, no
stats; its herald is "restricted", and text typed on its line is
refused, as on an unknown viewnode's. A goal keeps such a member,
and omits only members whose records are restricted; the ID rule
answers it where the goal names its gnid (see "the ID rule").

An occurrence of a graphnode that a skgrepo narrowing keeps out
becomes a restricted viewnode in place, keeping its viewnode ID,
fold state and children, and an occurrence again after a
skgrepo widening. A view root of such a graphnode becomes a
restricted viewnode whether or not a record names it. The
folders under such an occurrence describe a graphnode the restricted
viewnode shows nothing of, so they become tombstones, holding their
members, and stay tombstones after the skgrepo widening. In the diff
view, after a switch, a restricted graphnode's ghost becomes a
restricted viewnode, or a tombstone, by the same rule, since a
restricted graphnode is never a ghost. A restricted viewnode claims
membership as an unknown viewnode does: a save keeps its gnid in the
list it claims a place in, and deleting its line removes the record;
the save restores to a list only the members whose records are
restricted, which the buffer could not show. It takes a relRepo
request and a processed request. In the implementation it is a
gnid-only vognode (see "gnid-only vognode").

*** ancestor generation

How far up the view an ancestor is: 1 is the viewparent, 2 the
grandparent, counting folders. The '(rels ...)' form's '(ancestors GEN
...)' and its birth fact's GEN are ancestor generations, and heralds
show them as a, b, ... z, then {27}. A tree's plain "generation",
counting down from its root, is the opposite direction.

*** mirror, and a perspective's skgrepo list

A *fork*, bare, is a note that forks another: a *fork of* N is a
note that forks N (see "clone, and the fork operation" for the
gesture that makes one, which is always "the fork operation", never
bare "a fork"). A note's *forks*, as in "its forks", are the members
of its forks folder ("the forks folder"): its fork partners, either
way, the notes it forks and the notes that fork it, and its indirect
members. So "its forks" is wider than "the forks of it"; where only
one direction is meant, say "its forkees" or "its forkers", or "what
it forks". The
*mirrors* of N are N and every note joined to N by a path of
'forks' relationships, followed in either direction, every
relationship's relRepo and every note's home skgrepo on the path shown
by the skgrepo restriction ('mirrors_of' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph/mirrors.rs'). Two forks of one note are
mirrors of each other, and a cycle of forks is no special case. (Not
"mirror" in its other senses: the save is its mirror, a Neovim
function mirrors an Emacs one, a server mirror.)

A *perspective's skgrepo list* is the order in which the perspective
of a skgrepo S ranks a note's mirrors: S first, then every other
skgrepo in the config's declaration order, whose owned entries run
most public first ('SkgConfig::perspective_skgrepo_list'). Who owns a
skgrepo plays no part. It is not a privacy rank, nor the fold order.
The *user's own skgrepo list* is the skgrepo list of the perspective
of the user's first owned skgrepo in declaration order, the most
public (else the first declared:
'SkgConfig::first_owned_else_first_declared_skgrepo'); it ranks
search hits that are mirrors of one another ('docs/mirrors.org').
It is no perspective of a view's, and not the default perspective,
the root homes.

*** mirror substitution, and the vocabulary around it

Where a node N stands as content of an editable occurrence in a
content view, or as a root of it, the refresh shows instead the
*mirror shown* for N (see "mirror"): the mirror whose home skgrepo
comes first in the skgrepo list of the perspective of the
occurrence's top-level tree, ties broken as 'docs/mirrors.org',
"Which mirror stands", says ('mirror_shown' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph/mirrors.rs'). That perspective is the root
homes', the tree's root's home skgrepo
('perspective_skgrepo_of_tree_containing'), or one skgrepo's in every
tree of a view switched to it ('Perspective'; Emacs 'C-c v o'). The
mirror shown may be in any skgrepo: where the perspective's holds no
mirror of N, the first declared that holds one stands. In its own
skgrepo N itself stands, however many of its mirrors are there; so when
the mirror shown is N itself, nothing substitutes. An owned chain D
forks C forks N, kept in one skgrepo other than N's, shows D there;
visibility gating can cut the chain, so that a middle node shows.

The shown substitute carries the fact '(inPlaceOf N)'
(the crucial herald =ĥ= after =K=; 'in_place_of' on the occurrence),
naming the original it stands for. A viewnode's
collected ID is what it contributes to its parent's saved lists:
the fact's original if present, else its own ID -- so saving a
view that shows R in place of N keeps N in the parent's contains.
The headline bearing the fact is its *bearer* (not its "carrier",
which names the node holding an ID; see "recorder, carrier"). The
*tamper check* (also "tamper validation") is the save's refusal of a
fact whose bearer is no mirror of the original it names
('inPlaceOf_part_errors' in
'server/from_text/buffer_to_viewnodes/validate_tree.rs'), since the
fact is load-bearing text a hand edit could forge.

A node is *shown raw* where it stands as itself, not replaced by the
mirror shown for it. Only content and roots substitute, so a member
of a partner folder, a chain node and a ghost are shown raw, and so,
one level down, are the children of a node shown raw where another
mirror would stand ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Mirror substitution
and the inPlaceOf part").
A view opened on one node always shows the requested node itself:
drawn from the root homes, its root stands in its own home skgrepo,
where it stands as itself; so does each hit of a search buffer.
Switched to one skgrepo's perspective, the view shows the mirror of
the root that skgrepo's list ranks first instead, standing for it
('Perspective').

*** perspective

A view's *perspective* is one skgrepo, or its root homes, one per
tree; a skgrepo's list ranks the mirrors each tree shows (see
"mirror"; 'Perspective'; on the wire "perspective", the request "set
perspective"): its *root homes*, as every fresh view's is (each
top-level tree is drawn from the perspective of the home skgrepo of
the node its root stands for; on the wire "root homes"), or one
skgrepo's in every tree, owned or foreign, its roots included (on the
wire "skgrepo NAME"). So a
private and a public fork of one note can each be chosen. 'C-c v o'
(Neovim '<localleader>vo') cycles a view through the root homes and
then each skgrepo home to a mirror in the view whose perspective
would draw it differently, in declaration order
('skg-cycle-perspective'; 'docs/api-and-formats.org', "Set
perspective"). Messages name one "the perspective of tracy", by the
skgrepo's herald label.

*** root homes

The default perspective ('Perspective::RootHomes', on the wire "root
homes"): each top-level tree of a view is drawn from the perspective
of its root's home skgrepo, the root standing for its '(inPlaceOf N)'
else itself ('perspective_skgrepo_of_tree_containing'). See
"perspective".

*** the fork rules

The *fork rules* are rules 4 to 6 of the hard validation constraints
('docs/data-model_technical.org'), which a save of an owned note must
keep and the graph is checked against at startup: its home skgrepo is
one the config names; it forks, contains and hides nothing of itself;
and it neither contains what it forks, nor what forks it, nor forks
what contains it ('server/dbs/in_rust_graph/fork_rules.rs',
'ForkRuleViolation'). Most of them are about forks, whence the name;
the skgrepo rule and the self-containment and self-hide rules are
checked with them because they read the same records of the notes a
save touches. A violation a foreign note's record takes part in is a
startup warning (a "fork rule warning"), not a refusal.

*** retired keys

The *retired keys* are the '.skg' keys a server refuses to read since
November 2026: 'subscribesTo', 'overrides' and 'hidesFromSubs', and
the October 2026 names of the first two ('RETIRED_SKG_KEYS';
'docs/api-and-formats.org', "The .skg file format").

*** skgrepo, home skgrepo, skgrepo-set, gitrepo

A *skgrepo* is a directory of =.skg= files, configured by one
=[[repos]]= entry in =skgconfig.toml=. A node's *home skgrepo* is the
skgrepo that holds its home section (see below). A *skgrepo-set* is a
prefix of the config's privacy ladder, the owned skgrepos in privacy
order: the owned skgrepos made available together. A *gitrepo* is a Git repository. Skgrepos are usually gitrepos
too, but they need not be.

In prose, always say "skgrepo" or "gitrepo", never a bare "repo", so the
two cannot be confused. ("relRepo" is a single coined term, so it keeps
the short form.) Code says 'skgrepo' and 'gitrepo' too ('SkgrepoName',
'home_skgrepo'); a skgrepo's config entry is the 'Skgrepo' type.

The wire coins four more atoms, each one term, which keep their
spelling: =skgRepos=, the metadata form that holds a headline's
skgrepos; =nodeRepo= in it, the node's home skgrepo, and =relRepo=,
its relationship's; =repoBoundary=, the derived fact that an
occurrence is a root or that its node's home skgrepo differs from
that of the nearest occurrence above it (herald =⌂:LABEL=); and
=skgrepoLabels=, the table of skgrepo labels the herald rules come
with. A skgrepo's *skgrepo label* is the short name its heralds
show: its =abbreviation= in the config, or else its name
('herald_label' in code).

The *skgrepo restriction* ('SkgrepoRestriction') is what a connection
currently shows: a skgrepo-set, named by the most private owned
skgrepo it shows (its *rung*), and whether the foreign skgrepos are
shown, all of them or none, which they are unless the connection
hides them ('foreign_shown'). When the rung is the config's most
private owned skgrepo and the foreign skgrepos are shown it holds
every skgrepo and restricts nothing: the connection is
*unrestricted* (a term of plan 10), and has no skgrepo restriction.
The clients show the most private rung so by appending *" (no
restriction)"* (a term of plan 10) to that skgrepo's name where they
show the skgrepo restriction, words that never travel on the wire or
sit in the config. To *switch* the skgrepo restriction is to choose
another rung, or to show or hide the foreign skgrepos, with
'skg-restrict-repo-set' (=C-c v l=; =<localleader>vl=
in Neovim): the clients refuse while a view has unsaved edits, and
the server rerenders every open view under the new one, saying
nothing of what the rerender changed. A skgrepo inside the
skgrepo-set the skgrepo restriction shows is
*unrestricted*, one outside it *restricted*, and so are their nodes
and occurrences: the *gated graph*, the in-Rust graph as the
skgrepo restriction lets it be seen, answers 'restricted' for such a node,
as distinct from 'present' and 'absent' ('GnidPresence'), and the
view shows nothing of it but, where a visible record names it, a
restricted viewnode (see "restricted viewnode"). "Hidden" is reserved
for the =hides= relation.

The skgrepo restriction limits what can be edited as well as what can
be seen: no save, nodeMerge,
fork, deletion, commit or other write may remove, move, rewrite or
create a record, a section or a node file the skgrepo restriction
keeps out, nor act on the buffer's silence about such a record, since
what the user could not see the user did not edit. A save under a
skgrepo restriction therefore keeps every restricted record as it was
('preserve_invisible_members' in 'server/from_text/weave.rs') and asks
a record's visibility ('record_is_visible') before any decision that a
record's absence from the buffer would drive. It refuses a save that
would create or move a record into a restricted section, naming the
skgrepo, and a delete whose propagation would strip restricted records
of other graphnodes stops for the *delete-from confirmation* (a term
of plan 10): the user cancels, deletes from the visible records only,
or deletes from all owned records ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Save
buffer").

A *skgrepo narrowing* is a switch that shows fewer skgrepos (a
smaller skgrepo-set, or the foreign skgrepos hidden), and a *skgrepo
widening* one that shows more (both terms of plan 08). The words "restriction", "narrowing" and
"widening" always take the modifier "skgrepo". Where an occurrence of
a node stood when a skgrepo narrowing kept it out, a restricted
viewnode stands in its place where its record is visible, holding
the occurrence's children; where it is not, the occurrence vanishes
with them (see "vanish"), as does a member whose record alone the
skgrepo narrowing keeps out, and the skgrepo widening draws each
again where the graph puts it. A view root is the exception: it
stays, as a restricted viewnode, whether or not its record is
visible (see "restricted viewnode").

*** owned, foreign

A skgrepo is *owned* unless its config entry says =foreign = true=,
which makes it *foreign*. Only owned skgrepos have privacy levels;
the foreign ones are an unordered set, listed anywhere in the config
(see "privacy order and relRepo"). Every skgrepo has an *owner*
('SkgrepoOwner'): the user, for an owned one; for foreign ones, one
owner per set of skgrepos that dependencies manifests chain together.
Only owned sections are ever written. A real edit to a foreign node is
read as a request to fork it (see "clone, and the fork operation").
Say "owned" rather than "editable", and "foreign" rather than
"write-protected", when speaking of skgrepos: write-protection is a
property of a viewnode occurrence (see "editable, write-protected").

*** "telescope" = privacy telescope

In code and comments the short form "telescope" always means the
privacy telescope; see "privacy telescope" below.

*** privacy telescope, telescope section, home

One node can be recorded at several privacy levels at once: same-ID
=.skg= files across skgrepos, at most one per skgrepo. That family of
files is the node's *privacy telescope*; each file is one
*telescope section* ("section" for short in code), holding the
slice of the node recorded at its skgrepo's privacy level. The *home* is
the most public retained section; it alone holds title, body, and extra
ids. That the home carries the title is an invariant, not a
search rule: a node's text always lives in its most public
section, so a titleless section above the title is a violation
the composition reports (=CompositionWarning::TitleBelowHome=), not a shape to
look past. There is no linking relationship between sections —
sharing an ID is being the same node. See =docs/telescopes.org=
and =server/types/nodes/fs.rs=.

A telescope's sections are all one owner's (see "owned, foreign").
Before composing, sections of one PID in skgrepos of different owners
are separated: the home owner's are the telescope, and the rest are
*illegal edits to a foreign node* — shown, when in an owned skgrepo,
as an =illegalEditFolder= child of the node, and otherwise ignored,
with a startup warning and no filesystem edit (=docs/telescopes.org=,
"Illegal edits").

*** overPrivateText

This is kind of an error. Skg can handle it if it sees it in your data,
but it's a good thing to fix.

A privacy telescope has *overPrivateText* when its composed title or body is
selected from a section more private than its home (most public retained)
section. The flag applies to the whole telescope, even if only one text
field is below home. This violates the rule that the node's text lives at
home; see [[file:./telescopes.org][privacy telescopes]].

An unrestricted connection can show that text with a warning. A restricted request
asks for approval scoped to the affected node IDs and that request before
releasing it; search asks whether to include or exclude such telescopes.
Approval to display the text does not authorize *Hoist*, the separate
repair that moves the selected title and body to home (see "Hoist").

*** Hoist, the Hoist confirmation, Abort

*Hoist* is the save's repair of overPrivateText: it moves the
selected title and body of a privacy telescope up to its home
section, removes the lower copies of the title and body, and keeps
the lower sections' relationships and aliases where they are. The
name is the docs' and the wire's (=telescope-hoist-confirmation=,
=approved-hoist-pids=); the code is
=server/serve/handlers/telescope_hoist.rs=.

The *Hoist confirmation* is how a save offers the repair. After
validation and before any write, the save rereads every telescope it
would write; if one currently selects a title or body from below its
home, nothing is skgsave-committed and the client is asked to choose
*Hoist* or *Abort*, with the IDs and homes of the telescopes
concerned and no text. Hoist re-issues the same save carrying the
exact IDs the user approved; the server rereads and reclassifies them
as one batch on that retry, and a telescope that has become dirty in
the meantime raises a new confirmation. Abort writes nothing and
leaves every file as it was; the repair is then a hand edit of the
=.skg= sections. Among the save's confirmations the Hoist
confirmation comes after the delete-from and strandeds confirmations
and before the fork confirmation. Scripts and maintenance writers
have no answer to give and refuse the save instead.

Approving a Hoist publishes text on disk; it does not authorize
showing overPrivateText in a view, which is the text-release
approval's separate question (see "overPrivateText"). See
[[file:./telescopes.org][privacy telescopes]] for the invariant the
repair restores.

*** declaration order

The *declaration order* is the order in which the config lists every
skgrepo, owned and foreign ('SkgConfig::skgrepos_in_declaration_order',
'SkgConfig::declaration_rank'). Its owned entries are the privacy
order (see "privacy order and relRepo"), and the fold order of a
foreign node permutes its foreign ones. A user's *first-declared*
owned skgrepo is the first owned one in it, the most public.

*** privacy order and relRepo

The position of an owned skgrepo in the config's privacy order (most
public first), the order of the owned entries, is its privacy level;
the owned skgrepos in that order are the *privacy ladder*
('SkgConfig::privacy_ladder'). A foreign skgrepo has no level, and
no comparison of privacy involves one: it is neither more nor less
public than anything. The order the sections of one node fold in is
its *fold order* ('SkgConfig::ordered_skgrepos'): the privacy order
for an owned node, and for a foreign one the order its owner's
dependencies manifests give ('docs/api-and-formats.org',
"OTHER_SKGREPO_DEPENDENCIES.toml"). Live code does not use "level" for the
value carried by a relationship: every relationship member carries its
*relRepo*, the skgrepo whose section records it. In code
that is =RelPartner { relRepo, member }=; a collection of them is
a *list of relation partners*. "More private" = later in the
privacy order.

*** home skgrepo move, relRepo move

A *home skgrepo move* is a change of a node's home skgrepo (=C-c s s=,
then a save); a *relRepo move* is a change of a relationship's relRepo
(=C-c s r=, or a save that resets or raises it). The diff view shows
each move since HEAD as one change-forest leaf: a home skgrepo move as
the change viewnode "⌂ -OLD +NEW" under the node, a relRepo move as the
change viewnode "relationship moved from OLD to NEW" under the member, keyed
by the relationship, so the same move seen from either end is the
same leaf. A relRepo move puts only the recorder in C. The
skgrepo-changes buffer of the diff view shows the nodes whose home
skgrepo moved and the recorders of the relationships whose relRepo
moved, each with all of its changes. Say "home skgrepo move", not
"home move".

*** sticky rule (sticky-else-default), floor check

Where a relationship's relRepo comes from at save time:
a relationship already on disk keeps its relRepo (*sticky*); a new relationship
between owned nodes defaults to the more-private endpoint home. A new
relationship from an owned recorder to a foreign member defaults to the recorder's
home, deliberately making the foreign ID and relationship visible at
that owned skgrepo without proposing a foreign write. Every relRepo is
clamped to be no more public than the recorder's home. Hides have
a higher floor (at least the most public partner on record for them;
see "hides" under "relation, relationship, member, membership,
partner", and "floor" below). A
=(relRepo NAME)= atom (the =skg-set-relRepo= gesture)
overrides sticky with any skgrepo at least as private as the relationship's
default — raising privacy, or moving a stuck skgrepo back to the
default; a more-public choice is a save error. A legacy or
hand-authored relationship whose disk relRepo already sits more public than
its default may be held or made more private, never made still more
public. See =server/from_text/supplement_from_disk.rs=.

A relationship's default relRepo is its *floor*: the most public
skgrepo it may be recorded in. The save's *floor check*
('apply_sticky_relRepos_in_graph') refuses a relRepo request more
public than the floor, naming the relationship. The relRepo gesture
('skg-set-relRepo', =C-c s r=) offers only the skgrepos at least as
private as the floor when the server can say what the floor is; where it
cannot (a node not yet saved, or the recursive gesture,
'skg-set-relRepo-recursive', =C-c s R=; =<localleader>sR= in Neovim,
whose relationships have different floors), it offers every
skgrepo, and the floor check is what refuses a choice too public.

**** stranded relationship, strandeds

A home skgrepo move can strand a relationship touching the moved
node: a *stranded relationship* is one that sat at its default relRepo
before the move and that the sticky rule would leave off its new
default. *Stranded* can be both noun and adjective; "strand"
is only a verb. A save finds every one, of every relation, in both
directions, shown in some view or not ('server/from_text/strandeds.rs').
It raises the inbound strandeds of a privatizing move to the new home
by itself, since they would leak the member's ID, and asks the user
whether to reset the strandeds of a publicizing move to their new
defaults (the strandeds-confirmation). Mentions carry no relRepo, so
they are never stranded.

*** clone, and the fork operation

To *fork* a node N is to turn it into an editable *clone* C in a
skgrepo you own. C copies N's title, body, and child-list (the child IDs
only — shallow), and =forks= N, once (so N's later additions surface
to C as unintegrated forked content, and mirror substitution shows C
wherever N would appear, from the perspective of C's skgrepo, or of
one that holds no mirror of N where C's is declared first). Children of N
that the forking edit deleted become C's
=hides=, and thereafter any child removed from
an owned note's contains that some member its forks folder shows
still contains (a forkee, a forker it does not hide, or an indirect
member) is hidden likewise on save, so that it does not come back
under that member, unless another note of yours keeps it then (see
"integrated"; re-adding it unhides). The fork operation
refuses to fork a node you already fork, naming your fork of it:
silently making a second clone would more often be an accident. A
deliberate second fork is made by having another note of yours fork
N, which nothing refuses: any number of notes may fork one note.

Forking spans two gestures, foreign and owned:

- /implicit/ (foreign N): make N editable (=C-c s e=), edit it, and
  save; any real change to a foreign node is read as a fork request, and
  the save asks you to confirm. N is left untouched on disk.
- /explicit/ (owned N): =skg-fork-node= (=C-c m f=), for a node you
  already own — used to deepen a fork chain (D forks C forks
  N, all owned). N keeps its own content; only the clone is added. The
  clone is built from N's saved state, so the command refuses a dirty
  buffer.

See =docs/forks.org= and =docs/sharing-model.org=.

*** nodeMerge ("merge" is always qualified)

A /nodeMerge/ joins two graphnodes: the acquirer takes the
acquiree's content and IDs, and the acquiree's text is kept in a
text preserver. =C-c s m= ('skg-set-merge-request') writes the
request, =(editRequest (merge ID))=, and a save applies it; the diff
view's merges buffer shows each nodeMerge since HEAD. In prose, say
"merge nodes" or "nodeMerge", never a bare "merge"; the bare word
appears only in client-facing wire strings, where only nodes can
merge. The acquirer and the acquiree are the nodeMerge's two *merge
nodes*, and a save carries each merge request as a *merge pair*: the
acquirer's pid and the acquiree's. See =docs/merging.org=.

*** text preserver

A nodeMerge keeps the acquiree's text in a new node, its *text
preserver*: titled "MERGED: " and the acquiree's title, with the
acquiree's body, and placed first in the acquirer's content. The
acquiree's other content joins the acquirer's, and the acquiree's ID
becomes an extra ID of the acquirer. See =docs/merging.org=.

*** no search matching

A node-file flag (=NoSearchMatching= in YAML,
=noSearchMatching= on the wire) that prevents the node's title, aliases, and
body from producing a direct text-search hit. The node remains indexed for
exact-ID lookup and may appear as context around another hit. It is search
decluttering, not privacy.

*** prominent

Search ranks *prominent* nodes higher: a root, a member of a cycle,
a node linked to, a node that had an ID before import, or a
multiply contained node. Most nodes are not prominent.

*** flags folder and Flag

=flagsFolder= is a generated, write-protected non-vognode under a graphnode. Its
=(flag FLAG)= leaves list the node's true flags in canonical
order. Each leaf is titleless because its herald supplies the visible label.
The folder stays visible when empty. Edits within a retained folder are
rejected before save, but deleting the entire folder safely dismisses the
optional viewbranch and changes no flag. =noSearchMatching= is changed
through the dedicated setter, while the two org-roam import provenance
flags have no setter. A node with =n= true flags has the low-styled =Fn=
herald; no flag herald is shown at zero.

*** data root

The directory that holds 'skgconfig.toml'. Every relative path in
the config resolves against it ('docs/api-and-formats.org',
"skgconfig.toml").

*** export root, export marker

An *export root* is a node that the export to org
('skg-export-some-to-org', no key; =:SkgExportToOrg= in Neovim)
writes to a file of its own. It is marked by an *export marker*: one
of its =contains= children whose title links to the fixed ID
=3d9aa9be-d95a-48bc-b362-33f9e7ebdf6f= and whose body names the
=target_filepath=. The node that ID names only documents the
feature; the docs also call it the export instruction node. See
'docs/COMMANDS.org', "choosing what to export: export markers", and
'docs/api-and-formats.org', "Export to org".

*** linkstack

The *linkstack* is a stack of links the user has stored, to insert
later: 'skg-id-push' (=C-c u=) pushes a link to the node at point;
'skg-paste-id' (=C-c o i=), 'skg-paste-link' (=C-c o l=) and
'skg-paste-node' (=C-c o n=) paste the top one, as an ID, a link or
a write-protected occurrence of its node; and 'skg-pop-id' (=C-c O
i=) and 'skg-pop-link' (=C-c O l=) pop it. 'skg-view-linkstack' (=C-c v s=) shows the whole stack for
editing. Neovim's keys are in 'docs/COMMANDS-nvim.org', "the
linkstack".

*** metadata editor

The *metadata editor* is the buffer that 'skg-view-metadata'
(=C-c v m=; =<localleader>vm= in Neovim) opens on the metadata of the
headline at point, one part per line. It shows each field the
headline's kind takes, as 'shared/metadata-fields.json' lists them,
and S-left and S-right cycle a field through its values. =C-c C-c=
applies the edit to the headline.

*** node budget

How many editable occurrences' content a view draws at once
('initial_node_limit' in the config, 1000 by default). Once it is
spent, the occurrences a view draws are write-protected, and
'skg-set-editable' (=C-c s e=) makes one editable. See "extent,
extension, node budget" among the terms of the implementation.

*** non-vognode

A headline that stands for no graphnode: a folder, a property member
(an alias, an ID, a flag), a change viewnode of the diff view, and a
tombstone. The headlines that do stand for one (an occurrence, a
ghost, an unknown viewnode, a restricted viewnode) are the
*vognodes*. See "vognode, non-vognode" among the terms of the
implementation.

*** link status, link annotation

A link's *link status* is what the server says of its target: resolved
(with its pid and its home skgrepo's skgrepo label), restricted, or
missing ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Link statuses"). A client shows
it after the link as a *link annotation*, such as =[⌂:pub]= or
=[⌂:restricted]= ('docs/heralds.org', "Link annotations"), which is
not a herald.

*** Terms a user meets in the diff view

'docs/diff-view.org' describes the diff view; these are the terms
a user meets in it, and they mean nothing outside it.

**** diff view

Four buffers that show every change in the owned skgrepos since their
last commits, with the context needed to understand them: "all
changes", "merges", "skgrepo changes" and "order-only changes". Each is
an ordinary editable view. Foreign skgrepos are excluded; the
processed summary only counts their changed nodes.

**** change viewnode

A headline the diff view adds to describe a change
('ChangeViewnode'): a node's old text, with its body diff; its home
skgrepo move; its changeset markers; and, under a member, the relRepo
move of the member's relationship. At each refresh an occurrence's
change viewnodes are recomputed in place from the straddle: kept
while the change exists, drawn when missing, entombstoned when the
change is gone, the tombstone keeping what the user drew under it
('server/refresh/diff_view.rs'). The acquisition folders and the
changed folders are drawn the same way.

**** pruned change view

The viewforest the "all changes" buffer renders, defined by the sets
C, R, U and D (below; 'server/diff_view/pruned_change_view.rs',
functions 'prunedChangeView_...'). The merges and skgrepo-changes
buffers render pruned change views over subsets of the changes: the
nodeMerges, and the home skgrepo and relRepo moves.

**** the three kinds of change

The first three parts of a headline's '(diff ...)' form, each written
only when true ('DiffChange'): the node's existence, '(node added)'
or '(node removed)' (herald ∃+, ∃-), when only the after-graph or
only the before-graph has it ('GraphStraddle::node_existence'); the
existence of its relationship to its viewparent, '(rel added)' or
'(rel removed)' (herald ~+, ~-); and the node's other properties,
'(properties changed)' (herald M, "modified"), on a node in both
graphs that records at least one change-forest leaf
('GraphStraddle::node_properties_changed') that the object 'node'
names on that headline: one neither its own 'rel' nor a headline
under it shows. The two existences are
'Existence { Added, Removed }'. A member, a removed member, a folder's
member and a root carry the node's two; a ghost never carries
'(properties changed)'. The relationship is the viewparent's content
under a vognode, and, in a partner folder, the relationship of the folder's kind between
the member and the folder's viewparent; the two compound partner
folders' members carry none. On an alias, ID or flag member, '(rel
...)' is the member's existence in its list.

**** change facts

A headline's *change facts* are the parts of its '(diff ...)' form
that the server writes: the three kinds of change, the '(leaves
...)' count of its change-forest leaves, the '(processed ...)' processed state
(its processed marker), and in the skgrepo-changes buffer its
home skgrepo move and its moved-like-ancestor fact ('ChangeFacts' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs'). A
processed request, in the headline's '(editRequest ...)', is the
user's, not a change fact. The words are never shortened to
"facts".

**** diff object, diff aspect

A *diff object* is what a part of a '(diff ...)' form or a processed request
names on a headline: the node itself ('node'), or the node's
relationship to its viewparent ('rel'; in a partner folder, to the
folder's viewparent). There are exactly these two.
A change-forest leaf about a relationship has the relationship as its
object, never the member. 'node' names every leaf the node records but
the relationships shown on it or under it (its own 'rel', and those of
the members drawn under it), so a mark marks what its headline shows
('docs/diff-view.org', "What a request names"). On a changeset marker
'rel' names the relationships it gathers and 'node' their members' own
leaves. A request alone may also say 'newContent' or 'newOutward',
shorthand: each names leaves of the new nodes reached from the
headline's node through new nodes only, as the change forest says,
whatever the buffer shows, filtered by the letters beside it ('node'
their existence, 'rel' their relationships, neither both). 'newContent' follows content relationships
and names each new node's existence (which covers its text) and its
content relationships; 'newOutward' follows every relationship in its
recorded direction ("out") and names every leaf each new node
records. A *diff aspect* is what a
change-forest leaf is about: existence, text, home skgrepo, an alias,
an extra ID, a flag, or relRepo.

**** C, R, U, D, content origin

The sets the pruned change view is computed from: C, the changed
nodes; R, the content origins; U, the nodes to extend contentward; D,
the deduplicated content origins. *C*, the changed nodes, is the set of changed
graphnodes, in two halves: a node is changed *out* when its own file
records a change (it is new or deleted, or its text, aliases, extra
IDs, flags, home skgrepo or own relationships changed), and *in* when
its recorders' files do (its set of containers, mentioners,
forkers or hiders changed). A node can be both. A
relRepo move puts only the recorder in C, and a change of order alone
puts nothing in it. *R*, the content origins, is the set of content
origins reached from C by walking containerward through the union of
the straddle. A *content origin* is a node with no
container, a multiply-contained node, or a member of a rootlike cycle
(the stop rule of 'server/prominence.rs'). *U*, the nodes to
extend contentward, is R, C and every node on a containerward path
from a node of C to an origin. *D*, the deduplicated content origins,
the roots of the "all changes" buffer, is what remains of R when each
origin, in ID order, is discarded if its own containerward walk within
U reaches another origin still kept; of two origins on one cycle,
exactly one is kept.

A user-visible message that must name one of these sets says its long
name, then the letter in parentheses, then where the algorithm is
described: "the nodes to extend contentward (U; see
'docs/diff-view.org', "C, R, U and D", and the module comment of
'server/diff_view/pruned_change_view.rs')".

**** change-forest, change-forest leaf, recorder of a leaf

The *change-forest* is the picture of everything that can be marked
processed, built from the graph straddle, independent of any
view: its roots are the changed graphnodes; under each come its
changed aspects; its leaves, the *change-forest leaves* (*change
leaves* for short, as in 'ChangeLeaves'; "change leaf" is a term of
plan 06), are the individual changes
a user marks. The type 'ChangeLeaves'
('server/diff_view/change_leaves.rs') holds every leaf, keyed by its recorder,
its aspect and, for a relationship, the relationship ('LeafKey'). The
*recorder* of a leaf is the node whose file records the change, and
that node *records* the leaf; a relationship's leaf is recorded by
the relationship's recorder, so seen from either end it is the same
leaf.

**** mark, processed

A *mark* says that one change-forest leaf is processed; the
processed gestures set and clear marks. Editing a marked change
unmarks it. A leaf is *processed* when it is marked, and *unprocessed* otherwise.

**** processed aggregate

A *processed aggregate* is a thing with change-forest leaves
under it: a folder, a changeset marker, a node, a file, the whole
changeset. It is processed iff every leaf under it that can be processed is
processed; that derived value is *its processed state*. A view shows it as
'(processed ...)' in the '(diff ...)' form, which nothing reads at save;
a folder carries none, and each client computes whether a folder is processed
from its members (see "changed folder").

**** processed marker

The '(processed ...)' part of a headline's '(diff ...)' form, and its
herald: the derived processed state of a processed aggregate or of
the leaves of a headline with change facts. A changed folder's ✓,
which the clients draw from its members, is one too. A folder's
member takes one; a chain node takes none. It is never called a bare "marker", since a
changeset marker is another thing.

**** changeset marker

A childless change viewnode ('ChangeViewnode::ChangesetMarker') that
stands for a set of changes recorded in its parent's file, and carries
their derived processed state. There are three kinds: the content changeset
marker ("its content changed"), the relRepo changeset marker ("some
content relationships changed skgrepo") and the order-only changeset marker
("order-only change", which is always processed).

**** removed member, former member

A *removed member* is an occurrence with the change fact '(rel
removed)': its node survives, but its relationship to its viewparent
exists in the before-graph only. It carries a ghost warrant ('(view
ghostWarrant)') and, where its viewparent's claims are read, claims
nothing ('noClaim'), so a save leaves the relationship removed. It is
encoded so in content, in a partner folder and in a ghost's old
content alike. In content position it follows the editability rule
as any occurrence the refresh draws: editable at its node's first
occurrence in level order, write-protected at a later one. A *former
member* is any viewnode standing for such a before-only relationship,
ghosts included. The phrase, like "new member", names the node by its
membership's fate.

**** old text

The change viewnode ('(oldText ASPECT)') under a node whose title or
body changed since HEAD: labelled "old title and body", "old title" or
"old body", its headline is the old title and its body the old body.
When the body changed, a "body diff" change viewnode under it holds
the line diff. The text change is one change-forest leaf, marked on the old
text.

**** changed folder

A partner, alias, ID or flags folder the diff view shows because its
membership changed since HEAD. It is written as its bare folder atom,
holds the members of either graph, each tagged with the three kinds
of change, and reads "its X changed (this file)" or "(other files)"
(see "in, out") -- the forks folder, which holds both sides, reads
"its forks changed" -- with ✓ when every changed membership is processed: each
client sees the change in the members and draws that label in place
of the folder's own.

**** what a changed folder's member holds

Under each member of a changed folder hangs its containerward chain,
as under any folder's member, walked over both graphs of the
straddle, so that the user sees where the member sits; and before
the chain, where the member's relationship moved to another
skgrepo, that move.

**** moved-like-ancestor fact

In the skgrepo-changes buffer, a vognode whose home skgrepo moved
carries '(homeSkgrepoMove (old OLD) (new NEW))' in its '(diff ...)',
its herald "-OLD +NEW". When the nearest vognode ancestor whose home
skgrepo moved did so the same way, it also carries the *moved-like-ancestor fact*
'(movedLikeAncestor LETTER)', its herald "Δ⌂=a", where the letter
names that ancestor as an ancestor generation does (a, the
viewparent; b, the grandparent; ...).

**** removed alias, removed ID

An alias or extra ID of a node that HEAD has and the worktree lacks.
The diff view shows it in the node's alias or ID folder as an alias or
ID member with a ghost warrant, written 'alias noClaim (view
ghostWarrant)' or 'id (view ghostWarrant)', with '(diff (rel
removed))', where the LCS of the two lists puts it, with the '-'
herald. The save reads no alias from it, since it claims nothing; the
ID folder, whose claims the save never reads, carries no 'noClaim',
and the save's check of an ID folder refuses only one lacking an ID of
the node. Marking it marks the leaf of the removed alias or ID.

**** processed summary

The line a client shows after every save of a diff-view buffer, after the
"extend the diff view" command (C-c r u), and before a commit of
everything (C-c r C) that would take unprocessed changes: "12 of 14
changed nodes are processed (1 of them waits)", or "Commit ready. All
14 of 14 changed nodes are processed." It counts nodes, the unit a
commit takes. A changed node is processed when every change it records
is marked; a node whose only change is order is processed without
marks; a processed node waits when the commit of the processed (C-c r
c) would hold it back, because it needs a node that does not go in.
"Commit ready" means C-c r c would commit every changed node. The
summary also counts the changed nodes of foreign skgrepos and the
changes not editable in the view.

**** already-staged entry

An entry git's index holds when stage-and-commit runs, staged by hand
or by another tool. No commit takes it: a commit's tree is HEAD's
plus the committed nodes' files, and afterward only those paths' index
entries change, so the entry stays staged.

**** editable in the diff view

A buffer *makes a node's changes editable* when it holds an
editable occurrence of the node, wherever it sits, or the node's
ghost in content position
('nodes_editable_in_diffView'). The change-forest leaves those
nodes record are the changes editable in the buffer.

**** changes not editable in the view

The change-forest leaves recorded by the nodes whose changes a
buffer does not
[[*editable in the diff view][make editable]] are the *changes
not editable in the view*, which the processed summary counts. A
save never makes them editable; C-c r u extends the buffer to them,
as does the rerender of a clean buffer after a reload.

**** processed request, processed gesture

A *processed request* is a metadata form, '(processed OBJECTS)' or
'(unprocessed OBJECTS)' inside a headline's '(editRequest ...)', that the
save consumes, after its graph
edits succeed, to set or clear the marks of a headline's leaves. A
*processed gesture* is a gesture (C-c m, then n, r, b or h, then p or
u, and their recursive forms) that writes processed requests on one
or many headlines, then saves.

**** restore request

Either of the two edit requests that undo a removal in the diff
view. '(editRequest restoreNode)', on a ghost,
writes its node back as HEAD had it, in-links included;
'(editRequest restoreRel)', on a removed membership (a removed
member in content position, or a member of a partner folder whose
relationship to the folder's viewparent was removed), re-adds that
one relationship as HEAD recorded it. C-c s n ('skg-restore-deleted-node') and C-c s l
('skg-restore-removed-membership') write them, and a save applies
and consumes them as it does every edit request
('docs/diff-view.org', "Restoring what a change removed").

**** headline with change facts

A headline whose metadata carries a '(leaves ...)' change fact. It
is processed when its '(processed ...)' names every object its '(leaves ...)' lists,
and otherwise unprocessed. The navigation commands (C-c r n c and its
kin) move between headlines with change facts.

**** tag

To *tag* a headline of the diff view with its change is to give it
the change facts of its change: the refresh tags each member of a
list with the three kinds of change. Elsewhere "tag" has its
ordinary sense.

**** interleaved diff

The order in which a buffer of the diff view shows a list that
changed: the worktree's list, each member HEAD alone had placed
before the next member of HEAD's order that the worktree kept, or
last ('interleaved_diff_without_reorders'). A reorder alone shows
nothing.

**** context node

An occurrence the diff view draws only to show where a change sits:
its node is not among the changed nodes (not in C; see "C, R, U, D,
content origin"). The server draws a context node with its body
folded.

**** in, out, in-folder, out-folder, "(this file)", "(other files)"

A relationship is *out* from its recorder (the node whose file records
it) and *in* to its member. In the diff view, a folder of out-relationships
that changed, an *out-folder*, is labelled "its ... changed (this
file)", and one of in-relationships, an *in-folder*, "its ... changed
(other files)" ('docs/diff-view.org', "the in-folders and
out-folders"). The forks folder is neither: its members play either
side, so its label, "its forks changed", carries no suffix. Changeset
markers carry no suffix.

** Terms of the implementation
The words of the code, its comments and the technical docs. A user
almost certainly need not read this section.

*** update-relevant neighborhood

For a set of IDs in one graph snapshot, the *update-relevant
neighborhood* contains the IDs themselves, their immediate neighbors in
both directions along =contains=, =linksTo=, =forks=, and
=hides=, and every mirror of each ID, whatever any skgrepo
restriction shows (see "mirror"): a change to any relationship among a
note's mirrors can change which of them stands where another is
drawn. The mirrors are taken from the original IDs only, never from an
ordinary neighbor. Thus it includes no mixed ordinary/mirror path
and no two-hop ordinary path. Saves compute this
neighborhood independently in the before and after graph snapshots.
The collateral redraw of clean views uses the whole neighborhood;
the dirty-view conflict check uses it without =linksTo=, in both
directions ('update_relevant_neighborhood_over' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph/relation_accessors.rs').

*** vognode, non-vognode

A *vognode* is a viewnode standing for a gnid ('Vognode' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs'), one of three structs: an *occurrence*
('Occurrence'), of a graphnode the truth has; a *ghost* ('Ghost'),
in the diff view only, of a graphnode HEAD has and the worktree
lacks; and a gnid-only vognode ('GnidOnlyVognode', see "gnid-only
vognode"), an unknown viewnode or a restricted viewnode. An
occurrence of a restricted graphnode becomes a restricted viewnode
where its record is visible; where it is not, in a content view, it
vanishes (see "vanish"), and in the diff view it becomes a
tombstone.

The other viewnodes are *non-vognodes*: the *descriptors* -- folders,
property members and change viewnodes, which describe their
viewparent or its relationships rather than standing for a graphnode
-- the buffer root and tombstones.

*** gnid-only vognode

A *gnid-only vognode* (a term of plan 08; 'GnidOnlyVognode' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs') stands for a gnid and shows nothing of a
graphnode, for one of two reasons, its kind ('GnidOnlyKind'):
'Unknown', an unknown viewnode (no graphnode answers to the gnid), or
'Restricted', a restricted viewnode (the skgrepo restriction keeps
the graphnode out). The two take the same parts on the wire under
their own atoms, =unknown= and =restricted=; validation and the save
treat them alike, and the ID rule, the heralds and the parser's
refusal of text tell them apart. User text says "an unknown viewnode"
or "a restricted viewnode", never the type. The retired placeholder
'restrictedNode', which stood for nothing, is a different thing, and
the parser refuses its spelling.

*** open views, single-root

The server keeps, per connection, the views open in the client
('OpenViews' in 'server/types/views_state.rs'), each by its
viewforest id ('ViewforestId': a content view by its root's ID, a
search view by its query), with its viewforest, the IDs it shows and
its 'ViewnodeIdRecord'. A content view may have several roots; the
request that opens one today asks for a single root, so only the
request is "single-root".

*** recorder, carrier

A relationship is recorded in the .skg file of one of its two nodes, its
*recorder*: the node playing the relation's first role (the container,
the mentioner, the forker, the hider). The other node
is a member of the recorder's list. The relationship as the
recorder's file states it, in one of its sections, is a *record* (see
"record, restricted, visible").

The node that has an ID, as its PID or as an extra ID, is that ID's
*carrier*. (Not "owner": the user *owns* skgrepos.)

*** Folder, and its per-kind rules

How a folder (see "partner folder") is built. It is the
=ViewnodeKind::Folder= variant and the payload type =Folder=, one
enum for the eleven partner folders, the three property folders and
the illegal-edit folder.
“Folder” here describes an org non-vognode that groups viewnodes; it
does not mean a filesystem directory.  “Role col”, “relation col”,
and bare “sharing col” are retired names for the general concept.

Each folder is named by its external non-vognode atom. Methods on
=Folder= (=server/types/viewnode.rs=) give the per-kind rules that the
refresh, the save and the heralds consult, so a new kind cannot
acquire inconsistent treatment in different layers:
- =is_editable=: the save reads the folder's membership, so a member
  typed or removed there edits the graph. These are
  =forksFolder= (whose claiming members are the note's 'forks'; its
  forker-only and indirect members claim nothing, and deleting one
  hides it),
  =aliasFolder= and =hiddenOutsideOfForkPartnersFolder= (whose
  visible member set is an explicit edit of that subset of the hides;
  the hides' relRepos stay derived). An absent folder means "no opinion" (its field lowers to
  =MSV::Unspecified=, filled from disk); a present-but-empty one, an
  explicit empty set (=MSV::Specified(vec![])=). Every other folder is
  write-protected: its membership follows from the graph, and the
  refresh redraws it from the graph (see [[*write-protected set][write-protected set]]).
- =goal_is_ordered=: the viewparent's own file records the members'
  order, which the warranted members take: the editable folders,
  =hiddenFolder=, and =integratedElsewhereFolder= (in the order of the
  member's own list). In the forks folder the forkees take it, and its
  forker-only and indirect members follow them in the graph's and the
  config's order (see "the forks folder"); in the diff view's forks
  folder its forker-only members follow them in the order the view
  has ('Goal::ordered_then_unordered'). In every other folder the refresh
  moves no child: the inbound folders
  =hiderFolder=, =containerFolder= and =mentionerFolder= (the folders
  of =C-c l c= and =C-c l m=; the other side of each is the node's
  content, or its links, shown without a folder),
  =hiddenInForkPartnerFolder= and =hiddenByForkerFolder=
  (derived from hide state), the diff
  view's =acquiredByFolder= and =acquiredFolder= (derived from the
  graph straddle, and save-inert), and =idFolder= and =flagsFolder=.
- =members_are_properties=: the alias, ID and flags folders.
- =members_carry_containerward_chain= (asked with whether the view is
  the diff view): every folder but the property folders, the two
  acquisition folders and =integratedElsewhereFolder=, and outside the
  diff view but the forks folder; the members of these two carry
  their chain in a containers folder instead.

The ☮ herald on a write-protected folder's headline is a
herald-table fact only (=server/heralds.rs=), with no wire atom of
its own: which folders are write-protected is already known from the
folder atom. The editable folders carry none.

A folder left with no child is entombstoned, and so pruned, unless the
save reads its membership, so that the user could add to it, or it is
the flags folder, which stays to show that the node has no true flag
('folder_goes_when_empty' in 'server/refresh/visit.rs'). The folder of
what a note hides outside its fork partners is read by the save, but
goes when emptied, as it is drawn only when it holds something.

*** the containerward chain, as drawn

When the refresh draws a member of a folder whose members carry the
chain, it hangs the member's chain under it
('draw_containerward_chain' in
'server/refresh/chain.rs'); when it draws a member of a content
view's forks folder that is no repeat, it puts a containers folder
under it instead, and registers the folder so that the members its
own visit draws are drawn folded ('after_drawing' and
'add_containers_folder_under_member' in
'server/refresh/visit.rs', 'Walk::folders_whose_drawn_members_are_folded').
Since the member was just drawn, the folder is its first child: an
editable member's integrated-elsewhere folder, added at its visit,
goes before it, then its hidden-in folder, then a forker's hidden-by
folder; a forks folder, where one is asked for (=C-c l f=), and its
content go after it. A member that stood
in the buffer before gets the same folder at its visit
('add_containers_folder_to_a_standing_member'): in a forks folder,
when an editable-view request extends it (=C-c s e= on its note); in
an integrated-elsewhere folder, whenever the folder would name a
keeper, so that a member whose only keeper was a view-ancestor, given
another by a save, names it as a fresh view does. A member drawn into
a compound folder or a containers folder that already stood, all its
members folded, is drawn folded too, so that the folder stays
collapsed ('stands_drawn_folded'). The chain is drawn
walking the member's
containers breadth-first over the live graph or, in the diff view,
over the union of the straddle. The extent of a chain is none, so a
chain node is never reinstated: a chain that stays keeps its
viewnodes and their fold state, and only a newly drawn member gets
one. A chain node is known by where it stands:
'holds_containerward_chain' says whether a viewnode's vognode
children are chain nodes (it is a write-protected member of such a
folder, or itself a chain node), and 'stands_in_containerward_chain'
whether a viewnode is one ('server/types/tree/forest.rs'). Both take
whether the view is the diff view, since a forks folder's member
holds a chain there and nowhere else; the readers that serve only
the diff view (its change tags, its processed and restore leaves,
the parse's check of a restoreRel request) pass true. Its goal
is its containers, so a chain node that no longer contains the node
above it is unwarranted (⊥).

*** the refresh

The one operation that takes a buffer, parsed and validated, and the
truth, and brings the buffer up to date with the truth, in place
('refresh_viewforest' in 'server/refresh/mod.rs'). Every view is drawn
by it: a fresh content view (the refresh of a stub), every rerender
(the saved view, the collateral views, "rerender all views", a
switch of the skgrepo restriction), the search enrichment and the diff view's
buffers. It decides everything from the buffer and the truth as they
are now, and is a *fixpoint*: refreshing its own output changes
nothing, up to the viewnode IDs it assigns
('tests/idempotence.rs'). 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The
refresh: how a view follows the graph", states the whole model.

A view that does not exist yet is drawn from a *stub*, a viewforest
of little more than its roots, which the refresh fills: for a fresh
content view, one editable occurrence per requested node, asking for
its containers folder and its extension ('stub_of_fresh_content_view'
in 'server/update_buffer.rs'); for a search, the hits
('build_search_viewforest', which 'draw_search_buffer' refreshes, in
'server/serve/handlers/text_search.rs'); for a buffer of the diff
view, one *stub
root* per member of D ('docs/diff-view.org', "Stub roots").

The refresh walks the viewforest, visiting each parent once and
applying its rungs there (see "the refresh's rungs, unsayable"); its
*tail* is what it does once, after the walk ('server/refresh/tail.rs'):
the *stats pass*, which computes every occurrence's derived facts
('viewnodestats.rs', 'graphnodestats.rs' beside it); in the diff
view, the '(processed ...)' change facts and the home skgrepo moves; the
pruning of childless tombstones, and of folders the pruning empties;
and a viewnode ID for each viewnode lacking one. The tail renders
nothing: each caller renders the viewforest once, when it is done
with it.

*** the truth

What the refresh compares a buffer with ('Truth' in
'server/refresh/mod.rs'): the gated graph, the in-Rust graph as the
skgrepo restriction lets it be seen (see "skgrepo, home skgrepo,
skgrepo-set, gitrepo"), with the config. The in-Rust graph itself,
read from each skgrepo's worktree and kept current by every save, is
the *live graph*; the diff view compares it, as its after-graph,
with HEAD. For a gnid it answers
'Present', 'Restricted' or 'Absent' ('GnidPresence',
'gnid_presence_gated'). In the diff view the mode adds HEAD, through
the graph straddle.

*** mode, 'RefreshMode', 'RerenderMode'

Which kind of view a refresh refreshes ('RefreshMode' in
'server/refresh/mode.rs', a term of plan 06): 'ContentView', or
'DiffView', which carries three parts, the graph straddle, the buffer
kind and the processed store. It
decides whether a parent's goal includes what HEAD alone warrants,
whether ghosts and change viewnodes are drawn, and what the node
budget may refuse.

A rerender pass is told less: 'RerenderMode' (a term of plan 08, in
'server/rerender_pass.rs') is 'ContentView' or 'DiffView' with the
buffer kind, built from the view being rerendered; the rerender of a
buffer of the diff view adds the graph straddle the pass shares
across the views it rerenders ('RerenderPassContext' in
'server/rerender_pass.rs') and the processed store, making the
refresh's mode.

*** gnid, pid, extra ID

A *gnid* is a graphnode's ID (the type 'ID'): a node's primary gnid is
its *pid*, the others its *extra IDs*, acquired by nodeMerges; each of
them resolves to the node. On the wire it is =(gnid ID)=. A viewnode
ID ("vnid") names a viewnode, not a node.

*** the ID rule

What a headline standing for a gnid becomes, by five questions in
order, the first yes winning ('id_rule' in 'server/refresh/id_rule.rs'):
does the truth have the node (an occurrence); does the truth say the
node is restricted (a restricted viewnode where its viewparent's goal
names it or the headline is a view root, and otherwise a tombstone,
never a ghost); in the diff
view, does HEAD (a ghost); does its viewparent's list name the gnid
(an unknown viewnode); else a tombstone. The
refresh *re-kinds* a headline by it where it stands, keeping its
viewnode ID, fold state, children and, for an occurrence, its
editability; but an occurrence the rule makes a tombstone in a
content view vanishes, with its subtree (see "vanish").

*** goal

What the truth says a parent's children should be ('goal_of_parent'
in 'server/refresh/goal.rs'): an occurrence's =contains= list, a
folder's partners in its relation (or a compound folder's derived
list), a property folder's aliases, IDs or true flags, a chain node's
containers; in the diff view, the union of the parent's lists in the
two graphs of the straddle. Each *goal item* names a gnid or a
property value ('GoalItem', 'GoalMember'). The goal decides each
child's warrant, what is reinstated and, where it is ordered, the
children's order. A parent whose position implies no relationship
(the buffer root, a tombstone, an unknown viewnode, a restricted
viewnode) has none. Under a skgrepo restriction a goal omits the members whose
records are restricted and keeps the others, a restricted node's
too, which the ID rule makes a restricted viewnode.

*** the refresh's rungs, unsayable

The fixed order of *rungs* (a term of plan 06) the refresh applies at
each parent to its children, weakest first: re-kind, viewnode merge, warrant,
claims, reinstate, reorder, mirror substitution, view requests
('server/refresh/visit.rs'; 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The
walk and the refresh's rungs"). A descriptor -- a folder, a property member, a
change viewnode -- whose ancestors are not those the *ancestry table*
requires of its kind ('required_ancestry' in
'server/refresh/ancestry.rs', the table of the ancestors each kind of
descriptor may stand under, nearest first;
'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The ancestry table") is
*unsayable* there, and is
entombstoned, its children staying under the tombstone. An occurrence
is never unsayable. (Older text calls the rungs the ladder; they are not the privacy
ladder, the skgrepos in privacy order.)

*** viewnode merge, duplicate viewnode siblings

*Duplicate viewnode siblings* are siblings standing for one gnid
under one parent. The refresh's second rung ('viewnode_merge') makes
each set of them one viewnode, by a *viewnode merge*. The result
stands where the first of them stood and has a fresh viewnode ID.
It is folded only if every sibling was, and its body is folded only
if every sibling's was; it is focused if any sibling was; it is
editable if any sibling was, unless it stands for another node (a
node and the mirror shown for it stand for one gnid) whose editable
occurrence is elsewhere in the buffer; it claims membership if any
sibling did. It carries the union of the siblings' view requests,
and the edit request and relationship request of whichever sibling
carried one. It holds every sibling's children in sibling order;
at its own visit the claims rung makes those vanish that claim and
that the graph does not warrant there. So a copy pasted beside a
member is joined to it. A viewnode merge is neither a nodeMerge,
which joins two graphnodes, nor an instructionMerge, and is never
called a bare "merge". Both terms are coined by plan 06 ("tidy the
refresh").

*** extent, extension, node budget

The *extent* is how much of a parent's goal the refresh draws
('server/refresh/extent.rs'): all of it for a folder and for an
editable occurrence's content (in the diff view, also for a ghost's
old content where content stands); none of it, beyond what the buffer
already shows, for a write-protected occurrence's content and for the
containerward chain. An *extension* is one editable occurrence's
content drawn whole; the *node budget* ('initial_node_limit') counts
them, each charging it once at its visit, and once it is spent the
occurrences the refresh draws are write-protected (they carry an
editable-view request their visit consumes,
'consume_editableView_request'). A folder fills its members without spending any, but a forkee as
such it draws is an editable occurrence, which spends one at its
visit, in level order with content. The
module comment of 'server/refresh/extent.rs' states the extent, and
that of 'server/refresh/editability.rs' the node budget. The
server *renders* a viewforest when it produces its org text
('server/org_to_text.rs').

*** editability rule

Which occurrence of a node is editable: at most one per node in a
buffer. One the buffer holds keeps its editability; an editable-view
request moves it, write-protecting the node's other occurrence; among
the occurrences the refresh draws, the first of a node is editable
when its parent's children are editable content, or it is a member
as such in a content view (a member of the forks folder of an
editable occurrence, a forkee or forker as such), no other occurrence
of the node is, and the budget allows at its visit
('drawn_occurrence_editability', 'consume_editableView_request'), and
a repeat is write-protected. Level order decides which occurrence of
a node that is both a member as such and content elsewhere in the
view is editable; =C-c s e= moves it. The editable occurrence *holds* its
node's editability: the walk keeps where it stands, and an occurrence
write-protected, re-kinded or merged away releases it
('Walk::hold_editability', 'server/refresh/editability.rs').

A *repeat* is an occurrence of a node whose editability another
occurrence in the buffer holds ('Walk::is_a_repeat', keyed as
'Walk::editability_key' keys it). (It is not the containerward
chain's 'ContainerwardRoleTree::Repeated', a container met again on
the walk toward the roots.) Whether a drawn occurrence is one
is judged where it is drawn, in level order, so an occurrence made
editable later in the same refresh does not make an earlier-drawn one
a repeat. A repeat in the forks folder shows nothing, not even its
containers folder.
The diff view's deviations are in 'docs/diff-view.org', "Editability
in the diff view".

*** IdResolution

How the parse reads the gnids a buffer writes ('IdResolution' in
'server/from_text/buffer_to_viewnodes/add_missing_info.rs'):
'ThroughGraph' resolves each through the graph, so an occurrence's
gnid becomes its node's pid and, where the parse supplies a node's
home skgrepo, it reads the graph's; 'AsWritten' keeps every gnid as
written and consults no graph, for a caller that reads only what the
text says (the dirty-view dependency scan, tests of fully written
text).

*** save-inert

A viewnode is *save-inert* if it emits no fieldIntents: tombstones,
change viewnodes, the diff view's acquisition folders, a ghost that
claims nothing, and an alias or ID member that claims nothing (a
removed alias or ID among them). Its descendants may still emit them, so save
extraction still visits them.

*** MSV = maybe specified vector

=MSV<T>= distinguishes "no opinion" from "empty". =Unspecified= says a
file section or an edit has nothing to say about the field;
=Specified(vec![])= says the field is explicitly empty. Telescope
folding and partial nodeIntents both depend on the difference.

*** composition (visible vs full), decomposition

The *composition* of a telescope combines its sections, most public
first, into one effective node (=server/telescope/compose.rs=). The
*full composition* uses every section; a *visible composition* uses only the
sections unrestricted under the current skgrepo restriction, and is what a
restricted view renders. *Decomposition* is the save-side inverse:
splitting the effective node back into sections, byte-stably.

*** placement, more public anchor

How a more private section orders its members into a more public
list without the public file recording anything: in the section's
stored sequence, a =- anchor: ID= entry names a *more public anchor*,
a member of the more public list, and the members after it insert
immediately after that ID; members before the first anchor prepend.
Placements exist only on disk — more public anchors are derived at
save from the in-memory order, so they can never go stale in memory.
Say "more public anchor", never a bare "anchor".

*** col

*Col* was the old name for a folder viewnode (short for
"collection"). It has been expunged from production code, but may still
appear in old documentation, plans, or the knowledge graph.

*** view-lifetime

Some data regarding a node is view-lifetime data
(viewStats, for instance):
it lasts only as long as the view of it in some buffer.

Data on disk, by contrast,
remains when the app terminates,
and can be recovered by a later session.
*Session-lifetime* data lasts as long as one server session: the
in-Rust graph and the search index, together the *session-lifetime
stores*. The server rebuilds them from the .skg files when it starts,
or when asked ('skg-rebuild-session-lifetime-stores'); they outlive
every view. Say which lifetime is meant, never "ephemeral".

*** branching viewnode (vs a viewbranch)

A *branching viewnode* is a viewnode with more than one viewchild. Do
not confuse it with a *viewbranch* (see "view, buffer, viewforest"):
any viewnode together with its descendants, which may be degenerate,
its root having one viewchild or none.

None of this is related to forking a node (see "clone, and the fork
operation").

*** fieldIntent, nodeIntent, nodeInstruction, savePlan

What a save turns the buffer into, in order:
- a *fieldIntent* ('FieldIntent') says what one position in the buffer
  wants for one field of one node (its title and body, its contains,
  a hide, ...). Local fieldIntent collection emits these into a map
  keyed by ID ('CollectedFieldIntents').
- a *nodeIntent* ('NodeIntent') is all of one node's fieldIntents,
  lowered into a save or a delete. It may still be partial: fields
  the buffer said nothing about are 'MSV::Unspecified'.
- a *nodeInstruction* ('NodeInstruction': 'Save' or 'Delete') is a
  nodeIntent after disk supplementation: complete, ready to write.
- the *savePlan* ('SavePlan') is everything one save applies: its
  nodeInstructions, plus fork operations and nodeMerges.

*** the nodeInstructions phase, the nodeMerge phase

A save applies its savePlan in two phases, both prepared, files
included, before either is applied ('prepare_save_under_mutation_gate'
and 'PreparedSave::apply' in 'server/save.rs'). *The nodeInstructions
phase* prepares and applies the save's ordinary nodeInstructions:
those the buffer yields and those the save derives (delete
propagation, strandeds resets, Hoist repairs, restore requests), not
the ones a nodeMerge yields; its candidate graph is
'graph_after_save'. *The
nodeMerge phase* then builds the NodeMerge triples from that
candidate and prepares and applies their nodeInstructions too, as a
second graph update over it ('final_candidate'). The first phase's
name means the save's ordinary nodeInstructions, then, not that the
other phase applies none.

*** local fieldIntent collection

The save path's fieldIntent extraction: one pure DFS preorder over
the buffer's viewforest, in which each position reads only itself and
its direct children, plus a context that flows down from its
ancestors. Each visit emits fieldIntents into a map keyed by
node ID; downstream stages (validation, lowering, visibility
resolution, disk supplementation, the noop filter) consume the map.
Lives in =server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/=, entered
via =extract_nonmergeSavePlan_locally=.

This entry is the canonical sketch of the save pipeline. Its
surrounding stages are, in order: parse (which supplies each new
headline's gnid and skgrepo; see "IdResolution"), validate, place,
extract (this entry), foreign-node validation, nodeMerge build, graph
update, and the refresh of the saved and collateral views.

*** instructionMerge ("merge" is always qualified)

An /instructionMerge/ is the combining of emitted save intents into
the map during local fieldIntent collection. It shares nothing with
a nodeMerge (the =NodeMerge= type) but the word.

*** save envelope

The /save envelope/ is the length-prefixed S-expression a client
sends right after the "save buffer" request line. It carries
'saved-buffer' (the buffer's text, with the client's focus and fold
metadata added), 'baseline' (the same buffer's text as the server
last drew it, with that metadata removed; see "baseline, stale, edited, unedited") and
'other-views' (every other open view of the connection, each clean
or dirty with its baseline and current text). The server parses it
into 'SaveRequestEnvelope' ('server/serve/handlers/save_buffer.rs');
'docs/api-and-formats.org' documents it under "Save buffer". An
envelope missing a field, or carrying an unknown one, is refused
under the "NOTHING WAS SAVED" header, and nothing is written.

*** collateral view

When a save changes nodes that other open views show, those views are
*collateral*: the server rerenders them and streams each as a
=collateral-view= message. The save envelope's =other-views= lists every
other open view; the collateral views are the subset that needs a
rerender.

*** startup warnings channel

How the server's startup warnings reach the user: 'load_config'
returns warnings beside the config, 'main' records them once on the
runtime ('record_startup_warnings' in 'server/types/env.rs'), the
server serves them on request ("server warnings"), and each client
appends them on connect to its warnings buffer ('*SKG Server
Warnings*' in Emacs, 'skg://messages/server-warnings' in Neovim),
under a headline "Startup warnings", erasing nothing the buffer
holds.

*** hot reload

The server rebuilt and restarted by cargo-watch on every edit under
'server/'. It is on when 'bash/start-servers.sh' is given
=--hot-reload=, and off by default, when an edit under 'server/' takes
effect only once that script is run again. Not to be confused with
the server rereading changed '.skg' files ('server/repo_watch/'), nor
with the Emacs client's 'skg-reload'.

*** run folder

'CONFIG.running/', beside a config file whose path is CONFIG
('data/jeff/skgconfig.toml.running/' for 'data/jeff/skgconfig.toml'), where a
server's run-time files go. 'bash/start-servers.sh' creates it and
writes there 'stop-servers.sh', which stops that start (one run of
the script), and one 'start-servers.START.log' per start, the output
of the rerun loop (or of cargo-watch, with hot reload). The server
writes its logs into a subfolder, 'START/', one per server run (one
process of the server, from launch to exit). Both STARTs are UTC times
('YYYY-MM-DDTHH-MM-SSZ'), but not the same time: in the script log's
name it is when the start script began, in the subfolder's when that
server process began. So they usually differ by a few seconds, and a
start whose rerun loop (or cargo-watch) reruns the server has one
script log and several subfolders. Two servers on two configs over
one data root share no log and no stop script.
'docs/api-and-formats.org' says more under "Log files".

*** "snapshot" is always qualified: graph, git or buffer snapshot

A /graph snapshot/ is one immutable state of the in-Rust graph
('GraphSnapshot'). A /git snapshot/ is one of HEAD, the git index or
the worktree; 'server/git_ops/git_snapshot.rs' reads HEAD and the
worktree (the graph straddle's before-graph reads HEAD). A /buffer snapshot/ is a
buffer's text as a client sends it, for a dirty view in the save
envelope.

*** "index" is always qualified: search, git or graph index

The /search index/ is Tantivy's. The /git index/ is git's staging
area; next to HEAD and worktree it needs no qualifier ("HEAD vs
index"). A /graph index/ is a derived map inside the in-Rust graph:
the /recorderward relmaps/ ('contained_by', 'forkers_of', ...), plus
'extra_id_to_pid'. A recorderward relmap maps each member of one
relation to the recorders that list it. (Not "inverse": today every
relationship is recorded by its first member, but the data model may
someday let the second member record one; 'RecorderwardRelmap' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph.rs' says more.)

*** "commit" is qualified when ambiguous: git-, skgsave- or tantivy-commit

A /git-commit/ is git's. A /skgsave-commit/ is the step of a save that
writes the .skg files and swaps in the new graph; a save that stops to
ask for confirmation (fork operations, Hoist, strandeds, delete-from)
skgsave-commits nothing. A
/tantivy-commit/ makes search-index writes visible; a save's happens
later, on a background worker. Where context leaves no doubt (HEAD, a
merge commit, a 'git2' call) bare "commit" means a git-commit.

*** swap in (a runtime generation), vs publish

A save, rebuild or import ends by atomically *swapping in* a new
runtime generation (config, graph and search index): readers holding
the old one keep it, and the next reader gets the new one. "Publish"
means only making text more public: sharing a skgrepo, or writing text
into a more public telescope section. Hoist /moves/ text to home.

*** hiderel = "hides" relationship

A relationship of the relation =hides=, which a '.skg' file
records under the key 'hides': the hider keeps the hidden node out of
what it shows of every member of its forks folder (see "relation, relationship,
member, membership, partner"). See [[file:./data-model_technical.org][the technical data model]] for the hiding
relationship.

Sometimes an API response is prefixed with a length, letting the receiver know how many characters to expect in the response.

In Haskell, 'm' is sometimes prefixed to a word to indicate 'maybe'.

*** Terms of the diff view's implementation

How the diff view ('docs/diff-view.org') is computed.

**** buffer kind

Which of the four buffers of the diff view a buffer is
('DiffViewBufferKind'). A buffer of the diff view is refreshed over
the graph straddle; the straddle, the buffer kind and the processed
store are the three parts of the refresh's diff-view mode
('RefreshMode::DiffView').

**** graph straddle, before-graph, after-graph

The *graph straddle* ('GraphStraddle { before, after }',
'server/graph_straddle/') is what the diff view compares. A straddle stands
with one foot in the committed graph and one in the live graph. The
*before-graph* is an in-Rust graph built from each owned skgrepo at
HEAD (each foreign skgrepo at its worktree); the *after-graph* is the
live graph.

**** union walk, existence across the straddle

The *union walk* is the API over the graph straddle that answers
questions about the union of its two graphs
('server/graph_straddle/union_walk.rs'). Each node and each
relationship it reports carries its existence across the straddle
('existence_across_straddle'): 'Existence::Added' when only the
after-graph holds it, 'Existence::Removed' when only the before-graph
does, and none when both do. A view shows it as
[[*the three kinds of change][the three kinds of change]].

**** fingerprint, the processed store

Marks are stored in the server's processed marks file
('<data_root>/processed-marks-CONFIGSTEM.json' unless the config names
another; 'docs/api-and-formats.org', "Processed marks file"), each with a
*fingerprint* of the change it approved. A mark whose fingerprint no
longer matches the change counts as absent, which is how editing a
marked change unmarks it.

**** content position

A viewnode is in *content position* when its viewparent is the buffer
root, an occurrence, or a ghost that is itself in content position
('in_content_position'). A ghost in
content position holds its old content under it.

*** Terms of Tantivy, the search index

Tantivy is the search engine that holds the search index.

**** prominence source, prominence multiplier

A node's *prominence source* ('ProminenceSource') says why it is
prominent (see "prominent"). Each source has a *prominence
multiplier* applied to the text-match score.

**** Document

A Tantivy association. In my case, from a title or alias to an ID.
(Each ID can have multiple such associations.)
