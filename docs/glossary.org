* Glossary
The words Skg uses, in two parts: the terms a user meets, and the
terms of the implementation. Links inside it name headlines.

** Terms a user meets
What the clients show, what the heralds and the messages say, and
the words of the user docs ('docs/COMMANDS.org',
'docs/COMMANDS-nvim.org', 'docs/heralds.org').

*** graphnode, viewnode

A *graphnode* is a node of the graph, which a =.skg= file records; a
*viewnode* is a node of a view, which a buffer shows as a headline.
A graphnode can appear in one view as several viewnodes, or as none,
and some viewnodes stand for no graphnode: a folder, for instance,
names a relationship between its viewparent and its children. Say
"graphnode" and "viewnode" rather than "graph node" or "view node";
bare "node" is fine where only one kind is possible. The
implementation sorts viewnodes by whether they stand for a graphnode
(see "vognode, non-vognode").

*** "child" and "parent"

They are shorthand for 'viewchild' and 'viewparent' (likewise
viewancestor, viewdescendant). Code may use the short forms, since it
almost always handles one tree at a time. Docs should spell out the
long forms wherever a reader might mistake a child for content, which
in user-facing docs is almost everywhere.

There are two concepts of node in skg: graphnode and viewnode.
Viewnodes are nodes in a view, and some of them correspond to nodes in the graph,
but some of them don't -- they describe their parent,
or the relationship between their parent and their children,
or maybe something else.

The Skg graph contains a few (currently five) formal relationships -- =contains=,
=linksTo=, etc. In the graph, there is no concept of parent or child;
those are only meaningful in a view.
Where the code says "child" or "parent", it means the relationship between viewnodes
-- e.g., in Emacs, the relationship between headlines in an Org buffer
-- and might or might not correspond to a graph relation.

For one example, see the discussion of alias nodes in
[[file:../coding-advice/architecture.md][the architecture documentation]].

*** title != headline

Headline is a term from org-mode. It refers to a line that begins with some asterisks, followed by at least one whitespace, followed by at least one non-whitespace. The text after the first chunk of adjacent whitespace is what I'm calling the 'title'.

*** view, buffer, viewforest

A *view* is one open window onto the graph: a content view, which
shows nodes and what they contain, or a search view. A *buffer* is
the editor's container for one view (an Emacs or Neovim buffer). A
view's *viewforest* is its tree of viewnodes, under a buffer root;
say "viewforest" rather than "viewtree" or "forest". The buffer as a
whole is named by its viewforest id (see "viewnode ID, vnid,
viewforest id").

A *viewbranch* is any subtree of a viewforest: a viewnode with all its
descendants. A view request asks the server to add viewbranches: a
folder (with its members' chains), the flags folder. A
viewbranch may be degenerate,
with one viewchild or none; compare "branching viewnode".

*** viewnode ID, vnid, viewforest id

A *viewnode ID* identifies one viewnode within one buffer, for the
life of the buffer: the atom =(vnid X)=, X one or more lowercase
letters and digits, that begins every viewnode's metadata
('docs/api-and-formats.org', "The metadata headers"). "vnid" is the
wire's word; prose and messages say "viewnode ID", and code
'ViewnodeId' and 'viewnode_id'. A message names a headline by its
viewnode ID, which =C-c g v= finds (see "how a message names a
headline"). The view gives one to each viewnode that lacks one at
the end of a refresh and never reuses one, so a viewnode ID that
reappears after a cut and paste names the same viewnode moved. A
parsed headline carrying a viewnode ID the view does not know (typed
by hand, or brought back by an old copy of the buffer's text) loses
it and gets a fresh one. A skgrepo-set switch keeps the viewnode ID
of everything the restriction does not hide. The view's
'ViewnodeIdRecord' ('server/types/views_state.rs') holds the counter
and the viewnode IDs of the forest as last rendered.

A *viewforest id* names a whole buffer, not one viewnode:
'ViewforestId' in code, the key =view-id= on the wire, and "view ID"
in the clients' prose (see "view, buffer, viewforest").

*** how a message names a headline

A message from the server -- an error, a warning, a refused save --
names each headline it concerns as an org subheadline of its own,
below the message's own lines: the headline's title, or
'(untitled)' (a folder, say); then '- graphnode ID: X' when the
headline carries one; then '- viewnode ID: X. To find it: C-c g v X'
(in Neovim, '<localleader>gv X'), or '- not yet saved' for a
headline typed or copied since the last render, which has none.
=C-c g v= ('skg-goto-viewnode-id') puts point on the headline with
that viewnode ID and opens the folds around it. In code it is
'HeadlineName' ('server/types/errors.rs'); see
'docs/api-and-formats.org', "How a message names a headline".

*** metadata

Most headlines are prefixed with some metadata.
In Emacs, that metadata is represented as an s-expression like
(skg (vnid X) KIND (key value) bare_value ...):
its viewnode ID, the position atoms, the headline's kind, then any
number of key-value and bare-value parts in any order
('docs/api-and-formats.org', "The metadata headers").
The metadata is used for Rust to know what to do with it,
and for the client to let the user know how to interpret it.
It is not abbreviated "md", which means Markdown (as in
=import-md-and-org=).

*** declared parts, derived facts

Metadata holds two classes of thing ('docs/api-and-formats.org',
"The metadata headers"). The *declared parts* are what the client is
responsible for and the save or the refresh reads: the kind, the
viewnode ID, the position atoms =focused=, =folded= and =bodyFolded=
('FoldAndFocus' in code), =(gnid ID)=, =(skgRepos ...)=, =noClaim=,
=writeProtected=, =(overridesHere N)=, =(viewRequests ...)= and
=(editRequest ...)=. A headline whose metadata names no kind is a
node. The *derived facts* are what the server computes at every
rendering and nobody reads back: everything in =(view ...)= (the
relationship facts, the warrant, =cycle=, =repoBoundary=,
=omittedBody=) and in =(diff ...)= (the change facts). The parser
skips both forms whole, so editing them changes nothing. The one
load-bearing part of a substitution, the overridesHere part
=(overridesHere N)=, is declared; see "override substitution".

*** view request vs edit request

Both are requests the client writes into metadata and the server
consumes. A *view request* (=(viewRequests ...)=: =folder=,
=flags=, =editableView=, =writeProtectedView=,
=writeProtectedSubtreeView=) asks for more of the view
(viewbranches) or changes which occurrence of a node is editable; the
refresh fulfills it and drops it.
(=fork= is also written as a view request, but the save consumes
it.) An *edit request* (=(editRequest ...)=: =delete=, =merge=, =flag=,
=relRepo=, and in the diff view =restoreRel= on a removed membership
and =restoreNode= on a ghost) asks the save to change the graph; a
successful save consumes it, and a failed save leaves it in the
buffer. The two restore requests are described in
'docs/diff-view.org', "Restoring what a change removed". A
*readiness request* (see "readiness request, readiness gesture") is a
third kind, written in the same form: =(editRequest ...)= holds at
most one edit request and one readiness request. The save consumes a
readiness request after its graph edits succeed, to mark or unmark
changes commit-ready, and it changes neither the view nor the graph.
In code a node's own edit request is a 'NodeEditRequest', kept in
'Editability::Editable'; a request about a headline's relationship to
its viewparent (=relRepo=, =restoreRel=), which an occurrence, an
unknown or an alias may carry, is a 'RelationshipEditRequest'; a
readiness request is the viewnode's 'ReadinessRequest'.

*** gesture

A *gesture* is a client command that writes requests into headline
metadata for the server to apply at a save. The relRepo gesture
(=skg-set-relRepo=), the flag gesture (=skg-set-flag-search-matching=),
the fork gesture (=skg-fork-node=) and the readiness gestures are
kinds. The fork and readiness gestures save at once; the others leave
the save to the user.

*** occurrence

One appearance of a graphnode in a view: the vognode standing for a
node the truth has ('Vognode::Occurrence', the struct 'Occurrence' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs'). A graphnode can occur several times in
one buffer, at most once editable. Facts that depend on the position
(=cycle=, the warrant, the relRepo in =(skgRepos ...)=,
=(overridesHere N)=, and the =(birth ...)= facts, which name the
relationship to the viewparent) belong to the occurrence; graphStats
('GraphnodeStats') belong to the graphnode.

*** editable, write-protected

Each occurrence is either *editable* or
*write-protected* ('Editability' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'). Edits to
an editable occurrence's title, body and children are saved; a
write-protected occurrence shows no body and saves nothing of its own,
so a save is refused if one differs from the graph in its title,
skgrepo, aliases (one lacked, reordered, or with a relRepo request) or
flags. No child of a write-protected occurrence claims membership,
whatever it carries: one the user types there is a note, which the
refresh shows unwarranted. A buffer may hold at most one editable occurrence of a node,
so that two occurrences cannot contradict each other. Make one
editable with =skg-set-editable= (=C-c s d=), which writes a
=editableView= view request; make one write-protected with
=skg-request-write-protected= (=C-c s w=; =C-c s W= for its whole
content subtree), which writes a =writeProtectedView= or
=writeProtectedSubtreeView= view request.

*** relation, relationship, member, membership, partner

As in TypeDB, a *relation* is a kind of relationship: =contains=,
=linksTo=, =subscribesTo=, =hidesFromSubs=, =overrides=. Each names
its two roles: container contains content, mentioner linksTo
mentioned, subscriber subscribesTo subscribee, overrider overrides
overridden, hider hidesFromSubs hidden. =hidesFromSubs= is short for
"hides from its subscriptions": the hider hides the hidden node from
what the hider sees through its subscriptions. A *relationship* is one instance of a relation: this
node contains that one. (Graph theory would call it an edge; we don't,
because the word is opaque to newcomers.)

Either end of a relationship is the other's *partner*. One of the
two records the relationship in its file (see "recorder, carrier");
the other is a *member* of the recorder's list. In a view, the
children of an occurrence or a folder are its *members* too, those
of one monomorphic list: an occurrence's content, or a folder's
partners on one side of some relation with the folder's viewparent.
A property folder's members are its *property members*: aliases, IDs
or flags ('PropertyMember' in code).

A *membership* is the relationship that causes a node to be drawn
as a member of a list or folder; the member is the node.

*** claims membership, 'noClaim', pending detachment, detach, attach

A child *claims membership* when the save will read it as a member
of its viewparent's list. That is a declared part: the bare atom
=noClaim= says no, its absence yes ('claimsMembership' in code, on an
occurrence, a ghost, an unknown, an alias or an ID;
'docs/api-and-formats.org', "Content view"). It means something
only where the save reads the claims of its viewparent's children:
under an editable occurrence, and under an editable folder
(subscribee, overridden, hidden-outside-of-subscribee, alias) of one
('parent_reads_claims' in 'server/refresh/ancestry.rs'). Under any
other viewparent -- a write-protected occurrence, a write-protected
folder, a tombstone, the buffer root -- no child claims, whatever it
carries, and the server writes no =noClaim= there. A child that
claims nothing is in no list: the save neither adds nor removes a
relationship for it, and what it shows of its own node is saved or
not by its editability, as at any occurrence. So a headline typed
under a viewparent that reads no claims is saved as an *orphan*, a
node that no list contains (a term this glossary takes from
'docs/COMMANDS.org'), and the save warns of each one, naming its
headline ('orphan_warnings' in
'server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/mod.rs').

Under an editable parent the refresh marks =noClaim= on every child
it keeps that the graph does not relate to its viewparent (see
"warranted, 'noWarrant', 'ghostWarrant'"): one whose relationship a
save in another view removed, a removed member in the diff view, a
child a viewnode merge moved there. A child the graph does relate
there and that claims nothing is one the user marked =noClaim= to
detach it: a *pending detachment*, herald "detach", which the next
save takes out of the list. 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The
refresh: how a view follows the graph", says how claims and warrants
move.

Under a viewparent that reads claims, to *detach* a child is to write
=noClaim= on it, so that the next save takes it out of the
viewparent's list, and to *attach* it is to remove the atom, so that
the next save puts it in: two gestures on the declared part, no
request ('skg-detach' and 'skg-attach', C-c s t and C-c s a;
':SkgDetach' and ':SkgAttach' in Neovim). Both refuse under a
viewparent that reads no claims. "Detach" says what the user does to
a claim, never what the refresh does to the tree: the refresh never
removes a child.

*** warranted, 'noWarrant', 'ghostWarrant'

A child *is warranted* when the truth relates it to its viewparent as
its position implies: content its occurrence contains, a partner of
the folder's relation in a folder, a container of the node above it
in the containerward chain, a property of the node in a property
folder. In the diff view HEAD alone may warrant it: a *ghost warrant*,
for a relationship HEAD has and the worktree lacks (see "removed
member, former member"), or for a ghost under a parent that contained
it at HEAD. The warrant is a derived fact, recomputed at every
refresh, and written in =(view ...)= as =noWarrant= (herald ⊥) or
=ghostWarrant= (herald ᗣ), nothing meaning warranted by the truth; a
root carries none, and a ghost no =ghostWarrant=, its kind being its
own herald ('Warrant { ByGraph, ByHead, No }', 'warrant' in
code). 'Warrant::ByGraph' is the truth's warrant (see "the truth"):
the graph as the skgrepo restriction lets it be seen; a root, whose
position implies no relationship, carries it too, and the wire
writes nothing for it. Claim and warrant are independent: a note the user puts under
a write-protected parent is unwarranted and carries no =noClaim=,
since nothing claims there. The refresh keeps an unwarranted child
where it stands; it never removes a child.

*** stands as a member

An occurrence *stands as a member* of its viewparent when the
relationship its position implies is one its derived facts may
explain: its birth heralds and its relRepo. Where the viewparent
reads claims (see "claims membership"), that is its claim; at the
top of the buffer it always does; in a folder that reads no claims
(a container folder, say, or any folder of a write-protected
occurrence), it does when the truth warrants it;
anywhere else (under a write-protected occurrence, say) it does not
('stands_as_member' in 'server/refresh/viewnodestats.rs').
It is a third notion beside the claim and the warrant, since it is
neither: a warranted member of a write-protected folder claims
nothing but stands as a member, and a warranted child of an editable
occurrence that the user marked =noClaim= stands as none. It is not
written; the stats pass computes it where it computes those facts.

*** partner folder

Recall that viewnodes and graphnodes are distinct.
Some viewnodes correspond to individual graphnodes.
Other viewnodes mediate a relationship between their parent and their children.
For instance, a 'subscribee folder' viewnode contains its parent's subscribees:
#+begin_example
  * subscriber
  ** subscribee folder
  *** subscribee
  *** subscribee
#+end_example

In that example, the first viewnode represents a graphnode,
as do the last two. The subscriber subscribes to the two subscribees.
The second viewnode, though, corresponds to nothing in the graph.
It exists only to name the relationship between its children and its parent.

A *partner folder* is the general concept ('Folder' in code, one enum
with the three property folders; see "Folder, and its per-kind
rules").
Recall that there are five relations in the graph:
containment, link, subscription, hiding, and overriding.
Because a node can be on either side of each relationship --
it can be the container or the content, the hider or the hidden, etc. --
there are many kinds of partner folder. Each lists, as its children,
the nodes that play one role toward its parent. For each relation,
one folder could list the members its parent records, and another the
recorders that record its parent:

| relation      | members its parent records | recorders of its parent |
|---------------+----------------------------+-------------------------|
| containment   | not implemented[1]         | containerFolder         |
| link          | not implemented[2]         | mentionerFolder         |
| subscription  | subscribeeFolder           | subscriberFolder        |
| hiding        | hiddenFolder               | hiderFolder             |
| overriding    | overriddenFolder           | overriderFolder         |

1. A contentFolder would be silly: a node's contents are already its
   ordinary viewchildren.
2. A mentionedFolder would be silly: the links are already visible in
   the node's own text.

The two compound partner folders (see "compound partner folder")
each join three relationships -- subscription, hiding and containment:
- a *hiddenInSubscribeeFolder*, under a subscribee as such, lists that
  subscribee's contents which the subscriber hides;
- a *hiddenOutsideOfSubscribeeFolder*, under a subscribeeFolder, lists
  the nodes its parent hides that no subscribee contains.

The diff view adds two more, for nodeMerges since HEAD:
- an *acquiredByFolder* ("it was acquired by"), under an acquiree's
  ghost, holds its acquirer;
- an *acquiredFolder* ("it acquired"), under an acquirer, holds the
  ghosts of the nodes it acquired.

Membership edits in the subscribee, overridden and
hidden-outside-of-subscribee folders are graph edits. The others are
write-protected: their membership follows from the graph (see
"write-protected set"), and they carry the ☮ herald on their folder
headline, meaning "this folder cannot be changed from here" -- the
same sense ☮ (=writeProtected=) carries on a node.

*Default presentation is intentionally narrow.* An occurrence that an
editable-view request extends -- a fresh content view's roots, and
the editable content the request draws under them -- gets its
nonempty =subscribeeFolder= automatically, and a fresh content view a
=containerFolder= on each root that has containers ('add_default_folders' in
'server/refresh/visit.rs'). The other relation folders require an
explicit folder request (the =C-c l= commands), even when their
herald counts say they would be nonempty. The same rule applies when
a member of any partner folder is extended as editable. An editable
subscribee-as-such also gets its nonempty =hiddenInSubscribeeFolder=;
this is the hidden-in compound partner folder specific to how that
subscriber sees the subscribee. The diff view draws no default
folder. Under each member of a partner folder hangs its
containerward chain (see "foldering, the containerward chain, chain
node").

*** compound partner folder

A compound partner folder is a partner folder whose membership joins
more than one relationship, as against the ordinary partner folders,
which read one relation role. There are two, and each joins three
nodes -- a subscriber N, a subscribee S and a hidden node H -- by
"N subscribes to S", "N hides H" and "S contains H". The *hidden-in
compound partner folder* (=hiddenInSubscribeeFolder=) stands under S
inside N's subscribee folder and holds N's hides that S contains, in
the order of N's hides list. The *hidden-outside compound partner
folder* (=hiddenOutsideOfSubscribeeFolder=) stands in N's subscribee
folder beside the subscribees and holds N's hides that no subscribee
contains.

*** write-protected set

A write-protected set is a generated folder whose membership follows
from graph facts rather than direct edits to that folder.  A view
may preserve an order for the set locally, but adding or removing an
item in the generated folder does not add or remove the underlying
graph membership fact.  No child of such a set claims membership, so
the save reads nothing there: an item the user puts inside it is a
note, which the refresh keeps and shows unwarranted (⊥).  If the
refresh finds that a real member is missing from the view, it
reinstates that member (see "reinstated").

For instance, if nodes R and T subscribe to nodes N, the user might land
at a view like the following:
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** subscriberFolder
  *** R
  *** T
#+end_example
The subscriberFolder ("folder of subscribers") is a write-protected set.
Thus the user could flip the order of R and T in the subscriberFolder,
and this change would be respected in the display, but have no effect
in the graph. The user could insert X under subscriberFolder,
but because it is a write-protected set, X claims nothing there: it
is shown unwarranted (⊥), and has no effect on N.
(If the user wants X to subscribe to N, they must modify X, not N.)
The user could delete R, but it is reinstated as soon as they save,
because, again, the subscriberFolder is a write-protected set.

*** "subscribee as such"

In Skg some nodes are "subscribers", which "subscribe" to "subscribees".
(See [[file:./data-model_technical.org][the technical data model]].)
A node that plays the 'subscribee' role can be viewed as an ordinary node,
or /as/ a subscribee. In the latter case it appears
underneath the relevant subscriber, in a 'subscribeeFolder':
#+begin_example
  * subscriber
  ** subscribeeFolder
  *** subsribee1
  *** subsribee2
  ...
#+end_example

In the above view, subscribee1 and subscribee2 are both "subscribees as such":
They are not just subscribees, but they are being shown /as/ subscribees.
This changes the way edits are interpreted:
Edits to a "subscribee as such" can only affect
what the relevant subscriber hides, nothing else.

*** role tree, and the -ward adjectives

A *role tree* is what a view shows of a node's partners in one role
(see "relation, relationship, member, membership, partner"): for the
content role, the node's content; for every other role, the members
of the node's partner folder of that role, each with its
containerward chain. A role, unlike a relation, is directed, so a
role tree goes one way.

The adjectives containerward, contentward, mentionerward,
overriderward, overriddenward, and so on name the role a role tree
follows: a containerward role tree follows the container role.

*** foldering, the containerward chain, chain node

To *folder* a role tree is to draw every partner as a member of a
partner folder of its role, never directly under the node. Every
role tree but the contentward one is foldered, and is
write-protected throughout. A folder request ('(folder containers)'
and its siblings, the =C-c l= commands) draws one; a fresh content
view draws a containers folder on each root; the search enrichment
(what a text search draws under each hit, in the one message that
answers it) draws a containers folder under each hit, its two
override folders when it has such relatives, and its alias folder
when an alias of its node matched.

The *containerward chain* is the one tree that goes on below a
folder. Under each member of a partner folder, of any kind but the
diff view's two acquisition folders, hang that member's containers,
as write-protected occurrences claiming nothing; under each of
those, its containers;
and so on to the roots, or to the config's 'max_roleTree_depth'. A
container met again on the way is drawn once more, as a leaf. The
nodes of a chain are *chain nodes*. Nothing hangs under a ghost
member, nor under a property member, and the member's
partners in the folder's own role are not drawn below it: a folder
request on the member draws them. No request extends a chain; a
folder request on a chain node draws a folder under it. A chain node
carries no change facts and takes no commit-readiness marker; a
folder's member does both. Nothing in a chain node's metadata says
that it is one: it is known by where it stands.

*** extend, rerender, refresh

The server *extends* a view when it draws more of it: the content of
a node shown editable, or a viewbranch a view request asks for. A
*rerender* redraws a view, after a save or a request: it is a
*refresh* of what the view holds (see "the refresh" among the terms
of the implementation), which keeps what the user put in it. Org
folding keeps the words "fold" and "unfold"; drawing more of a view
is not called unfolding.

*** tombstone, entombstone

A *tombstone* is a viewnode with no title, no ID and no change facts,
shown as "something stood here", left where a headline stood that can
stand there for nothing, and holding that headline's children. The
refresh *entombstones* a headline (makes it one) when its gnid names
nothing the view may show (a node a save deleted, which in the diff
view becomes its ghost instead, or a node the skgrepo restriction now
hides), when it describes its viewparent from where it cannot (see
"the refresh's rungs, unsayable" among the terms of the implementation), when
the change it described is gone, when it is a folder left with
nothing in it that the user could add to, and when the user asks it
to of a ghost. The refresh prunes a childless tombstone before the
buffer is rendered, a root included, so a user sees one only where it
holds something. The save discards anything typed on its line, and no
child of a tombstone claims membership; every folder accepts one
among its children. In code the kind is 'ViewnodeKind::Tombstone',
and on the wire the atom =tombstone=.

*** reinstated

A goal item that no child stands for is *reinstated*: the refresh
draws it, as a new viewnode, in a folder and in an editable
occurrence's content. So a member the user deleted from a
write-protected folder comes back at the next save, since its
membership is a fact of the graph, edited from the other side of the
relationship; and a member that a save in another view added appears
in this one. The buffer correction "Drew what the graph says belongs here."
reports it, naming the headline ('BufferCorrectionKind::Reinstated' in
'server/refresh/buffer_corrections.rs'), except where drawing it is extent
(see "extent, extension, node budget"). "Reinstate" names only this:
a child the buffer already holds is never drawn again, but re-kinded
and re-warranted where it stands.

*** ghost, unknown

The two vognodes that stand for no node of the graph
('Vognode::Ghost', 'Vognode::Unknown' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs').
A *ghost*, drawn only in the diff view, stands for a node HEAD has
and the worktree lacks, write-protected, with HEAD's title and body
(=ghost (gnid ID) (skgRepos (nodeRepo REPO))=); it accepts
=restoreNode= and no view request, and is shown until it is
restored or its deletion is committed ('docs/diff-view.org', "deleted
and new nodes"). A ghost claims
membership only where its viewparent's list in the worktree still
names its gnid, a dangling reference to a node HEAD has, and the save
then keeps that gnid in the list, as it keeps an unknown's; anywhere
else it claims nothing. An *unknown* stands for a gnid its
viewparent's list names and no node answers to (see "unknown node,
dangling ID"). An occurrence whose node a save deleted becomes a
tombstone, or, in the diff view, its ghost. In a content view a ghost
is re-kinded by the ID rule: an unknown where the list names it, else
a tombstone.

*** unknown node, dangling ID

An *unknown node* is an ID that some node's contains, subscribesTo,
hidesFromSubs or overrides list names but that no node of the graph
answers to, as primary or extra ID; such an ID is *dangling*. A view
shows it as an unknown ("Reference to unknown node."), which claims
membership, since removing it would edit the list.
The "investigate unknown node" request says what git history knows of
it, and the delete-references-to-absent-node command removes the
references.

*** ancestor generation

How far up the view an ancestor is: 1 is the viewparent, 2 the
grandparent, counting folders. The '(rels ...)' form's '(ancestors GEN
...)' and its birth fact's GEN are ancestor generations, and heralds
show them as a, b, ... z, then {27}. A tree's plain "generation",
counting down from its root, is the opposite direction.

*** override substitution, and the vocabulary around it

Where a node N stands as content of an editable occurrence in a
content view, the refresh shows the effective overrider of N instead:
the end of the chain of owned, currently-visible
'overrides' relationships leading out of N. The chain is often one
relationship, but may be longer (an owned chain D overrides C overrides
N is legal; only a cycle is forbidden), and visibility gating can
stop it at a middle node when a later link's mentioner is restricted --
so the shown node is the last visible link, not necessarily the
chain's end. The resolver is 'resolve_override' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph/override_resolution.rs'. Foreign override
relationships are display-only facts and never substitute.

The shown substitute carries the fact '(overridesHere N)'
(the crucial herald =ĥ= after =O=), naming the original it stands for. A viewnode's
collected ID is what it contributes to its parent's saved lists:
the fact's original if present, else its own ID -- so saving a
view that shows R in place of N keeps N in the parent's contains.

An overridden-as-such is a member of an overriddenFolder --
the position that always shows the original; its editable
extension applies neither the recorder's hides nor substitution to
its immediate children.

An ordinary single-root visit always opens the requested node itself,
shown raw. In enriched search results, each result shows its override
folders, holding its visible direct override relatives in both
directions. Override facts also appear in explicitly requested override
folders.

*** skgrepo, home skgrepo, skgrepo-set, gitrepo

A *skgrepo* is a directory of =.skg= files, configured by one
=[[repos]]= entry in =skgconfig.toml=. A node's *home skgrepo* is the
skgrepo that holds its home section (see below). A *skgrepo-set* is a
prefix of the config's privacy order: the skgrepos made available
together. A *gitrepo* is a Git repository. Skgrepos are usually gitrepos
too, but they need not be.

In prose, always say "skgrepo" or "gitrepo", never a bare "repo", so the
two cannot be confused. ("relRepo" is a single coined term, so it keeps
the short form.) Code says 'skgrepo' and 'gitrepo' too ('SkgrepoName',
'home_skgrepo'); a skgrepo's config entry is the 'Skgrepo' type.

The *skgrepo restriction* ('SkgrepoRestriction') is the skgrepo-set a
connection currently shows; the skgrepo-set =all= means no
restriction. A skgrepo inside it is *unrestricted*, one outside it
*restricted*, and so are their nodes and occurrences: the gated
graph answers 'restricted' for such a node, as distinct from
'present' and 'absent' ('GnidPresence'), and the view shows nothing
of it. "Hidden" is reserved for hides-from-subscriptions. Where an occurrence of it stood when the restriction
tightened, a tombstone stands, holding the occurrence's children.

*** owned, foreign

A skgrepo is *owned* iff its directory lies under the config's
=owned_folder= (default =owned/=); every other skgrepo is *foreign*.
Only owned sections are ever written. A real edit to a foreign node is
read as a request to fork it (see "clone, and the fork operation").
Say "owned" rather than "owned", and "foreign" rather than
"write-protected", when speaking of skgrepos: write-protection is a
property of a viewnode occurrence (see "editable, write-protected").

*** "telescope" = privacy telescope

In code and comments the short form "telescope" always means the
privacy telescope; see "privacy telescope" below.

*** privacy telescope, telescope section, home

One node can be recorded at several privacy levels at once: same-ID
=.skg= files across skgrepos, at most one per skgrepo. That family of
files is the node's *privacy telescope*; each file is one
*telescope section* ("section" for short in code), holding the
slice of the node recorded at its skgrepo's privacy level. The *home* is
the most public retained section; it alone holds title, body, and extra
ids. That the home carries the title is an invariant, not a
search rule: a node's text always lives in its most public
section, so a titleless section above the title is a violation
the composition reports (=CompositionWarning::TitleBelowHome=), not a shape to
look past. There is no linking relationship between sections —
sharing an ID is being the same node. See =docs/telescopes.org=
and =server/types/nodes/fs.rs=.

Before composing, an owned-PID collision is normalized: if any owned
section exists for a PID, the entire owned telescope is retained and all
same-PID foreign sections are ignored (with a deterministic warning and no
filesystem edit). A telescope with no owned section remains wholly foreign.

*** overPrivateText

A privacy telescope is *overPrivateText* when its composed title or body is
selected from a section more private than its home (most public retained)
section. The flag applies to the whole telescope, even if only one text
field is below home. This violates the rule that the node's text lives at
home; see [[file:./telescopes.org][privacy telescopes]].

Skgrepo-set =all= can show that text with a warning. A restricted request
asks for approval scoped to the affected node IDs and that request before
releasing it; search asks whether to include or exclude such telescopes.
Approval to display the text does not authorize *Hoist*, the separate
repair that moves the selected title and body to home.

*** privacy order and relRepo

The position of a skgrepo in the config's privacy order (most public
first) is its privacy level. Live code does not use "level" for the
value carried by a relationship: every relationship member carries its
*relRepo*, the skgrepo whose section records it. In code
that is =RelPartner { relRepo, member }=; a collection of them is
a *list of relation partners*. "More private" = later in the
privacy order.

*** home skgrepo move, relRepo move

A *home skgrepo move* is a change of a node's home skgrepo (=C-c s s=,
then a save); a *relRepo move* is a change of a relationship's relRepo
(=C-c s r=, or a save that resets or raises it). The diff view shows
each move since HEAD as one change-forest leaf: a home skgrepo move as
the property "⌂ -OLD +NEW" under the node, a relRepo move as the
property "relationship moved from OLD to NEW" under the member, keyed
by the relationship, so the same move seen from either end is the
same leaf. A relRepo move puts only the recorder in C. The
skgrepo-changes buffer of the diff view shows the nodes whose home
skgrepo moved and the recorders of the relationships whose relRepo
moved, each with all of its changes. Say "home skgrepo move", not
"home move".

*** sticky rule (sticky-else-default)

Where a relationship's relRepo comes from at save time:
a relationship already on disk keeps its relRepo (*sticky*); a new relationship
between owned nodes defaults to the more-private endpoint home. A new
relationship from an owned recorder to a foreign member defaults to the recorder's
home, deliberately making the foreign ID and relationship visible at
that owned skgrepo without proposing a foreign write. Every relRepo is
clamped to be no more public than the recorder's home. Hides
floor higher (at least the most public subscription explaining them). A
=(relRepo NAME)= atom (the =skg-set-relRepo= gesture)
overrides sticky with any skgrepo at least as private as the relationship's
default — raising privacy, or moving a stuck skgrepo back to the
default; a more-public choice is a save error. A legacy or
hand-authored relationship whose disk relRepo already sits more public than
its default may be held or made more private, never made still more
public. See =server/from_text/supplement_from_disk.rs=.

**** stranded relationship, strandeds

A home skgrepo move can strand a relationship touching the moved
node: a *stranded relationship* is one that sat at its default relRepo
before the move and that the sticky rule would leave off its new
default. *Strandeds* is the noun for stranded relationships; "strand"
is only a verb. A save finds every one, of every relation, in both
directions, shown in some view or not ('server/from_text/strandeds.rs').
It raises the inbound strandeds of a privatizing move to the new home
by itself, since they would leak the member's ID, and asks the user
whether to reset the strandeds of a publicizing move to their new
defaults (the strandeds-confirmation). Mentions carry no relRepo, so
they are never stranded.

*** clone, and the fork operation

To *fork* a node N is to turn it into an editable *clone* C in a
skgrepo you own. C copies N's title, body, and child-list (the child IDs
only — shallow), =subscribesTo= N (so N's later additions surface to C
as unintegrated subscribed content) and =overrides= N (so
override substitution shows C wherever N would appear). Children of N
that the forking edit deleted become C's
=hidesFromSubs=, and thereafter any child removed from
an owned subscriber's contains that some subscribee still contains is
hidden likewise on save (re-adding it unhides). A node may have at
most one clone you own (override monogamy).

Forking spans two gestures, foreign and owned:

- /implicit/ (foreign N): make N editable (=C-c s d=), edit it, and
  save; any real change to a foreign node is read as a fork request, and
  the save asks you to confirm. N is left untouched on disk.
- /explicit/ (owned N): =skg-fork-node= (=C-c m f=), for a node you
  already own — used to deepen an override chain (D overrides C overrides
  N, all owned). N keeps its own content; only the clone is added. The
  clone is built from N's saved state, so the command refuses a dirty
  buffer.

See =docs/forks.org= and =docs/sharing-model.org=.

*** nodeMerge ("merge" is always qualified)

A /nodeMerge/ joins two graphnodes: the acquirer takes the
acquiree's content and IDs, and the acquiree's text is kept in a
text preserver. =C-c s m= ('skg-set-merge-request') writes the
request, =(editRequest (merge ID))=, and a save applies it; the diff
view's merges buffer shows each nodeMerge since HEAD. In prose, say
"merge nodes" or "nodeMerge", never a bare "merge"; the bare word
appears only in client-facing wire strings, where only nodes can
merge. See =docs/merging.org=.

*** text preserver

A nodeMerge keeps the acquiree's text in a new node, its *text
preserver*: titled "MERGED: " and the acquiree's title, with the
acquiree's body, and placed first in the acquirer's content. The
acquiree's other content joins the acquirer's, and the acquiree's ID
becomes an extra ID of the acquirer. See =docs/merging.org=.

*** no search matching

A node-file flag (=NoSearchMatching= in YAML,
=noSearchMatching= on the wire) that prevents the node's title, aliases, and
body from producing a direct text-search hit. The node remains indexed for
exact-ID lookup and may appear as context around another hit. It is search
decluttering, not privacy.

*** prominent

Search ranks *prominent* nodes higher: a root, a member of a cycle,
a node linked to, a node that had an ID before import, or a
multiply contained node. Most nodes are not prominent.

*** flags folder and Flag

=flagsFolder= is a generated, write-protected non-vognode under a graphnode. Its
=(flag FLAG)= leaves list the node's true flags in canonical
order. Each leaf is titleless because its herald supplies the visible label.
The folder stays visible when empty. Edits within a retained folder are
rejected before save, but deleting the entire folder safely dismisses the
optional viewbranch and changes no flag. =noSearchMatching= is changed
through the dedicated setter, while the two org-roam import provenance
flags have no setter. A node with =n= true flags has the low-styled =Fn=
herald; no flag herald is shown at zero.

*** Terms a user meets in the diff view

'docs/diff-view.org' describes the diff view; these are the terms
a user meets in it, and they mean nothing outside it.

**** diff view

Four buffers that show every change in the owned skgrepos since their
last commits, with the context needed to understand them: "all
changes", "merges", "skgrepo changes" and "order-only changes". Each is
an ordinary editable view. Foreign skgrepos are excluded; the
readiness summary only counts their changed files.

**** pruned change view

The viewforest the "all changes" buffer renders, defined by the sets
C, R, U and D (below; 'server/diff_view/pruned_change_view.rs',
functions 'prunedChangeView_...'). The merges and skgrepo-changes
buffers render pruned change views over subsets of the changes: the
nodeMerges, and the home skgrepo and relRepo moves.

**** the three kinds of change

The first three parts of a headline's '(diff ...)' form, each written
only when true ('DiffChange'): the node's existence, '(node added)'
or '(node removed)' (herald ∃+, ∃-), when only the after-graph or
only the before-graph has it ('GraphStraddle::node_existence'); the
existence of its relationship to its viewparent, '(rel added)' or
'(rel removed)' (herald ~+, ~-); and the node's other properties,
'(properties changed)' (herald M, "modified"), on a node in both
graphs that records at least one change-forest leaf
('GraphStraddle::node_properties_changed'). The two existences are
'Existence { Added, Removed }'. A member, a removed member, a folder's
member and a root carry the node's two; a ghost never carries
'(properties changed)'. The relationship is the viewparent's content
under a vognode, and, in a partner folder, the relationship of the folder's kind between
the member and the folder's viewparent; the two compound partner
folders' members carry none. On an alias, ID or flag member, '(rel
...)' is the member's existence in its list.

**** change facts

A headline's *change facts* are the parts of its '(diff ...)' form
that the server writes: the three kinds of change, the '(leaves
...)' count of its change-forest leaves, the '(ready ...)' readiness,
its commit-readiness marker, and in the skgrepo-changes buffer its
home skgrepo move ('ChangeFacts' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'). A
readiness request, in the headline's '(editRequest ...)', is the
user's, not a change fact. The words are never shortened to
"facts".

**** diff object, diff aspect

A *diff object* is what a part of a '(diff ...)' form or a readiness request
names on a headline: the node itself ('node'), or the node's
relationship to its viewparent ('rel'; in a partner folder, to the
folder's viewparent). There are exactly these two.
A change-forest leaf about a relationship has the relationship as its
object, never the member. A request, a '(leaves ...)' change fact and
a '(ready ...)' change fact may also say 'all', which names every leaf a
modified node records (so one gesture on it marks its file), or every
leaf of a commit readiness aggregate one level down; 'all' is shorthand, not a third
object. A *diff aspect* is what a
change-forest leaf is about: existence, text, home skgrepo, an alias,
an extra ID, a flag, or relRepo.

**** C, R, U, D, content origin

The sets the pruned change view is computed from: C, the changed
nodes; R, the content origins; U, the nodes to extend contentward; D,
the deduplicated content origins. *C*, the changed nodes, is the set of changed
graphnodes, in two halves: a node is changed *out* when its own file
records a change (it is new or deleted, or its text, aliases, extra
IDs, flags, home skgrepo or own relationships changed), and *in* when
its recorders' files do (its set of containers, mentioners,
subscribers, overriders or hiders changed). A node can be both. A
relRepo move puts only the recorder in C, and a change of order alone
puts nothing in it. *R*, the content origins, is the set of content
origins reached from C by walking containerward through the union of
the straddle. A *content origin* is a node with no
container, a multiply-contained node, or a member of a rootlike cycle
(the stop rule of 'server/prominence.rs'). *U*, the nodes to
extend contentward, is R, C and every node on a containerward path
from a node of C to an origin. *D*, the deduplicated content origins,
the roots of the "all changes" buffer, is what remains of R when each
origin, in ID order, is discarded if its own containerward walk within
U reaches another origin still kept; of two origins on one cycle,
exactly one is kept.

A user-visible message that must name one of these sets says its long
name, then the letter in parentheses, then where the algorithm is
described: "the nodes to extend contentward (U; see
'docs/diff-view.org', "C, R, U and D", and the module comment of
'server/diff_view/pruned_change_view.rs')".

**** change-forest, change-forest leaf, recorder of a leaf

The *change-forest* is the picture of everything commit-readiness can
be asked about, built from the graph straddle, independent of any
view: its roots are the changed graphnodes; under each come its
changed aspects; its leaves, the *change-forest leaves*, are the
individual changes a user marks. The type 'ChangeLeaves'
('server/diff_view/change_leaves.rs') holds every leaf, keyed by its recorder,
its aspect and, for a relationship, the relationship ('LeafKey'). The
*recorder* of a leaf is the node whose file records the change, and
that node *records* the leaf; a relationship's leaf is recorded by
the relationship's recorder, so seen from either end it is the same
leaf.

**** mark, readiness

A *mark* says that one change-forest leaf is commit-ready; the
readiness gestures set and clear marks. Editing a marked change
unmarks it. A leaf's *readiness* is whether it is marked.

**** commit readiness aggregate

A *commit readiness aggregate* is a thing with change-forest leaves
under it: a folder, a changeset marker, a node, a file, the whole
changeset. It is ready iff every leaf under it that can be ready is
ready; that derived value is *its readiness*. A view shows it as
'(ready ...)' in the '(diff ...)' form, which nothing reads at save;
a folder carries none, and each client computes a folder's readiness
from its members (see "changed folder").

**** commit-readiness marker

The '(ready ...)' part of a headline's '(diff ...)' form, and its
herald: the derived readiness of a commit readiness aggregate or of
the leaves of a headline with change facts. A changed folder's ✓,
which the clients draw from its members, is one too. A folder's
member takes one; a chain node takes none. It is never called a bare "marker", since a
changeset marker is another thing.

**** changeset marker

A childless change viewnode ('ChangeViewnode::ChangesetMarker') that
stands for a set of changes recorded in its parent's file, and carries
their derived readiness. There are three kinds: the content changeset
marker ("its content changed"), the relRepo changeset marker ("some
content relationships changed skgrepo") and the order-only changeset marker
("order-only change", which is always ready).

**** removed member, former member

A *removed member* is an occurrence with the change fact '(rel
removed)': its node survives, but its relationship to its viewparent
exists in the before-graph only. It carries a ghost warrant ('(view
ghostWarrant)') and, where its viewparent's claims are read, claims
nothing ('noClaim'), so a save leaves the relationship removed. It is
encoded so in content, in a partner folder and in a ghost's old
content alike. In content position it follows the editability rule
as any occurrence the refresh draws: editable at its node's first
occurrence in level order, write-protected at a later one. A *former
member* is any viewnode standing for such a before-only relationship,
ghosts included. These phrases, like "stale member" and "new member",
name the node by its membership's fate.

**** old text

The change viewnode ('(oldText ASPECT)') under a node whose title or
body changed since HEAD: labelled "old title and body", "old title" or
"old body", its headline is the old title and its body the old body.
When the body changed, a "body diff" property under it holds the line
diff. The text change is one change-forest leaf, marked on the old
text.

**** changed folder

A partner, alias, ID or flags folder the diff view shows because its
membership changed since HEAD. It is written as its bare folder atom,
holds the members of either graph, each tagged with the three kinds
of change, and reads "its X changed (this file)" or "(other files)"
(see "in, out"), with ✓ when every changed membership is ready: each
client sees the change in the members and draws that label in place
of the folder's own.

**** what a changed folder's member holds

Under each member of a changed folder hangs its containerward chain,
as under any folder's member, walked over both graphs of the
straddle, so that the user sees where the member sits; and before
the chain, where the member's relationship moved to another
skgrepo, that move.

**** moved-like-ancestor fact

In the skgrepo-changes buffer, a vognode whose home skgrepo moved
carries '(homeSkgrepoMove (old OLD) (new NEW))' in its '(diff ...)',
its herald "-OLD +NEW". When the nearest vognode ancestor whose home moved
moved the same way, it also carries the *moved-like-ancestor fact*
'(movedLikeAncestor LETTER)', its herald "Δ⌂=a", where the letter
names that ancestor as an ancestor generation does (a, the
viewparent; b, the grandparent; ...).

**** removed alias, removed ID

An alias or extra ID of a node that HEAD has and the worktree lacks.
The diff view shows it in the node's alias or ID folder as an alias or
ID member with a ghost warrant, written 'alias noClaim (view
ghostWarrant)' or 'id (view ghostWarrant)', with '(diff (rel
removed))', where the LCS of the two lists puts it, with the '-'
herald. The save reads no alias from it, since it claims nothing; the
ID folder, whose claims the save never reads, carries no 'noClaim',
and the save's check of an ID folder refuses only one lacking an ID of
the node. Marking it marks the leaf of the lost alias or ID.

**** readiness summary

The line a client shows after every save of a diff-view buffer, after the
"integrate unshown changes" command (C-c r u), and before a
stage-and-commit that would leave changes behind: "12 of 14 changed
files are ready", or "Commit ready. All 14 of 14 changed files are
ready." A changed file is ready when every change it records is
marked; a file whose only change is order is ready without marks. The
summary also counts the changed files of foreign skgrepos and the
changes not editable in the view.

**** already-staged entry

An entry git's index already holds when stage-and-commit runs, staged
by a hand or another tool. Stage-and-commit asks for confirmation
before committing a gitrepo that has one, and the git-commit then
includes it.

**** editable in the diff view

A buffer *makes a node's changes editable* when it holds an
editable occurrence of the node, wherever it sits, or the node's
ghost in content position
('nodes_editable_in_diffView'). The change-forest leaves those
nodes record are the changes editable in the buffer.

**** changes not editable in the view

The change-forest leaves recorded by the nodes whose changes a
buffer does not
[[*editable in the diff view][make editable]] are the *changes
not editable in the view*, which the readiness summary counts. A
save never makes them editable; C-c r u integrates them.

**** readiness request, readiness gesture

A *readiness request* is a metadata form, '(ready OBJECTS)' or
'(unready OBJECTS)' inside a headline's '(editRequest ...)', that the
save consumes, after its graph
edits succeed, to set or clear the marks of a headline's leaves. A
*readiness gesture* is a gesture (C-c r r, C-c r h and their
recursive forms) that writes readiness requests on one or many
headlines, then saves.

**** restore request

Either of the two edit requests that undo a removal in the diff
view. '(editRequest restoreNode)', on a ghost,
writes its node back as HEAD had it, in-links included;
'(editRequest restoreRel)', on a removed membership (a removed
member in content position, or a member of a partner folder whose
relationship to the folder's viewparent was removed), re-adds that
one relationship as HEAD recorded it. C-c s n ('skg-restore-deleted-node') and C-c s l
('skg-restore-removed-membership') write them, and a save applies
and consumes them as it does every edit request
('docs/diff-view.org', "Restoring what a change removed").

**** headline with change facts

A headline whose metadata carries a '(leaves ...)' change fact. It
is ready when its '(ready ...)' names every object its '(leaves ...)' lists,
and otherwise unready. The navigation commands (C-c r n c and its
kin) move between headlines with change facts.

**** in, out, "(this file)", "(other files)"

A relationship is *out* from its recorder (the node whose file records
it) and *in* to its member. In the diff view, a folder of out-relationships
that changed is labelled "its ... changed (this file)", and one of
in-relationships "its ... changed (other files)". Changeset markers
carry no suffix.

** Terms of the implementation
The words of the code, its comments and the technical docs. A user
almost certainly need not read this section.

*** update-relevant neighborhood

For a set of IDs in one graph snapshot, the *update-relevant
neighborhood* contains the IDs themselves, their immediate neighbors in
both directions along =contains=, =linksTo=, =subscribesTo=, and
=hidesFromSubs=, and every node reachable by each of the
two monotonic override walks. The overriderward and overriddenward walks
start independently at the original IDs, never reverse direction, and
never continue from an ordinary neighbor. Thus it includes no mixed
ordinary/override path and no two-hop ordinary path. Saves compute this
neighborhood independently in the before and after graph snapshots.

*** vognode, non-vognode

A *vognode* is a viewnode standing for a gnid ('Vognode' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs'), of one of three kinds: an *occurrence*,
of a node the truth has; a *ghost*, in the diff view only, of a node
HEAD has and the worktree lacks; an *unknown*, of a gnid its
viewparent's list names and no node answers to. An occurrence of a
node the skgrepo restriction keeps out (a restricted node) becomes a
tombstone.

The other viewnodes are *non-vognodes*: the *descriptors* -- folders,
property members and change viewnodes, which describe their
viewparent or its relationships rather than standing for a graphnode
-- the buffer root and tombstones.

*** open views, single-root

The server keeps, per connection, the views open in the client
('OpenViews' in 'server/types/views_state.rs'), each by its
viewforest id ('ViewforestId': a content view by its root's ID, a
search view by its query), with its viewforest, the IDs it shows and
its 'ViewnodeIdRecord'. A content view may have several roots; the
request that opens one today asks for a single root, so only the
request is "single-root".

*** recorder, carrier

A relationship is recorded in the .skg file of one of its two nodes, its
*recorder*: the node playing the relation's first role (the container,
the mentioner, the subscriber, the hider, the overrider). The other node
is a member of the recorder's list.

The node that has an ID, as its PID or as an extra ID, is that ID's
*carrier*. (Not "owner": the user *owns* skgrepos.)

*** Folder, and its per-kind rules

How a folder (see "partner folder") is built. It is the
=ViewnodeKind::Folder= variant and the payload type =Folder=, one
enum for the twelve partner folders and the three property folders.
“Folder” here describes an org non-vognode that groups viewnodes; it
does not mean a filesystem directory.  “Role col”, “relation col”,
and bare “sharing col” are retired names for the general concept.

Each folder is named by its external non-vognode atom. Methods on
=Folder= (=server/types/viewnode.rs=) give the per-kind rules that the
refresh, the save and the heralds consult, so a new kind cannot
acquire inconsistent treatment in different layers:
- =is_editable=: the save reads the folder's membership, so a member
  typed or removed there edits the graph. These are
  =subscribeeFolder=, =overriddenFolder=, =aliasFolder= and
  =hiddenOutsideOfSubscribeeFolder= (whose visible member set is an
  explicit edit of that subset of the hides; the hides' relRepos stay
  derived). An absent folder means "no opinion" (its field lowers to
  =MSV::Unspecified=, filled from disk); a present-but-empty one, an
  explicit empty set (=MSV::Specified(vec![])=). Every other folder is
  write-protected: its membership follows from the graph, and the
  refresh reports its buffer corrections (see [[*write-protected set][write-protected set]]).
- =goal_is_ordered=: the viewparent's own file records the members'
  order, which the warranted members take: the editable folders and
  =hiddenFolder=. In every other folder the refresh moves no child:
  the inbound folders =subscriberFolder=, =overriderFolder=,
  =hiderFolder=, =containerFolder= and =mentionerFolder= (the folders
  of =C-c l c= and =C-c l m=; the other side of each is the node's
  content, or its links, shown without a folder),
  =hiddenInSubscribeeFolder= (derived from hide state), the diff
  view's =acquiredByFolder= and =acquiredFolder= (derived from the
  graph straddle, and save-inert), and =idFolder= and =flagsFolder=.
- =members_are_properties=: the alias, ID and flags folders.
- =members_carry_containerward_chain=: every folder but the property
  folders and the two acquisition folders.

The ☮ herald on a write-protected folder's headline is a
herald-table fact only (=server/heralds.rs=), with no wire atom of
its own: which folders are write-protected is already known from the
folder atom. The editable folders carry none.

A folder left with no child is entombstoned, and so pruned, unless the
save reads its membership or it is the flags folder, where the user
could add something ('folder_goes_when_empty' in
'server/refresh/visit.rs').

*** the containerward chain, as drawn

When the refresh draws a member of a folder whose members carry the
chain, it hangs the member's chain under it
('attach_containerward_chains_under_members' in
'server/refresh/chain.rs'), walking the member's
containers breadth-first over the live graph or, in the diff view,
over the union of the straddle. The extent of a chain is none, so a
chain node is never reinstated: a chain that stays keeps its
viewnodes and their fold state, and only a newly drawn member gets
one. A chain node is known by where it stands:
'holds_containerward_chain' says whether a viewnode's vognode
children are chain nodes (it is a write-protected member of such a
folder, or itself a chain node), and 'stands_in_containerward_chain'
whether a viewnode is one ('server/types/tree/forest.rs'). Its goal
is its containers, so a chain node that no longer contains the node
above it is unwarranted (⊥).

*** the refresh

The one operation that takes a buffer, parsed and validated, and the
truth, and returns the corrected buffer and what it corrected
('refresh_viewforest' in 'server/refresh/mod.rs'). Every view is drawn
by it: a fresh content view (the refresh of a stub), every rerender
(the saved view, the collateral views, "rerender all views", a
skgrepo-set switch), the search enrichment and the diff view's
buffers. It decides everything from the buffer and the truth as they
are now, and is a *fixpoint*: refreshing its own output changes and
corrects nothing, up to the viewnode IDs it assigns
('tests/idempotence.rs'). 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The
refresh: how a view follows the graph", states the whole model.

*** the truth

What the refresh compares a buffer with ('Truth' in
'server/refresh/mod.rs'): the in-Rust graph as the skgrepo
restriction lets it be seen, with the config. For a gnid it answers
'Present', 'Restricted' or 'Absent' ('GnidPresence',
'gnid_presence_gated'). In the diff view the mode adds HEAD, through
the graph straddle.

*** mode

Which kind of view a refresh refreshes ('RefreshMode' in
'server/refresh/mode.rs'): 'ContentView', or
'DiffView', which carries the graph straddle and the buffer kind. It
decides whether a parent's goal includes what HEAD alone warrants,
whether ghosts and change viewnodes are drawn, and what the node
budget charges.

*** gnid, pid, extra ID

A *gnid* is a graphnode's ID (the type 'ID'): a node's primary gnid is
its *pid*, the others its *extra IDs*, acquired by nodeMerges; each of
them resolves to the node. On the wire it is =(gnid ID)=. A viewnode
ID ("vnid") names a viewnode, not a node.

*** the ID rule

What a headline standing for a gnid becomes, by four questions in
order, the first yes winning ('id_rule' in 'server/refresh/id_rule.rs'):
does the truth have the node (an occurrence); in the diff view, does
HEAD (a ghost); does its viewparent's list name the gnid (an
unknown); else a tombstone. A restricted node answers no to all four. The
refresh *re-kinds* a headline by it where it stands, keeping its
viewnode ID, fold state, children and, for an occurrence, its
editability.

*** goal

What the truth says a parent's children should be ('goal_of_parent'
in 'server/refresh/goal.rs'): an occurrence's =contains= list, a
folder's partners in its relation (or a compound folder's derived
list), a property folder's aliases, IDs or true flags, a chain node's
containers; in the diff view, the union of the parent's lists in the
two graphs of the straddle. Each *goal item* names a gnid or a
property value ('GoalItem', 'GoalMember'). The goal decides each
child's warrant, what is reinstated and, where it is ordered, the
children's order. A parent whose position implies no relationship
(the buffer root, a tombstone, an unknown) has none.

*** the refresh's rungs, unsayable

The fixed order of rungs the refresh applies at each parent to its
children, weakest first: re-kind, viewnode merge, warrant,
claims, reinstate, reorder, override substitution, view requests
('server/refresh/visit.rs'; 'docs/data-model_technical.org', "The
walk and the refresh's rungs"). A descriptor -- a folder, a property member, a
change viewnode -- whose ancestors are not those the ancestry table
requires of its kind ('required_ancestry' in
'server/refresh/ancestry.rs') is *unsayable* there, and is
entombstoned, its children staying under the tombstone. An occurrence
is never unsayable. (Older text calls the rungs the ladder; they are not the privacy
ladder, the skgrepos in privacy order.)

*** viewnode merge, duplicate viewnode siblings

*Duplicate viewnode siblings* are siblings standing for one gnid
under one parent. The refresh's second rung ('merge_siblings') makes
each set of them one viewnode, by a *viewnode merge*. The result
stands where the first of them stood and has a fresh viewnode ID.
It is folded only if every sibling was, and its body is folded only
if every sibling's was; it is focused if any sibling was; it is
editable if any sibling was, unless it stands for another node (a
node and its shown overrider stand for one gnid) whose editable
occurrence is elsewhere in the buffer; it claims membership if any
sibling did. It carries the union of the siblings' view requests,
and the edit request and relationship request of whichever sibling
carried one. It holds every sibling's children in sibling order,
each marked =noClaim= unless the graph warrants it there. So a copy
pasted beside a member is joined to it. The buffer correction
"merged" names the result. A viewnode merge is neither a nodeMerge,
which joins two graphnodes, nor an instructionMerge, and is never
called a bare "merge". Both terms are coined by plan 06 ("tidy the
refresh").

*** extent, extension, node budget

The *extent* is how much of a parent's goal the refresh draws
('server/refresh/extent.rs'): all of it for a folder and for an
editable occurrence's content (in the diff view, also for a ghost's
old content where content stands); none of it, beyond what the buffer
already shows, for a write-protected occurrence's content and for the
containerward chain. An *extension* is one editable occurrence's
content drawn whole; the *node budget* ('initial_node_limit') counts
them, each charging it once ('RefreshMode::charges_node_budget'),
and once it is spent the occurrences the refresh draws are
write-protected. A folder fills its members without spending any. The
module comment of 'server/refresh/extent.rs' states the rule. The
server *renders* a viewforest when it produces its org text
('server/org_to_text.rs').

*** editability rule

Which occurrence of a node is editable: at most one per node in a
buffer. One the buffer holds keeps its editability; an editable-view
request moves it, write-protecting the node's other occurrence; among
the occurrences the refresh draws, the first of a node is editable
when its parent's children are editable content, no other occurrence
of the node is, and the budget allows
('drawn_occurrence_is_editable'), and a repeat is write-protected.
The diff view's deviations are in 'docs/diff-view.org', "Editability
in the diff view".

*** buffer corrections

A buffer correction is a change the refresh made to what a buffer
said because the buffer was wrong about the graph ('BufferCorrection',
'BufferCorrectionKind' in 'server/refresh/buffer_corrections.rs'): a
headline reinstated, entombstoned, marked =noClaim=, the result of a
viewnode merge, re-kinded, or stripped of an edit request its new
kind has no slot for. What it draws as extent is no buffer
correction. Nor are two changes
the buffer was not wrong about, which the refresh learns of from
its caller ('BufferCorrectionPolicy'): a tombstone it leaves where
the saving user's own save deleted the node, and a member it draws
because the skgrepo restriction was widened (after a change of the
restriction it reports no tombstone either). The refresh reports
buffer corrections; it does not warn. The save and rerender
responses carry each view's buffer corrections, tagged with its view
ID ('ViewBufferCorrections'), in their field =(corrections ...)=, and
the clients show them in their warnings buffer, under a headline
"Buffer corrections in" the view's buffer name (see "startup warnings
channel").

*** IdResolution

How the parse reads the gnids a buffer writes ('IdResolution' in
'server/from_text/buffer_to_viewnodes/add_missing_info.rs'):
'ThroughGraph' resolves each through the graph, so an occurrence's
gnid becomes its node's pid and, where the parse supplies a node's
home skgrepo, it reads the graph's; 'AsWritten' keeps every gnid as
written and consults no graph, for a caller that reads only what the
text says (the dirty-view dependency scan, tests of fully written
text).

*** save-inert

A viewnode is *save-inert* if it emits no fieldIntents: tombstones,
change viewnodes, the diff view's acquisition folders, a ghost that
claims nothing, and an alias or ID member that claims nothing (a lost
alias or ID among them). Its descendants may still emit them, so save
extraction still visits them.

*** MSV = maybe specified vector

=MSV<T>= distinguishes "no opinion" from "empty". =Unspecified= says a
file section or an edit has nothing to say about the field;
=Specified(vec![])= says the field is explicitly empty. Telescope
folding and partial nodeIntents both depend on the difference.

*** composition (visible vs full), decomposition

The *composition* of a telescope combines its sections, most public
first, into one effective node (=server/telescope/compose.rs=). The
*full composition* uses every section; a *visible composition* uses only the
sections unrestricted under the current skgrepo restriction, and is what a
restricted view renders. *Decomposition* is the save-side inverse:
splitting the effective node back into sections, byte-stably.

*** placement, more public anchor

How a more private section orders its members into a more public
list without the public file recording anything: in the section's
stored sequence, a =- anchor: ID= entry names a *more public anchor*,
a member of the more public list, and the members after it insert
immediately after that ID; members before the first anchor prepend.
Placements exist only on disk — more public anchors are derived at
save from the in-memory order, so they can never go stale in memory.
Say "more public anchor", never a bare "anchor".

*** col

*Col* was the old name for a folder viewnode (short for
"collection"). It has been expunged from production code, but may still
appear in old documentation, plans, or the knowledge graph.

*** view-lifetime

Some data regarding a node is view-lifetime data
(viewStats, for instance):
it lasts only as long as the view of it in some buffer.

Data on disk, by contrast,
remains when the app terminates,
and can be recovered by a later session.
*Session-lifetime* data lasts as long as one server session: the
in-Rust graph and the search index, together the *session-lifetime
stores*. The server rebuilds them from the .skg files when it starts,
or when asked ('skg-rebuild-session-lifetime-stores'); they outlive
every view. Say which lifetime is meant, never "ephemeral".

*** branching viewnode (vs a branch)

A *branching viewnode* is a viewnode with more than one viewchild. Do
not confuse it with a *branch*: any viewnode together with its
descendants, which may be degenerate, its root having one viewchild
or none.

None of this is related to forking a node, below.

*** fieldIntent, nodeIntent, nodeInstruction, savePlan

What a save turns the buffer into, in order:
- a *fieldIntent* ('FieldIntent') says what one position in the buffer
  wants for one field of one node (its title and body, its contains,
  a hide, ...). Local fieldIntent collection emits these into a map
  keyed by ID ('CollectedFieldIntents').
- a *nodeIntent* ('NodeIntent') is all of one node's fieldIntents,
  lowered into a save or a delete. It may still be partial: fields
  the buffer said nothing about are 'MSV::Unspecified'.
- a *nodeInstruction* ('NodeInstruction': 'Save' or 'Delete') is a
  nodeIntent after disk supplementation: complete, ready to write.
- the *savePlan* ('SavePlan') is everything one save applies: its
  nodeInstructions, plus forks and nodeMerges.

*** local fieldIntent collection

The save path's fieldIntent extraction: one pure DFS preorder over
the buffer's viewforest, in which each position reads only itself and
its direct children, plus a context that flows down from its
ancestors. Each visit emits fieldIntents into a map keyed by
node ID; downstream stages (validation, lowering, visibility
resolution, disk supplementation, the noop filter) consume the map.
Lives in =server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/=, entered
via =extract_nonmergeSavePlan_locally=.

This entry is the canonical sketch of the save pipeline. Its
surrounding stages are, in order: parse (which supplies each new
headline's gnid and skgrepo; see "IdResolution"), validate, place,
extract (this entry), foreign-node validation, nodeMerge build, graph
update, and the refresh of the saved and collateral views.

*** instructionMerge ("merge" is always qualified)

An /instructionMerge/ is the combining of emitted save intents into
the map during local fieldIntent collection. It shares nothing with
a nodeMerge (the =NodeMerge= type) but the word.

*** collateral view

When a save changes nodes that other open views show, those views are
*collateral*: the server rerenders them and streams each as a
=collateral-view= message. The save envelope's =other-views= lists every
other open view; the collateral views are the subset that needs a
rerender.

*** startup warnings channel

How the server's startup warnings reach the user: 'load_config'
returns warnings beside the config, 'main' records them once on the
runtime ('record_startup_warnings' in 'server/types/env.rs'), the
server serves them on request ("server warnings"), and each client
appends them on connect to its '*SKG Server Warnings*' buffer, under
a headline "Startup warnings". The same buffer receives the buffer
corrections a save or a rerender reports, under a headline per view;
neither erases what the buffer holds.

*** "snapshot" is always qualified: graph, git or buffer snapshot

A /graph snapshot/ is one immutable state of the in-Rust graph
('GraphSnapshot'). A /git snapshot/ is one of HEAD, the git index or
the worktree; 'server/git_ops/git_snapshot.rs' reads HEAD and the
worktree (the graph straddle's before-graph reads HEAD). A /buffer snapshot/ is a
buffer's text as a client sends it, for a dirty view in the save
envelope.

*** "index" is always qualified: search, git or graph index

The /search index/ is Tantivy's. The /git index/ is git's staging
area; next to HEAD and worktree it needs no qualifier ("HEAD vs
index"). A /graph index/ is a derived map inside the in-Rust graph:
the /recorderward relmaps/ ('contained_by', 'subscribers_of', ...), plus
'extra_id_to_pid'. A recorderward relmap maps each member of one
relation to the recorders that list it. (Not "inverse": today every
relationship is recorded by its first member, but the data model may
someday let the second member record one; 'RecorderwardRelmap' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph.rs' says more.)

*** "commit" is qualified when ambiguous: git-, skgsave- or tantivy-commit

A /git-commit/ is git's. A /skgsave-commit/ is the step of a save that
writes the .skg files and swaps in the new graph; a save that stops to
ask for confirmation (forks, Hoist) skgsave-commits nothing. A
/tantivy-commit/ makes search-index writes visible; a save's happens
later, on a background worker. Where context leaves no doubt (HEAD, a
merge commit, a 'git2' call) bare "commit" means a git-commit.

*** swap in (a runtime generation), vs publish

A save, rebuild or import ends by atomically *swapping in* a new
runtime generation (config, graph and search index): readers holding
the old one keep it, and the next reader gets the new one. "Publish"
means only making text more public: sharing a skgrepo, or writing text
into a more public telescope section. Hoist /moves/ text to home.

*** hiderel = "hides" relationship

See [[file:./data-model_technical.org][the technical data model]] for the hiding
relationship.

*** lp = length-prefixed

Sometimes an API response is prefixed with a length, letting the receiver know how many characters to expect in the response.

In Haskell, 'm' is sometimes prefixed to a word to indicate 'maybe'.

For any function with a name like this, if its meaning is unclear,

*** Terms of the diff view's implementation

How the diff view ('docs/diff-view.org') is computed.

**** buffer kind

Which of the four buffers of the diff view a buffer is
('DiffViewBufferKind'). A buffer of the diff view is refreshed over
the graph straddle; the straddle and the buffer kind are the two
parts of the refresh's diff-view mode ('RefreshMode::DiffView').

**** graph straddle, before-graph, after-graph

The *graph straddle* ('GraphStraddle { before, after }',
'server/graph_straddle/') is what the diff view compares. A straddle stands
with one foot in the committed graph and one in the live graph. The
*before-graph* is an in-Rust graph built from each owned skgrepo at
HEAD (each foreign skgrepo at its worktree); the *after-graph* is the
live graph.

**** union walk, existence across the straddle

The *union walk* is the API over the graph straddle that answers
questions about the union of its two graphs
('server/graph_straddle/union_walk.rs'). Each node and each
relationship it reports carries its existence across the straddle
('existence_across_straddle'): 'Existence::Added' when only the
after-graph holds it, 'Existence::Removed' when only the before-graph
does, and none when both do. A view shows it as
[[*the three kinds of change][the three kinds of change]].

**** fingerprint, the readiness store

Marks are stored in '<data_root>/readiness-marks.json', each with a
*fingerprint* of the change it approved. A mark whose fingerprint no
longer matches the change counts as absent, which is how editing a
marked change unmarks it.

**** content position

A viewnode is in *content position* when its viewparent is the buffer
root, an occurrence, or a ghost that is itself in content position
('in_content_position'). A ghost in
content position holds its old content under it.

**** change viewnode

A headline the diff view adds to describe a change
('ChangeViewnode'): a node's old text, with its body diff; its home
skgrepo move; its changeset markers; and, under a member, the relRepo
move of the member's relationship. At each refresh an occurrence's
change viewnodes are recomputed in place from the straddle: kept
while the change exists, drawn when missing, entombstoned when the
change is gone, the tombstone keeping what the user drew under it
('server/refresh/diff_view.rs'). The acquisition folders and the
changed folders are drawn the same way. Drawing one, or entombstoning
one whose change is gone, is no buffer correction, since a change viewnode
describes the straddle.

*** Terms of Tantivy, the search index

Tantivy is the search engine that holds the search index.

**** prominence source, prominence multiplier

A node's *prominence source* ('ProminenceSource') says why it is
prominent (see "prominent"). Each source has a *prominence
multiplier* applied to the text-match score.

**** Document

A Tantivy association. In my case, from a title or alias to an ID.
(Each ID can have multiple such associations.)
