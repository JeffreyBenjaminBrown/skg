* Glossary
Some terms and abbreviations used in this code.

** Terms and abbreviations specific to Skg

*** update-relevant neighborhood

For a set of IDs in one graph snapshot, the *update-relevant
neighborhood* contains the IDs themselves, their immediate neighbors in
both directions along =contains=, =linksTo=, =subscribesTo=, and
=hidesFromSubs=, and every node reachable by each of the
two monotonic override walks. The overriderward and overriddenward walks
start independently at the original IDs, never reverse direction, and
never continue from an ordinary neighbor. Thus it includes no mixed
ordinary/override path and no two-hop ordinary path. Saves compute this
neighborhood independently in the before and after graph snapshots.

*** "telescope" = privacy telescope

In code and comments the short form "telescope" always means the
privacy telescope; see "privacy telescope" below.

*** skgrepo, home skgrepo, skgrepo-set, gitrepo

A *skgrepo* is a directory of =.skg= files, configured by one
=[[repos]]= entry in =skgconfig.toml=. A node's *home skgrepo* is the
skgrepo that holds its home section (see below). A *skgrepo-set* is a
prefix of the config's privacy order: the skgrepos made available
together. A *gitrepo* is a Git repository. Skgrepos are usually gitrepos
too, but they need not be.

In prose, always say "skgrepo" or "gitrepo", never a bare "repo", so the
two cannot be confused. ("relRepo" is a single coined term, so it keeps
the short form.) Code says 'skgrepo' and 'gitrepo' too ('SkgrepoName',
'home_skgrepo'); a skgrepo's config entry is the 'Skgrepo' type.

The *skgrepo restriction* ('SkgrepoRestriction') is the skgrepo-set a
connection currently shows; the skgrepo-set =all= means no
restriction. A skgrepo inside it is *unrestricted*, one outside it
*restricted*, and so are their nodes: an unrestricted vognode is shown
in full, a restricted one only as an anonymous placeholder
(=restrictedNode=).

*** owned, foreign

A skgrepo is *owned* iff its directory lies under the config's
=owned_folder= (default =owned/=); every other skgrepo is *foreign*.
Only owned sections are ever written. A real edit to a foreign node is
read as a request to fork it (see "clone, and the fork operation").
Say "owned" rather than "owned", and "foreign" rather than
"write-protected", when speaking of skgrepos: write-protection is a
property of a viewnode occurrence (see "editable, write-protected").

*** privacy telescope, telescope section, home

One node can be recorded at several privacy levels at once: same-ID
=.skg= files across skgrepos, at most one per skgrepo. That family of
files is the node's *privacy telescope*; each file is one
*telescope section* ("section" for short in code), holding the
slice of the node recorded at its skgrepo's privacy level. The *home* is
the most public retained section; it alone holds title, body, and extra
ids. That the home carries the title is an invariant, not a
search rule: a node's text always lives in its most public
section, so a titleless section above the title is a violation
the composition reports (=CompositionWarning::TitleBelowHome=), not a shape to
look past. There is no linking relationship between sections —
sharing an ID is being the same node. See =docs/telescopes.org=
and =server/types/nodes/fs.rs=.

Before composing, an owned-PID collision is normalized: if any owned
section exists for a PID, the entire owned telescope is retained and all
same-PID foreign sections are ignored (with a deterministic warning and no
filesystem edit). A telescope with no owned section remains wholly foreign.

*** overPrivateText

A privacy telescope is *overPrivateText* when its composed title or body is
selected from a section more private than its home (most public retained)
section. The flag applies to the whole telescope, even if only one text
field is below home. This violates the rule that the node's text lives at
home; see [[file:./telescopes.org][privacy telescopes]].

Skgrepo-set =all= can show that text with a warning. A restricted request
asks for approval scoped to the affected node IDs and that request before
releasing it; search asks whether to include or exclude such telescopes.
Approval to display the text does not authorize *Hoist*, the separate
repair that moves the selected title and body to home.

*** relation, relationship, recorder, member, membership, partner

As in TypeDB, a *relation* is a kind of relationship: =contains=,
=linksTo=, =subscribesTo=, =hidesFromSubs=, =overrides=. Each names
its two roles: container contains content, mentioner linksTo
mentioned, subscriber subscribesTo subscribee, overrider overrides
overridden, hider hidesFromSubs hidden. =hidesFromSubs= is short for
"hides from its subscriptions": the hider hides the hidden node from
what the hider sees through its subscriptions. A *relationship* is one instance of a relation: this
node contains that one. (Graph theory would call it an edge; we don't,
because the word is opaque to newcomers.)

A relationship is recorded in the .skg file of one of its two nodes, its
*recorder*: the node playing the relation's first role (the container,
the mentioner, the subscriber, the hider, the overrider). The other node
is a *member* of the recorder's list. Either end is the other's
*partner*. In a view, a folder's children are *members* too: entries of
one monomorphic list, usually all partners on the same side of some
relation with the folder's viewparent.

A *membership* is the relationship that causes a node to be drawn
as a member of a list or folder; the member is the node.

The node that has an ID, as its PID or as an extra ID, is that ID's
*carrier*. (Not "owner": the user *owns* skgrepos, and a
viewnode's [[*owner][owner]] is a reconciler.)

*** privacy order and relRepo

The position of a skgrepo in the config's privacy order (most public
first) is its privacy level. Live code does not use "level" for the
value carried by a relationship: every relationship member carries its
*relRepo*, the skgrepo whose section records it. In code
that is =RelPartner { relRepo, member }=; a collection of them is
a *list of relation partners*. "More private" = later in the
privacy order.

*** home skgrepo move, relRepo move

A *home skgrepo move* is a change of a node's home skgrepo (=C-c s s=,
then a save); a *relRepo move* is a change of a relationship's relRepo
(=C-c s r=, or a save that resets or raises it). The diff view shows
each move since HEAD as one change-forest leaf: a home skgrepo move as
the property "⌂ -OLD +NEW" under the node, a relRepo move as the
property "relationship moved from OLD to NEW" under the member, keyed
by the relationship, so the same move seen from either end is the
same leaf. A relRepo move puts only the recorder in C. The
skgrepo-changes buffer of the diff view shows the nodes whose home
skgrepo moved and the recorders of the relationships whose relRepo
moved, each with all of its changes. Say "home skgrepo move", not
"home move".

*** MSV = maybe specified vector

=MSV<T>= distinguishes "no opinion" from "empty". =Unspecified= says a
file section or an edit has nothing to say about the field;
=Specified(vec![])= says the field is explicitly empty. Telescope
folding and partial nodeIntents both depend on the difference.

*** composition (visible vs full), decomposition

The *composition* of a telescope combines its sections, most public
first, into one effective node (=server/telescope/compose.rs=). The
*full composition* uses every section; a *visible composition* uses only the
sections unrestricted under the current skgrepo restriction, and is what a
restricted view renders. *Decomposition* is the save-side inverse:
splitting the effective node back into sections, byte-stably.

*** placement, more public anchor

How a more private section orders its members into a more public
list without the public file recording anything: in the section's
stored sequence, a =- anchor: ID= entry names a *more public anchor*,
a member of the more public list, and the members after it insert
immediately after that ID; members before the first anchor prepend.
Placements exist only on disk — more public anchors are derived at
save from the in-memory order, so they can never go stale in memory.
Say "more public anchor", never a bare "anchor".

*** sticky rule (sticky-else-default)

Where a relationship's relRepo comes from at save time:
a relationship already on disk keeps its relRepo (*sticky*); a new relationship
between owned nodes defaults to the more-private endpoint home. A new
relationship from an owned recorder to a foreign member defaults to the recorder's
home, deliberately making the foreign ID and relationship visible at
that owned skgrepo without proposing a foreign write. Every relRepo is
clamped to be no more public than the recorder's home. Hides
floor higher (at least the most public subscription explaining them). A
=(relRepo NAME)= atom (the =skg-set-relRepo= gesture)
overrides sticky with any skgrepo at least as private as the relationship's
default — raising privacy, or moving a stuck skgrepo back to the
default; a more-public choice is a save error. A legacy or
hand-authored relationship whose disk relRepo already sits more public than
its default may be held or made more private, never made still more
public. See =server/from_text/supplement_from_disk.rs=.

**** stranded relationship, strandeds

A home skgrepo move can strand a relationship touching the moved
node: a *stranded relationship* is one that sat at its default relRepo
before the move and that the sticky rule would leave off its new
default. *Strandeds* is the noun for stranded relationships; "strand"
is only a verb. A save finds every one, of every relation, in both
directions, shown in some view or not ('server/from_text/strandeds.rs').
It raises the inbound strandeds of a privatizing move to the new home
by itself, since they would leak the member's ID, and asks the user
whether to reset the strandeds of a publicizing move to their new
defaults (the strandeds-confirmation). Mentions carry no relRepo, so
they are never stranded.

*** view, buffer, viewforest

A *view* is what the server tracks for one open window onto the graph:
a content view or a search view, identified by its 'ViewId'. A
content view may have several roots; the request that opens one today
asks for a single root, so only the request is "single-root". A
*buffer* is the editor's container for one view (an Emacs or Neovim
buffer). A view's *viewforest* is its tree of viewnodes, under a
buffer root; say "viewforest" rather than "viewtree" or "forest".

A *viewbranch* is any subtree of a viewforest: a viewnode with all its
descendants. A view request asks the server to add viewbranches: a
folder, a role tree, the flags folder. A viewbranch may be degenerate,
with one viewchild or none; compare "branching viewnode".

*** "child" and "parent"

They are shorthand for 'viewchild' and 'viewparent' (likewise
viewancestor, viewdescendant). Code may use the short forms, since it
almost always handles one tree at a time. Docs should spell out the
long forms wherever a reader might mistake a child for content, which
in user-facing docs is almost everywhere.

There are two concepts of node in skg: graphnode and viewnode.
Viewnodes are nodes in a view, and some of them correspond to nodes in the graph,
but some of them don't -- they describe their parent,
or the relationship between their parent and their children,
or maybe something else.

The Skg graph contains a few (currently five) formal relationships -- =contains=,
=linksTo=, etc. In the graph, there is no concept of parent or child;
those are only meaningful in a view.
Where the code says "child" or "parent", it means the relationship between viewnodes
-- e.g., in Emacs, the relationship between headlines in an Org buffer
-- and might or might not correspond to a graph relation.

For one example, see the discussion of alias nodes in
[[file:../coding-advice/architecture.md][the architecture documentation]].

*** col

*Col* was the old name for a folder viewnode (short for
"collection"). It has been expunged from production code, but may still
appear in old documentation, plans, or the knowledge graph.

*** PartnerFolder, and the folder-policy vocabulary

**** The idea

Recall that viewnodes and graphnodes are distinct.
Some viewnodes correspond to individual graphnodes.
Other viewnodes mediate a relationship between their parent and their children.
For instance, a 'subscribee folder' viewnode contains it's parent's subscribees:
#+begin_example
  * subscriber
  ** subscribee folder
  *** subscribee
  *** subscribee
#+end_example

In that example, the first viewnode represents a graphnode,
as do the last two. The subscriber subscribes to the two subscribees.
Ths second viewnode, though, corresponds to nothing in the graph.
It exists only to name the relationship between its children and its parent.

A *PartnerFolder* is the general concept.
Recall that there are five relations in the graph:
containment, link, subscription, hiding, and overriding.
Because a node can be on either side of each relationship --
it can be the container or the content, the hider or the hidden, etc. --
there are many kinds of PartnerFolder. Each lists, as its children,
the nodes that play one role toward its parent. For each relation,
one folder could list the members its parent records, and another the
recorders that record its parent:

| relation      | members its parent records | recorders of its parent |
|---------------+----------------------------+-------------------------|
| containment   | not implemented[1]         | containerFolder         |
| link          | not implemented[2]         | mentionerFolder         |
| subscription  | subscribeeFolder           | subscriberFolder        |
| hiding        | hiddenFolder               | hiderFolder             |
| overriding    | overriddenFolder           | overriderFolder         |

1. A contentFolder would be silly: a node's contents are already its
   ordinary viewchildren.
2. A mentionedFolder would be silly: the links are already visible in
   the node's own text.

Two more PartnerFolders filter the hiding relation by subscription:
- a *hiddenInSubscribeeFolder*, under a subscribee as such, lists that
  subscribee's contents which the subscriber hides;
- a *hiddenOutsideOfSubscribeeFolder*, under a subscribeeFolder, lists
  the nodes its parent hides that no subscribee contains.

The diff view adds two more, for nodeMerges since HEAD:
- an *acquiredByFolder* ("it was acquired by"), under an acquiree's
  historical phantom, holds its acquirer;
- an *acquiredFolder* ("it acquired"), under an acquirer, holds the
  historical phantoms of the nodes it acquired.

**** Some extremely technical details

It is the =ViewnodeKind::PartnerFolder= variant and the payload
type of the same name.  “Folder” here describes an org non-vognode that groups
viewnodes; it does not mean a filesystem directory.  “Role col”, “relation
col”, and bare “sharing col” are retired names for the general concept.

There are twelve PartnerFolders, named by their external non-vognode
atom. Each kind's behavior is decided by one method,
=PartnerFolder::policy= (=server/types/viewnode.rs=), returning a
*FolderPolicy*.  This single arbiter is consulted by reconciliation
dispatch, save extraction's write-protected treatment, and
herald/warning gating, so a new relation cannot acquire inconsistent
policies in different layers:

- *EditableSet* (=subscribeeFolder=, =overriddenFolder=): membership edits
  are graph edits.  An absent folder means "no opinion" (its field lowers
  to =MSV::Unspecified=, filled from disk); a present-but-empty folder
  means an explicit empty set (=MSV::Specified(vec![])=).
- *EditableFilter* (=hiddenOutsideOfSubscribeeFolder=): membership is
  derived from hide state, and a visible member set is an explicit
  edit of that subset; the hides' relRepos stay derived.
- *WriteProtectedSet*: membership is generated from graph facts; the
  user's order is respected view-locally; repairs warn.  See
  [[*write-protected set][write-protected set]].  These are the inbound folders
  =subscriberFolder=, =overriderFolder=, =hiderFolder= and
  =containerFolder= and =mentionerFolder= (the folders of =C-c l c= and
  =C-c l m=; the other side of each is the node's content, or its
  links, shown without a folder), the outbound =hiddenFolder=, and the
  diff view's =acquiredByFolder= and =acquiredFolder=, which are
  derived from the graph straddle and save-inert.
- *WriteProtectedFilter* (=hiddenInSubscribeeFolder=): membership is
  derived from hide state rather than from a single relation role.

The write-protected folders (those of the two write-protected
policies) carry the ☮ herald on their folder headline, meaning "this
folder cannot be changed from here" — the same sense ☮
(=writeProtected=) carries on a node.  The editable folders and
=aliasFolder= do not.  This is a herald-table fact only
(=server/heralds.rs=), with no wire atom of its own: which folders are
write-protected is already known from the folder atom.

Every policy's reconciler obeys [[*the one rule: convert, then prune][the one rule]]: a stale member or
an extra copy of a member is converted, not detached, and missing
graph members are restored.  A non-member standing for a node the
folder's goal lists again is adopted back as its member.

*Default presentation is intentionally narrow.* An editable node gets a
nonempty =subscribeeFolder= automatically. The other top-level relation
folders require an explicit folder request, even when their herald counts say
they would be nonempty. The same rule applies when a member of any
PartnerFolder is extended as editable. A subscribee-as-such also gets its
nonempty =hiddenInSubscribeeFolder=; this is the filter specific to how that
subscriber sees the subscribee.

*A write-protected folder's children stay where they stand.*  Its
reconciler moves no child it keeps, a non-member parked inside it
included, and puts a member it creates right after the member that
precedes it in the folder's goal list (=KeptChildOrder::Unmoved= in
=complete_managed_children=; see "What a reconciler keeps" in
=docs/diff-view.org=).  The alias folder and the editable folders,
whose order the save writes, follow the graph's order instead
(=KeptChildOrder::FollowsGoal=): a non-member parked in one of them
goes above the members, which follow the graph's order.

*** role tree, role graft, and the -ward adjectives
A *role tree* is extended under a node, its origin, by following one
relation role away from it: under the origin go the nodes playing that
role toward it, under each of those the nodes playing the role toward
them, and so on. A role, unlike a relation, is directed, so the tree
goes one way. A role-tree request '(roleTree ROLE)' extends one (e.g.
'skg-show-mentionerward-tree'); search enrichment extends containerward
and override role trees under results. A role tree extended on request
follows single partners until a node has several, then includes that
first layer; a full containerward role tree ('ContainerwardRoleTree')
goes as deep as 'max_roleTree_depth'.

A *role graft* is one node of a role tree other than its origin. It is
in the view because of the role, not because its parent contains it, so
it carries 'nonMember' and a birth fact naming its role's
relation. ("Role" is in the name because other things might someday be
grafted onto views.)

The adjectives containerward, mentionerward, overriderward,
overriddenward, and so on name the role a role tree follows: a
containerward role tree follows the container role.

*** view-lifetime

Some data regarding a node is view-lifetime data
(viewStats, for instance):
it lasts only as long as the view of it in some buffer.

Data on disk, by contrast,
remains when the app terminates,
and can be recovered by a later session.
*Session-lifetime* data lasts as long as one server session: the
in-Rust graph and the search index, together the *session-lifetime
stores*. The server rebuilds them from the .skg files when it starts,
or when asked ('skg-rebuild-session-lifetime-stores'); they outlive
every view. Say which lifetime is meant, never "ephemeral".

*** branching viewnode (vs a branch)

A *branching viewnode* is a viewnode with more than one viewchild. Do
not confuse it with a *branch*: any viewnode together with its
descendants, which may be degenerate, its root having one viewchild
or none.

A role tree is extended as paths that each end at their first
"nonlinearity" ('PathToFirstNonlinearity'): a cycle, or a node with
several partners in the direction walked, which the path shows
as a branching viewnode. (The path code, which walks the graph before
anything is rendered, calls that node a *branching graphnode*: one with
several partners in the direction walked, e.g. containerward-branching.) None of this is
related to forking a node, below.

*** clone, and the fork operation

To *fork* a node N is to turn it into an editable *clone* C in a
skgrepo you own. C copies N's title, body, and child-list (the child IDs
only — shallow), =subscribesTo= N (so N's later additions surface to C
as unintegrated subscribed content) and =overrides= N (so
override substitution shows C wherever N would appear). Children of N
that the forking edit deleted become C's
=hidesFromSubs=, and thereafter any child removed from
an owned subscriber's contains that some subscribee still contains is
hidden likewise on save (re-adding it unhides). A node may have at
most one clone you own (override monogamy).

Forking spans two gestures, foreign and owned:

- /implicit/ (foreign N): make N editable (=C-c s d=), edit it, and
  save; any real change to a foreign node is read as a fork request, and
  the save asks you to confirm. N is left untouched on disk.
- /explicit/ (owned N): =skg-fork-node= (=C-c m f=), for a node you
  already own — used to deepen an override chain (D overrides C overrides
  N, all owned). N keeps its own content; only the clone is added. The
  clone is built from N's saved state, so the command refuses a dirty
  buffer.

See =docs/forks.org= and =docs/sharing-model.org=.

*** kv-pair = key-value pair

*** fieldIntent, nodeIntent, nodeInstruction, savePlan

What a save turns the buffer into, in order:
- a *fieldIntent* ('FieldIntent') says what one position in the buffer
  wants for one field of one node (its title and body, its contains,
  a hide, ...). Local fieldIntent collection emits these into a map
  keyed by ID ('CollectedFieldIntents').
- a *nodeIntent* ('NodeIntent') is all of one node's fieldIntents,
  lowered into a save or a delete. It may still be partial: fields
  the buffer said nothing about are 'MSV::Unspecified'.
- a *nodeInstruction* ('NodeInstruction': 'Save' or 'Delete') is a
  nodeIntent after disk supplementation: complete, ready to write.
- the *savePlan* ('SavePlan') is everything one save applies: its
  nodeInstructions, plus forks and nodeMerges.

*** local fieldIntent collection

The save path's fieldIntent extraction: one pure DFS preorder over
the buffer's viewforest, in which each position reads only itself and
its direct children, plus a context that flows down from its
ancestors. Each visit emits fieldIntents into a map keyed by
node ID; downstream stages (validation, lowering, visibility
resolution, disk supplementation, the noop filter) consume the map.
Lives in =server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/=, entered
via =extract_nonmergeSavePlan_locally=.

This entry is the canonical sketch of the save pipeline. Its
surrounding stages are, in order: parse, enrich, validate, place,
extract (this entry), foreign-node validation, nodeMerge build, graph
update, rerender.

*** lp = length-prefixed

Sometimes an API response is prefixed with a length, letting the receiver know how many characters to expect in the response.

*** An 'm' prefix

In Haskell, 'm' is sometimes prefixed to a word to indicate 'maybe'.
The Rust term for Maybe is Optional, but I've used the Haskell idiom.
For any function with a name like this, if its meaning is unclear,
the type signature should clarify what's going on.

*** metadata

Most headlines are prefixed with some metadata.
In Emacs, that metadata is represented as an s-expression like
(skg (key value) bare_value ...),
with any number of key-value and bare-value pairs in any order.
The metadata is used for Rust to know what to do with it,
and for the client to let the user know how to interpret it.
It is not abbreviated "md", which means Markdown (as in
=import-md-and-org=).

*** display fact vs save input

Metadata holds two kinds of thing. A *display fact* is computed by the
server each time it renders: viewStats, the =(rels ...)= form,
=omittedBody=, the display =(relRepo NAME)=. The parser ignores or
discards display facts, so editing them changes nothing. *Save input*
is what a save reads and applies: titles, bodies, the order and
membership of children, and =editRequest= atoms. The one load-bearing
display fact is =(overridesHere N)=; see "override substitution".

*** view request vs edit request

Both are requests the client writes into metadata and the server
consumes. A *view request* (=(viewRequests ...)=: =folder=, =roleTree=,
=flags=, =editableView=, =writeProtectedView=,
=writeProtectedSubtreeView=) asks for more of the view (viewbranches),
or changes which occurrence of a node is editable; the server fulfills
it while completing the view and then drops it. (=fork= is also
written as a view request, but the save consumes it.) An *edit
request* (=(editRequest ...)=: =delete=, =merge=, =flag=, =relRepo=, and in
the diff view =restoreRel= on a removed membership and =restoreNode= on a
historical phantom) asks the save to change the graph; a successful save
consumes it, and a failed save leaves it in the buffer. The two restore
requests are described in 'docs/diff-view.org', "Restoring what a change
removed". A *readiness request* (see
"readiness request, readiness gesture") is a third kind: the save
consumes it after its graph edits succeed, to mark or unmark changes
commit-ready, and changes neither the view nor the graph.

*** gesture

A *gesture* is a client command that writes requests into headline
metadata for the server to apply at a save. The relRepo gesture
(=skg-set-relRepo=), the flag gesture (=skg-set-flag-search-matching=),
the fork gesture (=skg-fork-node=) and the readiness gestures are
kinds. The fork and readiness gestures save at once; the others leave
the save to the user.

*** collateral view

When a save changes nodes that other open views show, those views are
*collateral*: the server rerenders them and streams each as a
=collateral-view= message. The save envelope's =other-views= lists every
other open view; the collateral views are the subset that needs a
rerender.

*** startup warnings channel

How the server's startup warnings reach the user: 'load_config'
returns warnings beside the config, 'main' records them once on the
runtime ('record_startup_warnings' in 'server/types/env.rs'), the
server serves them on request ("server warnings"), and each client
shows them in its '*SKG Server Warnings*' buffer on connect.

*** mk = make / create

*** "merge" is always qualified: nodeMerge vs instructionMerge

A /nodeMerge/ is the acquirer/acquiree operation on graphnodes (the
=NodeMerge= type, requested via =(editRequest (merge ID))=). An
/instructionMerge/ is the combining of emitted save intents into the
map during local fieldIntent collection. In prose, say "merge nodes"
or "merge instructions"; bare "merge" appears only in client-facing
wire strings, where only nodes can merge.

*** text preserver

A nodeMerge keeps the acquiree's text in a new node, its *text
preserver*: titled "MERGED: " and the acquiree's title, with the
acquiree's body, and placed first in the acquirer's content. The
acquiree's other content joins the acquirer's, and the acquiree's ID
becomes an extra ID of the acquirer. See =docs/merging.org=.

*** "snapshot" is always qualified: graph, git or buffer snapshot

A /graph snapshot/ is one immutable state of the in-Rust graph
('GraphSnapshot'). A /git snapshot/ is one of HEAD, the git index or
the worktree; 'server/git_ops/git_snapshot.rs' reads HEAD and the
worktree (the graph straddle's before-graph reads HEAD). A /buffer snapshot/ is a
buffer's text as a client sends it: for search enrichment, or for a
dirty view in the save envelope. The wire atoms 'request-snapshot' and
'snapshot response' predate the rule and mean buffer snapshots.

*** "index" is always qualified: search, git or graph index

The /search index/ is Tantivy's. The /git index/ is git's staging
area; next to HEAD and worktree it needs no qualifier ("HEAD vs
index"). A /graph index/ is a derived map inside the in-Rust graph:
the /recorderward relmaps/ ('contained_by', 'subscribers_of', ...), plus
'extra_id_to_pid'. A recorderward relmap maps each member of one
relation to the recorders that list it. (Not "inverse": today every
relationship is recorded by its first member, but the data model may
someday let the second member record one; 'RecorderwardRelmap' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph.rs' says more.)

*** ancestor generation

How far up the view an ancestor is: 1 is the viewparent, 2 the
grandparent, counting folders. The '(rels ...)' form's '(ancestors GEN
...)' and a birth fact's GEN are ancestor generations, and heralds
show them as a, b, ... z, then {27}. A tree's plain "generation",
counting down from its root, is the opposite direction.

*** "commit" is qualified when ambiguous: git-, skgsave- or tantivy-commit

A /git-commit/ is git's. A /skgsave-commit/ is the step of a save that
writes the .skg files and swaps in the new graph; a save that stops to
ask for confirmation (forks, Hoist) skgsave-commits nothing. A
/tantivy-commit/ makes search-index writes visible; a save's happens
later, on a background worker. Where context leaves no doubt (HEAD, a
merge commit, a 'git2' call) bare "commit" means a git-commit.

*** swap in (a runtime generation), vs publish

A save, rebuild or import ends by atomically *swapping in* a new
runtime generation (config, graph and search index): readers holding
the old one keep it, and the next reader gets the new one. "Publish"
means only making text more public: sharing a skgrepo, or writing text
into a more public telescope section. Hoist /moves/ text to home.

*** mp = maybe-placed

A viewnode that might have an ID and skgrepo,
but might not, is maybe-placed.

*** override substitution, and the vocabulary around it

When view completion would create a viewnode for node N as
recursive content, it shows the effective overrider of N instead:
the end of the chain of owned, currently-visible
'overrides' relationships leading out of N. The chain is often one
relationship, but may be longer (an owned chain D overrides C overrides
N is legal; only a cycle is forbidden), and visibility gating can
stop it at a middle node when a later link's mentioner is restricted --
so the shown node is the last visible link, not necessarily the
chain's end. The resolver is 'resolve_override' in
'server/dbs/in_rust_graph/override_resolution.rs'. Foreign override
relationships are display-only facts and never substitute.

The shown substitute carries the fact '(overridesHere N)'
(the crucial herald =ĥ= after =O=), naming the original it stands for. A viewnode's
collected ID is what it contributes to its parent's saved lists:
the fact's original if present, else its own ID -- so saving a
view that shows R in place of N keeps N in the parent's contains.

An overridden-as-such is a member child of an overriddenFolder --
the position that always shows the original; its editable
extension applies neither the recorder's hides nor substitution to
its immediate children.

An ordinary single-root visit always opens the requested node itself,
shown raw. In enriched search results, each result has an overrideward
view-subtree containing its visible override relatives in both
directions. Override facts also appear in explicitly requested override
folders and role trees.

*** "subscribee as such"

In Skg some nodes are "subscribers", which "subscribe" to "subscribees".
(See [[file:./data-model_technical.org][the technical data model]].)
A node that plays the 'subscribee' role can be viewed as an ordinary node,
or /as/ a subscribee. In the latter case it appears
underneath the relevant subscriber, in a 'subscribeeFolder':
#+begin_example
  * subscriber
  ** subscribeeFolder
  *** subsribee1
  *** subsribee2
  ...
#+end_example

In the above view, subscribee1 and subscribee2 are both "subscribees as such":
They are not just subscribees, but they are being shown /as/ subscribees.
This changes the way edits are interpreted:
Edits to a "subscribee as such" can only affect
what the relevant subscriber hides, nothing else.

*** write-protected set

A write-protected set is a generated folder whose membership follows
from graph facts rather than direct edits to that folder.  A view
may preserve an order for the set locally, but adding or removing an
item in the generated folder does not add or remove the underlying
graph membership fact.  If a user labels a nonmember inside such a set,
the save treats that item as independent of the generated membership.
If completion discovers that a real member is missing from the view, it
restores that member.

For instance, if nodes R and T subscribe to nodes N, the user might land
at a view like the following:
#+begin_example
  * N
  ** subscriberFolder
  *** R
  *** T
#+end_example
The subscriberFolder ("folder of subscribers") is a write-protected set.
Thus the user could flip the order of R and T in the subscriberFolder,
and this change would be respected in the display, but have no effect
in the graph. The user could insert X under subscriberFolder,
but because it is a write-protected set, X will be
marked 'nonMember' (a non-member), and have no effect on N.
(If the user wants X to subscribe to N, they must modify X, not N.)
The user could delete R, but it will respawn as soon as they save,
because, again, the subscriberFolder is a write-protected set.

*** graphnode, viewnode, vognode, non-vognode

A *graphnode* is a node of the graph; a *viewnode* is a node of a view.
Say "graphnode" and "viewnode" rather than "graph node" or "view node";
bare "node" is fine where only one kind is possible.

A *vognode* is a viewnode representing a graphnode ('Vognode' in
'server/types/viewnode.rs'). It is one of three kinds:
- *unrestricted*: the graphnode is in an unrestricted skgrepo, so the view shows it;
- *restricted*: it is in a restricted skgrepo, so the view shows only an
  anonymous, dataless placeholder;
- a *phantom*: the graphnode is no longer in the graph, or never was.

The other viewnodes are *non-vognodes*: folders, properties, the buffer
root and dead viewnodes. They describe their viewparent, or its
relationships, rather than standing for a graphnode.

*** occurrence

One appearance of a graphnode in a view. A graphnode can occur several
times in one buffer. Facts that depend on the position (viewStats such
as =cycle=, the displayed relRepo, =overridesHere=; and the birth) belong
to the occurrence; graphStats belong to the graphnode.

*** view id, vid

A *view id* identifies one viewnode within one buffer, for the life of
the buffer: the atom =(vid X)= that ends every viewnode's metadata
('docs/api-and-formats.org', "The metadata headers"). "vid" is the
wire's word and the field's name ('Viewnode::vid'); prose, names and
messages say "view id". The view gives one to each viewnode that lacks
one at the end of a completion and never reuses one, so a vid that
reappears after a cut and paste names the same viewnode moved. The
view's 'ViewIdRecord' ('server/types/views_state.rs') holds the counter
and the owner of each viewnode a reconciler made. A view id is not a
view's 'ViewId' (see "view, buffer, viewforest"), which names the whole
buffer.

*** editable, write-protected

Each occurrence of an unrestricted vognode is either *editable* or
*write-protected* ('Editability' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'). Edits to
an editable occurrence's title, body and children are saved; a
write-protected occurrence shows no body and saves nothing of its own,
so a save is refused if one differs from the graph (in its title,
skgrepo, aliases, flags or partner folders) or has a child that
affects its parent.
The server adds no such child under one. A buffer may hold at most one editable occurrence of a node,
so that two occurrences cannot contradict each other. Make one
editable with =skg-set-editable= (=C-c s d=), which writes a
=editableView= view request; make one write-protected with
=skg-request-write-protected= (=C-c s w=; =C-c s W= for its whole
content subtree), which writes a =writeProtectedView= or
=writeProtectedSubtreeView= view request.

*** non-member

A viewnode that takes no part in the membership its viewparent
represents: its metadata carries the bare atom 'nonMember', where a
member's carries nothing ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "Content
view"). Under a vognode a non-member is not in the content list,
and in a folder it is not one of the folder's members, so a save
neither adds nor removes a relationship for it; what it shows of
its own node is saved or not by its editability, as at any
occurrence. A role graft is a non-member, and so are a removed
member, every vognode child of a write-protected occurrence, a
note the user parks in a folder, and what
[[*the one rule: convert, then prune][the one rule]] makes of a
stale vognode with a subtree.

*** editability rule, editable map

The *editability rule* decides, at each occurrence view completion
reaches in level order, whether it is editable: an occurrence without
a request is editable when its node is unclaimed in the *editable
map* and write-protected when another occurrence claimed it first.
The editable map is built empty at every completion, and only an
editable occurrence claims. The module comment of
'server/to_org/extend/editable.rs' states the whole rule, with the
cascade and the node budget.

*** view completion, extend, render, rerender

*View completion* extends a viewforest: it adds viewnodes to one
that stands ('complete_viewforest'). Extending is countable: an
*extension* is one editable vognode's content list, drawn whole,
and is what the node budget counts ('initial_node_limit'; a
folder fills its members without spending any). The module
comment of 'server/to_org/extend/editable.rs' states the budget.
The server *renders* a viewforest when it produces its org text
('server/org_to_text.rs'); a *rerender* is the request that
recompletes a view and sends its text. Org folding keeps the
words "fold" and "unfold".

*** reconciler

One of the two functions that bring an editable node's children
in line with what they should be: the older one
('complete_managed_children'), which reconciles the content list
and each folder's members in every view, and the diff view's
('reconcile_children_with_shallowModelSubtree'), which reconciles
the change viewnodes, the removed members and what stands below
them with a shallow model subtree. Both obey
[[*the one rule: convert, then prune][the one rule]].

*** managed child

A child viewnode is managed by the reconciler that owns it (its
[[*owner][owner]]): that reconciler matches the child by its
[[*key, match key][key]] against what it should show, and may
convert it
([[*the one rule: convert, then prune][the one rule]]). The older
reconciler finds the children it manages by predicates on their
kind and membership ('complete_managed_children'), and records
itself as the owner of each member it keeps; the diff view's
reconciler reads the view's record of owners, and manages the
children it owns of the kinds the model of each call concerns,
since it runs several models under one parent.

*** key, match key

A child's *key* is what a reconciler matches it by: for an
occurrence of a graphnode, the node's ID as the live graph
resolves it (so one shown by an extra ID matches its node); for
an entry of a property folder, the entry itself; for any other
viewnode, its kind. In the older reconciler it is the key of a
goal item ('Orderkey' in 'complete_managed_children'). The diff
view's reconciler calls it the *match key* ('MatchKey'), and
matches an unknown phantom by its raw ID and a historical phantom
by its ID at HEAD.

*** owner

The reconciler that created a viewnode, or nobody when the user
typed or pasted it ('Owner' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'): the
content reconciler, a folder's reconciler (one per folder kind),
the role-tree attach (which grafts a role tree under its origin),
the diff view's reconciler, or the stub roots (which make a
view's roots). A member of a content list is owned by the content
reconciler and a member of a folder by that folder's reconciler,
whoever typed it, since membership is a fact of the graph that
its reconciler maintains. The owner is recorded per
[[*view id, vid][view id]] in the view's state ('ViewIdRecord'),
never inferred from a viewnode's shape. In the diff view a member
that stops being one, because its relationship is in the
before-graph only or its viewparent became a historical phantom,
becomes the diff view's reconciler's, which shows it as removed.

*** the one rule: convert, then prune

A reconciler never detaches a child. A child it manages that the
graph no longer warrants -- a stale child, whose key the
reconciler's model lacks, or an extra copy of a key a sibling has
-- is *converted* ('convert_unwarranted_child' in
'server/update_buffer/util.rs'): an unrestricted vognode with a
subtree becomes a write-protected [[*non-member][non-member]],
which keeps its title, ID and birth, loses its change facts,
holds no member (its members become non-members) and no editable
claim, and can be adopted back when its membership returns; a
leaf, and any viewnode of another kind (a phantom, a restricted
vognode, a property, a folder), becomes a
[[*dead viewnode][dead viewnode]]. Of several copies of one key,
the one kept as the member is the editable one, else the first.
After every completion the postorder *prune*
('prune_self_deletable_when_empty' in
'server/update_buffer/complete.rs') removes the childless dead
viewnodes (and childless deleted phantoms and empty
write-protected folders), never a view root. A viewnode nobody
owns -- one the user typed or pasted -- is never converted, so it
holds its ancestors alive: whatever stands below a converted
child survives under it.

The diff view's reconciler converts by the same function, and
first converts what was made below the stale child for the same
model ('convert_unwarranted_subtree' in
'server/diff_view/shallow_model_subtree.rs'): postorder, every
viewnode below it but those nobody owns and the content
reconciler's (which a parked vognode keeps, as the older
reconciler's does), so that a folder's members go with the folder
and a member's role tree with the member. A viewnode left holding
only childless dead viewnodes is converted as a leaf.

A converted copy is the one duplicate shape the save accepts:
under an editable parent two editable copies of a node are
refused, and so are two members, in a content list or a folder.

The orphaned folder ('deaden_generalized_orphan_folder') and the
skgrepo-set switch ('convert_and_prune_for_skgrepo_switch')
convert by the same rule: a member of a folder whose viewparent
died, and a deleted or unknown phantom that a now restricted
skgrepo records.

*** dead viewnode

A viewnode ('ViewnodeKind::DeadViewnode', '(skg deadViewnode)')
with no title, ID, facts or key, which only holds what stands
below it. It is what the one rule makes of an unwarranted leaf or
non-vognode. The prune removes it once it is childless; the
parser discards text typed on its line; its member children
become non-members ('mark_orphans_under_dead_parents_false');
every folder accepts one among its children.

*** birth

Why a generated occurrence was created under its parent ('Birth'
in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'): either unremarkable, or a role
graft naming its role. It is view history, used for display,
stale-relation validation and the diff view, never by save
extraction. The =(rels ...)= form renders it as =(birth ...)=
facts. A role graft's birth is also written into its metadata as
=(birth roleGraft ROLENAME)= and read back, so it survives a
save; the diff view reads from it which relationship a graft
stands for.

*** phantom

A vognode standing for something that is not a current graph member
('Phantom' in 'server/types/viewnode.rs'). There are three kinds:
*Historical* (in the diff view, a node HEAD has and the worktree
lacks; it shows HEAD's title and body), *Deleted* (a save just deleted
it; it keeps its last title and body), and *Unknown* (a reference that
resolves to nothing; see "unknown node"). Phantoms are save-inert.

*** unknown node, dangling ID

An *unknown node* is an ID that some node's contains, subscribesTo,
hidesFromSubs or overrides list names but that no node of the graph
answers to, as primary or extra ID; such an ID is *dangling*. A view
shows it as an unknown phantom ("Reference to unknown node.").
The "investigate unknown node" request says what git history knows of
it, and the delete-references-to-absent-node command removes the
references.

*** save-inert

A viewnode is *save-inert* if it emits no fieldIntents: phantoms,
restricted vognodes, and the diff view's properties, among
them removed aliases and IDs. Its descendants may still emit them, so save
extraction still visits them.

*** title != headline

Headline is a term from org-mode. It refers to a line that begins with some asterisks, followed by at least one whitespace, followed by at least one non-whitespace. The text after the first chunk of adjacent whitespace is what I'm calling the 'title'.

** Terms of the diff view

'docs/diff-view.org' describes the diff view; these are its
terms, and they mean nothing outside it.

*** diff view

Four buffers that show every change in the owned skgrepos since their
last commits, with the context needed to understand them: "all
changes", "merges", "skgrepo changes" and "order-only changes". Each is
an ordinary editable view. Foreign skgrepos are excluded; the
readiness summary only counts their changed files.

*** buffer kind

Which of the four buffers of the diff view a buffer is
('DiffViewBufferKind'). A buffer of the diff view is rendered over the
graph straddle; the straddle and the buffer kind are separate fields
of the completion context.

*** pruned change view

The viewforest the "all changes" buffer renders, defined by the sets
C, R, U and D (below; 'server/diff_view/pruned_change_view.rs',
functions 'prunedChangeView_...'). The merges and skgrepo-changes
buffers render pruned change views over subsets of the changes: the
nodeMerges, and the home skgrepo and relRepo moves.

*** graph straddle, before-graph, after-graph

The *graph straddle* ('GraphStraddle { before, after }',
'server/graph_straddle/') is what the diff view compares. A straddle stands
with one foot in the committed graph and one in the live graph. The
*before-graph* is an in-Rust graph built from each owned skgrepo at
HEAD (each foreign skgrepo at its worktree); the *after-graph* is the
live graph.

*** union walk, presence

The *union walk* is the API over the graph straddle that answers
questions about the union of its two graphs. Each node and each
relationship it reports carries a *presence*: 'Both', 'OnlyBefore' or
'OnlyAfter'. A view shows it as [[*the three kinds of change][the three kinds of change]].

*** the three kinds of change

The first three parts of a headline's '(diff ...)' form, each written
only when true ('DiffChange'): the node's existence, '(node added)'
or '(node removed)' (herald ∃+, ∃-), when only the after-graph or
only the before-graph has it ('GraphStraddle::node_existence'); the
existence of its relationship to its viewparent, '(rel added)' or
'(rel removed)' (herald ~+, ~-); and the node's other properties,
'(properties changed)' (herald M, "modified"), on a node in both
graphs that records at least one change-forest leaf
('GraphStraddle::node_properties_changed'). The two existences are
'Existence { Added, Removed }'. A member, a removed member, a folder's
member and a root carry the node's two; a phantom never carries
'(properties changed)'. The relationship is the viewparent's content
under a vognode, the relation of its birth role on a role graft, and,
in a partner folder, the relationship of the folder's kind between
the member and the folder's viewparent; the two filter folders' members
carry none. On an alias, ID or flag entry, '(rel ...)' is the entry's
existence in its list.

*** diff object, diff aspect

A *diff object* is what a part of a '(diff ...)' form or a readiness request
names on a headline: the node itself ('node'), or the node's
relationship to its viewparent ('rel'; in a partner folder, to the
folder's viewparent). There are exactly these two.
A change-forest leaf about a relationship has the relationship as its
object, never the member. A request, a '(leaves ...)' fact and a
'(ready ...)' fact may also say 'all', which names every leaf a
modified node records (so one gesture on it marks its file), or every
leaf of a commit readiness aggregate one level down; 'all' is shorthand, not a third
object. A *diff aspect* is what a
change-forest leaf is about: existence, text, home skgrepo, an alias,
an extra ID, a flag, or relRepo.

*** C, R, U, D, content origin

The sets the pruned change view is computed from: C, the changed
nodes; R, the content origins; U, the nodes to extend contentward; D,
the deduplicated content origins. *C*, the changed nodes, is the set of changed
graphnodes, in two halves: a node is changed *out* when its own file
records a change (it is new or deleted, or its text, aliases, extra
IDs, flags, home skgrepo or own relationships changed), and *in* when
its recorders' files do (its set of containers, mentioners,
subscribers, overriders or hiders changed). A node can be both. A
relRepo move puts only the recorder in C, and a change of order alone
puts nothing in it. *R*, the content origins, is the set of content
origins reached from C by walking containerward through the union of
the straddle. A *content origin* is a node with no
container, a multiply-contained node, or a member of a rootlike cycle
(the stop rule of 'server/prominence.rs'). *U*, the nodes to
extend contentward, is R, C and every node on a containerward path
from a node of C to an origin. *D*, the deduplicated content origins,
the roots of the "all changes" buffer, is what remains of R when each
origin, in ID order, is discarded if its own containerward walk within
U reaches another origin still kept; of two origins on one cycle,
exactly one is kept.

A user-visible message that must name one of these sets says its long
name, then the letter in parentheses, then where the algorithm is
described: "the nodes to extend contentward (U; see
'docs/diff-view.org', "C, R, U and D", and the module comment of
'server/diff_view/pruned_change_view.rs')".

*** change-forest, change-forest leaf, recorder of a leaf

The *change-forest* is the picture of everything commit-readiness can
be asked about, built from the graph straddle, independent of any
view: its roots are the changed graphnodes; under each come its
changed aspects; its leaves, the *change-forest leaves*, are the
individual changes a user marks. The type 'ChangeLeaves'
('server/diff_view/change_leaves.rs') holds every leaf, keyed by its recorder,
its aspect and, for a relationship, the relationship ('LeafKey'). The
*recorder* of a leaf is the node whose file records the change, and
that node *records* the leaf; a relationship's leaf is recorded by
the relationship's recorder, so seen from either end it is the same
leaf.

*** mark, fingerprint, readiness

A *mark* says that one change-forest leaf is commit-ready. It is
stored ('<data_root>/readiness-marks.json') with a *fingerprint* of
the change it approved, and a mark whose fingerprint no longer
matches the change counts as absent: editing a marked change unmarks
it. A leaf's *readiness* is whether it is marked.

*** commit readiness aggregate

A *commit readiness aggregate* is a thing with change-forest leaves
under it: a folder, a changeset marker, a node, a file, the whole
changeset. It is ready iff every leaf under it that can be ready is
ready; that derived value is *its readiness*. A view shows it as
'(ready ...)' (in the '(diff ...)' form, or bare on a folder), which
nothing reads at save.

*** changeset marker

A childless property viewnode ('Property::ChangesetMarker') that
stands for a set of changes recorded in its parent's file, and carries
their derived readiness. There are three kinds: the content changeset
marker ("its content changed"), the relRepo changeset marker ("some
content relationships changed skgrepo") and the order-only marker
("order-only change", which is always ready).

*** content position

A viewnode is in *content position* when its viewparent is the buffer
root, an unrestricted vognode, or a historical phantom that is itself
in content position ('in_content_position'). A historical phantom in
content position holds its old content under it.

*** removed member, former member

A *removed member* is an unrestricted vognode, a
[[*non-member][non-member]], with the fact '(rel removed)': its
node survives, but its relationship to its viewparent exists in
the before-graph only. It is encoded so in content, in a partner
folder and in a historical phantom's old content alike. In
content position it follows the editability rule as a member
does: editable at its node's first occurrence in level order,
write-protected at a later one. A *former member* is any viewnode
standing for such a before-only relationship, historical phantoms
included. These phrases, like "stale member" and "new member",
name the node by its membership's fate.

*** change viewnode

A viewnode the diff view adds, at an editable node's visit, to show
a change of that node: its old text, its home skgrepo move, its
changeset markers, its "it acquired" folder, its changed folders, its
removed members, and, under a member, the relRepo move of the
member's relationship. A rerender keeps a change viewnode the shallow
model subtree still produces and converts the rest ([[*the one rule: convert, then prune][the one rule]]).

*** per-visit step

What the diff view does at an editable occurrence's visit, after the
content reconciler ('process_unrestrictedVognode_changes' in
'server/diff_view/changes.rs'): it tags the members with the three
kinds of change, adds the removed members, and for a node in C adds
its change viewnodes.

*** shallow model subtree

The scratch tree a visit builds of what a viewnode should hold, which
the diff view's reconciler matches the viewnode's children against.
It is shallow: it includes other vognodes but not what their own
visits add. A folder's goal list ('FolderGoalItem'), the input of the
folder reconciler, is a flat list of what each member should be, not
a tree.

*** old text

The change viewnode ('(oldText ASPECT)') under a node whose title or
body changed since HEAD: labelled "old title and body", "old title" or
"old body", its headline is the old title and its body the old body.
When the body changed, a "body diff" property under it holds the line
diff. The text change is one change-forest leaf, marked on the old
text.

*** changed folder

A partner, alias, ID or flags folder the diff view shows because its
membership changed since HEAD. It is written '(FOLDER changed)' in
place of the bare folder atom, holds the members of either graph,
each tagged with the three kinds of change, and reads "its X changed
(this file)" or "(other files)" (see "in, out"). Its '(diff ...)'
holds only '(diff (ready))', when every changed membership is ready.

*** member role tree

The role tree extended under each member of a changed partner folder, so
that the user sees where the member sits: under a container, its
containerward role tree; under a mentioner, subscriber, overrider or
hider, the role tree of that role, with a containerward role tree
under each role graft.

*** moved-like-ancestor fact

In the skgrepo-changes buffer, a vognode whose home skgrepo moved
carries '(homeSkgrepoMove (old OLD) (new NEW))' in its viewStats, its
herald "-OLD +NEW". When the nearest vognode ancestor whose home moved
moved the same way, it also carries the *moved-like-ancestor fact*
'(movedLikeAncestor LETTER)', its herald "Δ⌂=a", where the letter
names that ancestor as an ancestor generation does (a, the
viewparent; b, the grandparent; ...).

*** removed alias, removed ID

An alias or extra ID of a node that HEAD has and the worktree lacks.
The diff view shows it in the node's alias or ID folder as its own kind
('Property::RemovedAlias', 'Property::RemovedID'), where the LCS of
the two lists puts it, with the '-' herald. It is save-inert by kind:
the save reads no alias from it and does not count it among the
node's IDs. Marking it marks the leaf of the lost alias or ID.

*** readiness summary

The line a client shows after every save of a diff-view buffer, after the
"integrate unshown changes" command (C-c r u), and before a
stage-and-commit that would leave changes behind: "12 of 14 changed
files are ready", or "Commit ready. All 14 of 14 changed files are
ready." A changed file is ready when every change it records is
marked; a file whose only change is order is ready without marks. The
summary also counts the changed files of foreign skgrepos and the
changes not editable in the view.

*** already-staged entry

An entry git's index already holds when stage-and-commit runs, staged
by a hand or another tool. Stage-and-commit asks for confirmation
before committing a gitrepo that has one, and the git-commit then
includes it.

*** editable in the diff view

A buffer *makes a node's changes editable* when it holds an
editable occurrence of the node, wherever it sits, or the node's
historical phantom in content position
('nodes_editable_in_diffView'). The change-forest leaves those
nodes record are the changes editable in the buffer.

*** changes not editable in the view

The change-forest leaves recorded by the nodes whose changes a
buffer does not
[[*editable in the diff view][make editable]] are the *changes
not editable in the view*, which the readiness summary counts. A
save never makes them editable; C-c r u integrates them.

*** readiness request, readiness gesture

A *readiness request* is a metadata form, '(readyRequest OBJECTS)' or
'(unreadyRequest OBJECTS)', the last part of a headline's '(diff ...)'
form, that the save consumes, after its graph
edits succeed, to set or clear the marks of a headline's leaves. A
*readiness gesture* is a gesture (C-c r r, C-c r h and their
recursive forms) that writes readiness requests on one or many
headlines, then saves.

*** restore request

Either of the two edit requests that undo a removal in the diff
view. '(editRequest restoreNode)', on a historical phantom,
writes its node back as HEAD had it, in-links included;
'(editRequest restoreRel)', on a removed membership (a removed
member in content position, or a member of a partner folder
carrying '(rel removed)'), re-adds that one relationship as HEAD
recorded it. C-c s n ('skg-restore-deleted-node') and C-c s l
('skg-restore-removed-membership') write them, and a save applies
and consumes them as it does every edit request
('docs/diff-view.org', "Restoring what a change removed").

*** change headline

A headline whose metadata carries a '(leaves ...)' fact. It is ready
when its '(ready ...)' names every object its '(leaves ...)' lists,
and otherwise unready. The navigation commands (C-c r n c and its
kin) move between change headlines.

*** in, out, "(this file)", "(other files)"

A relationship is *out* from its recorder (the node whose file records
it) and *in* to its member. In the diff view, a folder of out-relationships
that changed is labelled "its ... changed (this file)", and one of
in-relationships "its ... changed (other files)". Changeset markers
carry no suffix.

** Terms specific to Tantivy

*** no search matching

A node-file flag (=NoSearchMatching= in YAML,
=noSearchMatching= on the wire) that prevents the node's title, aliases, and
body from producing a direct text-search hit. The node remains indexed for
exact-ID lookup and may appear as context around another hit. It is search
decluttering, not privacy.

*** prominent, prominence source, prominence multiplier

Search ranks *prominent* nodes higher. A node's *prominence source*
('ProminenceSource') says why it is prominent: it is a root, a cycle
member, mentioned (linked to), had an ID before import, or is
multiply contained. Each source has a *prominence multiplier* applied
to the text-match score. Most nodes have no prominence source.

*** flags folder and Flag

=flagsFolder= is a generated, write-protected non-vognode under a graphnode. Its
=(flag FLAG)= leaves list the node's true flags in canonical
order. Each leaf is titleless because its herald supplies the visible label.
The folder stays visible when empty. Edits within a retained folder are
rejected before save, but deleting the entire folder safely dismisses the
optional viewbranch and changes no flag. =noSearchMatching= is changed
through the dedicated setter, while the two org-roam import provenance
flags have no setter. A node with =n= true flags has the low-styled =Fn=
herald; no flag herald is shown at zero.

*** Document

A Tantivy association. In my case, from a title or alias to an ID.
(Each ID can have multiple such associations.)
