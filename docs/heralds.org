#+title: Reading Skg heralds and link annotations

* What a herald describes
An Skg headline begins with metadata. In a view, heralds condense that
metadata into short colored tokens. Toggle the compact display with
'skg-view-heralds-mode' (=C-c v h=) in Emacs or ':SkgHeraldsToggle'
(=<localleader>vh=) in Neovim. The expanded metadata is useful when you
need to inspect the exact facts or requests.

Relationship counts describe the latest rendered graph under the active
source-set. They count distinct visible nodes, including visible
relationship edges, and resolve extra IDs. They do not count repeated
links or unsaved buffer edits as new graph facts. A folded body can still
contribute links. A view's position can add ancestor flags and a birth
style, so two occurrences of the same node can have different heralds.

* Relationship tokens

The order is =C L S O H A I P=. =C= means contains, =L= text links to,
=S= subscribes to, =O= overrides the view of, and =H= hides from its
subscriptions. A number before the letter counts nodes pointing at this
node; a number after it counts nodes this node points at. =2C3= thus
means two containers and three direct contents. =C= and =L= are blue;
=S=, =O= and =H= are purple. Missing sides and empty relations are
omitted. =A3= counts aliases, =I2= extra IDs, and =P1= true file
properties; these action counts are cyan.

A yellow numeral inside =C= or =L= has a narrower meaning. In =2/5L3=,
five visible nodes link here, two are interesting, and this node links
to three distinct visible targets. A linking source is interesting if
it has nonempty body text, visible direct content, or links to more
than one distinct visible target. Whitespace-only bodies, repeated
links and alternate IDs for the same target do not add to that count.
The numerator can be zero (=5L3=), equal the total (=5/L3=), or the
only populated side (=L3=). =5L= has no resolved outgoing target;
there is no =L= token when both sides are empty. A self-link counts
when visible.

On a subscribee shown under a subscriber, outgoing =C= has a similar
fraction. =C2/8= means it has eight visible direct contents, two of
which the subscriber has neither directly included nor hidden. =C8=
means none remains unintegrated; =C8/= means all eight do. This count
belongs to that occurrence: another subscriber can give the same
subscribee a different numerator. Outside that role, =C= uses ordinary
counts. The incoming side can combine with it, as in =2C3/8=.

The slash is kept when a nonzero numerator equals its total; its
denominator is then omitted. The slash never means zero divided by
zero. Yellow also marks displayed =O= counts, even a count of one.
The =O= letter keeps its usual purple or birth style.

* Ancestors and birth

Lowercase flags name tracked ancestors: =a= is the visible parent,
=b= the grandparent, and so on, counting folder scaffolds too. After
=z=, a generation appears as ={27}=, ={28}=, etc. An =a= is muted
yellow; =b= and higher are orange. =aC= says the parent
contains this node; =Ca= says this node contains its parent. =Oa=
and =aO= distinguish the direction of an override edge.

A flag replaces a numeral when it fully explains a side. With
fractions, an ancestor appears once: before the slash if it belongs
to the numerator, otherwise after the denominator's numeral. =a/5L=
means the parent is the sole interesting source among five; =1/5aL=
means one other source is interesting and the parent is not. =a/L=
means the parent is the sole incoming source and is interesting;
=aL= means the sole parent source is uninteresting. =La= means the
sole outgoing target is the parent; =L3a= counts three targets,
including the parent. If distinct ancestors =a= and =b= are the only
inbound sources, and only =a= is interesting, =a/bL= expresses one
interesting source out of two. The denominator still includes =a=;
its numeral is omitted because both ancestors account for the total.

A black-on-white relationship letter identifies the relation that put
this occurrence here; its adjacent counts and slash keep their usual
styles. Ordinary =O= facts merely report an edge;
a birth-styled =O= explains this placement. An occurrence can have
more than one birth relation. Immediately after =O=, a pink =ĥ=
(precomposed h with circumflex) means this occurrence substitutes
for an overridden node. =2Oĥ3= is an example. It comes from the
load-bearing 'overridesHere' marker; an override folder or path
occurrence without that marker has no =ĥ=. The eighth ancestor is
plain orange =h=, so =Oĥh= shows the two different markers together.
The marker preserves the original membership ID when a substituted
view is saved. See [[file:override.org][override behavior]].

* Colors and customization

The herald faces in Emacs and their Neovim highlight groups are:

| Meaning | Emacs face | Neovim highlight group | Default style |
|---------+------------+------------------------+---------------|
| Highly interesting counts and markers | 'heralds-highly-interesting-face' | 'SkgHeraldHighlyInteresting' | black on yellow |
| Moderately interesting ancestors =b= and higher | 'heralds-moderately-interesting-face' | 'SkgHeraldModeratelyInteresting' | white on orange |
| Slightly interesting parent =a= | 'heralds-slightly-interesting-face' | 'SkgHeraldSlightlyInteresting' | white on muted olive |
| Confusable =ĥ= | 'heralds-confusable-face' | 'SkgHeraldConfusable' | pink foreground |
| =C= and =L= | 'heralds-blue-face' | 'SkgHeraldBlue' | white on blue |
| =S=, =O= and =H= | 'heralds-purple-face' | 'SkgHeraldPurple' | white on purple |
| Source and folder markers | 'heralds-green-face' | 'SkgHeraldGreen' | white on dark green |
| Requests and deletions | 'heralds-red-face' | 'SkgHeraldRed' | white on red |
| =A=, =I= and =P= counts | 'heralds-cyan-face' | 'SkgHeraldCyan' | black on cyan |
| Birth relation letter only | 'heralds-birth-face' | 'SkgHeraldBirth' | black on white |

Customize an Emacs face with 'customize-face'. In Neovim, override a
highlight group with ':highlight' or 'vim.api.nvim_set_hl' after Skg loads.
Confirmed broken link labels inherit 'heralds-confusable-face' and add
an underline in Emacs; Neovim uses 'SkgBrokenLink' with the same pink
foreground and an underline.

* Other heralds

Green =⌂:LABEL= marks a node's home-source boundary. A red =~NAME=
immediately before it reports a relationship stored at a more private
relSource than its default. A red =request:~NAME= is an explicit
pending request to change that relationship source on save. The
home source belongs to the node; relSource belongs to an edge. Green
folder labels, =ID= and =alias= describe their respective view
positions. The properties view spells out =☮ had ID before org-roam
import=, =☮ was overloaded during org-roam import=, and
=☮ no search matching= when true. =req:folder:...=, =req:path:...=,
=req:properties= and =req:definitive= request a view. Red =delete=,
=merge:...=, and =request:no search matching= or
=request:search matching= are save requests.

=☮= marks a write-protected position, with =B= attached when its body
exists but is hidden. Yellow =⊥= means a displayed child does not
affect its parent's membership on save. Blue =⟳= marks a cycle.
An unknown node is labeled =Reference to unknown node.= in the
interesting yellow style; an inactive source yields an anonymous
=node from inactive source= marker. Inactive placeholders reveal no
node title or source. Red =DELETED= marks a deletion in a diff or
edit, while =diff:not-in-git= marks a node without a Git counterpart.
=staged:= and =unstaged:= prefixes report Git changes; =X= and =-X=
mark existence changes, =M= and =-M= membership changes, and
=text changed= reports text changes. These are view facts unless
specifically identified above as requests.

* Link annotations

The current buffer can display a suffix after each literal
='[[id:ID][LABEL]]'= link with the target's home-source abbreviation.
Use 'skg-toggle-source-overlay-on-links' (=C-c v L=) in Emacs or
':SkgToggleSourceOverlayOnLinks' (=<localleader>vL=) in Neovim.
Suffixes start off and the toggle is local to the buffer. A resolved
target appears in green, for example =[⌂:pub]=. Extra IDs resolve
to their target's home source. =⌂:missing=, =⌂:inactive=,
=⌂:…= and =⌂:unavailable= distinguish missing, inactive, pending
and lookup-error states. The latter states reveal no source name.

Even with suffixes off, the label of a link confirmed missing in the
current graph is pink and underlined. Inactive or still unknown
targets are not marked broken. These display-only annotations do not
change link text, save contents, or exact-ID navigation.
