* Reading Skg heralds and link annotations

** What a herald describes
An Skg headline begins with metadata. In a view, heralds condense that
metadata into short styled tokens. (Decorations on text that is not
metadata, such as the label shown after a link or a title shown beside
an ID in magit, are *annotations*, not heralds.) Toggle the compact display with
'skg-view-heralds-mode' (=C-c v h=) in Emacs or ':SkgHeraldsToggle'
(=<localleader>vh=) in Neovim. The expanded metadata is useful when you
need to inspect the exact facts or requests.

Relationship counts describe the latest rendered graph under the unrestricted
skgrepo-set. They count distinct visible nodes, including visible
relationships, and resolve extra IDs. They do not count repeated
links or unsaved buffer edits as new graph facts. A folded body can still
contribute links. A view's position can add ancestor flags and a birth
style, so two occurrences of the same node can have different heralds.

** Styles
A herald's style states its importance tier, or for a few heralds a
polarity or kind, never its hue. There are ten styles.

| Style       | Look                           | Meaning                         |
|-------------+--------------------------------+---------------------------------|
| crucial     | black on cyan                  | the reader must understand this |
| high        | black on yellow                | high interest                   |
| medium      | white on orange                | medium interest                 |
| low         | white on olive                 | low interest                    |
| normal      | white on blue                  | ordinary                        |
| message     | black on white                 | prose meant to be read          |
| stop        | white on red                   | polarity: bad, or not yet       |
| go          | black on green                 | polarity: good, done            |
| nonstandard | white on purple                | requests and diff facts         |
| yucky       | pink foreground, no background | error-flavored, but not too bad |

The tiers, least to greatest, are normal, low, medium, high and crucial.

Each style has one name everywhere. The looks are defined in
[[file:../shared/herald-styles.json][shared/herald-styles.json]], which both clients read. In Emacs the
style's face is 'heralds-STYLE-face' (for example 'heralds-high-face');
customize it with 'customize-face'. In Neovim its highlight group is
'SkgHeraldSTYLE' (for example 'SkgHeraldHigh'); override it with
':highlight' or 'vim.api.nvim_set_hl' after Skg loads. The Neovim client
assumes 'termguicolors', since the styles are given as RGB colors.

** Relationship heralds
*** Letters and sides
The relations, in display order, are =C= contains, =L= links to, =S=
subscribes to, =O= overrides the view of, and =H= hides from its
subscriptions. A relation's token has an in-side before its letter and
an out-side after it. The in-side counts nodes that relate to this node
("N nodes contain it"); the out-side counts nodes this node relates to
("it contains N nodes"). =2C3= thus means two containers and three
direct contents. Missing sides and empty relations are omitted. The
property counts =A=, =I= and =F= follow the relations.

*** Tiers
Each side has a tier, which says how much it deserves attention.

| Relation              | in-side                         | out-side                                                                   |
|-----------------------+---------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| contains              | medium                          | normal; low on a write-protected occurrence; unintegrated numerator medium |
| links to              | medium; substantive subset high | normal                                                                     |
| subscribes to         | high                            | medium                                                                     |
| overrides the view of | low                             | medium                                                                     |
| hides                 | medium                          | normal                                                                     |

Most normal sides are normal because their members are usually visible
elsewhere: as viewchildren, in the text, or in the hidden-in and
hidden-outside-subscribee folders.

*** Ancestor flags and floors
Lowercase flags name tracked ancestors: =a= is the viewparent, =b=
the grandparent, and so on, counting folders as generations. After =z=,
a generation appears as ={27}=, ={28}=, etc. =aC= says the parent
contains this node; =Ca= says this node contains its parent. A flag
replaces a numeral when ancestors account for the whole side.

Every glyph has a floor, the least tier it shows: =a= is at least low,
=b= and higher at least medium, and every other glyph at least normal.

*** Carriers
A side's tier is shown by one part of the side, its carrier: its
numeral, if it shows one, and otherwise all its ancestor flags. The
carrier shows the greater of its floor and the side's tier; every other
glyph shows just its floor. On the subscribesTo in-side, whose tier is
high, =2aS= draws the 2 high and the 'a' low, while =aS= has no
numeral, so its 'a' carries the tier and is drawn high.

*** Fractions
Two sides can show a subset with a tier of its own, as a fraction.

On the linksTo in-side, the numerator counts substantive mentioners. A
mentioner is substantive if it has nonempty body text, visible direct
content, or links to more than one distinct visible target. In =2/5L3=,
five visible nodes link here, two are substantive, and this node links
to three distinct visible targets. Whitespace-only bodies, repeated
links and alternate IDs for the same target do not count. =5L= has no
resolved outgoing target. A self-link counts when visible.

On a subscribee shown under a subscriber, the contains out-side counts
the contents the subscriber has neither directly included nor hidden.
=C2/8= means eight visible direct contents, two of them unintegrated.
=C8= means none is unintegrated; =C8/= means all eight are. The count
belongs to that occurrence: another subscriber can give the same
subscribee a different numerator.

The numerator gets its own carrier, chosen the same way from its
glyphs; the side's carrier is chosen from the denominator's. The slash
shows the side's tier. =1/5aL= draws the 1 high, the 5 medium and the
'a' low. =a/5L= draws the 'a' high, because it carries the subset's
tier, and the 5 medium. In =5/L= the denominator shows nothing, so only
the slash shows the side's tier. An ancestor appears once: before the
slash if it belongs to the numerator, otherwise after the denominator's
numeral. =a/bL= says both ancestors link here and only 'a' is
substantive.

*** Birth
The letter of a relation that put this occurrence here is drawn high;
its counts keep their own styles. An occurrence can have more than one
birth relation. A side whose only members are ancestors that the birth
relation already accounts for has tier low: =aC= (the parent is the sole
container) draws the 'a' low. A subscribee's =bS= (its subscriber is the
grandparent) draws the 'b' medium, because of b's floor.

Immediately after =O=, a crucial =ĥ= (precomposed h with circumflex)
means this occurrence substitutes for a node it overrides. =2Oĥ3= is an
example. It comes from the load-bearing 'overridesHere' fact; an
override folder or path occurrence without that fact has no =ĥ=. The
eighth ancestor is a plain =h=, so =Oĥh= shows the two glyphs together.
The fact preserves the original membership ID when a substituted view
is saved. See [[file:override.org][override behavior]].

** Other heralds
=☮= marks a write-protected position: a node, a folder, or a flag row.
On a node, =B= rides the ☮ when the node's body exists but is omitted from the buffer.
=⊥= means a displayed child does not affect its parent's membership on
save. =⟳= marks a cycle.

=⌂:LABEL= marks a node's home skgrepo boundary. A =~NAME= immediately
before it reports a relationship stored at a more private relRepo than
its default; =request:~NAME= is an explicit pending request to change
that relationship's skgrepo on save. The home skgrepo belongs to the
node; relRepo belongs to a relationship.

Folder labels, =alias=, =ID= and the flag rows describe their view
positions. The flags folder spells out =had ID before import=, =was
overloaded during org-roam import=, and =no search matching= when true.
=req:folder:...=, =req:roleTree:...=, =req:flags= and =req:editable=
request a view. =delete=, =merge:...=, and =request:no search matching=
or =request:search matching= are save requests.

An unknown node is labeled =Reference to unknown node.=; a restricted
skgrepo yields an anonymous =node from restricted skgrepo= placeholder, which
reveals no node title or skgrepo. =DELETED= marks a deleted node, or a
viewnode whose node was deleted.

** Diff heralds
In Git diff mode, =✓= introduces staged changes and =✗= unstaged ones.
=N= and =-N= mark a node added or deleted; =R= and =-R= a relationship
added or removed. =text changed ✓ ✗= reports a title or body change,
showing whichever marks apply. =diff:not-in-git= marks a node without a
Git counterpart.

** Link annotations
The current buffer can display a suffix after each literal
='[[id:ID][LABEL]]'= link with the target's home skgrepo abbreviation.
Use 'skg-toggle-repo-overlay-on-links' (=C-c v L=) in Emacs or
':SkgToggleRepoOverlayOnLinks' (=<localleader>vL=) in Neovim.
Suffixes start off and the toggle is local to the buffer. A resolved
target shows its skgrepo, for example =[⌂:pub]=. Extra IDs resolve to
their target's home skgrepo. =[⌂:restricted]=, =[⌂:…]= and
=[⌂:lookup failed]= distinguish restricted, pending and lookup-failed
targets, and reveal no skgrepo name. A dangling link gets no suffix.

Even with suffixes off, the label of a link confirmed dangling in the
current graph is yucky and underlined. Restricted or still unknown
targets are not marked broken. These display-only annotations do not
change link text, save contents, or exact-ID navigation.

** Skg's faces in views
Skg dictates every face in a view, in both clients: a black background,
a white default foreground, and Skg's own faces for the Org elements a
view contains, such as headline levels and links. These are colors and
weights, not the typeface, so nothing from the user's theme shows. They
are defined in the view_faces of [[file:../shared/herald-styles.json][shared/herald-styles.json]]. Emacs
remaps them buffer-locally in 'skg-content-view-mode'; Neovim maps them
into a view window with 'winhighlight'.
