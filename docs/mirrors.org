* Fork relationships, substitution, and navigation
#+date: 2026-09-20

** The relationship

If =R= forks =N=, =R= is the /forker/ and =N= is the /forkee/.  The
relationship is stored on =R= as an entry in its =forks= list, which
a '.skg' file writes under the key =forks=:

#+begin_example
R.forks = [N]
#+end_example

The stored value is a =RelPartner<ID>=.  Its =member= is =N= and its
=relRepo= says which telescope section records the relationship.
The relRepo is independent of either endpoint's home skgrepo: two
public nodes can be connected by a =forks= relationship recorded only
in =private=.  The list is ordered, and a more private section places
its members among a more public section's with anchors, as =contains=
does ('docs/api-and-formats.org', "The .skg file format").

=R= forks =N= says two things.  =R= shows =N='s content that =R=
neither contains nor hides, nor keeps in another note of the user's:
in =R='s forks folder, claiming, =N= is a forkee as such
('docs/glossary.org', "forkee as such"; 'docs/forks.org', "What you
keep elsewhere").  And
=R= and =N= are mirrors, so either may stand in the other's place
(see "Mirror substitution").  So a fork shown, editable, in its
original's place draws, by default, its forks folder with the
original in it, and
two notes that fork one note can stand in each other's places.

A forker may fork any number of nodes, and any number of nodes, owned
or not, may fork one node.  Thus =R -> N1= and =R -> N2= are legal, as
are the linear chain =D -> C -> N=, two owned notes forking one
target, and owned mutual forks (=A -> B -> A=).  Mirror substitution,
which ranks a note's mirrors by skgrepo, has no special case for a
branch or a cycle (see "Mirror substitution").

What an owned note may not do, the save and the startup refuse
('docs/data-model_technical.org', "Hard validation constraints"):
fork itself; contain or hide itself; both fork and contain one note;
or contain a note that forks it.

** Direct visits are exact

Visiting an ID opens that exact node as the root of a content view:
from the root homes, its default perspective, the root's own home
skgrepo decides, and there the node stands as itself (see "Which
mirror stands").
It does not redirect to a fork and does not stop at a choice
buffer.  Re-visiting an ID that is already a root switches to its open
content view proper, never to a buffer of the diff view.  A node whose home skgrepo the current
skgrepo restriction keeps out cannot be visited.

This makes every viewnode used for navigation a stable destination.  In
particular, selecting a node in search results or a partner folder
opens the selected node itself.

The root's ordinary =K= relationship herald reports its visible
=forks= facts, and its forks folder shows every mirror of it
(=C-c l f=; drawn unasked on an editable occurrence with a fork
partner that is no member of another forks folder).

** Partner folders in search results

Search hits are raw, write-protected, non-content roots.  In the
search enrichment, each result N gains, folded, its forks folder
(the nodes N forks, then the nodes that fork N, then its other
mirrors), drawn only when it has a member.  The folder is flat, as
every forks folder is ('docs/forks.org', "Every mirror, flat"): no
member shows a forks folder of its own.  The members are
write-protected.  Node visibility and the relationship's relRepo
both gate them.

Search also groups hits that are mirrors of one another (see "Mirror
substitution").  Among them, the hit the user's own skgrepo list
ranks first stands: the list of the perspective of the config's first
owned skgrepo (the most public), then the others in declaration
order, so that among owned hits the most public stands.  Several in
one skgrepo tie: the only one none of the others forks stands, else
the least pid.  If the hit standing is owned, every other hit among
them is suppressed at the top level, and reappears in the forks
folder of the hit standing, which lists every mirror of it, the
indirect ones (related to it only through other mirrors) after its
fork partners.  So every suppressed hit stands in the one forks
folder of the hit that suppressed it (or, where that hit hides it,
in that folder's folder of what it hides outside its fork
partners).
A foreign hit never suppresses another top-level result, although
visible foreign relatives can occur in the folders: a
foreign mutual pair that both match shows both.

** Mirror substitution

Substitution is a recursive-content rendering rule, not a navigation
rule ('server/dbs/in_rust_graph/mirrors.rs').

*** Mirrors

A /fork/ of a note =N= is a note that forks =N=.  The /mirrors/
of =N= are =N= and every note joined to it by a path of =forks=
relationships, followed in either direction, each relationship's
relRepo and each note's home skgrepo shown by the skgrepo
restriction.  So two forks of one note are mirrors of each other, a
note's forkee is one of its mirrors, and a cycle of forks is no
special case.

*** Which mirror stands

Where =N= stands as content of an editable occurrence in a content
view, or as a root of it, the refresh shows the mirror of =N= that
the perspective's skgrepo list ranks first ('mirror_shown').  A
perspective is one skgrepo =S=, or the root homes, one per tree (see
"Perspectives"), and the list of =S=
('SkgConfig::perspective_skgrepo_list') is =S=, then every other
skgrepo in the config's declaration order, whose owned entries run
most public first.  The mirror shown is the one whose home skgrepo
comes first in that list; where several do, the tie is broken thus:

1. One mirror alone in that skgrepo stands.
2. Otherwise =N= itself stands, if it is one of them: two forks of =N=
   in =N='s own skgrepo, an owned chain =D forks C forks N= kept
   there, or a mutual pair in one skgrepo, show =N= as itself.
3. Otherwise, if exactly one of them is forked by none of the
   others, it stands.  A chain =D forks C forks N= kept in one
   skgrepo other than =N='s shows =D= there.
4. Otherwise the one nearest =N= along =forks= relationships, in
   either direction, and among equals the least pid.

When the mirror shown is =N=, nothing substitutes.  Who owns a
skgrepo plays no part: where =S= holds no mirror of =N=, the first
declared skgrepo that holds one decides, owned or foreign.  So in a
config that declares a foreign skgrepo first, from a perspective
holding no mirror of a note the foreign original outranks the user's
own fork; and of a public and a private fork of one note, each stands
from its own skgrepo's perspective, and the public one, declared
first, from any perspective holding neither.  For

#+begin_example
D forks C forks N
#+end_example

with =N= in owned skgrepo =base=, =C= in =mid= and =D= in =top=, the
config declaring =mid=, =base=, =top=, a view drawn from =top= shows
=D= where =N= occurs; under a skgrepo restriction keeping =top= out,
it shows =C=, =mid= being declared before =base=; from =base=, =N=
itself.

*** Perspectives

A view's perspective is one skgrepo, or its root homes, one skgrepo
per tree.  Until the user chooses a skgrepo, each top-level tree of
the view is drawn from its root's home skgrepo, the
/root homes/ ('perspective_skgrepo_of_tree_containing'): the home of
the node the root stands for (its =(inPlaceOf N)=, else itself),
throughout the tree, beneath notes homed elsewhere too.  So a view
of your own note is drawn from your skgrepo, and one of someone
else's from theirs.  A child the user forks in a view of a foreign
note is shown there, after the save, as the mirror the root's home
ranks first, usually the original (the save warns, naming it, and
says whether a perspective would show the clone); the clone is shown
from its own skgrepo's perspective.  Editing the foreign root itself
forks it, and the saved buffer's root becomes the clone (see below),
so that tree then is drawn from the clone's skgrepo.

A view can be switched among perspectives (Emacs 'C-c v o', Neovim
'<localleader>vo'; =docs/api-and-formats.org=, "Set
perspective"): the root homes, a fresh view's, and each skgrepo home
to a visible mirror of some original the view draws where
substitution acts, whose list would show some position of the view
differently, each followed in every tree, whatever the root's home,
in declaration order.  A skgrepo is offered whoever owns it: from a
private fork's skgrepo the view shows the private fork, from a public
one's the public fork.  The client keeps the perspective for its
buffer and names it in each save.  The view's roots are substituted
too, from every perspective: each root shows the mirror its
perspective's list ranks first of the node it stands for, re-pointed
where it stands; from the root homes that is the node itself, since
it stands in its own home ('substitute_roots' in
=server/refresh/visit.rs=). A re-pointed root's
folders are drawn afresh for the node it now shows, as a fresh view of
it draws them ('redraw_folders'): its containers folder; where the root
is editable, its forks folder where it has a fork partner;
and, where each other folder the old root held stood (mentioners,
aliases, forks, ...), the folders of its relation for the new node,
hidden in
the root if the old one was (its members are drawn anew, unfolded),
unless it would be empty and is one the user could type
into, whose emptiness would say the user asked for it. A
write-protected root, such as a search hit, stays so and gains no
content. An ID folder is not redrawn, since no request draws one. So
from the fork's skgrepo's perspective a view of a foreign note the user forked
shows the fork's forks folder, its original there counting what the user has
neither filed nor hidden. Since the view's root is now the fork,
asking to view that fork on the same connection lands in this
view, as following a link to any open root does.

Substitution is disabled in the diff view.  Every refresh of a content
view substitutes, deciding from the buffer and the graph as they are
now, so a raw content occurrence whose node gains a mirror ranked
before it shows that mirror at the next save or rerender, and an
occurrence already shown in another's place is re-pointed whenever it
is not the mirror shown (after a switch of perspective or of the
skgrepo restriction, say).  A re-pointed occurrence's folders are
drawn afresh for the node it now shows, as a re-pointed root's are
('redraw_folders'), since those it held describe the node it showed
before.  When the original itself becomes the
mirror shown, the refresh shows the headline that bore the part as itself, without the
fact, unwarranted and so claiming nothing, and reinstates the
original beside it.  The buffer whose save creates a fork has the post-save
exception described below.

The following positions show the raw node rather than an automatic
substitute:

- the members of a search result's folders;
- partner folder members and the containerward chain;
- ghosts.

When a node for which another mirror would stand is deliberately
shown raw ('is_raw_where_another_mirror_would_stand': a root whose
perspective would show another mirror, or a member of a folder but a
member as such of a forks folder), its immediate content children are
also shown raw.  Substitution resumes at
the grandchildren.  This one-level rule makes the raw node show its own
children rather than another mirror's interpretation of them.

*** The just-saved fork buffer

Once an approved save creates fork =K= from original =F=, the buffer that made
the edit must not continue to show editable =F=: editing it again would request
another fork.  The post-save update therefore makes one saved-view-only
exception to the rule that a root stands for the node it shows (see
"Mirror substitution"): a level-one =F= named by
one of this save's exact =ForkSpec=s is rewritten to =K=
('replace_saved_view_fork_roots' in =server/rerender_pass.rs=).  A
recursive-content occurrence of =F= is replaced by its newly visible
substitute =K= at any refresh, as substitution always does.

The content occurrence carries =(inPlaceOf F)=; the root does not.  The
root case is not ordinary recursive-content substitution; it is the one-time
identity transition of the view which performed the fork, and a root marked
=(inPlaceOf F)= stands for =F=, which the root homes show as itself.  The
re-rooted clone is substituted like any root: from the root homes, it stands
in its own home, so shows; a view switched to another skgrepo shows the mirror
that skgrepo ranks first, and the save warns of it.  After refreshing that viewforest, the server
stores it under the same view ID and recomputes the open-view root index, so
the buffer is thereafter registered as rooted at =K= rather than =F=.

Collateral rerenders do not apply the root exception; their content
occurrences substitute as every refresh's do.  A later independent direct
visit to =F= still opens =F= itself, in accordance with the exact-visit rule.

*** The =inPlaceOf= part

A substituted viewnode carries =(inPlaceOf N)=, where =N= is the
original node for which the displayed viewnode substitutes.  The part
is load-bearing during save: the viewnode contributes =N= to its parent's
stored relationship list rather than its own ID.  Editing the
viewnode's title, body, or folders still edits the node it shows.

The server rejects a forged or stale part whose bearer (the headline
bearing it) is not a mirror of =N=, naming the mirror the tree's
perspective would show (the tamper check).
Any mirror may bear it: which one the refresh shows depends on the
perspective and the skgrepo restriction.  The check reads the mirrors
under no skgrepo restriction, so that a part which was honest when
rendered does not become invalid merely because the skgrepo
restriction changed.

** Heralds and reasons for placement

See [[file:heralds.org][the herald legend]] for the full relationship
notation. A crucial =ĥ= directly after =K= marks actual substitution at
this occurrence.

An ordinary relationship =K= says that the node participates in a
visible =forks= relationship.  A highlighted birth =K= says that a
=forks= relationship is why this particular viewnode is present beneath
its org parent.

Substituted viewnodes carry =forks=
birth information: a mirror shown in =N='s place carries
=(forks out)= when it reaches =N= by forker steps only (it
forks a note that ... forks =N=), =(forks in)= when by
forkee steps only, =(forks out)= where both hold (mutual forks), and
none otherwise (a sibling fork, say).  A
node can have ordinary =K= facts without a highlighted birth =K= when
its placement comes from some other relationship.

** How fork relationships are created

*** Forking

Editing an editable foreign node and saving requests an implicit fork.
After confirmation, Skg creates an owned clone which forks the
foreign original, recording =forks: [N]= once.  It shallow-copies the edited content
IDs and hides original children removed by the edit.  The original is
unchanged.

=skg-fork-node= (=C-c m f=) explicitly forks an owned node, commonly to
add a more-private layer such as =D= over =C=.  Forking a substituted
viewnode forks the node shown on screen and therefore deepens the chain.
Either gesture is refused on a note you already fork, naming your fork
of it ('docs/forks.org', "Once forked: the fork operation's guard");
a second owned fork made otherwise (by a nodeMerge, in a forks
folder, or in a note's file) is saved.

*** Direct editing and imported data

The forks folder is editable: a member typed there makes its
viewparent fork it, and a forkee removed from it ends that =forks=
relationship (a mutual removed from it is hidden too); a forker, or
another mirror (an indirect member), removed from it is hidden by its
viewparent (sharing-model.org, "The forks folder: what a note forks,
and what forks it").  Raw or imported =.skg= data can contain =forks= facts
too.

** Merge behavior

When node =A= acquires node =B=, their outbound =forks=
lists are combined, less any partner that is one of the merged
node's own IDs.  Duplicate partners keep the more-private
relRepo, and each relRepo is clamped no more public than =A='s
home.  References to =B='s primary or extra IDs resolve canonically to
=A= after the merge.

Merge validates the result as a save does: a merge that would leave an
owned note containing a note it forks, or one that forks it, is
rejected ('docs/data-model_technical.org', "Hard validation
constraints").  Two owned notes forking the merged node, or a cycle
of forks, are no reason to refuse.

** Deletion and dangling references

Deleting a node removes its primary and extra IDs from the outbound
relationship lists of owned referencers, including
=forks=.  Foreign files are not rewritten.  The explicit
absent-reference cleanup can remove an exact dangling =forks= partner
from owned data.

A deleted fork shown in its original's place, marked
=(inPlaceOf N)=, under a container the user does not own, still
collects =N= for that container's list
('deleted_mirror_keeps_its_original' in
=server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/predicates.rs=): the
deletion takes the fork out of the graph, not =N= out of the
container, so the container is not forked, and the rerender draws =N=
there again. Under an owned container the deletion removes =N= from
its list too ([[file:./forks.org][forks.org]], "Taking back a fork").

A forkee that is an unknown graphnode cannot substitute,
because there is no node to show.

** Skgrepo-set behavior

The partner folders, the containerward chain, search grouping, =K=
relationship counts, and automatic substitution all
derive their topology from relRepo-gated relationship accessors.

For mirror substitution, a =forks= relationship joins two mirrors
only when its relRepo and both notes' home skgrepos are unrestricted.
A relationship outside the skgrepo restriction therefore cannot
silently select a node which the corresponding folders and heralds
omit, and a restricted note in the middle of a path cuts it.

*** Other relationships

Initial construction of the default =forksFolder= also asks the
relRepo-gated accessors, in both directions, whether the note has a
fork partner ('has_a_fork_partner') before drawing the folder.  A private =forks= relationship between
two public nodes therefore creates neither a member nor an empty default
folder in a public-only view.  An explicitly requested editable folder may
still be created empty, as intended.

The other relationship-driven presentation paths do not have the same
problem:

- =contains= memberships are relRepo-gated before the refresh computes a
  content goal, including what a member as such of a forks folder
  shows (its content less what the note contains or hides, and less
  what the user keeps elsewhere);
- =hides= and the containment memberships used to
  derive the hidden-in and hidden-outside folders are gated in the
  refresh's goals;
- ordinary relationship folders, the containerward chain, herald counts, and
  ancestor heralds use the generic gated accessors for every stored
  node-to-node relationship;
- =linksTo= has no independently movable relationship skgrepo: it is
  derived from the skgrepo node's title and body, so its effective relRepo is
  that node's home skgrepo.

Raw relationship reads in save, merge, deletion, and invariant-checking code
are intentionally full-graph operations rather than skgrepo-set presentation.

** Org export

Org export follows the stored, repo-gated =contains= graph.  It does
not perform interactive mirror substitution.
