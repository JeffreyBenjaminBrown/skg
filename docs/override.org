* Override relationships, substitution, and navigation
#+date: 2026-09-20

** The relationship

If =R= overrides =N=, =R= is the /overrider/ and =N= is the
/overridden/ node.  The relationship is stored on =R= as an entry in
its =overrides= list:

#+begin_example
R.overrides = [N]
#+end_example

The stored value is a =RelPartner<ID>=.  Its =member= is =N= and its
=relRepo= says which telescope section records the relationship.
The relRepo is independent of either endpoint's home skgrepo: two
public nodes can be connected by an override relationship recorded only
in =private=.

An overrider may override any number of nodes, and any number of
nodes, owned or not, may override one node.  Thus =R -> N1= and
=R -> N2= are legal, as are the linear chain =D -> C -> N=, two owned
notes overriding one target, and owned mutual overrides
(=A -> B -> A=).  Mirror substitution, which ranks a note's mirrors by
skgrepo, has no special case for a branch or a cycle (see "Mirror
substitution").

What an owned note may not do, the save and the startup refuse
('docs/data-model_technical.org', "Hard validation constraints"):
override or subscribe to itself; contain or hide itself; both override
(or subscribe to) and contain one note; or contain a note that
overrides (or subscribes to) it.

** Direct visits are exact

Visiting an ID opens that exact node as the raw root of a content view.
It does not redirect to an overrider and does not stop at a choice
buffer.  Re-visiting an ID that is already a root switches to its open
content view proper, never to a buffer of the diff view.  A node whose home skgrepo the current
skgrepo restriction keeps out cannot be visited.

This makes every viewnode used for navigation a stable destination.  In
particular, selecting a node in search results or an override folder
opens the selected node itself.

The root's ordinary =O= relationship herald reports its visible
override facts.  The user can then inspect those facts through the
two override folders, which search results also show.

** Override folders in search results

Search hits are raw, write-protected, non-content roots.  In the
search enrichment, each result N gains, folded, its overridden folder
(the nodes N overrides) and its overrider folder (the nodes that
override N), each drawn only when it has a member.  The members are
write-protected, and show N's direct override relatives only.  Node
visibility and the override relation's relRepo both gate them.

Search also groups hits that are mirrors of one another (see "Mirror
substitution").  Among them, the hit the user's skgrepo list ranks
first stands, ties going as rules 1 and 2 of "Which mirror stands"
say and then to the least pid; if it is owned, every other hit among
them is suppressed at the top level, and one it overrides or that
overrides it directly reappears in one of its two override folders.
A foreign hit never suppresses another top-level result, although
visible foreign override relatives can occur in the folders: a
foreign mutual pair that both match shows both.

** Override folders

The folder command =C-c l o= requests both directions for the node at
point.

*** =overriddenFolder=: what this node overrides

Under overrider =R=, =overriddenFolder= is the editable projection of
=R.overrides=.  Adding or deleting a member edits that stored
list.  This is the direct way to create or remove an override
relationship without forking.

The folder is an editable set, so an explicit request creates it even
when it is empty.  Absence means that the buffer expresses no opinion
about the stored list; a present empty folder means to set the visible
list empty.  The folder's members are /overridden-as-such/: they show
the original target rather than a substitute.  The outbound member is
also the surface from which the relationship's relRepo can be edited.

*** =overriderFolder=: what overrides this node

Under overridden =N=, =overriderFolder= is generated by finding nodes
whose =overrides= list contains =N=.  Its membership is
write-protected because the relationship is stored in each overrider's data.
Deleting a real member is reinstated by the refresh; inserting a
node there does not create an override relationship.  An empty
=overriderFolder= is pruned.

The viewnodes are initially write-protected because the folder membership
is an inverse view.  A viewnode can still be visited, or made editable as
the node itself, without making the inverse membership editable.

*** Initial folder presentation

An ordinary editable node automatically includes only a nonempty
=subscribeeFolder=.  Override folders are never part of its initial
content merely because they would be nonempty; the herald advertises
their existence and the folder command asks for them.

The same narrow rule applies when a hidden-as-such, hider-as-such,
overridden-as-such, or overrider-as-such viewnode becomes editable.  A
subscribee-as-such is the exception: in addition to its ordinary
content, it automatically includes a nonempty folder for content hidden
there.

Under a skgrepo restriction, both override folders omit relationships
whose relRepo is restricted, and show a partner whose home skgrepo is
restricted, where its relationship's relRepo is not, as a restricted
viewnode, which shows nothing of it. Saving the visible subset
preserves memberships stored in restricted telescope sections.

** Mirror substitution

Substitution is a recursive-content rendering rule, not a navigation
rule ('server/dbs/in_rust_graph/mirrors.rs').

*** Mirrors

A /fork/ of a note =N= is a note that overrides =N=.  The /mirrors/
of =N= are =N= and every note joined to it by a path of override
relationships, followed in either direction, each relationship's
relRepo and each note's home skgrepo shown by the skgrepo
restriction.  So two forks of one note are mirrors of each other, a
note's forkee is one of its mirrors, and a cycle of forks is no
special case.

*** Which mirror stands

Where =N= stands as content of an editable occurrence in a content
view, the refresh shows the mirror of =N= that the perspective's
skgrepo list ranks first ('mirror_shown').  The list of the
perspective of an owner =O= ('SkgConfig::perspective_skgrepo_list')
is =O='s skgrepos, most private first, then every other skgrepo in
the config's declaration order.  The mirror shown is the one whose
home skgrepo comes first in that list; where several do, the tie is
broken thus:

1. One mirror alone in that skgrepo stands.
2. Otherwise, if exactly one of them is overridden by none of the
   others, it stands.  An owned chain =D overrides C overrides N=, all
   in one skgrepo, shows =D=.
3. Otherwise =N= itself stands, if it is one of them: two forks of =N=
   in =N='s own skgrepo, or a mutual pair in one skgrepo, show =N= as
   itself.
4. Otherwise the one nearest =N= along override relationships, in
   either direction, and among equals the least pid.

When the mirror shown is =N=, nothing substitutes.  The perspective's
owner need not own the mirror shown: where =O= has no mirror of =N=,
the first by declaration order stands, whoever wrote it.  So, for

#+begin_example
D overrides C overrides N
#+end_example

with =C= and =N= in the user's public skgrepo and =D= in the user's
private one, an unrestricted view from the user's perspective shows
=D= where =N= occurs; restricted to the public skgrepo, it shows =C=.

*** Perspectives

The perspective of a top-level tree of the view is its root's
author's ('perspective_owner_of_tree_containing'): the user, where
the tree's root is a node the user owns (a fork included), and
otherwise the root's author, throughout the tree, beneath a third
author's nodes too. A view rooted at a foreign node shows what its
author would see. So a child the user forks in such a view is shown
there, after the save, as the mirror the author's list ranks first,
usually the original (the save warns, naming it); the clone is shown
where the user's perspective is followed. Editing the foreign root
itself forks it, and the saved buffer's root becomes the clone (see
below), so that tree then is drawn from the user's perspective.

A view can be switched among perspectives (Emacs 'C-c v o', Neovim
'<localleader>vo'; =docs/api-and-formats.org=, "Set overrides
followed"): the root authors', a fresh view's, and the user's and
each owner's of a mirror of some original the view draws where
substitution acts, whose list would show some position of the view
differently, each followed in every tree, whoever wrote its root.
From the user's, a view of a foreign node shows the user's mirror
wherever the user has one, so a child forked there is shown, after
the save, as its clone; from a foreign owner's, the view shows what
that owner would see. The client keeps the perspective for its
buffer and names it in each save. From one owner's
perspective the view's roots are substituted too: each root shows
the mirror that owner's list ranks first of the node it stands for,
re-pointed where it stands; from the root authors', it shows that node raw again
('substitute_roots' in =server/refresh/visit.rs=). A re-pointed root's
folders are drawn afresh for the node it now shows, as a fresh view of
it draws them ('redraw_folders'): its containers folder; where the root
is editable, its subscribee folder with its count where it subscribes;
and, where each other folder the old root held stood (mentioners,
aliases, overrides, ...), that folder for the new node, hidden in
the root if the old one was (its members are drawn anew, unfolded),
unless it would be empty and is one the user could type
into, whose emptiness would say the user asked for it. A
write-protected root, such as a search hit, stays so and gains no
content. An ID folder is not redrawn, since no request draws one. So
from the user's perspective a view of a foreign note the user forked
shows the fork's subscribee folder, counting what the user has
neither filed nor hidden. Since the view's root is now the overrider,
asking to view that overrider on the same connection lands in this
view, as following a link to any open root does.

Substitution is disabled in the diff view.  Every refresh of a content
view substitutes, deciding from the buffer and the graph as they are
now, so a raw content occurrence whose node gains a mirror ranked
before it shows that mirror at the next save or rerender, and an
occurrence already shown in another's place is re-pointed whenever it
is not the mirror shown (after a switch of perspective or of the
skgrepo restriction, say).  When the original itself becomes the
mirror shown, the refresh shows the carrier as itself, without the
fact, unwarranted and so claiming nothing, and reinstates the
original beside it.  The buffer whose save creates a fork has the post-save
exception described below.

The following positions show the raw node rather than an automatic
substitute:

- content-view roots and search-result roots, from the root authors'
  perspective (from one owner's, each shows the mirror that owner's
  list ranks first);
- the members of a search result's folders;
- partner folder members and the containerward chain;
- ghosts.

When a node for which another mirror would stand is deliberately
shown raw ('is_raw_where_another_mirror_would_stand'), its immediate
content children are also shown raw.  Substitution resumes at
the grandchildren.  This one-level rule makes the raw node show its own
children rather than its overrider's interpretation of them.

*** The just-saved fork buffer

Once an approved save creates fork =K= from original =F=, the buffer that made
the edit must not continue to show editable =F=: editing it again would request
another fork.  The post-save update therefore makes one saved-view-only
exception to the rule that roots are shown raw from the root authors'
perspective (a view switched to one owner's perspective shows the mirror of
each root that owner's list ranks first; see "Mirror substitution"): a level-one =F= named by
one of this save's exact =ForkSpec=s is rewritten to =K=
('replace_saved_view_fork_roots' in =server/rerender_pass.rs=).  A
recursive-content occurrence of =F= is replaced by its newly visible
substitute =K= at any refresh, as substitution always does.

The content occurrence carries =(overridesHere F)=; the root does not.  The
root case is not ordinary recursive-content substitution; it is the one-time
identity transition of the view which performed the fork, and a root marked
=(overridesHere F)= stands for =F=, which the root authors' perspective shows
raw.  After refreshing that viewforest, the server
stores it under the same view ID and recomputes the open-view root index, so
the buffer is thereafter registered as rooted at =K= rather than =F=.

Collateral rerenders do not apply the root exception; their content
occurrences substitute as every refresh's do.  A later independent direct
visit to =F= still opens raw =F=, in accordance with the exact-visit rule.

*** The =overridesHere= part

A substituted viewnode carries =(overridesHere N)=, where =N= is the
original node for which the displayed carrier substitutes.  The part
is load-bearing during save: the viewnode contributes =N= to its parent's
stored relationship list rather than the carrier's ID.  Editing the
carrier's title, body, or folders still edits the carrier itself.

The server rejects a forged or stale part whose carrier is not a
mirror of =N=, naming the mirror the tree's perspective would show.
Any mirror is honest: which one the refresh shows depends on the
perspective and the skgrepo restriction.  The check reads the mirrors
under no skgrepo restriction, so that a part which was honest when
rendered does not become invalid merely because the skgrepo
restriction changed.

** Heralds and reasons for placement

See [[file:heralds.org][the herald legend]] for the full relationship
notation. A crucial =ĥ= directly after =O= marks actual substitution at
this occurrence; ordinary override folders have no =ĥ=.

An ordinary relationship =O= says that the node participates in a
visible override relationship.  A highlighted birth =O= says that an
override relationship is why this particular viewnode is present beneath
its org parent.

Substituted viewnodes and override-folder members carry override
birth information: a mirror shown in =N='s place carries
=(overrides out)= when it reaches =N= by overriding only (it
overrides a note that ... overrides =N=), =(overrides in)= when by
being overridden only, and none otherwise (a sibling fork, say).  A
node can have ordinary =O= facts without a highlighted birth =O= when
its placement comes from some other relationship.

** How override relationships are created

*** Forking

Editing an editable foreign node and saving requests an implicit fork.
After confirmation, Skg creates an owned clone which subscribes to and
overrides the foreign original.  It shallow-copies the edited content
IDs and hides original children removed by the edit.  The original is
unchanged.

=skg-fork-node= (=C-c m f=) explicitly forks an owned node, commonly to
add a more-private layer such as =D= over =C=.  Forking a substituted
viewnode forks the carrier on screen and therefore deepens the chain.
Either gesture is refused on a note you already fork, naming your fork
of it ('docs/forks.org', "Once forked: the fork operation's guard");
a second owned overrider made otherwise (in the overridden folder, or
by a nodeMerge) is saved.

The subscription and override are paired by the fork operation, not by
the data model.  Removing either relationship later does not
automatically remove the other.

*** Direct editing and imported data

Adding a member to an owned node's =overriddenFolder= creates an
override relationship without creating a subscription.  Raw or
imported =.skg= data can likewise contain existing override facts.

** Merge behavior

When node =A= acquires node =B=, their outbound =overrides=
lists are combined.  Duplicate partners keep the more-private
relRepo, and each relRepo is clamped no more public than =A='s
home.  References to =B='s primary or extra IDs resolve canonically to
=A= after the merge.

Merge validates the resulting owned override graph atomically.  If
owned =R1= overrides =A= and owned =R2= overrides =B=, merging =A= and
=B= would make two owned nodes override one canonical target.  The
merge is rejected and identifies the participants so the user can
establish an explicit chain first.  Cycle creation is rejected for the
same reason.  Merge does not create a subscription-and-override pair.

** Deletion and dangling references

Deleting a node removes its primary and extra IDs from the outbound
relationship lists of owned referencers, including
=overrides=.  Foreign files are not rewritten.  The explicit
absent-reference cleanup can remove an exact dangling override partner
from owned data.

A deleted overrider shown in its original's place, marked
=(overridesHere N)=, under a container the user does not own, still
collects =N= for that container's list
('deleted_overrider_keeps_its_original' in
=server/from_text/local_fieldintent_collection/predicates.rs=): the
deletion takes the overrider out of the graph, not =N= out of the
container, so the container is not forked, and the rerender draws =N=
there again. Under an owned container the deletion removes =N= from
its list too ([[file:./forks.org][forks.org]], "Taking back a fork").

An override target that is an unknown graphnode can otherwise remain
visible as an unknown viewnode, as a folder member.  It cannot substitute because there is no node to show.

** Skgrepo-set behavior

Override folders, the containerward chain, search grouping, =O=
relationship counts, and automatic substitution all
derive their topology from relRepo-gated relationship accessors.

For mirror substitution, an override relationship joins two mirrors
only when its relRepo and both notes' home skgrepos are unrestricted.
A relationship outside the skgrepo restriction therefore cannot
silently select a node which the corresponding folders and heralds
omit, and a restricted note in the middle of a path cuts it.

*** Other relationships

Initial construction of the default =subscribeeFolder= also obtains its
members through the generic directional, relRepo-gated accessor before
deciding whether the folder would be empty.  A private subscription between
two public nodes therefore creates neither a member nor an empty default
folder in a public-only view.  An explicitly requested editable folder may
still be created empty, as intended.

The other relationship-driven presentation paths do not have the same
problem:

- =contains= memberships are relRepo-gated before the refresh computes a
  content goal, including the contains-minus-hides treatment of a subscribee;
- =hidesFromSubs= and the containment memberships used to
  derive the hidden-in and hidden-outside folders are gated in the
  refresh's goals;
- ordinary relationship folders, the containerward chain, herald counts, and
  ancestor heralds use the generic gated accessors for every stored
  node-to-node relationship;
- =linksTo= has no independently movable relationship skgrepo: it is
  derived from the skgrepo node's title and body, so its effective relRepo is
  that node's home skgrepo.

Raw relationship reads in save, merge, deletion, and invariant-checking code
are intentionally full-graph operations rather than skgrepo-set presentation.

** Org export

Org export follows the stored, repo-gated =contains= graph.  It does
not perform interactive override substitution.
