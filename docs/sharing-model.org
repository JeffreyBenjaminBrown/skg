* How sharing works in skg

Each author or skgrepo you want access to should be downloaded to some local folder. Some of these notes will, presumably, be ones you wrote; others can be ones others wrote. Your own skgrepos form a privacy ladder, and a single node of yours can live at several privacy levels at once — see [[file:./telescopes.org][telescopes.org]] for that half of the sharing story: what a restricted view shows, how to privatize a single relationship, and what can leak.

You can navigate and edit them as if you owned them all. When you "modify" someone else's original note, you actually create a clone of it, in your data rather than theirs. The clone is implemented as an ordinary note just like the original, with the same text and branches, and with a =forks= relationship to the original: the clone forks the original. You can change the text of the clone, rearrange its branches, discard them, scatter them across your other files.

*The gesture.* You do not run a clone command. You make the foreign node /editable/ with =C-c s e=, edit it, and save. Any real change to a foreign node is read as a request to fork it; the save first shows a confirmation buffer — each clone-to-be over the original it forks — and waits for your approval (=C-c C-c= to approve, =C-c C-k= to decline). Each clone needs a skgrepo you own: if your edit already specified one (explicit owned skgrepos on new children), it is shown settled; otherwise the client prompts you to choose, suggestion pre-filled (=C-c s s= on the clone-to-be also works), and approving before every clone's skgrepo is set is refused. See [[file:./forks.org][forks.org]] for the walkthrough.

All of the original content is considered "integrated" into the clone. Branches you then delete from the clone, or move elsewhere, become branches the clone "hides"; these hides are recorded explicitly in the filesystem on save. There is no need to /also/ record that the clone's own integrated content is hidden from its forks — the display rule already excludes it. *A forkee as such shows only what its forker neither hides nor contains.* So any branch that appears as the clone's ordinary integrated content does not also show up as unintegrated forked content. (This is also why a /fresh/ clone — whose =contains= equal the original's — shows empty unintegrated content until the original gains new children. And why moving a forked child into the clone's own graph, which deliberately records no hide, still drops it from the forkee as shown in the clone's forks folder: it is now contained.)

If, later, the other author adds content to their note, it will be visible from the clone, because the clone forks the original, as "unintegrated forked content". You can if you wish proceed to incorporate that content into yours, too.

Because nodes in Skg are represented as flat files, this way of forking foreign data duplicates very little of it. For instance, if the original had two branches when the clone was made, the clone would only need to =contain= two IDs. None of the descendents of those IDs need to be mentioned, unless and until one of them is also modified.

** Editing forked content in a view

The viewchildren of a forkee as such can be edited (in limited ways) to control what its forker hides from it.

Let's call the forker R and the forkee E: R forks E. In a view where R is editable, E stands in R's forks folder drawn editable, as content is (unless another occurrence of E in the buffer is editable, or the node budget is spent), showing its content less what R contains or hides.

If N is graph-content of E, and you delete N as a viewchild of E, you have indicated to Skg that you would like to hide N from R's view of E. Skg obliges by creating a hides relationship from R to N. This does /not/ change what E graph-contains; it just changes how R views E. (Remember, it's likely that E is not even something you own.)

If you later put that same child back among E's children in R's forks folder and save, the hide is removed.

This rule only applies to the forkee's children. Edits to deeper descendants have the usual effect, subject to ownership.

Only an R you own records hides: where R is foreign, a save that would hide or unhide anything in R is refused, naming R.

If you type a new headline under E, or move there a node E does not contain, it joins E's content, right after the child it follows, when you own E; under a foreign E the save is refused, since only E's owner can change its content (make E your own first, from a view of E as itself, where an edit starts the fork operation).

Title and body edits to E made there are saved when you own E. A foreign E's are refused: make them from some view that does not present E as a forkee as such (C-c g RET from E will open such a view), where an edit starts the fork operation.

Two generated folders show what R currently hides. Under E, the hidden-in folder (=hiddenInForkPartnerFolder=, "☮ It contains these, but c hides them.") lists what R hides that E contains; it is a write-protected display, and editing it is not how you hide or unhide forked content. To hide something, remove it from E's immediate visible graph-content in R's forks folder. To unhide it, put it back there. Last in R's forks folder, the hidden-outside folder (=hiddenOutsideOfForkPartnersFolder=, "b hides these, but no fork partner shown contains them.") lists what R hides that no fork partner shown in that folder contains (no forkee, and no forker R does not hide), a forker R hides among them; it is editable, and removing a member there unhides it (=glossary.org=, "compound partner folder").

** Forking: seeing your version where the original would appear

When you clone a foreign node, the clone =forks= the original. The clone is the /forker/, the original the /forkee/, and the two are /mirrors/ of each other. That changes what gets shown: wherever the original would appear as recursive content of some other node, Skg shows the mirror the view's perspective ranks first in its place. This is /mirror substitution/. So once you have cloned =mystical=, every view of yours that would have shown Egg Man's =mystical= as content shows your =eggs / mystical= instead. A view rooted at a node of Egg Man's shows what Egg Man would see: his own forks, not yours (below).

Substitution is careful in four ways:

- *It round-trips to the original on save.* The shown fork carries an =inPlaceOf= part (shown as pink =ĥ= after =K=; see [[file:./heralds.org][the herald legend]]) naming the node it stands for. When you save the container, Skg records the /original/'s ID in the container's content list, not the fork's, so showing your version never silently rewrites someone's contains list. Under a container that is not yours, deleting the shown fork deletes it and leaves the original in the list, so that container is not forked, and the original is drawn there again; under one of yours, the deletion removes the original from the list too, as removing the fork's headline does anywhere ([[file:./forks.org][forks.org]], "Taking back a fork").
- *It is one level deep.* A node that is itself shown raw — a member of a folder other than a forkee as such (a member of a forks folder that claims), or a view root — shows its own children raw. You are looking at the original there, so its children are the original's. Substitution resumes one level further down.
- *A view follows one author's perspective: its root's.* Under a root of your own (a clone included), the mirror your list of skgrepos ranks first stands: your own skgrepos, most private first (those under your =owned= folder; see =docs/telescopes.org=, "Ownership and folders"), then every other in the config's order (=docs/mirrors.org=, "Which mirror stands"). So your fork of a note stands in its place, and if someone else's node forks one of yours, yours still stands; their fork is a display-only fact there (it can earn an herald). Under a root of someone else's, that author's list ranks the mirrors, throughout the view, even beneath a third author's notes: you see what they would see. So a node you fork in such a view is shown there, after the save, as the mirror that author's list ranks first, usually the original; your clone is shown where your perspective is followed. To see your version in context, switch the view to your perspective (=C-c v o= in Emacs, =<localleader>vo= in Neovim, which cycles through every author whose forks would show the view differently, then back), view your clone of the root, or edit the root, which forks it and makes the clone the view's root.
- *Visibility gates substitution.* A fork whose skgrepo the current skgrepo restriction keeps out cannot be shown, so it does not substitute; the original is shown instead. Switching skgrepo-sets can therefore change which node a view shows.

Any number of notes, owned or not, may fork one node, and forks may form chains and cycles: you may fork your own fork, so =D forks C forks N= (all owned) is fine, and so are two of your notes forking one node, or two forking each other. Which of them a view shows in a node's place is mirror substitution's choice (=docs/mirrors.org=, "Which mirror stands"), which has no cycle case. The motivation for chains is layered forks — clone a foreign node into a public clone =C=, then fork =C= again into a private =D= that forks it (see =docs/forks.org=, the explicit fork gesture). What an owned note may not do, checked both when you save and when the databases are rebuilt, is fork itself, contain or hide itself, or contain a note it forks or that forks it (=docs/data-model_technical.org=, "Hard validation constraints").

The node Skg shows for =N= is the mirror of =N= (=N= or a note joined to it by =forks= relationships, either way) that the perspective's list of skgrepos ranks first; within one skgrepo, an owned chain shows its end (=docs/mirrors.org=, "Mirror substitution"). Under a skgrepo restriction that keeps out the chain's end, the shown node is a /middle/ of the chain; its =(inPlaceOf N)= part is still honest, as any mirror's is, and the save still round-trips =N= into the container's contains.

When you ask for a brand-new content view of a node, Skg opens the requested node itself, shown raw. It does not show one of the root's mirrors in its place, until you switch the view to an author's perspective (=C-c v o=), which shows that author's mirror of the root, as of every other node. Fork facts remain visible in the heralds (the =K= token); automatic substitution applies only when the original occurs as recursive content.

** The forks folder: what a note forks, and what forks it

A view of a note N shows, unasked wherever N has one, its =forksFolder= ("It forks these, or these fork it."): every note N forks (its forkees, in N's own order) and then every note that forks N and that N neither forks nor hides (its forker-only members), each once. A note that both forks N and is forked by it, a /mutual/, stands once, among the forkees. The heralds tell them apart: =bK= (N forks it), =Kb= (it forks N), =bKb= (both).

A member claims membership exactly when N forks it, and the save sets N's =forks= to the claiming members. So:
- typing a note into the folder makes N fork it;
- deleting a forkee stops N forking it;
- deleting a forker-only member hides it in N: it leaves the forks folder for the folder of what N hides outside its fork partners ("b hides these, but no fork partner shown contains them."), the last thing in the forks folder; removing it from there unhides it, and it is drawn in the forks folder again;
- deleting a mutual stops N forking it and hides it, likewise; detaching it (=C-c s t=) only stops the fork, and it stands on as a forker, claiming nothing;
- attaching a forker-only member (=C-c s a=) makes N fork it: a mutual;
- deleting a member shown as "restricted" (a note the skgrepo restriction keeps out) changes nothing: the save leaves the forks and hides involving it as they are, and the member is drawn again.
A forker-only member's record of its fork is in its own file, so the relRepo gesture refuses on it; to make it stop forking N, edit it, not N.

In a content view a forker-only member (a /forker as such/) is drawn as a forkee as such is: editable, unless it is a repeat or the node budget is spent, showing what it adds to N's content (its content less what N contains or hides), with its own folders. Under it stand the hidden-in folder, of what N hides of its content ("☮ It contains these, but c hides them."), and the hidden-by folder (=hiddenByForkerFolder=, "☮ a hides these, which c contains."), of what it hides of N's content: what that forker deleted from your note. Both are write-protected displays; the forker's hides are its owner's to edit, in its own view. Its title and body are saved when you own it and refused when it is foreign; a headline typed under it, or a node moved there, joins its content when you own it. The save never reads what a forker shows as its whole list, so nothing N contains or hides is ever removed from the forker. Moving a child out of its hidden-in folder to stand under it unhides that child in N, as under a forkee. Cutting a child from it makes N fork it ("If it is cut from a forker-as-such, that forker becomes a forkee, too."): the forker becomes a mutual, redrawn among the forkees, claiming, without its hidden-by folder (drawn under a forker only), and N hides the child unless N contains it after the save. So cutting a child from a forker and pasting it into N's own content takes that child into N, hides nothing, and makes N fork the forker, to follow what else it adds. The fork is recorded in N's file, so under a foreign N such a save is refused, as a cut from a forkee there is.

N's hides apply to its forkers' content as to its forkees': hides are symmetric. A hide of something only a forker contains stands under that forker, in its hidden-in folder, and not in the hidden-outside folder, since it has a reason: the forker would show it. Likewise, a child you remove from N's own content that a forker (one N does not hide) still contains is hidden in N, as one a forkee contains is, so that it does not come back under that forker; and that hide is recorded no more publicly than the forker's records (its =forks= record of N and its =contains= record of the child): the forker is a partner on record for it (=glossary.org=, "hides").

** The write-protected folders: who hides this node

A view of a node N can also show, as generated org folder viewnodes, the nodes that point /at/ N:

- a =hiderFolder=: the nodes that hide N from their forks.

These are /write-protected sets/ (see =glossary.org=). Their membership is generated from graph facts, not edited directly: you may reorder the members and the order is respected within that view, but inserting a node relabels it independent (it does not create the relationship), and deleting a member only makes it respawn on save. To actually add or remove one of these relationships, edit the node at the /other/ end — the hider — not N's folder. No child of such a folder claims membership, so the save reads nothing there: a save restores a deleted member and keeps an inserted child, shown unwarranted (⊥).
