* Privacy telescopes: one node, many privacy levels

Skg lets a single node live at several privacy levels at once. The
mechanism is the *privacy telescope*: same-ID =.skg= files across
your skgrepos, each file one *telescope section* holding the slice
of the node recorded at that skgrepo's privacy level. There is no linking
relationship between the sections — sharing an ID /is/ being the
same node. (Why "telescope"? Because it extends one section at a
time: a skgrepo widening to the next privacy level pulls out
exactly one more section per node, whereas an accordion — the
term's rejected predecessor — expands uniformly.)

** The privacy order

Your =skgconfig.toml= lists the skgrepos you own in privacy order,
most public first (see =api-and-formats.org=). That order is the
ladder of *privacy levels*. Foreign skgrepos have no level: privacy
is about what you share and withhold, and a foreign skgrepo is not
yours to share or withhold, so they form an unordered set beside the
ladder and may be listed anywhere. A *skgrepo-set* is a prefix of
the ladder — choosing one is answering "what is the most private
skgrepo I want visible right now?" — and the most private skgrepo's
set is the whole ladder. Apart from that, the foreign skgrepos are
shown or hidden, all at once; they are shown unless you hide them.
The whole ladder with the foreign skgrepos shown is no skgrepo
restriction. Screen-sharing with a colleague, you might switch to
the =public= prefix; alone, back to the most private skgrepo.

** What lives where

The node's *home* is its most public retained section. It holds the title,
body, and extra IDs. That the home is the section with the title is
an invariant rather than a definition: it would be silly to share a
node without sharing its text, so a node's text always lives in its
most public section. Data that breaks the rule — a title or body
selected below the home section — still loads, but the composition reports
it. An interactive save offers Abort or Hoist before changing
anything. Hoist explicitly moves the selected title and body to
home, removes lower title/body copies, preserves lower relationships and
aliases, then rereads the files and requires the telescope to be
clean before updating the in-memory graph or session-lifetime stores.
Abort leaves every file alone; manual repair requires making the same
title/body-only edits in the =.skg= sections. Noninteractive and
maintenance writers have no implicit Hoist answer and refuse. Every
relationship (a =contains=
membership, a subscription, a hide, an override) carries its own
relRepo: the skgrepo of the section that records it.

Reading the node *composes* the sections, most public first, into one
effective node. (A foreign node has no privacy levels here; its
sections fold in the order its publisher's manifests give — see
"Ownership and folders", below.) Under a restricted skgrepo-set you see the *visible
composition* — only the unrestricted sections' contributions — while with no
skgrepo restriction you see the *full composition*. A more private section can both /add/ members and
/place/ them: its members interleave into the more public list via
placement anchors, so your private ordering of a public list
survives without the public file recording anything. Saving decomposes
the node back into sections, byte-stably: sections you did not
affect do not change on disk, and foreign sections are never
written.

A telescope's sections are all one owner's: yours, or one foreign
owner's. Sections of one PID in skgrepos of different owners are not
one telescope; see "Illegal edits", below.

If composing has to select a title or body below home, Skg marks the
whole telescope *overPrivateText*. An unrestricted connection displays that
text with a warning. A restricted skgrepo-set asks before releasing it, using an
approval scoped to the exact PIDs and the single request; search asks
whether to include or exclude overPrivateText telescopes before it matches. This
release approval does not authorize Hoist. Conversely, approving
Hoist does not become a standing permission to display unrelated overPrivateText
text. Saved and collateral views are rendered in memory and checked
before their text is streamed or their open-view registry entries are
changed.

** Where new relationships land: the sticky-else-default rule

When you save, each relationship keeps the relRepo it already had
on disk (*sticky*), unless an =(editRequest (relRepo ...))= request explicitly
names another skgrepo at least as private as the relationship's default. A new
relationship between owned nodes defaults to the more-private endpoint home —
the most public skgrepo that leaks neither endpoint. A new relationship from
an owned recorder to a foreign member instead defaults to the recorder's
home, regardless of the foreign home: Skg never proposes writing the
foreign skgrepo. This deliberately exposes the foreign node's ID and
the relationship to readers of the owned skgrepo. An explicit
An explicit request may select any configured, owned skgrepo at least as private
as that recorder home. Every relRepo is clamped to be no more
public than the recorder's home (a section more public than the home
would imply a title-less public face). Hides floor higher:
a hide reveals that you hide something, so it must be at least as
private as both endpoints and the most public explanation of it. An
explanation is a subscription whose subscribee contains the hidden
node, and stands at the more private of the subscription's relRepo
and the relRepo of that contains relationship, so a public hide never
names a private membership.

** Setting a relationship's skgrepo

=skg-set-relRepo= (=C-c s r=, see =docs/COMMANDS.org=;
formerly =skg-privatize-relationship=) sets the relRepo of the
relationship the headline at point represents. It asks the server
for the relationship's default and current relRepos (the "relRepo info"
endpoint, see =api-and-formats.org=) and offers the skgrepos at least
as private as the default. A relationship sitting more private than its default is
marked with a red =~NAME= herald and a display =(relRepo NAME)= fact;
the pending write is =(editRequest (relRepo NAME))=. The server re-checks at save that any offered skgrepo is no
more public than the relationship's default. The canonical use: your public
reading-list node contains a book you would rather not advertise —
privatize the /membership/ and the book stays public, the list
stays public, but the relationship between them lives in your private
section.

The same gesture lowers privacy: choose the default or any
more-private skgrepo, and the next save moves the relationship's membership
line. For an owned-to-owned relationship there is nothing more public to
choose — that would leak the more-private endpoint's ID. To publicize
such a relationship further, publicize the more-private endpoint
(move its home) first; the relationship's default follows it. Note that
removing the atom is not a way toward the default: no atom means "no
opinion", so a saved relationship keeps its sticky skgrepo.

A legacy or hand-authored relationship can already sit /more public/ than its
default. Such a relationship renders with the atom (it is off-default), saves
back unchanged, and can be made more private — but never moved still
more public.

** What cannot be expressed

Two things are documented inexpressibles, by design:

- *Private links.* A link lives in the body, and the body
  lives in the home section. A link in a public body is public;
  there is no per-link relRepo. Keep the sentence in a
  private child instead.
- *Moving a relationship more public than its default.* As above:
  a relationship may move to a more-private skgrepo, never to one
  more public than its default.

** What leaks, and what repair fixes

A leak is data you intend to share revealing data you intend not to
share, so only your own files can leak: an owned skgrepo naming a node
whose home is a more private owned skgrepo is a "leak-shaped
membership", while nothing a foreign skgrepo records is a leak, whatever
it names, and neither is an owned skgrepo naming a foreign node. A
leak-shaped membership can arrive from a hand edit or a pull, and it is what
this model exists to prevent. The server warns about these
(=telescope-warnings.org= in your data root) rather than erroring: a
pull must never brick a skgrepo. Repair one with
=skg-set-relRepo= (=C-c s r=), which moves the
membership into the right section. What no repair can fix: if a
public skgrepo ever /committed/ a leaked ID, its git history still holds
it; rewriting history is manual.

(A wholesale migration, =skg-migrate-to-telescopes=, existed while
pre-telescope data did. It is buried in
=_burying/not_using/obsolete/migrate-to-telescopes/=; every user now
starts with telescopes in place.)

** Ownership and folders

A skgrepo is yours unless its config entry says =foreign = true=;
no folder name is special, and a foreign entry may sit anywhere in
the list — see =api-and-formats.org=, "Skgrepos". Only
owned sections are ever written; a save that empties an owned
section deletes the file, and re-privatizing later recreates it.
Each owned skgrepo also receives a generated =OTHER_SKGREPO_DEPENDENCIES.toml=
naming the rungs of your ladder it may reference (itself and every
more public owned skgrepo), so a skgrepo you share carries its own
dependency claim. It never names a foreign skgrepo.

A receiver, to whom those skgrepos are foreign, has no order of its
own for them; the manifests supply it, and say whose they are. Two
foreign skgrepos that manifests chain together (one's manifest lists
the other, directly or through others) are one owner's ladder, and a
node with sections in both is a telescope of that owner's, folded in
the manifest's order. Should two manifests disagree about a pair, the
receiver folds it as the manifest of the more private one says (the
one whose own manifest lists the other) and warns at startup, naming
the node. Foreign skgrepos no manifest chains are different owners'.
See =api-and-formats.org=, "OTHER_SKGREPO_DEPENDENCIES.toml".

** Illegal edits

A node belongs to one owner, and only its owner writes it: a save
never writes into another owner's node (an edit to a foreign node
forks it). A section of a node in a skgrepo of another owner than its
home's, which only a hand-written file can make, is therefore an
*illegal edit to a foreign node*, not part of the node. Which section
is the node: where your skgrepos and a foreign one share a PID, the
foreign one, since only you can have written into another's node here;
where two foreign owners' skgrepos share one, the owner of the one
listed first in the config.

The server reports each at startup, naming the node and the skgrepo
holding the illegal section ("Server warnings" in
=api-and-formats.org=; the per-node report is
=telescope-warnings.org=). It never edits the file. Beyond that:
- An illegal section in one of your skgrepos — one you wrote into
  someone else's node — is shown to you alone, under every occurrence
  of the node, as a child headline heralded *ILLEGAL EDIT TO FOREIGN
  NODE*, holding its title, body and what it contains
  (=illegalEditFolder=, write-protected and never saved). It is no
  part of the node: the node's owner, and everyone else, does not see
  it. Move what it says into a node of your own, and delete the file.
- An illegal section in a foreign skgrepo is ignored.
