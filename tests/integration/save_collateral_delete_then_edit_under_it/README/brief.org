A save in one buffer deletes a node that another buffer shows, and
that other buffer, now holding tombstones, is then edited and saved
twice. 'detail.org' says what each phase checks.

The data (titles equal IDs, as usual in tests): node 1 contains 11;
11 subscribes to subee, hides subee-1 and also-hidden, and has the
alias "eleven"; subee contains subee-1; also-hidden is related to
nothing else.

Buffer 1, a view of 1 with 11's alias folder requested, and a second
root, subee, appended by hand:

#+begin_example
  * 1
  ** 11
  *** (aliasFolder)
  **** (alias) eleven
  *** (subscribeeFolder)
  **** subee
  **** (hiddenOutsideOfSubscribeeFolder)
  ***** also-hidden
  * subee
  ** subee-1
#+end_example

Buffer 2, written by hand:

#+begin_example
  * (writeProtected) 11
  * subee
  ** subee-1
#+end_example

Buffer 2 makes 11 editable, asks to delete it, and saves. Buffer 2
loses its root 11, a childless tombstone, which the refresh prunes.
Buffer 1, rerendered after the save, reads:

#+begin_example
  * 1
  ** (tombstone)        ;; where 11 stood
  *** (tombstone)       ;; where its subscribee folder stood
  **** subee
  **** (tombstone)      ;; where its hidden-outside folder stood
  ***** also-hidden
  * subee
  ** subee-1
#+end_example

Then buffer 1 adds subee-2 under its root subee and saves; both
buffers show subee-2 under their subee root. Last, buffer 1 adds a
root, new-root, and saves; the tombstones come back unchanged, and
new-root gets an ID.
